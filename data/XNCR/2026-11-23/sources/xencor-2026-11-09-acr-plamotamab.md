# Xencor Presents Initial Plamotamab Data in Rheumatoid Arthritis at ACR Convergence 2026 and Updates Progress in First-in-Human Study of XmAb657

-- Subcutaneous plamotamab (CD20 x CD3) demonstrated deep B-cell depletion and reductions in disease activity at Week 12 in higher-dose cohorts --

-- XmAb657 (CD19 x CD3) demonstrated dose-dependent B-cell reductions in healthy participants; patient enrollment planned to begin in the first quarter of 2027 --

PASADENA, Calif. --(BUSINESS WIRE)--Nov. 9, 2026-- Xencor, Inc. (NASDAQ:XNCR), a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company developing engineered antibodies for the treatment of cancer and autoimmune diseases, today announced initial dose-escalation data from its ongoing Phase 1b study of subcutaneous plamotamab, a bispecific CD20 x CD3 T-cell engager, in adults with rheumatoid arthritis. The data are being presented in a poster session at the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) Convergence 2026 conference in Orlando, Florida. Xencor also provided a progress update on its ongoing first-in-human study of XmAb657, a bispecific CD19 x CD3 T-cell engager, in healthy participants, with enrollment of patients with severe autoimmune diseases planned to begin in the first quarter of 2027.

As of the September 21, 2026 data-cut, nine patients with active rheumatoid arthritis had enrolled across four dose cohorts. Dose Levels 1 and 2 (DL1 and DL2) enrolled one patient each, Dose Level 3 (DL3) enrolled three patients, and Dose Level 4 (DL4) enrolled four patients. DL1 through DL3 evaluated single doses, and DL4 evaluated a two-dose regimen.

Mean disease duration was approximately 16 years, and all patients were receiving conventional synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs at baseline. Across the cohorts, patients had substantial baseline disease activity, with a mean Clinical Disease Activity Index (CDAI) score of 36.4. Eight patients had a Disease Activity Score using 28 joints and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (DAS28-ESR) above 5.1 (high disease activity), and one had a DAS28-ESR score above 3.2 and at or below 5.1 (moderate disease activity). At baseline, all patients were positive for anti-citrullinated protein antibody (ACPA), and five patients were positive for rheumatoid factor (RF).

Subcutaneous plamotamab demonstrated deep B-cell depletion following single and multiple doses, with greater than 99% reduction of total B cells and a longer duration of depletion observed following multiple doses. Greater depth and duration of B-cell depletion were associated with greater reductions in disease activity. In DL4, memory B cells were rapidly depleted and showed limited reconstitution through Week 12 and subsequent follow-up, even as total B-cell counts began to recover.

Preliminary clinical improvements were observed at Week 12 in DL3 and DL4, with greater reductions in disease activity observed at DL4.

* Moderate-to-good response in DAS28-ESR at Week 12. Mean changes from baseline in DAS28-ESR were -1.2 and -3.3 in DL3 and DL4, respectively.
* Clinically meaningful decreases in CDAI at Week 12. Mean changes from baseline in CDAI were -15.3 and -25.0 (greater than 50%) in DL3 and DL4, respectively.
* Improvement of more than 0.22 points in the Health Assessment Questionnaire Disability Index (HAQ-DI), observed in DL4 at Week 12.
* Tender and swollen joint count improvements were also observed in all cohorts. One patient in DL3 and two patients in DL4 had complete resolution of swollen joints at Week 12.

Treatment with plamotamab was well tolerated across both single-ascending dose (SAD) and multiple-ascending dose (MAD) cohorts. One patient in DL4 experienced Grade 1 cytokine release syndrome (CRS). No events of immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) were observed. The most common treatment-emergent adverse events were pyrexia in five patients, injection site erythema in four patients, and decreased or increased neutrophil count in three patients. A patient in DL4 with a history of nephrolithiasis experienced a serious adverse event deemed unrelated to plamotamab, nephrolithiasis complicated by pyelonephritis, and discontinued study treatment.

Update on Progress Achieved in the Ongoing First-in-Human Phase 1 Dose-Escalation Study of XmAb657, a Bispecific CD19 x CD3 T-cell Engager

Xencor is conducting a first-in-human, Phase 1, open-label, dose-escalation study of subcutaneously administered XmAb657 in healthy participants and patients with idiopathic inflammatory myopathies, systemic sclerosis, and Sjögren's disease. As of November 1, 2026, 30 healthy participants had enrolled across five single-ascending dose cohorts. One subject in Cohort 5 experienced Grade 1 CRS. No events of ICANS had been observed. Injection site reactions were the most commonly reported adverse events, with Grade 1 and Grade 2 reactions reported in six and nine participants, respectively.

XmAb657 demonstrated early, dose-dependent reductions in B-cell counts during single-ascending dose escalation. In Cohort 5, the highest single-dose cohort evaluated to date, all participants had reductions in B-cell counts from baseline by Day 2, with a median maximum reduction of 86% from baseline.

Xencor anticipates completing one additional single-dose cohort in healthy participants by year-end 2026. In the first quarter of 2027, the Company plans to begin enrolling patients with severe autoimmune diseases, including idiopathic inflammatory myopathies, Sjögren's disease and systemic sclerosis, in MAD cohorts.

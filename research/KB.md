## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

KB Financial Group is the holding company for Kookmin Bank, which had about 34.7 million customers and 771 branches at the end of 2025 (FY2025 20-F), together with a securities firm, two insurers, a card company, a capital company and an asset manager. The group earned KRW 5.83 trillion for its shareholders in 2025 (FY2025 20-F) on KRW 797.9 trillion of assets and about KRW 54.7 trillion of common equity. In the first nine months of 2026 it earned KRW 5.64 trillion, up 10.2% year on year, after an amendment on November 12 that took KRW 148.6 billion out of Q3 for two regulatory fines.

For most of the last decade the stock traded at 0.4x to 0.6x book. Converting the year-end ADS price at each year's December USD/KRW rate and dividing by book value per share from the 20-F filings, the multiple was 0.53x at the end of 2016, 0.73x at the end of 2017, between 0.37x and 0.52x from 2018 through 2023, 0.59x at the end of 2024 and 0.81x at the end of 2025. Today it is about 0.94x. Over the same decade ROE, measured as profit to parent over year-end parent equity, stayed between 7% and 10%. The multiple more than doubled while returns on equity moved by about two percentage points.

What changed was capital return. In October 2024 KB published a Corporate Value Enhancement Plan under which it aims to return capital above a 13% common equity Tier 1 ratio at year end, with a second tranche above 13.5% in the second half (6-K of March 27, 2026). The policy is explicitly subject to change, including for inorganic growth, so an acquisition can take the surplus instead of buybacks. In 2026 that has meant KRW 1.90 trillion of buybacks, all for cancellation (KRW 600 billion from February to April at an average KRW 153,847; KRW 600 billion from April to July at KRW 167,522; KRW 698 billion of a KRW 700 billion trust from July to October), plus quarterly dividends of KRW 1,143, KRW 1,155 and KRW 1,167. Shares outstanding fell from 358.6 million at the end of 2025 to 347.2 million in October, and issued shares fell from 372.9 million in April to 354.7 million after cancellations.

### The Core Debate

The bull case is that Korean banks are still in the early part of a multi-year re-rating. Payout ratios are moving toward 50% and above, the government is using tax incentives to encourage dividends (KB qualified as a "High-Dividend Company" under the special taxation act in March 2026), and fee income is booming with the domestic stock market. If ROE moves to 12-13% and the market pays 1.1-1.2x book, as it does for well-capitalized banks elsewhere earning that return, the stock compounds at 20% a year.

The bear case is that the multiple has already run ahead of returns and that Korean banks carry three risks the market has learned to ignore. The government regularly asks banks to fund policy goals (the 20-F describes the New Leap Fund launched in October 2025 to buy and forgive long-delinquent small debts, and "inclusive finance" lending pressure). Regulators have been fining the sector repeatedly (KRW 333 billion of provisions for ELS sales and LTV penalties in 2025, a KRW 332 billion fine on KB Securities on November 11, 2026 for Treasury-auction collusion). And Korean household debt and real-estate project finance remain the obvious credit risk if growth slows.

### Current View

At $114.75 the stock is a reasonable holding, not a bargain. The base case, which keeps today's multiple and today's ROE, returns about 12% a year, almost all of it from book-value growth, dividends and buybacks bought near book. That is a good return for a bank, but it requires nothing to go wrong in a credit system that has had three regulatory hits in eighteen months. Weighting a 25% bear case pulls the expected return to about 10.5% a year. I would want the stock near $100, or evidence that ROE is moving to 12%, before rating it Own.

## 2. Company Story

### Origin And Evolution

KB Financial Group was formed in 2008 as the holding company for Kookmin Bank, itself the product of the 2001 merger of Kookmin Bank and Housing and Commercial Bank. The largest shareholder is the Korean National Pension Service, which held 9.17% on October 19, 2026 (6-K of October 26). There is no controlling family or chaebol owner, which matters for capital allocation: the board and the CEO nominating committee choose management, and the government's influence comes through regulators and the pension fund rather than through ownership.

Since 2014 the group has built a non-bank business through acquisitions: LIG Insurance (now KB Insurance) in 2015, Hyundai Securities (merged into KB Securities) in 2016, and Prudential Life Korea (merged into KB Life) in 2020. The nominee for chairman and CEO at the November 20, 2026 EGM, Jae Keun Lee, was head of financial planning and CFO during those deals and ran Kookmin Bank from 2022 to 2024 (6-K of October 28, 2026).

### Why The Company Matters

KB is one of four large Korean financial groups (with Shinhan, Hana and Woori) that hold most of the country's deposits. Its earnings are a direct read on Korean household credit, deposit pricing and the domestic capital market. For a US investor the ADS is also the most liquid way to own Korean bank equity, and it carries a currency exposure: the ADS price is the Korean share price divided by the USD/KRW rate.

## 3. Business Model

### How The Company Makes Money

Most of KB's earnings come from the spread between what Kookmin Bank earns on loans and what it pays on deposits. In 2025 net interest income was KRW 13.07 trillion against KRW 4.10 trillion of net fee income and KRW 1.30 trillion of net insurance income (FY2025 20-F). Trading gains (KRW 3.38 trillion) were offset by other insurance finance expenses and other operating expenses, so net operating revenue, defined here as operating profit before provisions plus general and administrative expenses, was KRW 17.95 trillion. General and administrative expenses took KRW 7.06 trillion, provisions KRW 2.36 trillion, and the group earned KRW 8.52 trillion of net operating profit and KRW 5.83 trillion for its shareholders.

The bank's spread has been remarkably stable through a falling-rate cycle. Average won loan yields fell from 4.67% in 2024 to 4.19% in 2025 and 4.06% in the first half of 2026, while the average cost of won deposits fell from 2.45% to 2.07% and 1.90% (6-K of August 14, 2026, sources of funds and uses of funds tables). The loan-deposit spread was therefore 2.22, 2.12 and 2.16 percentage points. Net interest income rose 1.9% in 2025 and 1.7% in the first half of 2026: loan growth of 2-4% a year with a flat spread.

### The Economic Engine

Profit equals assets times a thin return. With KRW 866.9 trillion of assets at June 30, 2026 and KRW 60.9 trillion of parent equity (including KRW 4.2 trillion of hybrid capital securities), common equity is about 6.5% of assets. A return on assets of 0.75% therefore becomes a return on common equity of about 11%. Small changes in credit costs move that number a lot: KRW 1 trillion of extra provisions after tax is roughly 1.3 points of ROE.

The constraint on growth is capital, not deposits. Every won of loans carries a risk weight; group risk-weighted assets were KRW 369.75 trillion at June 30, 2026, up 3.6% in six months. To keep CET1 above 13%, retained earnings have to grow about as fast as risk-weighted assets, and the rest goes back to shareholders. That is why the return policy is written in CET1 terms: at 11% ROE and 4-5% RWA growth, roughly half of earnings can be paid out without the ratio falling.

### Unit Economics / Customer Economics

The group's cost-to-income ratio, G&A over net operating revenue, was 39.4% in 2025 (KRW 7.06 trillion over KRW 17.95 trillion) and about 36% in the first half of 2026, when fee income jumped. Credit cost was KRW 2.36 trillion in 2025, about 0.48% of KRW 492 trillion of loans, after KRW 2.04 trillion in 2024 and KRW 3.15 trillion in 2023. First-half 2026 provisions were KRW 1.01 trillion, 23% lower than a year earlier. The 20-F reports the NPL ratio on loans to small and medium enterprises at 0.29% at the end of 2025, down slightly from 0.30% at the end of 2024 but up from 0.13% at the end of 2023: low in absolute terms, and more than double its level two years earlier.

### Competitive Position

KB, Shinhan, Hana and Woori dominate Korean deposits, and their products are largely the same. The real competition comes from the internet-only banks the 20-F names (Kakao Bank, in which Kookmin Bank owns 4.9%, Toss Bank and K Bank), which compete on deposit rates and unsecured lending without branches. KB's advantage is its retail deposit base and its brand with older and mass-market customers, which keep its won deposit cost at 1.90%. That advantage is regulated in two directions: the government protects the incumbents from serious disruption and in return expects them to act as policy instruments.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Earnings quality is reasonable but noisier than the headline growth suggests. Three items deserve attention.

First, fee income is cyclical. Net fee income jumped 50.6% in the first half of 2026, from KRW 1.97 trillion to KRW 2.96 trillion, almost all of it in brokerage and wealth management while the Korean stock market rallied. KB Securities' operating revenue (gross) in the first nine months was KRW 26.1 trillion against KRW 9.0 trillion a year earlier. I treat about KRW 600-700 billion of 2026 fee income as above trend; in the base case it does not recur in full, which is part of why FY2027 base EPS (KRW 18,127) is only about 2% above my FY2026 estimate.

Second, regulatory charges recur. The group booked KRW 744.8 billion of ELS compensation in 2024, KRW 333 billion of ELS and LTV penalty provisions in 2025, and the FSS and KFTC fines in Q3 2026. The KFTC's 2024 investigation into LTV information-sharing among the four big banks is still described in the 20-F as unresolved. A business that pays a fine every year should be valued as if it will keep paying one. The base case's 11% ROE already includes a charge of this size each year.

Third, provisions have been low. 2025 credit costs of 0.48% of loans and a 23% fall in the first half of 2026 are below the 2023 level (KRW 3.15 trillion). Part of that is a better economy and part is reserve release. The bear case assumes credit costs move back above KRW 3 trillion.

Overall: clean on accounting, with no aggressive capitalization or reserve games visible, but the 2026 profit growth rate overstates the trend. The trend is mid-single-digit profit growth plus 3-4% a year from share count reduction.

### Forecast Logic

The model runs on common equity, not on revenue. Starting common equity at end-2026 is estimated at KRW 57.2 trillion on 347.15 million shares (KRW 164,795 per share). Each year:

1. Profit to common shareholders equals ROE times beginning common equity (after about KRW 200 billion of hybrid distributions).
2. A share of profit is paid as dividends and a share is used for buybacks at a stated price-to-book multiple; bought shares are cancelled.
3. Retained profit adds to equity; book value per share grows from both retention and buybacks below book.
4. The year-3 value is end-2029 book value per share times an exit P/B, plus dividends received in 2027-2029, converted at KRW 1,341.91 per dollar with no discounting.

### What The Current Price Implies

At 0.94x estimated book and a cost of equity of 11%, a perpetual-growth justified P/B of (ROE − g)/(COE − g) equals 0.94 at an ROE of about 10.6-10.8% for growth rates between 4% and 8%. In other words, the price assumes KB sustains roughly its 2025-2026 ROE and does not need it to improve. The one Street target ($168.02) implies about 1.25x end-2027 book on my base path (KRW 178,877, or $133.30), a further re-rating.

### Projection Takeaway

The base case earns about 12% a year at a constant multiple. Half of that comes from book value growth (retained earnings plus buybacks below book) and the rest from dividends. The stock does not need a re-rating to work; it does need ROE to hold at 11% through a credit cycle and a change of CEO. That is a solid Watch.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

The board resolved on October 2, 2026 to hold an EGM on November 20 to appoint Jae Keun Lee as executive director, nominated by the CEO nominating committee for a three-year term to November 2029 (6-Ks of October 2 and 28). Lee joined the group 34 years ago, was CFO of the holding company in 2017, CEO of Kookmin Bank from 2022 to 2024 and Chief Business Officer for global, wealth management and SME business from 2025. The board's recommendation credits him with the acquisitions of LIG Insurance and Hyundai Securities. That history is the main governance question: a CEO with a deal-making record could favour M&A over buybacks. The return policy, however, is published, tied to CET1, and supported by the tax incentives the group qualified for in March 2026.

Capital allocation in 2026 has been disciplined. All buybacks are cancelled rather than held: the April 23 board resolved to cancel existing treasury shares, and the July programme is earmarked for cancellation. Buyback prices rose through the year (KRW 153,847, then KRW 167,522, then about KRW 176,300 for the trust), so the group has bought at about 0.95-1.08x book. At those prices buybacks add little to book value per share; they still add to EPS.

The group has also put KRW 1.7 trillion of new capital into KB Securities this year (KRW 700 billion in February and KRW 1 trillion in July). The stated purpose is working capital, which in practice means more balance-sheet capacity for trading and investment banking. That is a bet on the brokerage cycle, and the same subsidiary has just been fined KRW 332 billion.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

### What Bulls Believe

Korea is in a structural shift toward shareholder returns, and banks are the first and clearest beneficiaries. Dividend tax relief for high-payout companies, quarterly equal dividends and mechanical buybacks will keep compressing the discount between Korean banks and global peers. Earnings are growing 10% a year and the share count is shrinking 3% a year.

### What Bears Believe

The re-rating has run its course. At about 1x book the shareholder-return story is in the price, and Korean banks still answer to the government. Lower interest rates will compress margins, the brokerage boom will fade, and regulators will keep taking a cut. The won is volatile, and a US holder takes that risk on top of the equity.

### What The Market May Be Missing

The market may be underestimating how much of the 2026 earnings growth is cyclical. The 10.2% nine-month profit growth rests on a 50.6% jump in fee income in the first half, while net interest income grew less than 2%. If the stock market cools, profit growth reverts to low single digits and the remaining driver is the buyback. That does not make the stock expensive at 0.94x book, but it makes the bull case (12.5% ROE and 1.2x book) harder than it looks.

## 10. Bull Case

ROE rises to 12.5% because fee income holds near its 2026 level, credit costs stay near 0.45% of loans, and margins hold as deposit costs keep falling with rates. The group returns 60% of profit, buying back about 3.5% of its shares a year. The market comes to treat Korean banks like other well-capitalized banks earning above their cost of equity and pays 1.2x book by the end of 2029. End-2029 book value is KRW 213,650 per share, the stock is worth $191.06 ex-dividend, and with KRW 18,950 of dividends the three-year value is $205.18, about 21% a year. The bull case needs both higher ROE and a multiple above any year-end level of the past decade; I give it 25%.

## 11. Bear Case

Korean credit turns. Household and SME delinquencies rise, project-finance exposures in real estate and construction take losses (the largest non-performing loans in Kookmin Bank's June 30 list are in real estate, construction and wholesale trade), and provisions move back above KRW 3 trillion a year. The government asks banks to absorb part of the cost through relief programmes, the brokerage boom fades, and fines continue. ROE falls to 8.5%, the group cuts buybacks to keep CET1 near 13%, and the multiple returns to 0.55x book, slightly below the 0.59x of the end of 2024. End-2029 book value is KRW 203,686 per share, the stock is worth $83.48, and with KRW 11,265 of dividends the value is $91.88, a 20% loss over three years, before any currency move.

## 12. Base Case

Nothing dramatic happens. ROE stays at 11%: brokerage fees fall back somewhat, net interest income grows 2-4% a year, provisions run around KRW 2.3-2.6 trillion, and one regulatory charge of a few hundred billion won lands each year. The group keeps paying out about 55% of profit, roughly KRW 1.7-1.8 trillion of dividends and KRW 1.8-1.9 trillion of buybacks a year (KRW 1.76 trillion in 2027), buying at about 0.93-0.95x book. Shares fall from 347 million to about 314 million by the end of 2029, EPS rises to KRW 21,350, and book value per share to KRW 210,523. At an unchanged 0.95x book the stock is worth $149.04, and with KRW 15,972 of dividends the three-year value is $160.94, about 12% a year.

### Primary Valuation Framework

P/B against ROE is the right lens because KB's value is its equity compounding at a regulated return, and earnings are volatile from provisions and fines. With a cost of equity of 11% (an assumption: a Korean-won risk-free rate in the high 2s to 3%, plus an equity premium and a country premium) and 4-5% long-run book growth, the justified multiple is roughly (ROE − g)/(COE − g): 0.94x at 10.6% ROE, 1.0x at 11%, 1.25x at 12.5% and 0.65x at 8.5%. The scenario exit multiples sit at or slightly below these justified values, to allow for persistent regulatory and governance risk.

## 17. Pre-Mortem And Kill Criteria

It is November 2028 and the ADS is at $70. What happened?

1. The credit cycle turned. Real-estate project finance and SME loans soured, provisions reached KRW 3.5 trillion in 2027 and ROE fell to 8%. Early signal: a quarterly provision above KRW 900 billion, or the SME NPL ratio in the 20-F above 0.4%.
2. The government took the excess. A levy on bank interest income or a compulsory relief fund cut ROE by a point, and the FSC discouraged buybacks. Early signal: a draft bill or FSC guidance on bank windfall contributions.
3. The fines kept coming. The LTV collusion case ended in a large fine and the FSS sanctioned more product sales. Early signal: a KFTC decision on the LTV case above KRW 500 billion for KB, or another 6-K/A amending results for fines.
4. The new CEO bought something. A large insurance or overseas acquisition absorbed the CET1 surplus and buybacks stopped for a year. Early signal: any acquisition announcement above KRW 2 trillion in 2027.
5. The won fell. USD/KRW went to 1,550 and the ADS lost 13% on currency alone. Early signal: USD/KRW above 1,500 for a month.

## 19. Conclusion

KB is a well-run, well-capitalized bank that has done what the Korean value-up programme asked: it pays out more than half its earnings and cancels the shares it buys. The market has noticed, and at $114.75, or 0.94x book, it pays a fair price for an 11% ROE. The probability-weighted value of $154.74 implies about 10.5% a year over three years, which is a Watch with a positive bias. The main reason to like it is that the base case earns about 12% a year without needing a higher multiple; the main risk is that ROE falls back toward 8.5% in a credit or policy shock while the multiple falls with it. The next trigger is the FY2026 results and the 2027 capital-return announcement, expected around February 5, 2027. The stock becomes an Own near $100, or on clear evidence that ROE is moving to 12%.

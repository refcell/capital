## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

Banco Macro is one of Argentina’s largest privately owned universal banks, with a provincial-government franchise, a still-dense branch network, and a balance sheet that is overcapitalized by any Basel standard. The ADR has been cut nearly in half from the fifty-two-week high even as second-quarter 2026 adjusted annualized ROE printed 14.3% and management raised full-year adjusted ROE guidance to about 12% from 8%. The market is not arguing that Macro is insolvent. CET1 is 28% against an 11.5% requirement, liquid assets are 74% of deposits, and excess capital is roughly ARS 4.1 trillion, or about $2.7 billion at the implied TTM FX of ARS 1,484 per dollar. The argument is whether 0.87x book is cheap because Argentina is being priced as a permanent 13% cost-of-equity market, or whether trailing 7% ROE is the honest through-cycle number and the 12–14% quarterly prints are a disinflation sugar high.

The accounting is the first filter. Argentine banks report under IAS 29 purchasing-power restatement. Fiscal 2023 revenue of ARS 8.15 trillion and net income of ARS 1.67 trillion are not a run-rate. They are an inflation-year spike. Fiscal 2025 revenue fell 33% in restated pesos to ARS 4.06 trillion and net income fell 32% to ARS 289 billion. Trailing twelve-month figures through June 2026 show revenue ARS 4.26 trillion and net income ARS 383 billion — a bounce, not a new high. Provisions, meanwhile, have gone the other way: ARS 142 billion in FY2024, ARS 538 billion in FY2025, ARS 757 billion TTM. Credit costs are catching the consumer book just as headline inflation and policy rates come down. That is the classic Argentine bank sequence: margins compress before volumes arrive, and NPLs lag the rate cut.

The second filter is what 0.87x book is actually underwriting. A simple residual-income identity, (ROE − g) / (Ke − g) with ROE 7.07%, g 2%, and Ke 13.41%, justifies about 0.44x book, or roughly $29 per ADR. The stock at $57.99 is therefore not cheap on trailing returns. It is a claim that ROE will live near management’s 12% 2026 guide, not near the 7% TTM print. If that claim is right, 0.95x on a book that compounds at 7% for five years is $88.56. If it is wrong, 0.60x on 3% book growth is $46. Probability-weighted, the workbook lands at $88.04, 52% above the close, but the base case is still 16% below the $105.29 average sell-side target. Street is writing the 2030 20% ROE story. This memo writes the 12% story and still finds upside, which is why the stance is Watch rather than Own: the upside is real only if Q3 and Q4 confirm that 12% is a floor, not a first-half peak.

### Current View

Banco Macro is a Watch. The probability-weighted fair value of $88.04 sits 52% above $57.99 because the base case takes management’s 12% ROE guide as the medium-term mean and applies a 0.95x exit multiple, still below the 1.0–1.2x band EM private banks can earn when country risk is fading. The bear case is not insolvency. It is a reversion to 5% ROE and 0.60x book, $46.23, which is 20% below the close and still well above a residual-income $29 if one takes trailing ROE as permanent. That asymmetry is why the stock screens cheap on forward P/E 7x and why nine analysts still print a $105 average target even after UBS cut to $90 on October 13 and Itaú BBA went to Market Perform at $91.

Three facts keep this off Own. First, trailing ROE is 7%, not 12%, and IAS 29 plus securities gains make the gap hard to close from public pages alone. Second, consumer NPLs are 8.4% and TTM provisions have quintupled in two years; coverage of 95% is adequate, not fortress, against a system household NPL of 12.8%. Third, the ADR is a pure Argentina beta instrument. A 6.5% country-risk overlay on a 0.41 beta still produces Ke of 13.4%. If that overlay goes to 10%, justified P/B on 12% ROE collapses. The next trigger is Q3 FY2026 on November 25, 2026. The report that matters is not EPS versus ARS 3,136 of Yahoo quarterly consensus. It is Stage 3, cost of risk, and whether adjusted ROE is still in the low teens after another quarter of NIM compression.

## 2. Company Story

### Origin And Evolution

Banco Macro’s corporate biography is a provincial-franchise story, not a Buenos Aires wholesale-bank story. The group traces to Hamburgo Investment Company in 1975 and to a non-bank financial institution in 1985. The Brito and Carballo families remain the controlling shareholders. Local shares listed on the Buenos Aires exchange in November 1994; ADRs listed on the NYSE in March 2006 under CUSIP 05961W105, ten Class B shares per ADR. Headquarters are at Avenida Eduardo Madero 1182 in Buenos Aires. Juan Martín Parma is CEO; Jorge Francisco Scarinci is CFO.

What distinguished Macro from peers was inorganic assembly of interior franchises plus financial-agency contracts with provincial governments — tax collection, payroll, and utility payments that pin retail deposits to a geography rather than to a rate. That is why the Q2 call still talks about a “cautious, strict” credit culture and about geography as a credit-quality explanation, not just a distribution map. The bank is a universal bank aimed at low- and mid-income individuals and SMEs, with corporate lending into mining, oil and gas, agribusiness, and, management hopes, construction. Consumer still dominates the gross book at 71% at the end of Q2 2026, commercial 29%. Private-sector loan market share was 8.2% and stable.

The last eighteen months are a restructuring story inside a disinflation story. Headcount on the Q2 call was 8,180, down 8% year over year; StockAnalysis still shows 7,796 and Yahoo 8,490 — another data conflict, but the direction is not. Branches fell 89 over twelve months to 402 at June 30, with a year-end 2026 target near 370. After-tax restructuring charges were ARS 14.2 billion in Q2, similar to Q1. Management is explicit that 2026 absorbs the charges and 2027 shows the savings. On March 20, 2026 the bank and Fintech Digital LLC signed a share-purchase agreement for Banco Sáenz S.A. Terms are not fully visible on the public pages used here. On October 28, 2026 the board authorized a repurchase of up to ARS 160 billion, about $108 million, for 45 days, citing a market price that “does not adequately reflect the fair value of the Bank’s assets.” That authorization arrived with the ADR already down 35% year to date.

### Why The Company Matters

Macro matters as a listed, overcapitalized claim on Argentine financial deepening. Management’s own 2030 framing is the industry’s: loans-to-GDP near 11% versus regional 30–50%, NIM compressing as inflation falls, volumes eventually more than offsetting. Primary customers are 30% of the base and the plan is 50%. Fee income from insurance and wealth is the margin-compression hedge. That is a real strategy. It is also a five-year option, not a 2026 earnings story. For a $3.7 billion ADR, the near-term economic engine is still peso NIM on a consumer-heavy book, a large government-securities portfolio (about 25% of assets in Q2), and the option to spend 16.5 points of surplus CET1.

It also matters as a test of whether Argentine private banks can re-rate before the sovereign does. GGAL, BBAR, and SUPV are the related tickers on Yahoo. If Macro can hold 12% ROE with NPLs inside the guide while the ADR sits at 0.87x, the re-rating is a country-risk story and the bank is a clean vehicle. If ROE fades with NIM and the consumer book, the 0.87x is the right multiple for a 7% ROE franchise and the fifty-two-week high of $106 was the error.

## 3. Business Model

### How The Company Makes Money

Macro earns a spread between peso and dollar assets and a cheaper provincial-and-retail deposit base, then layers fees, securities results, and FX. In the restated income statement, TTM interest income on loans is ARS 5.33 trillion, interest expense ARS 1.93 trillion, net interest income ARS 3.40 trillion. Non-interest income is ARS 1.62 trillion, of which ARS 493 billion is gain on sale of investments — not a banking spread. After ARS 757 billion of provisions, reported “revenue” on StockAnalysis is ARS 4.26 trillion. That revenue line is NII plus non-interest income minus provisions, which is why USD TTM revenue of $2.87 billion is not comparable to a US regional bank’s NII-centric figure.

Q2 2026 is the cleaner window into the current machine. NII was ARS 1.03 trillion, flat quarter on quarter and up 11% year on year. NIM including FX was 24%, down from 25%, mostly a lower FX contribution; excluding FX, management described NIM as roughly stable around 23.5–23.8%. Asset yields fell 280–327 basis points to 41% as lending rates came down. Funding costs fell faster, 350–500 basis points depending on the print, to 19%, because private-sector peso deposit rates collapsed. Deposits were 76% of liabilities. That is the H1 surprise that caused the ROE guide raise: the liability side deflated faster than the asset side. It is also the reason the surprise may not repeat. Intermediation spreads, Scarinci said, should narrow; bond and FX income are expected to fill the gap. That is a mix shift, not a structural NIM defense.

### Economic Engine

The engine has four pistons. First, consumer and SME credit in the interior, still 71% of the gross book, high yield, high NPL. Second, commercial credit into export sectors, 29% of the book, lower NPL (2.9% versus system 3.5%), the growth vector for the next two quarters. Third, a government-securities book at 25% of assets that produced a large share of FY2024–FY2025 non-interest income and still marks to a disinflation path. Fourth, operating leverage from cutting 89 branches in a year and targeting 370 by year-end, with 2027 as the savings year. Capital is not a piston. It is the flywheel: 28% CET1 lets the bank keep paying cash dividends, buy back stock into a drawdown, and bid for Sáenz without raising equity. It also means ROE is mechanically capped until the bank either leverages the balance sheet or returns the surplus. Management’s 2030 20% ROE target assumes the leverage happens through loan-to-GDP, not through a thinner CET1 by choice.

### Unit Economics

On a USD TTM basis the bank earned $258 million of net income on $2.87 billion of stated revenue, an 9.0% net margin, and 7.07% ROE on $4.25 billion of book. ROA of 1.73% looks excellent versus a US regional 1.0% until one remembers that Argentine nominal rates and inflation still embed a 20%+ NIM. As inflation goes to single digits, that ROA has to be rebuilt from volume and fees, which is exactly Parma’s 2030 speech. Efficiency ratio in Q2 was 33.9%, up from 32% in Q1 as employee benefits rose 7% and administrative expenses 8% even while branches closed — restructuring noise. A 34% efficiency ratio would be elite in the US; in Argentina it is the number Macro is defending while it pays for digital and early-retirement. Loan-to-deposit 79% and liquid assets 74% of deposits mean the bank is not loaned-up. The constraint is credit demand at current real rates, not funding.

## 4. Products, Segments, And Moat

### Franchise Map

Retail products are the standard bundle: savings and checking, time deposits, cards, consumer and auto and mortgage loans, overdrafts, insurance, tax and utility collection, ATMs, transfers. Corporate products are working-capital lines, trade finance, leasing, factoring, payroll, and cash management. The differentiated piece is the provincial financial-agency role — four provincial governments, in the IR description — which produces sticky operating deposits and fee flow that a Buenos Aires wholesale bank does not automatically get. Digital and mobile are the replacement for the 120-odd branches that will have disappeared between mid-2025 and end-2026.

There is no US-style reportable segment table on StockAnalysis. The economic segments that matter are consumer credit, commercial credit, and the securities/FX book. Consumer is the earnings concentration and the NPL concentration. Commercial is the 2026 growth call (mining, oil and gas, agribusiness). Securities and FX are the swing factor that made FY2024 non-interest income ARS 4.04 trillion and then ARS 1.53 trillion in FY2025.

### Competitive Position

Private-sector loan share of 8.2% is mid-pack for a listed Argentine bank, not a monopoly. The competitive claim is credit culture plus interior distribution plus surplus capital. Q2 consumer NPL of 8.4% versus system 12.8%, and commercial 2.9% versus 3.5%, is the evidence. Coverage 95.4% versus system 86.3% is the second exhibit. Those gaps can be geography, underwriting, or lag. Management says both geography and policy. A bear reads the 8.4% consumer print as the gap closing. Galicia (GGAL), BBVA Argentina (BBAR), and Supervielle (SUPV) are the listed comps; public banks and fintechs are the unlisted ones. Fee compression from larger platforms is less the issue than sovereign risk, which hits every Argentine bank’s cost of equity at once.

### Moat Assessment

The moat is moderate and local. Switching costs on payroll and provincial-agency deposits are real. Brand in the interior is real. Scale in capital is real — 28% CET1 is a weapon in a system where weaker banks will have to shrink. None of that is a global moat. It does not travel, it does not protect USD book value from a devaluation, and it does not keep NIM at 24% if inflation is 8%. The 2030 plan — primary-customer mix to 50%, wealth and insurance fees, efficiency — is an attempt to manufacture a fee moat before the spread moat decays. Until those fees show up in the mix, the franchise is a well-run, overcapitalized Argentine consumer bank. That is worth a P/B inside 0.7–1.1x depending on the ROE path, not a developed-market 1.5x.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Earnings quality is noisy, not fraudulent. The operating bank produces real NII and, in Q2, real sequential profit growth. Three distortions sit on top of that engine.

The first is IAS 29. Hyperinflation restatement made FY2023 look like a record and FY2024–FY2025 look like a collapse. Neither is a clean economic run-rate. TTM profit margin of 9% and TTM ROE of 7% are the least-bad trailing snapshot, and even they mix two inflation regimes. Rating: the operating trend in 2026 is usable; the 2021–2024 peso columns are not a CAGR base.

The second is below-operating-line and securities noise. Gain on sale of investments was ARS 2.96 trillion in FY2024 and ARS 453 billion in FY2025. Q2’s beat was “mainly driven by higher results from financial instruments at fair value through P&L and lower loan loss provisions,” plus a smaller monetary-position loss. NII was flat. A bank that needs securities marks to print 14% ROE does not have 14% ROE. Management’s own split — reported 9–10% ROE versus adjusted 12% for 2026 — is an admission that charges and mix matter. Adjusted is the right lens if restructuring is truly one-time; it is the wrong lens if securities gains are the plug.

The third is credit cost timing. Provisions of ARS 757 billion TTM against ARS 12.4 trillion of gross loans is a 6% charge-off-equivalent run-rate, in line with the 6.5–7% cost-of-risk guide. That is expensive credit. Coverage of 95% of reported NPLs and 149% of Stage 3 is solvent. It is not conservative if consumer NPLs keep marching toward the system’s 12.8%. OCF of −ARS 2.06 trillion in FY2025 flipping to +ARS 1.89 trillion TTM is a working-capital and deposit story, not an earnings-quality story; FCF is marked N/A for banks and those prints should not be used as owner earnings.

Tax is a fourth, smaller distortion. FY2024 effective tax of 9.3% versus 43.1% in FY2025 and 38.5% TTM means residual-income models should use ~38–40%, not the FY2024 gift. Overall: noisy / transitionary. The franchise generates cash-like NII. Trailing GAAP ROE understates 2026 run-rate if one believes the guide, and overstates it if securities gains fade and provisions do not.

### Forecast Logic

Scenarios are P/B, not FCF. Current ADR BVPS is $66.47. Bear compounds book at 3% (ROE stuck ~5%, dividend and credit costs eating retained earnings) and exits at 0.60x, a crisis-adjacent EM multiple. Base compounds at 7% (12% ROE, high payout, some buyback accretion) and exits at 0.95x, a “guide delivered, country still noisy” multiple. Bull compounds at 11% (ROE toward the high teens as volumes replace NIM) and exits at 1.15x, still short of a 1.4x developed-market bank. Five-year terminal prices: $46.23, $88.56, $128.80. Weights 25/50/25 produce $88.04, +51.8%. Bear is 20% below the close. Base is 16% below the $105 street target, which is the intended calibration: this memo is less willing than the street to underwrite 20% ROE by 2030 inside a five-year window.

Reverse valuation: at $57.99 and $66.47 book, the market pays 0.87x. With Ke 13.4% and g 2%, 0.87x is the justified multiple for about 12% ROE — coincidentally the company’s own 2026 adjusted guide. The stock is not discounting 7% and it is not discounting 20%. It is discounting “they hit the guide.” Miss the guide, and 0.87x is too high. Beat into the mid-teens and hold it, and 0.87x is too low. That is the entire variant.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Parma (born 1973) and Scarinci (CFA, born 1970) are a financially literate pair. The Q2 call is unusually explicit about the margin-then-volume sequence, about Stage 3 versus reported NPL, and about reported versus adjusted ROE. That is good. The 2030 20% ROE slide assuming single-digit inflation is a strategy document, not a forecast, and they mostly treat it that way.

Capital allocation is aggressive relative to a 7% trailing ROE. The cash dividend is $3.67 trailing on the ADR, 6.3% yield, 94% of StockAnalysis EPS. Q2 commentary was “continue as far as we can, paying cash dividends on a yearly basis.” On top of that, the October 28 buyback of up to ARS 160 billion is about 3% of market cap, justified in the press release as a fair-value defense. Buybacks at 0.87x book add BVPS if the book is real. They destroy capital if the next four quarters print 7% ROE and a 10% devaluation. Sáenz is the third claim on surplus CET1. Without a disclosed price, it is a question, not a thesis. Insider ownership on StockAnalysis is 19.94% and institutions 42.15% — a controlled, still-liquid float. Short interest 2.49% of shares out is not a squeeze setup.

Governance risk is Argentina more than the board. A cluster of 6-Ks through May–June 2026 is normal for an ADR, not a Rule 425 M&A cluster. The Sáenz 6-K is the one that matters. Family control is a double-edged alignment: long-term franchise, limited takeout premium.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

Bulls see a 7x forward P/E, 0.87x book, 28% CET1 private bank at the lows, with ROE already 14% adjusted in Q2 and a board buying stock. They map loans-to-GDP from 11% to 30% and do not need the whole journey to make 1.1x book work. Bears see a consumer NPL at 8.4% and rising, a securities-gain beat, a 35% YTD drawdown that UBS and Itaú have already ratified with lower targets, and a cost of equity that makes 7% ROE value-destructive. The street average $105 is the bull case in one number; the $90 UBS target is closer to this memo’s $88 base.

What the market is missing, or at least not stating cleanly, is that 0.87x already assumes the 12% guide. The cheap-looking forward P/E is the same statement in earnings language: $8.30 of implied USD EPS at 7x is a 2026–2027 recovery number, not TTM. The variant is not “hidden book.” The variant is path. If Q3–Q4 hold adjusted ROE in the low teens with Stage 3 under 4%, the ADR can re-rate toward $88–$104 without any 2030 miracle. If NIM compression and consumer credit eat the guide, the multiple does not have to compress much — book growth simply stalls and $58 was fair. Country risk is the fat tail in both directions and is not in the beta of 0.41.

## 10. Bull Case

The bull case is that H1 2026 is the new run-rate, not a gift. Funding costs have reset lower, NIM excluding FX is stable above the old 20% guide, commercial loan demand in mining and energy offsets a flattish consumer book, and cost of risk peaks inside 6.5–7% with Stage 3 back under 4% by year-end. Restructuring charges roll off in 2027 and the efficiency ratio holds ~34% on a smaller branch base. Surplus CET1 funds a completed Sáenz deal that is earnings-accretive, a finished ARS 160 billion buyback at prices below book, and an ongoing dollar dividend. ROE lives at 12–15% for five years, book compounds at ~11%, and the multiple moves to 1.15x as Argentina’s country-risk premium fades a couple of points. Terminal BVPS $112 times 1.15x is $129, in line with Yahoo’s high target of $130.59 and JPMorgan’s June $132. Required: inflation keeps falling, the peso’s 12–13% second-half 2026 crawl is orderly, and consumer NPLs stabilize rather than converge on 12.8%.

## 11. Bear Case

The bear case is that 14% adjusted ROE was a first-half NIM gift plus securities marks. Intermediation spreads compress as promised, bond and FX income do not fill the hole, and consumer NPLs follow the system. Cost of risk stays at 7% or worse, coverage is spent down toward 90%, and TTM ROE remains 7% or drifts to 5%. The buyback is a 3% share-count event that does not offset a devaluation of USD book. Sáenz adds NPLs. The dividend is cut when CET1 is needed for credit. Exit 0.60x on $77 of book is $46, 20% below the close. Residual income at 5–7% ROE versus 13.4% Ke says the stock can trade 0.4–0.6x until the country premium moves. The 52-week high of $106 is then remembered as a 2025 inflation-trade leftover. Required for this case: a messy 2027 election path, capital-control relapse, or a household credit cycle that management’s “new vintages are cleaner” comment does not catch.

## 12. Base Case

The most likely outcome is the one management is already describing. Margins stay “above 20%” NIM into year-end but do not repeat the H1 overshoot. Real loan growth lands in the 2–5% guided range, commercial over consumer. Reported ROE for 2026 is 9–10%, adjusted about 12%. NPLs finish 5.5–6%, coverage above 90%. 2027 brings the cost saves and a slower compounding of book, call it 7% BVPS CAGR once the 94% payout is respected. The multiple neither crashes nor re-rates to 1.2x because country risk has not been retired; 0.95x on $93 of year-5 book is $89. That is 53% above the close and 16% below street, which is the point. Own this only if you are willing to sit through Argentine vol for a 12% ROE that is already in the price, harvesting the extra 0.08x of multiple and the 7% book growth. That is a Watch that becomes an Own if Q3–Q4 print the guide without a credit scare.

## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The biggest risk is that trailing 7% ROE is the normalized number and 0.87x is therefore too high, with consumer credit as the mechanism that keeps it there. Country risk is the fat tail: devaluation, capital controls, or a 2027 political break that reprices every Argentine financial at once. Credit risk is specific: household NPLs at 12.8% system-wide, Macro at 8.4% and rising, TTM provisions already 6% of loans. Securities and sovereign-bond concentration at 25% of assets is a rate-and-FX P&L risk. Dividend-and-buyback-and-M&A tri-claim on CET1 is a capital-allocation risk if credit costs gap up. Data quality is a research risk: ADR share count, USD EPS, FY2025 revenue, and employee count all conflict across vendors. IAS 29 is an analytical risk: using FY2023 as a base will overstate compounding. None of these are accounting red flags in the fraud sense. They are why the stance is Watch.

## 16. What Would Change My Mind

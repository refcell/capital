## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

Constellation Brands looks like a fallen consumer-staples compounder. Modelo Especial, Corona Extra, and Pacifico remain unusually valuable beer franchises, yet the stock is sitting near its 52-week low and at barely ten times forward adjusted earnings. That headline valuation is tempting. It reflects a real deterioration in near-term demand, however, not merely indiscriminate multiple compression. Management described August off-premise demand as “lackluster” at the September 8 Barclays conference, and high-end beer purchases remain exposed to a stretched U.S. consumer, regional weakness among Hispanic households, higher logistics costs, and tariffs on a portfolio produced in Mexico.

The real question is whether this is a temporary volume air pocket inside a durable share-gaining franchise or the beginning of structurally slower growth. Beer now supplies approximately 92% of trailing revenue after Constellation divested mainstream wine and spirits brands. The simplification improves strategic clarity but removes diversification: Modelo and Corona must carry nearly the entire earnings story. If category demand normalizes while Modelo and Pacifico continue taking share, today’s multiple understates the cash-generating power of the remaining company. If high-end imported beer is losing purchase frequency because affordability, demographics, moderation, and policy have changed, the low multiple is not obviously wrong.

The thesis works without heroic revenue growth. FY2027 consensus revenue is $9.10 billion, down 0.4%, and FY2028 headline consensus is $9.27 billion, up 1.9%. At $122.45, the market asks only that Constellation sustain roughly $11.83 of FY2027 adjusted EPS, preserve a 20% free-cash-flow margin, and avoid permanent brand impairment. Share repurchases can create meaningful per-share growth even if revenue grows in the low single digits. The thesis fails if depletion remains negative after macro conditions ease, Corona cannot stabilize, tariffs structurally compress beer margins, or management prioritizes buybacks over reducing a $10.4 billion net-debt position.

### The Core Debate

The debate is not whether Modelo is a strong brand or whether STZ is statistically cheap. Both are visible. The debate is whether current weakness is cyclical enough for pricing, cost control, and share repurchases to bridge the company back to growth. The most important evidence will not be a one-quarter EPS beat. It will be beer depletion, shipment versus depletion alignment, brand-level share, and the source of margin movement.

### Current View

The current view is Watch, with a probability-weighted five-year fair value of approximately $167 per share and a broad $90-$238 scenario range. The base case is $170.40, close to the $170.83 analyst average target. That outcome assumes revenue resumes roughly 1.5% annual growth after FY2028, adjusted EPS reaches $14.20, and the stock earns 12x forward earnings. It does not assume a return to the 19x-20x forward multiples visible in FY2022-FY2024.

The opportunity is real because the market is pricing a good business as though its growth engine is permanently impaired. The caution is equally real because current evidence does not yet disprove that impairment. October 6, 2026 earnings should determine whether STZ deserves promotion from Watch: beer depletion must improve, Corona’s stabilization should be visible in measured channels, and guidance should be sustained without relying on tax, buybacks, or accounting adjustments.

## 2. Company Story

Constellation traces its roots to 1945 and evolved from a wine company into a broad beverage-alcohol owner through acquisitions, licenses, and portfolio reshaping. The defining strategic move was acquiring the U.S. rights associated with Grupo Modelo brands. Constellation now holds an exclusive perpetual license to produce its beer portfolio and import, market, and sell it in the United States. That arrangement made Modelo Especial, Corona Extra, Pacifico, Victoria, and Modelo Cheladas the center of the enterprise.

The economic history matters because the company investors bought several years ago is not the company reflected in today’s reported revenue. Beer revenue rose from $6.75 billion in FY2022 to $8.36 billion TTM. Wine and spirits revenue fell from $2.07 billion to $693 million over the same period as management sold mainstream brands and related assets. Total revenue therefore declined 10% in FY2026 even though the core beer franchise remained much larger than in FY2022. The headline contraction mixes genuine beer-demand weakness with deliberate portfolio shrinkage.

The latest chapter is a leadership and operating-model transition. At the September 2026 Barclays conference, new CEO Nicholas Fink described a shift from a long capacity-building phase toward sharper execution, efficiency, pricing discipline, and brand productivity. That is a sensible response: with Mexican brewery capacity substantially built and the wine portfolio simplified, the next phase must generate returns from assets already in place rather than continually adding scale.

Constellation matters because it owns U.S. access to Mexican brands with cultural relevance, pricing power, broad distribution, and scale that is difficult to reproduce. The company is not the global owner of every brand right; its moat is specifically the perpetual U.S. license and the manufacturing, marketing, and distributor system built around it. That distinction is important. The opportunity is unusually attractive, but it also exposes earnings to U.S.-Mexico trade relations and to continued consumer affinity for imported Mexican beer.

## 3. Business Model

Constellation sells beer, wine, and spirits through a regulated three-tier distribution system. Wholesalers buy product and distribute it to off-premise retailers such as grocery, convenience, and liquor stores and to on-premise venues such as bars and restaurants. Revenue depends on shipments into the distributor channel, while depletion — product leaving distributors for retailers — is the better near-term measure of consumer pull. A shipment beat without depletion improvement may merely move inventory between periods.

Beer generated $8.36 billion of the company’s $9.06 billion in trailing revenue. Modelo Especial is the flagship, while Corona Extra, Pacifico, Victoria, Modelo Cheladas, and adjacent offerings broaden occasions and price points. Constellation has meaningful pricing power because consumers buy brands rather than undifferentiated alcohol, but that power is not unlimited. The portfolio carries a premium to the broader beer market, so reduced trip frequency and spend per trip show up when household budgets tighten.

Wine and spirits is now a much smaller, deliberately higher-end business built around brands such as The Prisoner Wine Company, Robert Mondavi Winery, Kim Crawford, Schrader Cellars, Lingua Franca, Mi CAMPO Tequila, and High West Whiskey. Management sold lower-growth mainstream labels to improve portfolio quality. This improves mix in theory, but the remaining segment’s 5%-6% margin discussed in September is not yet evidence of a high-quality earnings contributor. Inventory normalization and operating efficiency must raise returns.

The engine is: maintain cultural relevance and shelf velocity for Modelo, Corona, and Pacifico → use distributor reach and merchandising to expand occasions and points of distribution → price enough to absorb commodities, freight, labor, and tariffs without damaging volume → convert a 50%+ gross margin and 30%+ operating margin into cash → allocate cash among Mexican capacity, debt, dividends, and repurchases → reduce the share count and compound per-share earnings.

The weak link is consumer pull. Production capacity, shelf space, and marketing do not create value if depletion declines. The second weak link is concentration: portfolio simplification means beer can no longer be offset by a large wine operation. The third is capital allocation. Repurchasing shares at ten times forward earnings is attractive if normalized EPS is durable; it is value destructive if debt remains high while earnings reset lower.

Traditional customer acquisition metrics are not the right unit economics. The relevant units are revenue and gross profit per case, depletion velocity per outlet, incremental distribution, promotion intensity, and return on brewery capacity. Constellation does not publicly provide enough detail to calculate those returns precisely. Investors should therefore use brand-level volume, price/mix, beer operating margin, and capex as practical proxies.

## 4. Products, Segments, And Moat

The Beer segment is the thesis. Modelo Especial provides scale, strong recognition, and a favorable position within high-end beer. Pacifico has been a distribution-led growth story, and management said it reached the U.S. top ten. Corona Extra is more mature and has needed renewed cultural and occasion-based activation. Modelo Cheladas and non-alcoholic or alternative beverage products expand usage occasions, but none should be assumed to replace core flagship economics until disclosed results prove it.

Wine and Spirits has a different task. It does not need to recover its old revenue base because much of that revenue was intentionally sold. It needs to demonstrate that a concentrated premium portfolio can earn an acceptable margin and stop consuming management attention. The September conference commentary that the segment outperformed the category is encouraging, but a 5%-6% margin leaves a large gap versus beer and little room for execution errors.

| Wine & Spirits | Portfolio-quality option | Premium mix, but currently low segment margin | Category outperformance after divestitures | Inventory normalization and weak wine category |

The moat is a combination of legal rights, brands, distribution, and scale. The perpetual U.S. beer license cannot be replicated by a new entrant. Modelo and Corona have awareness and cultural meaning that require decades to build. Constellation’s distributor relationships, marketing budget, Mexican brewing network, and ability to secure shelf and cooler placement reinforce those rights. These are real advantages, not merely brand-story language.

The moat does not eliminate category and policy risk. Consumers can moderate alcohol consumption, trade down, move between beer and spirits, or shift occasions. Retailers can allocate shelf space toward faster-growing categories. Tariffs can tax the imported-beer economics at the border. Competitors including Anheuser-Busch InBev, Molson Coors, Heineken, Diageo, and fast-growing local brands can spend heavily. Constellation’s edge lowers competitive risk; it does not guarantee category growth.

The current moat assessment is strong but narrower than before. Modelo’s continuing share gains show that brand strength remains intact. The unanswered question is whether share of a pressured category produces enough absolute volume growth to support historical margins and capital returns.

The latest balance sheet had $22.11 billion of assets and $13.56 billion of liabilities. Cash was only $96.6 million against $10.53 billion of debt. The $8.55 billion of total shareholders’ equity includes $5.25 billion of goodwill and $2.54 billion of other intangibles, leaving tangible book value of only about $470 million. That does not make the company insolvent; brand value is the reason it earns high returns. It does mean an impairment could erase accounting equity quickly if long-range assumptions weaken.

Debt is meaningful but serviceable. Debt/EBITDA was 2.89x and interest coverage 8.73x, according to StockAnalysis on September 11. Most borrowings are fixed-rate senior unsecured notes with maturities extending to 2050. The September 18 redemption of $600 million of 4.350% notes due 2027 addresses a near-term maturity, but the funding source should be confirmed in Q2. A debt redemption financed with new debt is a maturity extension, not deleveraging.

Consumer-products businesses can operate with low liquidity because inventory turns, distributors pay, and cash flow is recurring. Nevertheless, the small cash balance increases dependence on operating cash generation and committed credit facilities. The combination of leverage, dividends, and buybacks leaves less room if demand falls sharply.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Earnings quality is **clean at the operating and cash-flow level, noisy at the GAAP net-income level**. Gross and operating margins have remained robust, and operating cash flow has stayed between $2.67 billion and $3.15 billion for five fiscal years. Free cash flow has remained positive despite more than $1 billion of annual capex during the brewery expansion phase. That consistency is stronger evidence of franchise health than reported net income.

GAAP net income is not a reliable historical trend line. The cash-flow statement shows asset writedown and restructuring costs of $3.28 billion in FY2025, compared with $110 million in FY2026 and $76 million TTM. It also shows large gains and losses associated with investments and asset sales. Those items explain why FY2025 recorded an $81 million net loss despite $3.51 billion of operating income and $1.94 billion of free cash flow. The loss was accounting noise around portfolio exits, not evidence that the beer engine stopped earning money.

Cash conversion is strong. TTM operating cash flow of $2.69 billion was 148% of $1.82 billion of net income. After $859 million of capex, free cash flow was $1.83 billion, essentially equal to net income and equal to 20.25% of revenue. Stock-based compensation was only $72 million, so free cash flow is not being manufactured by unusually large non-cash employee expense.

The quality caveat is that current free cash flow includes a lower capex period relative to FY2023-FY2025. Capex fell from $1.27 billion in FY2024 to $859 million TTM. That may represent the intended benefit of completing capacity investments, but investors need a maintenance-versus-growth breakdown before treating 20% as the permanent FCF margin. Conversely, the $850.5 million of divestiture proceeds shown in investing cash flow are not included in the standard FCF calculation and should not be mistaken for recurring cash generation.

Buybacks are economically useful but complicate EPS interpretation. Shares outstanding fell 3.36% year over year, and the company spent $852 million on repurchases TTM. Adjusted EPS can therefore rise with flat net income. That is genuine per-share accretion when shares are repurchased below intrinsic value, not poor-quality accounting. It becomes a concern if repurchases are debt-funded or used to distract from persistent volume decline. The quality score is therefore “good cash earnings, noisy GAAP presentation, capital-allocation dependent.”

## 7. Forward Projections And Scenario Model

The public consensus is deliberately modest. StockAnalysis/S&P Global’s September 11 forecast showed FY2027 revenue of $9.10 billion, down 0.43%, and adjusted non-GAAP EPS of $11.83, effectively flat. The headline FY2028 values were $9.27 billion and $12.39, implying 1.87% revenue growth and 4.74% adjusted EPS growth. Detailed FY2028-FY2029 tables were gated, so this report does not pretend that later public estimates were visible.

Consensus expects three-year revenue growth of 1.25% and EPS growth of 3.72%. The gap is consistent with modest margin recovery, lower shares, and possibly lower interest expense. It is also an important warning: most prospective per-share growth is not coming from category expansion. If revenue remains flat and buybacks slow, the EPS bridge weakens quickly.

The bear case does not require a collapse. Revenue declines slightly, the beer margin faces tariffs and negative operating leverage, and adjusted EPS falls to $10 even after repurchases. A 9x multiple produces $90. This is a real downside scenario because it combines weaker earnings with evidence that the company deserves a structurally lower valuation.

The base case assumes FY2027 and FY2028 approximately match consensus, followed by 1.5% revenue growth. Modelo and Pacifico grow enough to offset a mature Corona and a small wine portfolio. Operating discipline and a lower share count lift adjusted EPS to $14.20. A 12x multiple — well below the 19x-20x forward levels visible in FY2022-FY2024 — produces $170.40.

The bull case assumes the current air pocket is cyclical. Beer growth returns, Corona stabilizes, Pacifico distribution expands, and wine margins normalize. Adjusted EPS reaches $17 and the market pays 14x, producing $238. This is above the current $209 analyst high and should not be treated as central. It is useful as an expression of what intact brands plus operational leverage could achieve.

At $122.45 and $11.83 of FY2027 consensus EPS, investors pay 10.35x. The reverse valuation is straightforward: the market does not require growth, but it requires the $11-$12 earnings base to be durable. If normalized EPS is actually $9 and a challenged company deserves 9x, fair value is $81. If $12 is durable and 12x is warranted, fair value is $144 before material growth. The low price protects against disappointment only if earnings do not reset sharply.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Nicholas Fink’s operating emphasis is appropriate for this stage of Constellation’s development. The company spent years adding Mexican capacity and building distribution. It now needs to demonstrate asset productivity, cost control, and brand-level return discipline. The shift from “builder” to “operator” should be judged through capital turns, depletion, beer margin, and lower capex rather than through slogans.

Capital allocation is aggressive. TTM repurchases were $852 million and dividends were $712 million, together consuming approximately 85% of $1.83 billion of FCF. The annual dividend of $4.12 yields 3.37%, with a 39% earnings payout ratio. After Q1, the company had repurchased 1.465 million shares for $223.8 million and another 714,387 shares for $100 million after quarter-end. At June 26, $2.752 billion remained on the authorization through February 2028.

Those purchases occurred around $140-$159 per share, above the September quote. That does not prove they were mistakes; the model’s base value remains near $170. It does show why governance matters. Management must be willing to slow repurchases if operating evidence worsens or debt reduction offers a better risk-adjusted return. The authorization permits repurchases funded from operations, borrowings, or divestiture proceeds. Borrowing to repurchase stock while demand visibility is low would weaken the thesis.

Debt actions are moving in the right direction. Proceeds from the 2025 wine divestitures were used to repay debt, and the company called $600 million of notes in September 2026. The quality of that action depends on funding. Investors should demand a transparent capital-allocation waterfall: maintenance investment first, leverage target second, dividend third, and buybacks only when the stock is below a conservative value estimate.

Governance also includes portfolio honesty. Management has acknowledged that mainstream wine was not earning an attractive return and sold assets rather than defending past acquisitions. The resulting impairments hurt GAAP results but improve strategic clarity. The next test is whether the remaining premium wine operation can earn a better margin or whether it should be simplified further.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

Bulls focus on franchise value and arithmetic. Modelo and Pacifico continue to gain share. The company retains a perpetual U.S. beer license, 52.7% gross margin, 33.6% operating margin, and 20.3% free-cash-flow margin. The stock yields 3.4%, the share count is falling 3.4%, and the forward P/E is 10.3x. A business does not need fast growth to create attractive returns from that starting point.

Bears focus on denominator risk. Beer volume is exposed to a financially pressured consumer, and management’s own August commentary remained soft. Hispanic consumers are disproportionately important, with uneven regional conditions. Tariffs and Mexican production create a policy risk that domestic competitors do not share to the same degree. Alcohol moderation and category switching may be structural rather than cyclical. A high-margin company can still produce poor equity returns if volume declines, fixed costs deleverage, and the multiple remains low.

The variant view is that the market may be conflating a portfolio reset with a broken beer franchise. Total revenue is down 10%, but wine and spirits revenue has been deliberately reduced by more than $1.3 billion since FY2022. Beer revenue is still $1.61 billion above FY2022. The current quarter is weak, but the multi-year core record is not the same as the consolidated headline.

The counter-variant is that share gains can coexist with absolute volume decline. If the entire addressable category shrinks, winning share may not restore historical growth. Investors should resist treating “share gainer” as sufficient. The key is share gain plus improving depletion and stable contribution margin.

Recent target cuts show a divided market. The average remains $170.83, but Deutsche Bank cut to $136 on September 11 and Barclays maintained $132 on September 9. Bank of America’s $145 Sell target still sits above the current quote. The dispersion from $115 to $209 is evidence that the disagreement is about earnings durability and terminal multiple, not about a few cents of quarterly EPS.

## 10. Bull Case

The bull case begins with consumer normalization rather than a new product miracle. Fuel and logistics costs ease, household purchasing frequency improves, and geopolitical anxiety fades. Modelo Especial and Pacifico convert share gains into positive absolute depletion. Corona’s renewed occasion-led marketing stabilizes the brand and on-premise distribution adds a channel where Constellation has been underrepresented.

That volume improvement creates operating leverage on capacity already built in Mexico. Beer pricing offsets residual inflation and tariffs without causing meaningful trade-down. Wine and spirits continues to outperform its category, inventory normalizes, and segment margin rises from 5%-6%. Capex stays below the FY2023-FY2025 peak, allowing FCF to remain near or above 20% of sales.

Management uses FCF to redeem debt, pay the dividend, and repurchase shares below conservative value. Adjusted EPS compounds faster than revenue and reaches approximately $17 in five years. A 14x multiple is still not extravagant for a focused premium brand owner, producing $238 per share. The bull case requires the October report to show improving volume and intact guidance; a tax- or buyback-driven EPS beat alone does not qualify.

## 11. Bear Case

The bear mechanism is persistent affordability pressure. High-end beer consumers reduce trips and spending, while moderation and category switching make some of the decline permanent. Modelo and Pacifico gain share but cannot offset category contraction. Corona fails to regain relevance. Constellation must choose between price increases that hurt volume and promotion that hurts margin.

Tariffs, freight, aluminum, agricultural inputs, and peso exposure add cost pressure. Hedges delay but do not eliminate the impact. Fixed brewery and distribution costs deleverage. The remaining wine portfolio stays at a mid-single-digit margin, offering little offset. Reported operating cash flow remains positive, but FCF falls as working capital and maintenance spending consume more cash than expected.

Management continues dividends and some repurchases, slowing debt reduction. Adjusted EPS falls toward $10 despite a lower share count. Investors conclude that STZ is an ex-growth, concentrated beverage company and apply 9x earnings. The resulting $90 value is 26% below the current price. A more severe recession or policy shock could produce lower temporary prices, but $90 is an appropriate normalized bear case rather than a liquidation scenario.

## 12. Base Case

The base case treats 2026 weakness as partly cyclical and partly a lower-growth reset. FY2027 revenue is approximately flat at the $9.10 billion consensus anchor. FY2028 grows toward $9.27 billion. Modelo and Pacifico continue taking share, Corona stabilizes gradually, and the small wine portfolio stops shrinking after remaining divestitures close.

Revenue then grows about 1.5% annually. Pricing and cost savings preserve the low-30% operating margin, while capex remains below the prior expansion peak. Debt declines slowly and repurchases reduce shares without relying on incremental leverage. Adjusted EPS reaches $14.20 in five years, a modest progression from FY2028’s $12.39 headline estimate.

A 12x exit multiple produces $170.40, almost identical to the current analyst average. The multiple remains well below the company’s prior-cycle valuation because category growth is slower and policy risks persist. This scenario offers roughly 39% price upside over five years, plus dividends, but the annualized return is much less dramatic than the point-to-point upside suggests. It is attractive if confirmed by operating evidence, not an automatic bargain today.

## 13. Valuation

Forward P/E is the primary framework because net debt is 5.69x TTM FCF. An enterprise-value-to-FCF bridge would amplify small changes in terminal assumptions when converting back to equity value. Forward adjusted earnings also aligns with the sell-side consensus and excludes known portfolio accounting noise. EV/EBITDA remains useful as a capital-structure cross-check, and FCF yield tests whether adjusted earnings convert to distributable cash.

The current 10.3x is not a normal multiple. It prices materially lower growth and higher uncertainty. A base-case 12x therefore assumes only partial normalization.

The probability-weighted value is $167.20 using 25% bear, 50% base, and 25% bull weights. That is 36.5% above the current price. The distribution is wide and asymmetric because the current multiple limits some downside while brand recovery creates substantial upside. This output should not be mistaken for precision. The inputs that matter most are terminal EPS and whether the market continues to assign a single-digit or low-teens multiple.

The analyst average target is $170.83, with a $115 low and $209 high. The model’s $170.40 base is deliberately calibrated near the average, while the $90 bear is below the sell-side low and the $238 bull is above the high. That framing avoids pretending that the model has discovered an exact value outside the market’s plausible range.

Reverse valuation is more useful than a complex DCF here. At the current price and 10.34x forward P/E, the market capitalizes $11.83 of adjusted EPS as a no-growth or declining stream. If the company can preserve that earnings base, the dividend plus buyback yield can produce an acceptable return even without rerating. If $11.83 proves peak or over-adjusted and normalized earnings fall below $10, cheapness disappears quickly.

## 14. Catalysts

The first catalyst is Q2 FY2027 results on October 6 after the close, followed by the October 7 call. The report must show beer depletion, shipment, and operating-margin evidence. A headline EPS beat driven by tax, fewer shares, or timing would not resolve the thesis. Investors should listen for August-to-September demand progression, Hispanic-consumer regional patterns, Corona velocity, and whether full-year guidance is reiterated.

The second catalyst is the September 18 redemption of $600 million of 4.350% notes and the Q2 balance-sheet disclosure. If total debt falls by a similar amount, leverage and interest expense improve. If the notes are refinanced, the event only changes maturity timing. The distinction is material to equity value.

The third catalyst is visible margin improvement in Wine and Spirits. The segment’s reduced scale makes it less important, but moving from 5%-6% toward a sustainable double-digit margin would turn a distraction into an earnings contributor. Continued divestitures at sensible proceeds could also unlock value if the cash reduces debt.

The fourth catalyst is evidence that brewery capex has normalized. TTM capex of $859 million is below the $1.2-$1.3 billion levels of FY2024-FY2025. If that decline is durable without constraining capacity or maintenance, free cash flow can remain strong even with muted sales growth.

## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The biggest risk is that current demand weakness is structural. Constellation has high fixed costs, a premium price position, and extraordinary dependence on imported beer. Persistent lower purchase frequency would hit volume, operating leverage, and the multiple simultaneously.

Consumer concentration is central. The Hispanic consumer is not monolithic, and management described different conditions across California, New York, Florida, and Texas. Still, Modelo and Corona’s cultural strength creates exposure to economic and policy developments that disproportionately affect those households. Investors need measured regional evidence rather than broad demographic narratives.

Trade and input risk is material. Beer is produced in Mexico for the U.S. market. Tariffs can directly affect cost, while peso, aluminum, corn, natural gas, diesel, and freight move the margin. The company had hedged approximately 89% of forecast transactional commodity exposures for the remaining nine months of FY2027 at May 31, but future periods will reset.

Leverage magnifies mistakes. Debt is serviceable at 2.89x EBITDA and 8.73x interest coverage, but $10.44 billion of net debt is large relative to $1.83 billion of FCF. An acquisition, sustained repurchases funded by borrowings, or a sharp EBITDA decline could make refinancing and rating considerations more important.

Accounting equity is fragile. Goodwill and intangibles total roughly $7.79 billion against $8.26 billion of common equity. An impairment does not consume cash immediately, but it would signal that prior portfolio assumptions were too optimistic and could affect covenants, ratings, or investor trust. FY2025’s $3.28 billion of writedown and restructuring costs shows that this is not hypothetical.

Regulatory and social risks include alcohol advertising restrictions, taxes, health guidance, legal claims, and long-term moderation. Non-alcoholic offerings can address some occasions, but Constellation’s economics remain linked to beverage alcohol. Competitive risks include promotion, distributor attention, shelf allocation, and substitution among beer, spirits, ready-to-drink products, cannabis, and non-alcoholic drinks.

## 16. What Would Change My Mind

The stance would become more bullish if two consecutive quarters showed positive beer depletion, Modelo and Pacifico continued gaining share, Corona stabilized, and beer operating margin held despite tariffs. Confirmation that the $600 million note redemption reduced gross debt, together with capex below $900 million and continued FCF near $1.8 billion, would make the buyback-and-deleveraging algorithm more credible. A base value above $170 would then require only modestly higher terminal EPS, not a heroic multiple.

The stance would become bearish if October guidance were cut because of volume rather than timing, if share gains occurred alongside worsening absolute depletion, or if management described moderation as a lasting category change. A decline in beer operating margin without a clear temporary cost bridge would be particularly damaging. Debt-funded repurchases or another large acquisition before leverage falls would also weaken conviction.

The model would need revision if FY2027 adjusted EPS moved outside approximately $11-$12.50, if FY2028 revenue fell materially below $9.27 billion, or if tariff policy changed. The valuation should follow verified earnings power; the low share price is not itself evidence that assumptions are conservative.

## 18. Conclusion

Constellation Brands is a high-quality but newly concentrated beverage franchise at an unusually low valuation. Modelo, Corona, and Pacifico support margins and cash conversion that most consumer companies cannot match. The portfolio generates an 8.8% FCF yield, pays a 3.4% dividend, and is shrinking the share count. At 10.3x forward adjusted earnings, investors do not need a return to historical growth or multiples for the stock to work.

The reason not to call it an immediate Own is evidence. August demand remained lackluster, beer now represents approximately 92% of revenue, and the company carries $10.4 billion of net debt. The distinction between a temporary consumer air pocket and a structural category reset is worth far more than a one-turn difference in the P/E ratio. October 6 is close enough that waiting for depletion and margin evidence has modest opportunity cost.

The current fair-value framework is $90 bear, $170.40 base, and $238 bull, with a $167.20 probability-weighted value. The stance is Watch. The main reason to care is a rare combination of brand quality, cash yield, and low expectations. The main risk is that imported premium-beer demand has permanently slowed while tariffs and leverage constrain the response. The next trigger is Q2 FY2027: positive beer depletion and intact margin guidance would materially improve the case; another demand warning would validate the low multiple.

Most of the 17% headline growth is the Rockstar acquisition. Like for like, revenue grew 7.6%. The portfolio is gaining share: retail sales of the three brands rose 14.7% in tracked U.S. channels, and Celsius held 20.7% of U.S. ready-to-drink energy. Alani Nu's retail sales rose 57.9%, while CELSIUS fell 7.0% and Rockstar 13.2%, both partly from planned SKU cuts. Profit did not follow. Gross profit grew 6% and adjusted EBITDA fell 3%. Commodity inflation, mainly aluminum and fuel, a higher share of sales through Pepsi's DSD system and promotional allowances rising to 38.6% of net revenue outweighed the integration savings. SG&A fell to 26.1% of revenue from 28.4%. Gross margin has fallen through 2026: 48.3%, 48.1% and 46.8%.

Pepsi's weight keeps rising. It accounted for $547.4 million of Q3 revenue (65%) and $390.3 million of receivables, 58.5% of the total against 46.2% in December. Accrued promotional allowances rose to $479.2 million from $307.9 million, $285.3 million of it owed to Pepsi; the 10-Q puts part of the rise down to payment timing.

Cash flow leaned on working capital and on Pepsi. Nine-month operating cash flow was $450.2 million against capex of $51.2 million. The distributor termination accrual fell $262.0 million even after $88.8 million of new fees, so Celsius paid out about $351 million. Pepsi's money covered most of that: $141.1 million of restricted cash held at December, a $64.2 million receivable collected this year and the $81.1 million reimbursed under the May amendment, about $286 million in all. Operating cash flow also absorbed the $60 million first settlement payment and gained $171.2 million from the promotional accrual build and from better collections. Without the accrual build, cash flow would have fallen well short of adjusted EBITDA. Celsius bought back 5.6 million shares for $170.5 million at an average of $30.18 and has $91.4 million left under its program. It also pays about $57 million a year of cash dividends on the Pepsi preferred. Celsius gives no formal revenue or EBITDA guidance. It announced a brand-led reorganization with new presidents for CELSIUS and for Rockstar and emerging brands.

How the May call's checklist and "what would change my mind" items fared:

- **Fired against the thesis:**
  - The CELSIUS brand did not reaccelerate: revenue fell 6% and retail sales 7%. "CELSIUS stays mid-single digit or worse while Alani slows" half fired: CELSIUS fell, but Alani's retail sales still grew 57.9%.
  - Rockstar kept declining, with retail sales down 13.2%.
  - Gross margin, at 46.8%, sits below the 49% bearish line; "gross margin above 50% and FCF tracking EBITDA better" failed.
  - "Pepsi receivables and promo allowances stay elevated" fired: allowances rose to 38.6% of net revenue and Pepsi receivables to 58.5% of the total.
  - Pepsi concentration stayed extremely high, at 65% of revenue.
- **Fired for the thesis:** Alani sell-through stayed strong after the transition, with retail sales up 57.9%.
- **Mixed:**
  - International grew 78% but is still only 4.8% of revenue.
  - Cash flow covered earnings, but with help from working capital and Pepsi-funded termination fees.
- **Unchanged:** the preferred terms, which the May call said could invalidate the thesis if they limit common upside. The values below count the preferred explicitly.

## Estimate And Value Changes

The May values were 2030 values on FCF. This update states three-year values at the end of 2029, undiscounted. Each value is an exit P/E on 2029 adjusted net income before preferred dividends, plus the net cash built up by then, less any preferred that stays unconverted. All of that is divided by common shares plus whichever preferred series is in the money at the resulting price.

- **Adjusted net income** is adjusted EBITDA less stock compensation ($36 million a year) and D&A, taxed at 22%. It carries no interest, because the term loan is netted in net cash. On a trailing basis this is about $497 million before interest (nine-month D&A was $29.9 million), or about $473 million after it, which matches the reported $1.45 of adjusted EPS once preferred dividends and Pepsi's participation are added back.
- **Net cash** starts at −$17 million, after the $25 million settlement due January 5, 2027. Q4 2026 adds the scenario's Q4 EBITDA (above). From 2027, calendar-year EBITDA rises in a straight line from FY2026 to 2029, less tax, capex at 2.2% of revenue, cash interest on the term loan and working capital at 10% of the revenue increase. Each year also deducts about $57 million of preferred dividends and $36 million of buybacks that offset grants, so the common count stays at 252.8 million.
- **The preferred.** Series A converts at any value above $25.00 and Series B above $51.75, though neither converts automatically before August 2031. A converted series also costs the dividends it earns from 2030 until conversion, about $46 million for A and $49 million for B. An unconverted series is deducted at stated value plus the dividends it earns from 2030 until the company can redeem it in August 2032, about $73 million for A and $78 million for B.

- **Bear.** Alani's growth fades the way energy-drink fads do, CELSIUS keeps shrinking about 5% a year, and Pepsi's trade spend and aluminum push the margin down to 19%. Revenue grows 2% a year, about the 33rd percentile for companies this size. Even here Alani, Rockstar and international together grow about 7% a year, since CELSIUS is about 42% of revenue; a sharper Alani fade would put the value below the bear. The market pays 12x for a shrinking-margin brand that depends on one distributor. The preferred stays a $1,135 million senior claim.
- **Base.** Revenue grows 8% a year, about the 69th percentile, close to Q3's 7.6% like-for-like growth and below the portfolio's 14.7% retail growth. CELSIUS stabilizes, Alani grows high single digits and international keeps compounding. The margin recovers to 24%, below FY2025's 24.6% and Q3 2025's 28.4%, as commodity costs ease and pricing lands. The 2027 path is 23.0%, which Q3 2026 already delivered (23.4%) at a 46.8% gross margin, so the base does not need gross margin back at 48%. Adjusted net income before interest grows about 10.5% a year. The 18x exit is below today's 19.4x on the same basis as growth slows.
- **Bull.** Alani and international keep growing in the mid-teens, the margin returns to 26%, and the market pays 24x, still well below Monster.

Each point of 2029 EBITDA margin moves the base by about $2.31 a share.

**What the values leave out.** The 10-Q lists litigation and regulatory matters, most of them about Alani Nu, the brand carrying growth:
- A consolidated securities class action over the Pepsi distribution agreement is pending, with a motion to dismiss before the court.
- A second securities class action, filed September 4, 2026, alleges Celsius failed to disclose caffeine health risks of Alani Nu energy drinks and its marketing practices (class period February 21, 2025 to June 3, 2026). Lead-plaintiff motions were filed November 3; Celsius must respond by November 24.
- A wrongful-death suit filed April 8, 2026 against Glazer's, a former Alani Nu distributor, alleges caffeine-related cardiomyopathy. Celsius is not named but must indemnify the distributor.
- The Texas Attorney General sent a civil investigative demand in June 2026 about representations and practices concerning Celsius products.
- Two consumer class actions, Nielsen (Washington, October 26) and Valente (Illinois, October 28), allege misleading CELSIUS label claims.

None of these is reserved beyond the settlement and $0.2 million on one other matter. A finding that Alani Nu needs new warnings or marketing limits would hit the brand the base relies on. Pepsi's upfront payments are amortizing into revenue ($486.9 million of deferred revenue) while $784.1 million of deferred costs amortize against it. The net is a non-cash drag of about $17 million a year, which the values ignore, since the preferred already captures what Pepsi paid.

## Action

Keep the stance at Watch, move the bias to neutral from negative, and set the fair value at $43.12 against the May call's $50.00 base.

The evidence this quarter was mostly negative:

- The founding brand shrank.
- Gross margin fell 450 bps.
- Promotional allowances took a larger share of revenue.
- Adjusted EBITDA fell.
- Pepsi's share of revenue, receivables and promotional spend kept rising.

The bias moves to neutral anyway because the price already discounts much of this. The stock trades at about 20x trailing adjusted earnings with Series A as converted and no meaningful net debt. The weighted return of 9.6% sits in the middle of the Watch band, well clear of both Own and Avoid. The base needs margins to recover to 24%, and Q3 moved the other way, so the call has no strong variant view. Where an edge exists it is analytical: the per-share value turns on the preferred conversion math and on like-for-like growth, which the headline 17% obscures.

Kill criteria name what would move the stance to Avoid:

- Q4 2026 adjusted EBITDA below $125 million;
- FY2027 revenue growth below 4%;
- FY2027 adjusted EBITDA below $740 million;
- FY2027 CELSIUS brand revenue down more than 3%.

Each moves the weights to about 60/40/0 (fair value $30.87), which is Avoid while the price stays above about $31. A price at or below about $28.35, or gross margin back above 48% with CELSIUS growing again, would move the call toward Own. The next catalyst is Q4 2026 results, expected around February 25, 2027. ## Independent Review

An independent reviewer (agent:reviewing-research) read two drafts of this note against the sources.

- **Round 1, grade C.** It found these problems:
  - net interest counted twice;
  - the May items reported selectively;
  - a generous reading of cash flow;
  - 17% growth inflated by the Rockstar acquisition;
  - a Q4 stub set too high;
  - claims clustered near 50%;
  - an unsupported behavioral tag;
  - kill criteria that could not lead to Avoid;
  - the settlement, the litigation and the post-2029 preferred dividends left out.
- **Round 2, grade B.** Every value and sensitivity recomputed. Problems still open:
  - The Q4 stub was 20.3% of revenue, not the 18.6% the note stated.
  - The early kill criterion sat below every modelled path.
  - 70% of the weight was on margin recovery, while the note's own claim put that recovery at 0.35.
  - The litigation list missed the Texas AG demand, a second securities class action over Alani Nu caffeine risk, two label suits and the Glazer indemnity.
  - Unconverted preferred carried no dividends to redemption.

**Changes after round 2:**
- Each scenario now has its own Q4: base $140 million, with the Alani load-in benefit in Q4 2025 noted.
- The early kill threshold is now $125 million, above the bear's $118 million and below the base's $140 million.
- Weights moved to 35/50/15.
- Every matter in the 10-Q is now listed.
- Dividends to 2032 are now deducted on unconverted preferred.
- 2027 figures are now calendar years.
- Both multiples are now restated on a pre-interest basis.
- The fair value fell from $46.67 to $43.12, 9.6% a year.

We did not adopt one point. The reviewer's counter-case puts 65% on the bear, which would rate Avoid. We kept the bear at 35% because the base's 2027 margin (23.0%) is already at Q3's level and does not need gross margin back at 48%.

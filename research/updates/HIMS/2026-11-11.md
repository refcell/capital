## What We Expected

The June call valued HIMS on FY2030 free cash flow at an exit multiple, undiscounted. The base case was $5.0 billion of revenue, an 11% free-cash-flow margin and 24x, about $51 a share (the report's discounted range was $28–35). The bear was $3.6 billion at 5% and 18x for about $13, and the bull $6.6 billion at 16% and 30x for about $116. It gave no weights. It expected Q2 and Q3 to show whether revenue accelerated, how far margins fell in the shift to branded GLP-1 drugs, and whether the Eucalyptus acquisition was strategic or "just expensive M&A". It set no kill criteria or probability claims.

## What Happened

Two releases have landed since the June call, both after the close: Q2 on August 10 and Q3 on November 9 (8-K Item 2.02).

Revenue accelerated as the June call hoped: Q3 grew 49%, with US revenue up 21% and rest-of-world revenue up 321% on the Eucalyptus acquisition, which closed in June. The quality moved the other way. Gross margin fell to 57% from 74%. Subscribers fell by about 103,000 in the quarter, from 2,891,000 to 2,788,000, while revenue per subscriber rose to $105. Fewer customers paying more is what a shift to branded GLP-1 drugs and acquired international revenue looks like. Q3 landed inside the August guide ($880–900 million of revenue, $75–95 million of adjusted EBITDA), but the full-year guide moved down. Revenue narrowed to $3.13–3.15 billion from $3.1–3.3 billion, and adjusted EBITDA was cut to $257–277 million from $275–325 million, 11% lower at the midpoint. Management said it was "intentionally pacing marketing spend ahead of several planned expansions to our platform in the fourth quarter".

The balance sheet changed most. Eucalyptus cost $968.5 million for accounting purposes, and the 10-Q shows $733.8 million still owed for acquisitions: deferred acquisition payable of $478.6 million current and $84.5 million non-current, and earn-out consideration of $86.6 million current and $84.1 million non-current. Most of it is Eucalyptus, about $610 million; the rest is earn-outs for Zava, Medici and YourBio. For Eucalyptus the company "has the option, at its sole discretion, to settle a significant majority of the deferred consideration and earn-out payments in shares of Class A common stock", capped at 19.9% of shares outstanding; at $19.13 that is at most about 32 million shares, or about 14% dilution. HIMS also issued $402.5 million of 0% convertible notes due 2032 in May (conversion price $29.53, with capped calls to $50.15), on top of $1.0 billion of 0% notes due May 2030 (conversion $70.67). Cash and short-term investments were $712 million at September 30, and working capital swung to a $114.8 million deficit from a $363.2 million surplus at the end of 2025.

Cash flow is weaker than the reported free cash flow suggests. In July two pharmacy subsidiaries entered an "uncommitted facility with an aggregate limit at any time of $400.0 million" with JPMorgan to sell receivables, and in Q3 they sold receivables with a carrying value of $434.5 million for $433.3 million of cash, which counts in operating cash flow. Receivables still rose to $427.8 million from $32.1 million at the end of 2025, because branded-drug sales tie up working capital. Nine-month operating cash flow was $119 million; after $108 million of capital spending and capitalized software (4.8% of revenue, against 10.3% a year earlier), company-defined free cash flow was about $11 million. The facility can hold at most $400 million of sold receivables at any time, so without it free cash flow would have been as low as about −$389 million; the 10-Q does not give the balance outstanding at September 30. Stock compensation was $143 million, and $81 million of cash went to taxes on net share settlement of employee awards. The Q4 revenue guide of $875–895 million is roughly flat with Q3's $890 million.

No Street estimate snapshot was captured for this update, so results are compared with the company's own guidance and the prior year.

## Estimate And Value Changes

The June values were FY2030 terminal values. This update states three-year values on FY2029 owner free cash flow at an exit multiple, plus cash at the end of 2029, less the converts and acquisition obligations, on projected shares. Owner free cash flow is the company's free cash flow (operating cash flow less capital spending and capitalized software), less cash paid for taxes on net share settlement, and without receivables-sale proceeds. Stock compensation is otherwise counted through 2.5–4.5% a year share dilution. The June values are not directly comparable.

**Margin anchor.** Committed SEC facts put trailing operating cash flow at 17% of revenue at the end of 2024, 12.8% at the end of 2025 and 6.3% at September 30, 2026; the last figure includes the $433 million receivables sale. Capital spending and capitalized software ran at 10.3% of revenue in the first nine months of 2025 and 4.8% in 2026. The base case takes owner free cash flow to 6% of revenue by 2029. That needs branded-drug working capital to stop growing faster than revenue and capital spending to stay near 2026 levels.

**Exit multiples.** `python3 -m capital.multiples` shows no usable own free-cash-flow history for HIMS (too few positive months). The digital-health peers captured for this update trade far lower than HIMS's own past: Teladoc at 0.3x EV/sales and 6.2x free cash flow, LifeMD at 0.3x EV/sales, and GoodRx at 1.8x EV/sales and 6.2x free cash flow (its own 68-month median is 15.7x). The exits are 8x free cash flow in the bear (near the 6.2x peers, no growth), 16x in the base (near GoodRx's own median) and 22x in the bull. These are applied to free cash flow before net debt, so they act as enterprise multiples.

The base case is worth 29% less than today's price, and its exit implies about 1.0x FY2029 revenue. The bull carries 60% of the fair value. At 25/50/25 weights the fair value would be $19.46, about 0.6% a year (Watch), so the stance depends on the bear getting more weight than the bull. The evidence since June supports that: subscribers fell, the EBITDA guide was cut, gross margin fell 17 points, and reported cash flow leaned on a facility the bank has not committed to keep.

## Action

Lower the value and change the stance from Watch to Avoid. At $19.13 the probability-weighted value implies about −4.0% a year over three years, below the rule's 0% line. The decline already prices a lot, but the base case does not support today's price once the converts, acquisition obligations and working-capital needs of branded drugs are counted. Kill criteria name what would overturn the Avoid view: subscribers growing again, a 2027 EBITDA guide that implies the margin turns, or positive free cash flow once the sold receivables still outstanding are taken out. The next catalyst is FY2026 results and the first 2027 guide, expected around February 23, 2027 (last year's release was February 23, 2026, after the close).

## Independent Review

A second reader graded the first draft C. It found that the draft missed the $433 million receivables sale that produced almost all of the reported nine-month free cash flow, understated capital spending (4.8% of 2026 revenue, 10.3% a year earlier, not 3–4%), counted all $734 million of acquisition obligations as Eucalyptus and settleable in stock, placed the scenarios in the wrong base-rate bucket, compared EV/sales on two different bases, and did not record its cash paths. Its counter-case argued for Avoid: the base case was worth less than the price, and Watch rested on a bull at the 94th percentile carrying half the value. This version adds the receivables sale and net-share-settlement taxes to owner free cash flow, records the cash paths, corrects each figure, and lowers the base margin to 6%. A second round graded B and corrected the size of the receivables pull-forward (at most $400 million, the facility limit), the kill criterion's measure, the missing Q4 2026 cash flow and the bear multiple. Fair value is $16.92 and the stance is Avoid.

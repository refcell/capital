The quarter answered the August question. The Q2 release said its 9.5% organic growth included "the expected benefit of advance orders placed ahead of ERP cutovers", and that its higher adjusted margin came from "a combination of IEEPA tariff refund and ERP-timing benefit". In Q3 those advance orders reversed, taking 630 basis points off organic growth. Over nine months organic growth is 2.9% and adjusted EPS is $5.48, up 20.9%, but the 10-Q says cost of product fell "primarily driven by the IEEPA tariff refunds of $100 million received in 2026". The 10-Q says the whole $100 million pre-tax benefit was booked to cost of product in Q2. It is not adjusted out, so it added about $0.46 a share to Q2 adjusted EPS at a 20% tax rate, and Q3's 19.9% adjusted margin contains none of it. The cash came later: Solventum had recovered $118 million of the refund by September 30, and the release credits the refund for Q3 free cash flow coming in "ahead of expectations". The prior-year GAAP EPS of $7.22 includes the $1,518 million gain on the Purification and Filtration sale. The initiation attributed that gain to HIS, which Solventum still owns.

Guidance moved up. Adjusted EPS is now $7.20–7.30, from $7.10–7.20 in August and $6.40–6.60 at the start of the year. Organic growth is expected toward the high end of +2.5% to +3.0%. Free cash flow is affirmed at $200–300 million. Without the refund, the midpoint is about $6.79, almost exactly the August base case of $6.80.

Cash is the weak line. Nine-month free cash flow was −$32 million even with the $118 million of refund cash recovered by September 30, about −$150 million without it, and the full-year guide implies $232–332 million for Q4, against adjusted net income of about $1.25 billion for the year. Trailing operating cash flow and capex are both $310 million. GAAP EPS for nine months was $0.54 against adjusted $5.48. The gap is amortization ($271 million) plus restructuring, separation and litigation costs. Restructuring and separation costs are mostly cash this year, as the ERP cutovers finish and Solventum exits about 90% of its 3M transition agreements. Litigation costs are mostly not yet paid: accrued legal, which covers litigation accruals and other legal fees, rose to $244 million from $43 million at the end of 2025. The largest exposure is Bair Hugger: 3M is the named defendant in about 8,400 U.S. lawsuits, and Solventum indemnifies 3M for uninsured liabilities, so that cash leaves in a later year.

Solventum bought back 5.3 million shares for $403 million in nine months under a $1 billion program, leaving $597 million. Only 0.6 million shares ($48 million) were bought in Q3, and the nine-month total came from the balance sheet: cash fell to $451 million from $878 million while free cash flow was negative. On HIS, the 10-Q says only that the company "is evaluating a range of separation pathways". HIS earned $424 million of segment operating income on $1,052 million of sales in nine months (40.3%), about 30% of segment operating income ($1,416 million).

No Street estimate snapshot was captured for this update, so results are compared with guidance and the prior year.

## Estimate And Value Changes

The August values applied a multiple to roughly current-year EPS. This update states three-year values at the end of 2029, undiscounted: FY2029 adjusted EPS times an exit P/E. Adjusted EPS already deducts stock compensation, which Solventum does not adjust out. The base for growth is FY2026 adjusted EPS without the refund, about $6.79. Values are on a consolidated basis, so they assume the HIS separation is value-neutral: in a spin-off holders keep both parts, and in a sale the proceeds replace HIS earnings. FY2026 sales are taken as about $8.23 billion: $6,165 million for nine months plus about $2.06 billion for Q4.

Each scenario's EPS is (revenue × adjusted operating margin − interest and other) × (1 − 20% tax) ÷ diluted shares.

| Scenario ($m) | 2026–2029 revenue growth | FY2029 revenue | Adj. operating margin | Interest and other | Diluted shares end-2029 | FY2029 adj. EPS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | +1.0% a year | 8,479 | 20.0% | 300 | 170M | $6.57 |
| Base | +3.5% a year | 9,125 | 22.2% | 300 | 166M | $8.32 |
| Bull | +5.0% a year | 9,527 | 23.0% | 300 | 164M | $9.23 |

The bear keeps stranded costs after HIS leaves and repeats the Q3 Dental weakness, so the margin stays near 2026's underlying 21% less tariffs. The base lifts the margin about one point as transition costs end and buys back about 1% of shares a year from the 170.8 million of Q3. The bull converts more of its earnings to cash and buys back about 1.4% of shares a year from free cash flow. It does not also count HIS proceeds, because its revenue still includes HIS.

**Exit multiples.** GAAP multiples are distorted for Solventum. `python3 -m capital.multiples SOLV` shows a trailing GAAP P/E of 98.6x and an own P/E history that ranges from 8.4x to 33.7x in 21 months, so neither checks an adjusted multiple. Sales multiples are cleaner. Solventum trades at 2.4x EV/Sales against its own median of 2.3x and a peer median of 3.1x (BAX 1.8x, BDX 3.1x, TFX 3.3x, captured for this update). At the base value, end-2029 EV/Sales would be about 2.5x, if net debt stays near $4.7 billion with buybacks funded from free cash flow, and the price would be about 16x FY2029 free cash flow if 80% of adjusted net income converts to cash. Today's price is 12.4x the FY2026 guide midpoint and 13.3x without the refund. The exits are 9x in the bear, where adjusted EPS stays unbacked by cash, 13x in the base (the August base multiple) and 14x in the bull, below the August bull's 16x because cash conversion has not yet recovered. Outside Solventum's own P/E, the sales multiples bound them: the bear implies 1.74x end-2029 EV/Sales (below its own p10 of 2.0x and near BAX's 1.8x), the base 2.48x and the bull 2.72x (below the 3.1x peer median). Each assumes net debt stays near $4.7 billion. A sum of the parts gives the other side: HIS earns about $565 million a year of segment operating income, so at 15x it alone would be worth about $8.5 billion, which values the rest of Solventum near 1.7x sales. That is the bull's route, but a sale of a spun-off business carries a large tax cost and leaves stranded corporate costs, and no terms exist yet, so the values do not add a separation premium.

## Action

Raise the fair value to $97.66 from $93.60, keep the stance at Watch, and set a negative bias. The increase reflects the move to a three-year horizon, not better news: without the refund the 2026 EPS run-rate is $6.79, the August base case. At $90.08 the value implies about 2.7% a year, between the rule's 0% and 15% lines but nearer the bottom. The bias is negative because cash conversion is unproven, buybacks are running ahead of free cash flow and Bair Hugger cash is still to come. Own would need a price at or below about $71 with the business on the base path, the base value of $108.16 discounted at 15% a year for three years. Kill criteria name what would move the stance: FY2026 adjusted EPS below $7.10, FY2026 free cash flow below $200 million, or an initial FY2027 EPS guide midpoint below $7.00. The next catalyst is Q4 2026 results and the first FY2027 guidance, expected around February 25, 2027 (last year's release was February 26, 2026, after the close).

## Independent Review

A second reader graded the first draft B. It found that the bull counted HIS twice: its revenue still included HIS while HIS sale proceeds also bought back shares and cut interest. It also found that the bear's 10x was generous for adjusted earnings that are not backed by cash, and that nothing outside the stock's own P/E supported the exits. On the facts, the draft overstated HIS as a third of segment profit, called litigation costs cash when they are mostly accrued, rated buybacks as strengthened although they slowed to $48 million in Q3 and came from cash, and had the claim probabilities clustered between 0.45 and 0.65. Its counter-case argued that the three-year return could be zero or negative. This version keeps the bull at $300 million of interest with buybacks from free cash flow only, sets the bear at 9x, adds EV/Sales bounds for each exit, moves the weights to 30/50/20 and sets a negative bias. The fair value is $97.66. A second round also graded B, with valuation now 4. It corrected debt principal to about $5,131 million (the maturity table leaves out $48 million of other borrowings), tied the Own price to the base path (about $71), and asked that the Bair Hugger exposure not be read as the whole $244 million of accrued legal. Its counter-case argued that a sum of the parts makes the negative bias too conservative: HIS alone may be worth about $8.5 billion at 15x. The note now shows that arithmetic. It does not add a separation premium while no terms, tax cost or stranded costs are known.

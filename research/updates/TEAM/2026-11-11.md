## What Happened

Q1 FY2027 (the quarter ended September 30) landed on November 5 after the close (8-K Item 2.02).

Revenue beat the top of the target by $57 million. Subscription ARR grew 23% to $6.81 billion, and remaining performance obligations grew 44% to $4.75 billion. The FY2027 targets rose: revenue growth to about 13.75% (from about 13%) and the non-GAAP operating margin to about 25.5% (from 25%). The Data Center decline target is now about 16%, from 17%.

The same targets imply a sharp slowdown for the rest of the year. The Q2 revenue target of $1,825–1,835 million is 15.4% above Q2 FY2026's $1,586.3 million, down from 23.7% in Q1. Applied to FY2026 revenue of $6,572.3 million, 13.75% growth gives about $7,476 million for FY2027. That leaves about $3,874 million for the second half, 9.0% above the second half of FY2026. Most of the gap is Data Center: it grew 4.8% in Q1 but is targeted to fall about 16% for the year, as Atlassian winds down the product. The filings give no reason for the 16% fall on November 6; the slowdown the targets imply is the plainest candidate.

Per-share economics have not changed. Stock compensation was $356.5 million, 20.1% of revenue, against 24.4% ($1,606.6 million) for FY2026. Free cash flow after stock compensation was about $106 million in Q1, helped by $458 million of receivable collections. Atlassian spent $581.4 million buying back 3.3 million shares at $174.10, 126% of the quarter's free cash flow; it issued 2.1 million shares for compensation, so the count fell a net 1.2 million. $1.3 billion of authorization remains. Cash was $1.12 billion, against $2.32 billion a year earlier; most of that decline paid for acquisitions in FY2026. The 10-Q says Data Center term-license sales to existing customers stop in March 2028, so the wind-down runs through FY2028. The 8-K also reported that the Chief Accounting Officer is leaving after 12 years, "not the result of any disagreement".

No Street estimate snapshot was captured for this update, so results are compared with the company's targets and the prior year.

## Estimate And Value Changes

The September values were discounted five-year values. This update states three-year values at the end of 2029, undiscounted: FY2029 (June 2029) free cash flow at an exit multiple, plus net cash, on projected diluted shares. Reported free cash flow adds back stock compensation, which ran at 24.4% of FY2026 revenue and is targeted near 19% for FY2027, so that cost is counted through the share count. Buybacks are assumed to use the free cash flow, so net cash stays near today's $0.12 billion. Because stock compensation roughly equals free cash flow, buybacks only offset issuance: the diluted count of 256.85 million stays flat in the bear and falls 0.5% a year in the base and 1% in the bull, where a 27% free-cash-flow margin against about 19% stock compensation leaves about $800 million a year of net buybacks.

**Growth.** The bear misses the FY2027 target (12%) and then grows 4% and 3% as Data Center sales to existing customers stop in March 2028. The base meets the 13.75% target and then grows 11% and 10%. The bull beats it (14.5%) and then grows 16% and 15% on Cloud and AI.

**Margins.** The FY2026 free-cash-flow margin was 20.1%; the trailing twelve months are at 24.1% after a strong collections quarter. The base case takes 23% for FY2029, near the trailing level, with the non-GAAP operating margin rising from the 25.5% target.

**Exit multiples.** `python3 -m capital.multiples TEAM` puts Atlassian at 22.9x trailing free cash flow, below its own 99-month p10 of 29.5x; the SIC 7372 peers have a median of 21.1x. Because stock compensation takes about as much as free cash flow, the exits sit below Atlassian's history: 14x in the bear, 20x in the base (near the peer median) and 26x in the bull.

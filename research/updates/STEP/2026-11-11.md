## What We Expected

The July call was a backfill from the initiation, and the initiation misread the company. It put the GAAP losses down to "acquisition accounting" and treated the FY2023–FY2026 revenue growth as acquisition-driven. StepStone's last large acquisition, Greenspring, closed in September 2021, before that period. The GAAP losses are mark-to-market expense on the profits interest in StepStone Private Wealth (SPW) and the effect of consolidated funds. GAAP revenue swings with unrealized carried interest. Debt principal was $275 million at March 31, 2026, not $1.31 billion, and AUM was $233.3 billion, not $13.1 billion. The initiation valued the company on a GAAP free-cash-flow margin of 2.5–8%. GAAP operating cash flow includes the investment purchases of consolidated funds, so that margin says little about the fee business. It produced a $25.61 base and a $33.26 fair value, 18% below the price.

The initiation named the August 6 report (Q1 FY2027) as the trigger. It quoted a Street FY2027 EPS of $2.49 and a $72.50 mean target. It labelled the $2.49 as EPS; STEP coverage normally estimates ANI per share, and the note compares it with ANI on that assumption. The initiation set no kill criteria or probability claims.

What the initiation missed entirely is the SPW buyout. In November 2022 StepStone gave the SPW management team a profits interest in SPW, together with an option agreement. StepStone may call the interest at the end of any quarter after June 30, 2027. The holder could have put it to StepStone from June 30, 2026 and has not done so. StepStone says it expects to exercise the call when it first can. The 10-Q puts the obligation at $2,885.3 million at fair value and $3,588.3 million undiscounted. The expected settlement is 25% in cash ($897.2 million) and 75% in Partnership units exchangeable into Class A shares, with tax receivable agreement rights attached. The price is SPW's adjusted net income times an adjusted trading multiple for STEP stock: 14.2x at September 30, when STEP closed at $43.40, or 18.39x trailing ANI. The 14.2x matches STEP's own P/E restated before tax (18.39 × 0.774 = 14.23), so the buyout is priced at STEP's own multiple, not at a discount. Its quarterly mark-to-market is the GAAP equity-based compensation that ANI excludes: $372.1 million this quarter. The board declared no FY2027 supplemental dividend and is keeping that cash toward the payment. Last year's supplemental was $0.55.

## Kill Criteria And Pre-Mortem

The July call set no kill criteria, pre-mortem or claims, so this update sets them (see `call.json`).

- **Core FRE.** The first criterion uses the bear's own path: core FRE (FRE less the profits interest's share) below $356 million for the four quarters to September 2027, which is 5% growth on $339.0 million.
- **Fee-earning AUM.** The second fires if fee-earning AUM is below $171 billion at September 30, 2027. That is 8% growth, below which fee growth would be tracking the bear.
- **SPW settlement.** The third tests the settlement against the 10-Q formula, which issues about 0.58 million units for each $1 million of the profits interest's trailing net income, divided by STEP's trailing ANI per share. It fires if the settlement issues more than 0.67 on the same basis, which would mean terms 15% worse than the formula. A large dollar payment caused by a high STEP P/E does not fire it.

## Estimate And Value Changes

The July values were five-year values on GAAP free cash flow. This update states three-year values at the end of 2029, undiscounted, on calendar-2029 ANI per share at an exit P/E. Pre-tax ANI has three parts:

- **Core FRE.** This is FRE less the profits interest's share: $339.0 million TTM, with other non-controlling interests taking 21.9% of it.
- **Non-SPW net PRE.** Performance fees net of all non-controlling interests: $104.6 million TTM.
- **SPW earnings.** The profits interest's net income, which is its FRE plus its share of performance and incentive fees: $172.0 million TTM, including $83.2 million from the December-quarter incentive fee. StepStone recaptures these after the buyout, less retention pay for the SPW team. Retention is our assumption, at 10% of SPW earnings; no filing gives a figure.

Pre-tax ANI also includes investment income and net interest. Net interest is the TTM −$11.1 million plus 5.5% on the cash part of the payment beyond the retained supplemental (about $70 million). The model holds that debt flat to 2029, which is conservative. ANI is taxed at the 22.6% blended rate.

The payment follows the 10-Q formula on the same earnings measure that is recaptured:

$3,588M × (SPW earnings over the four quarters before settlement ÷ $252.7M) × (STEP P/E at settlement ÷ 18.39)

The $252.7 million is the earnings the 10-Q estimate implies at 14.2x. A $83 million December incentive fee plus a continued rise in the profits interest's FRE gets close to it. A higher STEP price raises the payment, so units issued are 0.58 × SPW earnings ÷ STEP's trailing ANI per share at settlement, whatever the share price. Shares also rise 9.75 million over 3.25 years, the trailing 3.0 million a year from RSUs and Class D units.

- **Bear.** Institutional fundraising slows and core FRE grows 5% a year. SPW's earnings reach $200 million at settlement and then fall 5% a year, which is the peak-at-settlement risk in the pre-mortem. Non-SPW PRE slips to $80 million, and the exit multiple falls to 13x.
- **Base.** Core FRE grows 12% a year as the $42.4 billion of undeployed fee-earning capital turns on. SPW earns $250 million in the year before settlement, about what the 10-Q estimate implies, and grows 12% a year after it. Non-SPW PRE is $150 million, up from $104.6 million TTM, as the accrued carry pool is up 37%. Gross FRE then grows 16.4% a year. At a 42% margin that is about 14.6% fee growth, near the 82nd percentile for companies of this revenue size. We accept it because the commitments are signed and fee revenues grew 32% in the first half.
- **Bull.** Core FRE grows 16% a year, SPW earns $290 million before settlement and grows 18% a year after it, and carry realizations pick up. The implied fee growth is about 20% a year, near the 91st percentile, but below the 32% StepStone grew in the first half.

**The buyout dilutes at settlement and only helps through growth after it.** Because the 14.2x is STEP's own multiple before tax, each new unit buys $2.52 of ANI in the base: SPW earnings net of retention, after tax, less interest on the cash. That compares with $2.70 of ANI per share STEP is assumed to earn by then. Without a buyout, base ANI would be $3.10 a share on 135.2 million shares ($55.80 at 18x), against $3.17 with it. The 2% gain comes entirely from SPW growing 12% a year after the price is set. With SPW flat after settlement the base would be $2.93, or $52.74. Each 5 million units above the base's 53.6 million costs about $1.44 of base value.

Tax is not modelled as an upside. The SPW expense is not currently deductible and has added $93.4 million to deferred tax assets this fiscal year. But the consideration includes tax receivable agreement rights, which pass most of the tax savings on the units to the sellers.

**Exit multiples.** `python3 -m capital.multiples STEP` works on GAAP earnings, which are negative here, and its SIC 62xx peers are exchanges and brokers. Hamilton Lane, the closest listed peer, does not tag diluted EPS in its XBRL facts after FY2022, so the tool cannot compute its P/E either; it was left out of the dataset. The check is STEP's own multiple: 20.8x trailing ANI today and 18.4x on September 30. The base exit of 18.0x matches September's multiple and sits below today's. At 20.8x the base would be $65.94 and the fair value $61.33, 7.6% a year. That is still Watch.

## Independent Review

A second reader graded the first draft C. Its main points:

- **The SPW payment was modelled wrongly.** The draft held the payment fixed while the unit price varied by scenario. The 10-Q prices the buyout off STEP's own trading multiple, so the unit count depends on SPW's earnings, not the share price.
- **The buyout was called neutral.** Its no-buyout comparison could not be reproduced, and the kill criterion on units would have fired on the 10-Q's own terms.
- **Facts and framing were off.** It misstated March 31 AUM, left out the initiation's bearish checklist items that fired, cited SPW twice in the weights, set claims below the base, and assumed share creation below the trailing rate.

This version ties the payment to the formula, splits FRE into core and SPW, uses 9.75 million of share creation, adds retention pay, restates the checklist and corrects the AUM.

A second round graded B and found that the "discount" the draft relied on was the tax rate: 14.2x is STEP's own P/E restated before tax. It also found these problems:

- **Mismatched earnings measure.** The payment was computed on the profits interest's FRE, while the recapture included SPW incentive fees.
- **Kill criteria.** The FRE criterion sat below the bear path, and the SPW criterion could not fire.
- **Bear case.** It did not contain the SPW peak risk the note names.
- **Percentiles.** They were understated.

This version prices and recaptures the same SPW earnings (FRE plus incentive fees), shows the buyout as dilutive at settlement ($2.52 per new unit against $2.70) and the flat-SPW base ($52.74), and lets SPW fall 5% a year after settlement in the bear. Its kill criteria are core FRE on the bear path and a units ratio tied to the formula, and it restates the percentiles. The fair value falls to $56.89 from $62.56.

Its counter-case argued that the buyout is a transfer to the SPW team and the bias should be negative. We agree that the buyout is a cost, and the values now carry it. The bias stays neutral because every operating pillar strengthened this quarter. A negative bias would need the business evidence to weaken, not just the price to look full. The stance is Watch, and Own would need about $37.

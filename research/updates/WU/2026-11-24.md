The beat was real but not all of it will repeat. The adjusted operating margin recovered to 18% because the Beyond efficiency program cut costs faster than planned: management said it is ahead of its $50 million run-rate target for year-end, after almost $40 million of severance and early-retirement costs and a 650-plus headcount reduction. The CFO also credited "the timing of expenses" and a 9% adjusted tax rate, against 12% a year earlier. The guide rose $0.03, to $1.28-$1.38, which implies a Q4 of $0.31-$0.41 against $0.45 in Q4 2025. Revenue told the old story. CMT revenue fell 2% on 2% more transactions. North America region adjusted revenue fell 6% and LACA transactions 12%, while MEASA transactions rose 29% on lower revenue per transaction. Branded Digital revenue grew 8% on 21% more transactions. About half of that growth came from one Saudi partner whose account-payout business Western Union is now exiting for thin margins and working-capital problems, so Q4 digital revenue and transactions will be lower.

Cash was weaker than earnings. Nine-month operating cash flow of $321.2 million less $130.3 million of capex leaves $190.9 million, against $226.5 million of dividends paid. Management expects about $190 million of capex for the year, in a heavy renewal cycle for agent contracts. The Street's 2026 free-cash-flow estimate is $166.5 million, against a dividend of about $293 million; that implies a Q4 operating cash flow near $36 million. Our base assumes $135 million, the median of the last four fourth quarters ($59M, $265M, $134M, $135M), for about $456 million in the year. The FY2026 cash flow statement in February settles which is right. Gross leverage was 3.3x and net 2.0x, above the 2.5-3.0x target, which is why buybacks stay paused.

Intermex is over, at least on Western Union's side. The second HSR waiting period expired on November 2. On the evening of November 10, the outside date, California's DFPI delivered final conditions that Western Union said would have required "material changes" to its business model and agent relationships, and Western Union delivered notice of termination the same evening, effective November 12. No termination fee is payable either way. Intermex rejected the notice, and on November 24 it sued in the Delaware Court of Chancery for specific performance at $16.00 a share and damages. Its complaint alleges that Western Union "deliberately stalled" with the DFPI, that its CEO met the DFPI's head privately about whether $16 was too high, and that he told Intermex employees on October 13 the outside date would be extended if needed. Western Union says it acted within the agreement and will defend the suit. The deal would have cost about $500 million in cash plus repayment of Intermex's revolver.

Two financing moves followed. On November 10 Western Union drew the full $800 million of its January 2026 delayed-draw term loan, which matures three years from the draw, and used it about November 17 to repay the $800 million 2024 term loan due December 2027. Debt is unchanged, but the next big maturities are now the $615 million of 4.750% notes in June 2029 and the new term loan in November 2029.

Analysts did not move much. The S&P Global consensus is Sell across 18 analysts (1 Buy, 11 Hold, 2 Sell, 4 Strong Sell) with a $6.73 mean target ($6.00-$8.50); Cantor and Barclays kept $6 Sell targets and J.P. Morgan $7 Sell after the termination, and UBS raised to $6.50 from $6.25 at Hold. Consensus adjusted EPS is $1.32 for 2026 and $1.46 for 2027, but the page was last updated on November 13, so the 2027 figure may still include Intermex.

## Kill Criteria And Pre-Mortem

The October call had no kill criteria, so this call sets three. The first fires if Western Union declares a quarterly dividend below $0.235, or suspends it, before the end of 2027. The second fires if the Chancery court orders Western Union to complete the merger, or Western Union agrees to pay Intermex more than $150 million, before the end of 2027. The third fires if the FY2027 adjusted operating margin comes in below 15%, or if the February 2027 guide puts FY2027 adjusted EPS below $1.20 at the midpoint.

Of the initiation's risks, one became fact and one moved. The acquisition will not close on its terms unless a court forces it, and that is now a litigation risk rather than a leverage risk. Dividend coverage got worse in cash terms this year and better in balance-sheet terms this month. The pre-mortem keeps four failure stories: a dividend cut with the Q4 results; an adverse ruling or an expensive settlement in Delaware; mix erosion that eats the savings and pushes the margin back to 14%; and digital price competition after the Saudi exit. It also records how the call could be wrong on the upside: the dividend holds, the savings drop through and migration flows recover.

The base asks for little: revenue roughly flat after the 2027 Saudi exit, and a margin of 16.5%, below Q3's 18% and 2025's 19.5%, because the $200 million Beyond run-rate due by end-2027 is mostly spent offsetting mix. Base FY2027 EPS of $1.34 is below the Street's $1.46 for that reason, and because the Street's figure may still carry Intermex. The exit multiples are on adjusted EPS. The trailing gap to GAAP is about 35% ($1.42 against $1.05), but $0.12 of that is the one-off Beyond charge; the recurring gap, mostly the non-cash tax charge from the international reorganization, is about 15-20%. On that basis 5.0x is 5.8-6.0x GAAP, between the 5.6x 25th percentile of WU's own ten-year P/E and its 7.1x median, and 6.0x is about 7x GAAP, at the median. The payments peers in WU's SIC group trade at 26x and are not comparable; captured remittance peers IMXI and EEFT have ten-year median P/Es of 15.8x and 25.6x because they grew. The bear's 3.5x is about 4.0x GAAP, at WU's 3.9x 10th percentile, for a company that has just cut its dividend. The bear's $300 million Intermex cost stands for a court-ordered close at $16 (about $500 million plus Intermex's revolver, for a business worth less than that to Western Union) or damages of similar size; the call puts a forced close at 20%, and a $600 million outcome is a sensitivity. Across the cases the chance of a dividend cut before 2028 is 0.35 + 0.45 x 0.15 = 0.42.

Free cash flow drives only the net-debt path and the dividend coverage line: adjusted net income plus $60 million, less cash restructuring and $170-180 million of capex. In 2026, operating cash flow of about $456 million sits about $37 million above adjusted net income of about $419 million after Beyond severance. The $200 million end-2027 Beyond target dates from the program's July launch (recorded in the initiation); on the Q3 call management said it is "setting us up well to deliver against our 2027 Beyond Efficiency targets."

## Action

Raise, and keep Watch with the bias moved to positive. The published number rose only because dividends now count; on price alone our $6.51 end-2029 value is below October's $7.30 and agrees with the Street's $6.73, so the business view did not improve. What changed in the business is narrower. Q3 showed that the cost program works faster than expected. The termination removed a debt-funded purchase of more US retail cash business, though it leaves a lawsuit we cannot size well. Cash coverage of the dividend is still thin. At 11.4% a year the call sits below the 15% Own line, which is about $5.67. A 2027 guide of $1.45 or more with the dividend held, or a dismissal of Intermex's suit, would move the weights toward 25/50/25, at the Own line ($9.41, 14.7% a year). A dividend cut, a court order to close Intermex or a guide below $1.20 would make the bear the anchor. The first checkpoint is the December dividend declaration. The next hard one is the Q4 report and 2027 outlook around February 19, 2027. Next review: February 20, 2027.

## What We Expected

The June call was a backfill from the initiation report, written before the spin-off. It valued the whole of Middleby, Midera included, on five-year FCF multiples: the base was 5% revenue growth, a 15% FCF margin and 15x, $215; the bear $100 and the bull $359. At 25/50/25 the fair value was $222.25. On the spin-adjusted basis those figures are about $80, $173, $289 and $179. The initiation said the stance was "Watch / Needs more work" until the spin showed whether the remaining foodservice business could "compound per-share value without leverage excess", and it named post-spin operating margin and per-share FCF as the metrics that would settle it. It set no kill criteria or probability claims.

## What Happened

Since June, Middleby has become a single-business company. Midera was spun off in July. Middleby sold 51% of the Residential Kitchen business for $564.6 million net of cash, keeping 49% as an equity-method investment ($73.4 million) and an $84.1 million note receivable. In September it announced the closure of its Brewing businesses by year end. The Q3 figures below are continuing operations, which are now commercial foodservice only.

Q3 beat the top of the company's ranges for sales, adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS. The FY2026 outlook rose to net sales of $2,519–2,534 million (7.5% organic growth), adjusted EBITDA of $589–593 million and adjusted EPS of $7.13–7.29. The Q4 outlook implies organic growth slowing to 4.4%.

GAAP EPS fell to $0.43 because of $25.9 million of Brewing closure charges, mostly non-cash impairments, and $38.7 million of equity losses from the 49% Residential stake. Both are excluded from adjusted EPS. The Residential losses are a real cost to holders: $28.9 million in Q2 and $38.7 million in Q3 ($0.86 a share), a run-rate of about $135–155 million a year, against a stake carried at $73.4 million and an $84.1 million note receivable. At the Q3 rate the stake's carrying value reaches zero in about two quarters, and the note is then the exposure. On GAAP continuing EPS of $4.12 over the last four quarters, the stock trades at 33.6x.

Adjusted EPS grew 19% while adjusted EBITDA grew 9.5%. Most of the gap is the share count: Middleby bought back 4.8 million shares, 9.7% of the count, in nine months. That was paid for by the one-off $564.6 million Residential sale, not by the business: nine-month free cash flow fell 13.1% to $239.9 million from $276.1 million, so free cash flow per diluted share was flat ($5.24 against $5.22). Net debt fell to $1.64 billion from $2.01 billion at the start of the year, and net leverage is 2.8x. That is still above the $1.5 billion of net debt the initiation named as a bearish trigger for the post-spin foodservice business. On November 4 Middleby refinanced its bank debt into a five-year $3.0 billion secured facility ($2.0 billion revolver and $1.0 billion term loan).

On November 5 Middleby also announced that CEO Tim FitzGerald will step down on March 31, 2027. Steven Spittle, Chief Commercial Officer since 2021 and a Middleby manager for more than a decade, became President immediately and becomes CEO on April 1, 2027.

No Street estimate snapshot was captured for this update, so results are compared with the company's outlook and the prior year.

## Estimate And Value Changes

The June values were five-year FCF-multiple values for the pre-spin company. This update states three-year values at the end of 2029 for the remaining foodservice business, undiscounted: FY2029 adjusted EPS times an exit P/E. Adjusted EPS is adjusted EBITDA less depreciation (about $32 million) and stock compensation (about $25 million), less net interest, taxed at 25%, over diluted shares. Applied to the nine months, that method gives $241.5 million against the reported $245.3 million. It is 1.5% conservative because it leaves out about $3 million of other income and the deferred-financing amortization the company adds back. Adjusted EPS excludes intangible amortization (about $0.75 a share a year after tax) and the Residential equity losses, so it overstates owner earnings. The call gives the Residential stake and note no separate value. FY2026 sales are taken as $2,507 million, the outlook midpoint less about $20 million of Brewing sales that end this year.

Free cash flow is split between buybacks, debt and acquisitions in each case, at about 5.5% interest on end-2029 net debt (interest on the 2029 average would lower the base by about $1). Depreciation and stock compensation are held flat, which flatters the bull slightly.

- **Bear.** Restaurant equipment spending turns down after 2026's 7.5% organic growth, and the margin slips back to 22% as Brewing stranded costs and tariffs bite. Free cash flow of about $280 million a year buys back only about 2% of the shares a year (about $275 million over three years), and the rest goes to a mid-size acquisition at a low return, which the call gives a 20% chance of exceeding $500 million. Net debt stays near $1.64 billion, the condition under which the initiation named 13x.
- **Base.** Sales grow 5% a year, the initiation's base rate, and the margin settles at 24% once Brewing is gone. Free cash flow of about $350 million a year (about $1.05 billion over three years) buys back about 3.5% of the shares a year (about $680 million), and the rest cuts net debt to about $1.27 billion.
- **Bull.** Share gains with chains and in beverage and ice lift growth to 7%, and the margin reaches 25.5%. Almost all free cash flow, about $1.1 billion, buys back about 4.5% of the shares a year at higher prices, so net debt stays near $1.64 billion.

Base adjusted EPS grows 13.5% a year from the $7.21 outlook midpoint, of which about a third is buybacks and about a tenth lower interest. The growth rates sit at the 29th, 51st and 62nd percentiles of the base-rate distribution for companies with $1.5–3 billion of revenue. The initiation called the old company's revenue flat for three years, and Q4's outlook slows organic growth to 4.4%, so 5% a year is not a low bar.

**Exit multiples.** Today MIDD trades at 19.2x the FY2026 adjusted EPS outlook and 13.3x adjusted EBITDA. `python3 -m capital.multiples MIDD` shows its own GAAP P/E history at a 16.0x p10 and a 20.1x median over 114 months, and its EV/EBIT at a 13.3x p10 and a 16.5x median. Those are GAAP figures for the old conglomerate, so they run higher than an adjusted multiple would. The tool's peers, NDSN and SPXC (29.2x GAAP earnings), are industrial machinery makers, not foodservice equipment companies, so they are not used. The exits are 13x in the bear (the initiation's multiple for net debt of $1.5 billion or more), 17x in the base and 19x in the bull. With the net debt above, they imply end-2029 EV/EBITDA of 10.1x, 12.3x and 13.7x. The bear and base are below today's 13.3x; the bull is slightly above it.

## Action

Keep the stance at Watch with a neutral bias, and lower the fair value to $165.04 from the June call's spin-adjusted $178.80. The operating business answered the initiation's question well: organic growth of 7.7% and a 24% margin without Brewing. The per-share gains leaned on a one-off sale, though, per-share free cash flow was flat, and net debt is still above the initiation's $1.5 billion trigger. The price has caught up: at 19.2x adjusted earnings and 33.6x GAAP earnings it already pays for most of the base case. We have no view that differs much from the market's, which is itself a reason for Watch. Kill criteria name what would move the stance to Avoid: FY2027 organic sales growth below 0%, the adjusted EBITDA margin below 22% in any quarter of 2027, or net leverage above 3.5x. Own would need a price at or below about $109, or evidence that the bull path is under way. The next catalyst is Q4 2026 results and FY2027 guidance, expected around February 25, 2027 before the open (last year's release was February 26, 2026). The CEO change takes effect April 1, 2027.

## Independent Review

A second reader graded the first draft B. It found that the base case bought back 3.5% of the shares a year while holding net debt flat, which free cash flow could not pay for. It also found that the Residential losses were understated about fourfold, that the bull's exit was above today's EV/EBITDA, and that net debt had already passed the initiation's own $1.5 billion bearish trigger without being mentioned. Its counter-case argued for a negative bias. This version splits free cash flow between buybacks and debt in every case, restates the Residential exposure, and names the trigger.

A second round also graded B. It found that free cash flow per share was flat rather than lower, that the buyback pillar's evidence pointed to weakened, and that the bear used the initiation's 13x while ending with little debt. It also found that the weights contradicted their own reasons. This version keeps the bear's net debt at $1.64 billion through an acquisition, marks the buyback pillar weakened, and moves the weights to 30/50/20. The fair value is $165.04. Its counter-case again argued for a negative bias. We keep it neutral, because the weights already lean toward the bear and the operating results beat what the June call assumed.

## What We Expected

The July call was a backfill from the initiation report. It valued Archer as a cash NAV floor plus the option of FAA certification in "the 2027-2028 window". The base case was $8.66: a $1.66 NAV floor after one 1.4x dilution and $7.00 of pipeline value, on a burn of $500 million a year and a runway of 3.6 years. The bear was $1.16 with no certification and 2.0x dilution, and the bull $26.86 with certification ahead of schedule. The weights were 25/50/25, for a fair value of $11.34, and it stated no horizon. It set no kill criteria, pre-mortem or probability claims. Its biggest risk, "stated plainly", was certification slipping while the cash burned.

Q3 adjusted EBITDA of −$178.5 million landed at the favorable end of the −$170 to −$200 million guide. The Q4 estimate is revenue of $5–6 million and an adjusted EBITDA loss of $185–205 million, so the loss is still growing. Liquidity fell $215 million in Q2 (including $25 million for the Hawthorne airport FBO) and $207 million in Q3 (including a $10 million private investment). There was no equity raise in 2026: nine-month financing inflows were $16.8 million.

On a like-for-like basis with the July call, operating cash use was $156.4 million in Q2 and $164.6 million in Q3, $642 million a year, 28% above the call's $500 million base burn and 32% above the $487.4 million trailing figure it used. Capital spending adds $37.1 million and $31.7 million a quarter. Cash use also understates the burn, because Archer pays some vendors in stock. The equity roll-forward shows Class A shares issued for $42.1 million in Q1, $28.5 million in Q2 and $52.2 million in Q3 2026, $122.8 million in all, which the 10-Q describes as "Vendor Share Issuances" for "current and future obligations". Over the nine months, operating cash use ($470.1 million), capital spending ($101.4 million) and vendor shares ($122.8 million) come to about $0.93 billion a year. Shares outstanding rose from 744.0 million at the end of 2025 to 782.5 million at September 30 without any cash raise, about 51 million shares a year from vendor shares, awards and small acquisitions. The stock compensation the releases report ($242.5 million) includes the vendor shares; the separate stock-based compensation line in the equity roll-forward was $126.8 million for the nine months.

Operations moved forward. Midnight received an FAA Market Survey Special Airworthiness Certificate, "unlocking operations in Los Angeles in coming weeks" toward passenger flights under the DOT's eVTOL Integration Pilot Program and the LA28 Games. The Q3 letter says Midnight "is in the fourth and final phase of the FAA's type certification process, the implementation phase", but gives no type-certificate date. Piloted transition, the move from vertical lift to wing-borne flight with a pilot aboard, is still not complete: "we are closing in on completing that milestone by the end of the year." The Halo/Thunder hybrid aircraft with Anduril began turboshaft engine tests, and its "first full-scale flight [is] planned for late 2027". Archer delivered its first engines for Anduril's Omen.

The Boeing deal is the largest change. On August 9 Archer agreed to buy Wisk Aero (Boeing's autonomous eVTOL program), Insitu (ScanEagle and other Group 2 and 3 drones) and SkyGrid (airspace software). The price is Class A shares equal to about 19.75% of the shares outstanding just before closing, about 155 million shares on the November 4 count, plus two warrants of $100 million each, exercisable at $13.00 and $17.88 from the first anniversary. At $4.71 the shares are worth about $728 million. Insitu is "a profitable business with over $200 million in annual revenue"; no margin is given. Wisk's spending is not disclosed. Archer says it plans to grow Insitu's revenue "which can then meaningfully offset our continued spend to bring Midnight to market". Closing is "much sooner" than year-end, with "only one of the nearly 20 governmental and third-party approvals outstanding". The 10-Q says cash and investments "will be sufficient to fund the Company's current operating plan for at least the next 12 months".

No Street estimate snapshot was captured for this update, so results are compared with the company's own guidance and the prior quarters.

- Bear ($0.6 billion): type certification slips past 2029, Halo is late, and spending is cut. 2029 revenue is about $260 million, almost all Insitu. What is left is Insitu at about 1.6x its roughly $240 million of revenue ($0.4 billion), plus about $0.2 billion for intellectual property.

- Bull ($25 billion): certification in 2028, LA28 service, a Thunder program of record, and $900 million of revenue at about 28x.

The bull carries 49% of the fair value. At the July weights (25/50/25) the fair value would be $8.34, 21.0% a year (Own). At 30/50/20 it would be $7.33, 15.9% a year (Own). At 40/45/15 it would be $6.05, 8.7% a year. So the stance depends on the weights, and the bull weight is the judgment. The higher burn is not a reason for the cut: it is already in every scenario's share count. Nor is the missing type-certificate date, which was also missing in July. The cut corrects the July call's calibration. Its bull had no base-rate check; this one needs revenue to grow 61% a year, which about 2% of companies of this size have done, and an equity raise at $10, more than twice today's price. A quarter of the probability on that outcome was too much. The 15% still gives the bull nearly half the fair value. The base raise price is also a judgment: $5.00 is the average across raises spread over 2027–2029 in a path where certification progresses. Priced at $4.00, the base would be $5.50 and the fair value $6.08, 8.9% a year, still Watch.

## Action

Keep the stance at Watch and lower the fair value to $6.32 from $11.34. At $4.71 it implies about 10.3% a year over three years, between the rule's 0% and 15% lines. The July call's $11.34 against $5.18 would have rated Own under today's rule; the backfill kept Watch without a stated reason, and this update corrects the weights instead. The Boeing businesses add a revenue base and a strategic holder, but every path needs about $2–3 billion of new equity before 2030. Kill criteria name what would move the stance: piloted transition not done by March 31, 2027, a raise priced below $3.00, or combined 2027 cash use guided above $1.15 billion, which would be above the base case. The next catalyst is the Boeing deal closing (expected within weeks). After that come FY2026 results, expected around March 1, 2027 (last year's release was March 2, 2026, after the close).

## Independent Review

A second reader graded the first draft B. It confirmed the figures and arithmetic, and found five problems. The draft compared operating cash flow plus capital spending with July's operating-cash-only burn, which gave a "60%" increase that is 28% like-for-like. It counted vendor shares inside stock compensation and then again. Its scenarios did not end 2029 with the year of cash the lens promised. Its base cash use left almost nothing for Wisk. It called $260 million Insitu's revenue when that was the combined figure. Its counter-case argued for Own: the higher burn is already in every scenario's share count, so cutting the bull weight for it counts it twice, and at 25/50/25 the value is 21% a year. This version corrects each figure, raises base cash use to $1.0 billion, ends every scenario with a year of cash, and drops the burn as a reason for the weight. It keeps 15% on the bull as a calibration correction: the bull needs 98th-percentile growth and a raise at more than twice today's price. A second round also graded B. It confirmed the new values and asked for the nine-month burn figure ($0.93 billion), a correct label for stock compensation, kill criteria and catalyst lines that sit above the base case rather than on it, and a test of a cheaper base raise. Its counter-case argued for Avoid if the base case's 86th-percentile growth were also down-weighted. Fair value is $6.32 and the stance is Watch.

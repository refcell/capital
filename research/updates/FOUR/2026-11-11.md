## Estimate And Value Changes

The May values were 2030 values on adjusted FCF. This update states three-year values at the end of 2029, undiscounted, as calendar-2029 adjusted EBITDA times an exit EV/EBITDA multiple, less net debt, the TRA liability and non-controlling interests ($127 million at book), divided by fully diluted shares with the preferred as converted. EBITDA is the better anchor here because Shift4 guides to it and its conversion into adjusted FCF is stable. Adjusted FCF is operating cash flow less capex, so it is already after interest and taxes. The EBITDA paths start from the FY2026 guide midpoint and include Paramount's roughly $20 million from 2027.

Net debt rolls forward from $4,297 million at September 30, 2026:

- **Plus** the Paramount payment ($143 million) and the share of its earnout each scenario pays.
- **Less** adjusted FCF from Q4 2026 ($160 million, the guide midpoint) through 2029, at each scenario's conversion of EBITDA.
- **Plus** what adjusted FCF leaves out:
  - integration and other costs for 3.25 years, at a rate set against the trailing $109 million;
  - about $108 million of preferred dividends, seven cash payments of $15.4 million until conversion in May 2028;
  - $43 million of after-tax interest income lost on the $633 million of cash that repays the convertible in August 2027.
- **Plus** $90 million a year of buybacks that only offset equity grants (about $94 million a year), so the fully diluted common count stays at 77.76 million.

The term loan's full-year interest also sits above the FY2026 baseline behind the 42% conversion, by about $25 million after tax over 2027–2029. The model leaves that out, along with the operating leverage a flat 42% misses when interest is fixed (roughly $70 million in the base); the two roughly offset.

The scenarios are bands as much as points. The bear covers 2027 EBITDA growth below about 3%, the base about 3–9%, and the bull more than 9%. That puts about 40% on a 2027 guide of 6% or more, half the base plus the bull, which is the probability the claims use.

The preferred converts into 12.53 million shares at the maximum rate of 1.2224 at any price below $81.81, which covers the bear and base. In the bull, the price is above $81.81 and it converts at a lower rate.

## Action

Keep the stance at Watch, set the bias to neutral, and lower the fair value to $57.54 from $97.50. The May fair value was the base case alone, on a 2030 multiple that left out leverage and the preferred's conversion.

The cash flow is real: a record $200 million of adjusted FCF, and an FCF guide that held while EBITDA was cut. But the business evidence this quarter points down. Organic growth slowed to 8%, guidance was cut a second time, Q4 EBITDA is guided below last year, and buybacks were funded while net debt rose to 3.8x EBITDA. We put 40% on a 2027 guide of 6% or more, and 35% on the bear's flat year.

## Independent Review

A second reader graded the first draft B.

- **Organic growth overstated.** The draft cited payments-based growth of 18–23% and organic GRLNF of 9–11%. Organic payments growth was 14% in every 2026 quarter, and organic GRLNF was 8% in Q3.
- **Costs set too low.** Base integration costs of $40 million a year were far below the trailing $109 million. Preferred dividends were understated at $97 million, and non-controlling interests were left out.
- **Refinancing counted twice.** The bear counted it both in its lower conversion and as a separate cost, although the July term loan had already funded the convertible's repayment.
- **Paramount half-modelled.** Its price was in net debt, but its EBITDA was not addressed.
- **Probabilities did not match the weights.** The draft gave a 2027 guide that reaches the base a 45% chance while putting 70% of the weight on base and bull.
- **Kill criteria and checklist.** The leverage kill could fire on buybacks that add value per share, and the May call's change-my-mind items were not reported.

Its counter-case argued for a neutral Watch near $51–55. This version uses organic figures, charges integration at $80–109 million a year, $108 million of preferred dividends and the $127 million of non-controlling interests, replaces the refinancing double count with $43 million of lost interest income, and puts Paramount's EBITDA in the paths. It moves the weights to 35/50/15 and the bias to neutral. The fair value falls to $57.54 from $63.35.

A second round also graded B and reproduced every figure. It found a few remaining problems:

- **EBITDA kill.** Excluding Paramount made it unobservable, since Shift4 does not report Paramount's EBITDA.
- **Bear barely fires the other kills.** The bear only just tripped the leverage and FCF kills.
- **Claim against the weights.** The 40% claim matched the weights only if the scenarios are read as bands, and the note did not say so.
- **Interest cost.** The term loan's carry above the 2026 baseline was left out.
- **May items.** Three of them were still unreported.

This version states the EBITDA kill on reported figures plus $20 million, sets the leverage kill at 3.5x and the FCF kill at $470 million, states the bands, and explains the carry and operating-leverage offset. It also reports all six May items.

Its counter-case argued the call is now too conservative. Organic payments growth held at 14%, and the Q3 organic slowdown is mostly subscription that management is trading away. Holding today's 7.4x in the base, 6.0x in the bear and 30/50/20 weights would give $66.60, 15.05% a year, an Own. We keep the weights because the company itself guides Q4 EBITDA below last year after two cuts, so the bear's flat 2027 is close to the current exit rate, not a remote tail. We keep the multiples because a 7.0x base exit, a slight de-rating from 7.4x, suits a business whose guidance has fallen 6% in a year.

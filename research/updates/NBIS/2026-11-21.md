The headline beat rests on revenue that will not recur on the same schedule. $325.1 million of Q3 revenue, a third of the total, was "non-recurring revenue as part of our asset-light model", recognized at a point in time when partners deploy Nebius architecture on capacity they fund. Without it, revenue was $655.7 million, below both the $907.5 million consensus and the initiation's $850 million bearish line. Nebius does not disclose what that revenue cost. If it carried no cost, AI cloud adjusted EBITDA on the recurring base was $193.1 million, a 29.8% margin, against 49.7% in Q2. If it carried half its revenue in cost, the recurring margin was 54.8%. AI cloud costs rose 58% in the quarter while recurring revenue rose 13–32%, which points to the lower half of that range: lease and site costs for capacity that is not yet billing. September's ARR of $6.4 billion annualizes one month. At $533 million it was well above Q3's recurring average of about $219 million a month, so the arithmetic says much of the partnership revenue landed in September. The letter says partnership revenue "can add choppiness to ARR" and retires the metric. Two related points: Nebius now holds $348.6 million of notes receivable from, and $1.08 billion of lease prepayments to, two unconsolidated entities building data centers for it. It advanced a further $230.6 million on October 1 and intends up to $662.2 million more. Its maximum exposure was $1.46 billion at September 30. Cash did not keep up with the prepayments either: receivables rose to $4.04 billion from $720 million at December 31, 75% of them owed by one customer, and Q3 operating cash flow was −$273 million although deferred revenue rose $3.43 billion, which points to prepayments invoiced but not yet collected.

Guidance moved in three directions:

- **2026.** Revenue now at the low end of $3.0–3.4 billion (LSEG had $3.37 billion) because "some third-party capacity" moved into the first half of 2027. That implies Q4 revenue of about $1.04 billion, 58% above Q3's recurring $655.7 million. The adjusted EBITDA margin stays at about 40%. Capex rises to about $27 billion from $20–25 billion, spent on owned sites for 2027. The $7–9 billion December ARR target becomes more than $7 billion in February.
- **2027, first outlook.** Revenue $12–13 billion (LSEG $11.98 billion; S&P Global $12.36 billion). Capex $40–45 billion, against FactSet's $28.77 billion. Pre-tax breakeven "in Q4 2027", after financing costs and on five-year depreciation. More than 1 GW of new capacity a year. $17–20 billion of customer prepayments, after $10 billion in 2026. Prepayments and asset-backed financing on investment-grade contracts are to cover 75–85% of the capex. The guidance page adds "along with our balance sheet". The CEO letter's version does not.

## Kill Criteria And Pre-Mortem

The October call set no kill criteria and no pre-mortem, so none fired. This call sets five kill criteria. Any one of them moves the call to Avoid:

- the FY2027 revenue outlook is cut below $11 billion by the Q1 2027 report;
- shares outstanding plus pre-funded warrants exceed 370 million at any quarter-end through Q3 2027;
- RPO falls quarter on quarter, or a customer termination or reduction affecting more than $1 billion of contract value is disclosed, through Q3 2027;
- Nebius sells equity below $50 a share, or prices new secured debt above SOFR + 5%, before the end of 2027;
- group adjusted EBITDA margin falls below 30% in any 2027 quarter, or below 35% in two consecutive 2027 quarters.

Of the failure stories in the new call, delivery slipping at third-party sites has already started. The others are a GPU-pricing reset on renewals, funding at punitive terms, customer concentration, and the value proving to be the asset-light one-offs rather than the recurring cloud. They have not shown up yet, but the recurring margin is the one to watch, which is why it is now a kill criterion. The sixth story is that Watch is wrong on the upside: the selloff was indiscriminate and the stock re-rates before it reaches our price.

## Estimate And Value Changes

The October values were FY2031 values. This update states three-year values at the end of 2029, undiscounted, so the rating rule can read them, and uses the method of the November 11 IREN update. Each value is 2030 adjusted EBITDA (the forward year at the end of 2029) at an exit EV/EBITDA, less end-2029 net debt, less 75% of end-2029 deferred revenue, plus the $1.60 billion book value of equity stakes, over end-2029 shares. Equity value is floored at zero. `models/_build_NBIS_2026-11-21.py` reproduces every figure and prints every sensitivity below.

- **Revenue** is megawatts billing times a blended annual revenue per MW. Starting capacity and pricing are fitted to the FY2026 guide and the FY2027 outlook; the letter's capacity charts are images and were not read.
- **Capex** is $40 million per MW added ($42 million in the bear), half paid the year before the capacity bills, and all of the 2030 additions are paid for by the end of 2029, because the forward EBITDA counts them. The $40 million comes from the guide: $40–45 billion of 2027 capex "sized for more than 1 GW", which includes owned-site shells, power and pre-build. The letter's 1 year 7 months payback "on a revenue basis" at more than $25 million of ACV per MW implies only about $20–22 million of GPU and fit-out capex per MW on Q3 deals, without the shell. This is the most sensitive input: at $35 million the fair value is $131.01 (17.2% a year), at $45 million $87.73 (2.6%).
- **Net debt** rolls forward from September 30 with unrestricted cash only. It adds capex, the planned VIE advances and interest on the average balance, and subtracts EBITDA, deferred-revenue inflows and equity raised. Interest is the convertibles' cash coupons (about $263 million a year; their accretion is already in the $16.48 billion of principal) plus 7.0–9.0% on the rest. Receivables of $4.04 billion are collected down to 45 days of revenue.
- **Shares** grow 1.5% a year from stock compensation. A convertible series converts only when the case's value is above its conversion price times its accretion ratio; otherwise its accreted principal stays debt.
- **Deferred revenue**: 75% of the end-2029 balance is deducted. A prepayment is cash already in the net-debt figure, while the revenue it later becomes brings in no cash, and Nebius itself accounts for the prepayments as carrying a financing component ($121.1 million of interest accreted in nine months). The remaining quarter is float that new prepayments keep replacing. The end-2029 balances ($14 / $24 / $32 billion) are assumptions; Nebius gives no recognition schedule to derive them from the $17–20 billion of 2027 prepayments it guides. Deducting 50% or 100% moves the fair value between $119.20 and $99.60.

- **Bear.** New-deal pricing collapses toward $10–11 million per MW as GPU supply catches up and renewals reprice. The 2027 build lands late and smaller (0.6 GW), and 2027 revenue is $9.0 billion against the $12–13 billion outlook. Nebius cuts capex but still ends 2029 with $42.0 billion of net debt against $7.1 billion of 2030 EBITDA, and sells $8 billion of equity near $35. Enterprise value at 5.0x is below net debt, so the equity is worth nothing.
- **Base.** Nebius adds 0.75, 0.8 and 0.7 GW in 2027–29, below its "more than 1 GW a year", and 0.5 GW in 2030. 2027 revenue is $12.3 billion, at the outlook's low end. Blended revenue per MW falls from $17 million to $14.5 million as lower-priced hyperscaler capacity (the initiation put 2026 contracts at a base of about $12 million per MW) and repriced older GPUs mix in. Capex is $96.6 billion from Q4 2026 through 2029 plus $10 billion for the rest of the 2030 capacity; the 2027 figure of $31 billion is below the $40–45 billion guide because the base adds less capacity. $8 billion of equity is sold at about $90, and only the June 2025 notes convert.
- **Bull.** Nebius delivers its plan of about 1 GW a year with pricing near today's, and Token Factory and enterprise demand hold blended revenue per MW near $18 million. Every note series converts (68.2 million shares).

The exit multiples are half a turn below the November IREN update's 5.5x, 6.5x and 8.0x, and 1.5–2 turns below October's. Less of Nebius's base is contracted: RPO of $38.7 billion is 0.9 times base 2030 revenue, against IREN's backlog of about 1.6 times its base FY2030 revenue. The multiples are also lower than they look in cash terms. In the base, the 6.0x exit puts enterprise value at about $43.5 million per MW billing in 2030, roughly what each MW cost to build. On a five-year GPU life, base 2030 D&A is about $18.8 billion, so EBIT is about $1.8 billion (EV/EBIT about 67x) and pre-tax income is about −$2.3 billion. The base value is therefore what a buyer pays for a fleet still growing about 18% a year, not for its current earnings, and management's Q4 2027 pre-tax breakeven needs better pricing or lower capex per MW than the base assumes. `python3 -m capital.multiples NBIS` gives no usable check: Nebius files 20-F annual XBRL only, and its SIC 7370 peers are software companies.

On base rates, all three revenue paths (bear 77%, base 127%, bull 156% a year from $3.0 billion in 2026) sit above the 99th percentile for companies starting at $1.5–3 billion of revenue. Nebius is building a business from a small base, as IREN is, and that is a calibration warning in its own right.

## Action

Keep Watch, move the bias to neutral from positive, and lower the fair value to $109.41 from $288.98, now stated at the end of 2029.

The quarter was mixed. Contract terms improved and the funding mix tilted further toward customer prepayments. But a third of revenue was a one-off, the recurring margin cannot be read, receivables quintupled, delivery slipped two months, and 2027 capex came in about $14 billion above what the Street modeled. The stock fell 47% in seven sessions, mostly with the whole group, and now prices the base case at about 5.7x 2030 EBITDA, close to our 6.0x.

At $81.31 the weighted value is 10.4% a year, inside the Watch band. The bias moves to neutral because, on these numbers, the halved price roughly matches a value that also halved; the October positive bias rested on FY2031 values the 2027 capex plan no longer supports. Own needs a price near $72, evidence that capex per MW is nearer $35 million than $40 million, or Q4 results that justify a 20% bull: recurring revenue of at least $1.0 billion and RPO of at least $50 billion. The kill criteria above would move the call to Avoid.

The next catalyst is the Q4 2026 results and the first full FY2027 guidance, expected around February 11, 2027 (last year's came on February 12). The next review is February 12, 2027. The Citi facility matures around October 21, 2027 and needs a term-out before then.

## Independent Review

A second reader graded the first draft C. That draft rated Nebius Own at a $150.20 fair value (22.7% a year). The reviewer found the following problems:

- **Capex.** The 2030 EBITDA counted 500 MW of 2030 additions whose capex was not in end-2029 net debt, and the "about $33 million per MW" figure divided by MW that included 2030.
- **Cash.** Receivables of $4.04 billion and restricted cash of $1.22 billion were treated as cash.
- **Cost of capital.** Interest was charged on opening balances at 6.0–7.5%, with no stock-comp dilution.
- **Convertibles.** The conversion test ignored accretion.
- **Prepayments.** The 50% deduction was a pick, although Nebius accretes interest on them.
- **Claims.** The probabilities contradicted the scenario weights.
- **Multiple and weights.** The 6.0x exit was never tested against cash returns, and the weights were not defended.

The second draft fixed those and returned to Watch at $121.82 (14.4% a year). A second round graded it B. It found that half of the 2030 capacity was still unpaid at the end of 2029, that the 7.0–9.0% rate was charged on convertibles whose accretion was already in the debt, that the margin kill criterion sat inside the base path, that the $40 million per MW was unsourced, and that one margin sensitivity did not reproduce. This version charges all of the 2030 capacity by end-2029, charges the convertibles their cash coupons, moves the margin kill line to 30%, derives the capex per MW and prints its sensitivity, and prints every sensitivity from the script. The two model fixes move the value in opposite directions; together they lower it to $109.41.

Its counter-case argued for Own: the payback disclosure implies capex near $20–22 million per MW, and prepayments above 100% of capex make the bear a credit scenario the funding mix avoids. We keep Watch. The payback figure covers GPUs and fit-out on one quarter's deals, not the owned-site shells and power the $40–45 billion 2027 guide pays for, and at $35 million per MW the call would still need Q4 evidence to clear 15% with room. The bear's 35% reflects $42 billion of net debt, a 364-day bridge and pricing that can reset, not today's deal terms.

The quarter's profit comes from accounting, not from a change in the business. Under the July 29 settlement, Alexion pays $105 million in two $52.5 million installments, in August 2026 and Q3 2027, and stops paying U.S. Ultomiris royalties. Xencor recognized $27.6 million of it in Q2 for U.S. sales through June 30, and the other $77.4 million in Q3 because it has no further performance obligations. That revenue is booked now, but half of its cash arrives in Q3 2027. Astellas paid $25.0 million when it dosed the first Phase 3 patient with ASP2138 in gastric cancer. Incyte paid $1.5 million when it started a Phase 3 of VGA039, and Zenas's partner triggered $2.5 million with a Japanese filing for obexelimab. Q3 royalties were $87.5 million. Less the $77.4 million settlement, they were about $10 million: about $6.1 million of ex-U.S. Ultomiris royalties and $4.0 million of Monjuvi royalties. The Ultomiris royalties go to OMERS, which is owed up to $35 million a year through 2028 and $12 million a year after. Xencor has kept the Monjuvi royalties since OMERS's $29.3 million Monjuvi cap was reached in Q2. FY2027 consensus revenue of $115.79 million (S&P Global, November 19) therefore assumes about $75 million of milestones.

The balance sheet is what improved. Xencor sold 2.1 million Zenas shares for $66.9 million and keeps 1.0 million, worth $27.0 million at September 30. Cash and marketable debt securities fell only $55 million in nine months, to $555.7 million. The year-end 2026 guide rose to $480-500 million from $420-440 million, and the runway moved to "into 2029" from "through 2028." The midpoint implies about $66 million of Q4 cash use. The OMERS royalty liability fell to $95.1 million from $119.7 million and accretes at a 17.0% effective rate. The settlement has a cost the headline hides. Under it, Xencor must fund shortfalls in the royalties owed to OMERS, and holds $5.0 million of restricted cash against that. At Q3's ex-U.S. run rate of about $24 million a year against OMERS's $35 million, the shortfall could be about $11 million a year through 2028. The 10-Q does not give the terms.

The clinical news was about plans, not efficacy. XmAb819's 400 µg/kg IV dose, which gave the 45.5% confirmed response rate in 11 efficacy-evaluable ccRCC patients at ESMO, is now the proposed Phase 3 dose, and the expansion cohorts have completed enrollment. Xencor plans to meet the FDA in 1H27 about an accelerated path "in subsets of patients with advanced ccRCC who have exhausted standard-of-care treatment options." It will present the fuller 400 µg/kg cohort and the subcutaneous dose escalation in 1H27. Subject to FDA feedback, it plans a global randomized Phase 3 against tivozanib by the end of 2027, in patients after one PD-(L)1 inhibitor and at least one VEGFR TKI. Several sub-studies are enrolling or planned: papillary RCC, colorectal and lung cancer; TKI-naive ccRCC after nivolumab/ipilimumab; and a nivolumab combination safety study. XENITH-UC (XmAb942) is on track for a blinded interim around year-end and its primary endpoint in 2H27. XmAb412 healthy-volunteer data come in 1H27.

At ACR on November 9, Xencor reported subcutaneous plamotamab (CD20 x CD3) data from nine rheumatoid arthritis patients with about 16 years of disease. It depleted more than 99% of B cells. The four patients at the two-dose level had a mean CDAI fall of 25.0 points (more than 50%) and a mean DAS28-ESR fall of 3.3 at week 12. There was one Grade 1 CRS and no ICANS. XmAb657 (CD19 x CD3) gave a median 86% B-cell reduction in its highest healthy-volunteer cohort, and patient dosing starts in 1Q27. These are encouraging signals in a crowded field of autoimmune T-cell engagers, but from too few patients to value separately.

Analysts mostly held their targets. J.P. Morgan raised its target to $37 from $21 at Neutral after calls with renal oncologists. RBC ($43), Wedbush ($45), Canaccord ($40) and Leerink ($41) kept Buy. The S&P Global mean of 12 price targets is $43.83 ($26-$70), with 14 ratings, up from $40.25 at the October call.

## Kill Criteria And Pre-Mortem

The October call had no kill criteria, so this call sets three. The first fires if the 1H27 update of the 400 µg/kg cohort shows a confirmed response rate below 25% or related discontinuations above 10% (5.9% at ESMO). It also fires if no confirmed-ORR update covering at least 20 efficacy-evaluable patients is out by July 31, 2027. The second fires on equity sold below $22 before 2028. The third fires if the Phase 3 has not dosed a patient by June 30, 2028, or if the FDA rules out both an accelerated path and the tivozanib design.

None of the pre-mortem's failure stories showed up this quarter, because nothing tested them. The likeliest failure is the plain one: the response rate in 11 patients was a small-sample high. Five confirmed responses in 11 have a 95% interval of roughly 17% to 77%, and later cohorts of solid-tumor T-cell engagers usually come in below the early ones. The next cut will be the first real test. The other failure stories are that tolerability and logistics cap uptake, that burn outruns the plan into a raise at a low price, and that XENITH-UC misses. The pre-mortem also records how this call could be wrong on the upside: a clean 1H27 cohort and FDA alignment.

## Estimate And Value Changes

The method moves from a today-value to an end-2029 value, undiscounted, as the IONS and NBIS updates did, because the rating rule compares a three-year value with today's price. Net cash rolls forward from the midpoint of the year-end guide. Each year it pays operating expenses less stock comp and D&A, and receives cash revenue, which is revenue less the ex-U.S. Ultomiris royalties OMERS takes. It also pays the OMERS shortfall, receives the final Alexion installment in 2027, and adds equity raised. The Zenas stake is left out in case the guide already counts a Q4 sale; it is worth $0.27 a share. XmAb819 keeps October's peak sales and close to October's probabilities: no efficacy data have come out since ESMO, so the risk resolves by 2029 but does not shrink on average. The one deliberate uplift is October's NPV-to-peak multiples rolled forward three years at its 12% discount rate, 2.0x to 2.8x and 2.5x to 3.5x. Spending through 2029 is already in the burn, so this roll-forward is if anything conservative. The royalty and milestone portfolio, other pipeline and corporate overhead stay at October's today-values and are not rolled forward, because their 2027-2029 receipts and costs are already in the burn. What is left of the OMERS liability at end-2029 is deducted, as October deducted the whole liability: about $44 million, from $95.1 million accreting at its 17.0% effective rate less about $35 million a year paid in 2027-2028 and $12 million in 2029. It costs $0.45 a share.

## Action

Lower: Watch, with the bias moved to negative. Q3 made the downside safer but did not make the upside likelier. The cash guide rose, the runway moved into 2029, and Alexion's final payment is contracted. But the case for the stock is still a 45.5% response rate in 11 patients, and the price rose 11% while the market fell. The base case alone is now worth 1.3% a year. The stock closed at $35.17 on November 23, above our $34.66 value, so on that day's price the rule reads Avoid; the call keeps Watch at its $33.26 reference price, and a price still above about $34.70 at the January review moves it to Avoid. Own needs a price near $22.80. A 1H27 update with a confirmed rate at or above 45% in 25 or more patients and FDA agreement on an accelerated path would move the weights toward 20/55/25, and with the bull at a $3.5 billion peak and 75% probability the value would be $46.38 (11.7% a year), still short of Own at $33.26. The next hard checkpoint is the January corporate update with preliminary year-end cash (last year it came on January 8), then FY2026 results around February 24. Next review: January 9, 2027.

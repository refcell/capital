The contracts held and pricing rose. The AI Cloud segment gross margin was 86%. Prices on new contracts rose 11% for three-year deals and 12% for five-year deals. After the quarter IREN signed a five-year contract at about $22 million of revenue per MW, with 90% of the GPU capex prepaid, and a short-term contract at $40 million per MW. About two-thirds of the $20.3 billion backlog is with investment-grade counterparties. Operating ARR doubled to about $1 billion, with Horizon 1 accepted by Microsoft in August.

Delivery slipped. The "more than $4 billion" ARR target, previously year-end 2026, is now "targeted for January 2027" and "may extend to February 2027". IREN cites "a customer-requested change and supplier timing". The 10-Q says Microsoft asked for a network design change across the Horizons. Under the contract, delivery dates extend without delay credits for changes Microsoft requests, and that change "is being implemented in parallel with discussions regarding delivery dates and the application and payment of fees, credits and cost reimbursements". The 10-Q also cites third-party delays, which that protection does not cover.

Funding is still the main risk. Remaining FY2027 capex from September 30 is $15 billion. Of that, $9 billion is covered by cash, committed GPU financing and prepayments, and "$6 billion of target GPU financing, prepayments, data center financing and other corporate level sources" is "subject to market conditions". Capital commitments were $18.9 billion. The new GPU financings carry 9.0–9.5% stated rates and 10.4–10.7% effective rates, and they amortize over 30–36 months. The October call assumed 6–9% for secured GPU debt. Shares outstanding rose to 424.36 million on October 30, from 380.71 million on June 30 and the 394.06 million in the October call. At-the-market sales under the $6.0 billion program reached 76.8 million shares for about $3.7 billion by October 30, and 12.6 million shares paid for Mirantis. A further 31.7 million RSUs and 30.0 million Investment Rights were outstanding at September 30, so about 486 million shares are already outstanding or committed. That assumes the Investment Rights, whose terms the saved sources do not give, are exercised. The 5.1 million options are almost all out of the money. The convertible notes could add up to 127.2 million more shares. The share-price conversion trigger on the 2029 and 2030 notes ($445 million, convertible at $13.64 and $16.81, about 29.7 million shares) was met at September 30.

Bitcoin mining revenue fell to $28 million as the fleet is decommissioned. Stock compensation was $106 million, up from $43 million.

No Street estimate snapshot was captured for this update, so results are compared with the company's targets and the prior quarter.

## Estimate And Value Changes

The October values were FY2031 values discounted to today. This update states three-year values at the end of 2029, undiscounted, so they can be compared with the rating rule. Each value is FY2030 (June 2030) adjusted EBITDA, the forward year at the end of 2029, at an exit EV/EBITDA, less net debt at the end of 2029, over shares at the end of 2029. Stock compensation is counted through the share count.

The exit multiples are one turn lower than October's 6.0x, 7.5x and 9.0x: 5.5x, 6.5x and 8.0x. The new GPU debt costs 10.4–10.7% effectively, against the 6–9% the October call assumed. At that cost, interest takes almost all of the base case's operating profit. With FY2030 depreciation at about 38% of revenue, stock compensation at 3%, and interest blended at about 8.7% on $33.6 billion of net debt (convertible notes near 1%, secured debt at 10.5%), base FY2030 pre-tax normalized earnings are about −$0.7 billion. The base value therefore rests entirely on what the market will pay for growing EBITDA, 18.9x FY2030 EBIT at 6.5x EBITDA. A lower multiple is the honest response.

The share counts start from the roughly 486 million already outstanding or committed.

- **Bear.** The build stops near 0.6GW and 2027 capacity sells about 10% below today's blended $13.3 million per MW. Net debt still rises by about $13.4 billion to $18 billion, against $18.9 billion of capital commitments, some of which can be cancelled with charges. The $6 billion gap is filled partly with equity at low prices. The 2029 and 2030 notes stay below their conversion prices and are repaid. The equity is close to worthless, but positive.
- **Base.** IREN delivers the >$4 billion ARR by early 2027 and about 0.8GW by the end of 2027, as targeted, and about 1.0GW is billed in FY2030. About 114 million more shares are issued, near the remaining at-the-market capacity. The $445 million of 2029 and 2030 notes convert into about 29.7 million shares, which removes that debt, and the 2031–2033 notes stay debt.
- **Bull.** The bull reaches about 1.3GW at higher prices. A recovering stock lets all the convertible notes convert: about 97.5 million shares for the $6.3 billion of 2031–2033 notes and 29.7 million for the 2029 and 2030 notes, which removes that debt.

Only the >$4 billion ARR program, about 0.3GW, is contracted today. The base needs about 0.7GW more to be sold and built.

In the base, each 1.0x of exit multiple is worth $11.97 a share and each $5 billion of net debt $7.94. `python3 -m capital.multiples IREN` gives no usable peer check, because IREN still carries its mining-era SIC code (6199) and its only peer is SOFI.

The values are close to October's for offsetting reasons. Removing the October discount raises the base from $27.06 to the $58.62 it showed for FY2031. Valuing it 1.5 years earlier, at a lower multiple and on more shares, takes it back to $24.54. All three revenue paths sit above the 99th percentile of the base-rate distribution for companies starting under $1.5 billion of revenue, because IREN is replacing its business rather than growing it. That is a calibration warning in its own right.

The bull carries $12.35 of the $25.24. At the October weights of 30/50/20 the fair value would be $29.27, 14.0% a year, just under the Own line; at 40/50/10 it would be $21.22, 2.5% a year. The base alone returns 7.5% a year. Own would need a price at or below about $16.60 at these weights.

## Action

Keep the stance at Watch and move the bias to neutral from positive. Lower the fair value to $25.24 from $32.79. The stock fell 17.6% in eleven days, but the quarter's facts point both ways. Contracts and pricing are better. Delivery is one to two months later, the new GPU debt costs over 10%, and about 486 million shares are already outstanding or committed, against 394 million in the October call. Kill criteria name what would move the stance to Avoid: the >$4 billion ARR not operating by March 31, 2027; shares outstanding above 520 million on the FY2027 10-K cover; less than $3 billion of the $6 billion gap committed by June 30, 2027; or a customer termination or payment default affecting more than $100 million of backlog, or Microsoft fee reductions or credits above $100 million. Own would need a price at or below about $17, or committed data center financing with share issuance slowing. The next catalyst is the Horizon 2–4 deliveries in January and February 2027 and Q2 FY2027 results, expected around February 4, 2027. Last year's Q2 release was on February 5, 2026, after the close.

## Independent Review

A second reader graded the first draft C. That draft rated IREN Own at a $45.47 fair value. The reviewer found the following problems:

- **Shares.** It ignored about 62 million RSUs and Investment Rights, and gave the bull fewer shares than the base although the bull converts the notes.
- **Bear.** It priced capacity above the base.
- **Debt.** It missed the 10.4–10.7% effective cost of the new GPU debt, at which base FY2030 equity earnings are about zero.
- **Kill criteria.** They could not catch the bear's dilution path.
- **Fair value.** It credited part of the rise to the earlier valuation date, when removing the discount explains all of it.

Its counter-case argued for Watch. This version starts the share paths from the committed count, lowers the exits by one turn to 5.5x, 6.5x and 8.0x because of the debt cost, prices the bear at $11.7 million per MW, and moves the weights to 35/50/15. It returns to Watch.

A second round graded B. It converted the 2029 and 2030 notes in the base, corrected the bull's conversion shares to about 97.5 million plus 29.7 million, put net debt on a September 30 basis, costed secured debt at 10.5%, and asked for testable thresholds in the funding and customer kill criteria. The fair value is $25.24.

Its counter-case argued that the equity earns nothing at the end of the horizon, so the call is closer to Avoid. We keep Watch. That argument values a business still growing about 25% a year as if it were mature, and the bear at $1.79 already carries 35%. The kill criteria on dilution, funding and Microsoft fees are where the Avoid case would show first.

## What We Expected

The May call was backfilled from a report written just after a weak Q1. That quarter had revenue up 21% to $173.9 million, operating income down 52%, a service gross margin of 26.5% and negative free cash flow after $36.7 million of capex. The report framed the debate as whether OCS and the National OCS Program (NOP) could earn owner returns once aircraft, pilots, clinical staff, trials and facilities were paid for. Its base case was 15% revenue growth to about $1.3 billion in 2030, a 13% FCF margin and 20 times FCF less obligations, worth about $75. Its bear was about $15 and its bull about $195. It gave no weights and no horizon, and it set no claims or kill criteria, so nothing resolves in this update.

Its monitoring list asked for revenue tracking the $727–757 million guide, product gross margin holding 77–80%, service gross margin moving toward 30%, capex falling as a share of revenue, heart and lung reaccelerating, the material weakness remediated, and stable dilution. It said service gross margin in the low 30s with revenue growth above 20%, and consistently positive FCF, would make it more bullish.

Q1 2026 figures are from the May report and SEC XBRL; Q2 product and service margins were not extracted for this update. Q3 FCF is year-to-date operating cash flow of $77.8 million less the first half's $41.8 million, minus year-to-date capex of $52.1 million less the first half's $43.7 million. Nine-month operating cash flow fell to $77.8 million from $158.3 million because receivables grew $28.1 million and inventory $19.4 million.

Guidance rose twice. The May guide was $727–757 million. Before Q3 it stood at $737–757 million (the "prior guidance" in the November release), and on November 5 it rose to $750–760 million excluding PAD Aviation, or $764–774 million including about $14 million from PAD. That implies Q4 revenue of $197–207 million, 23–29% above Q4 2025's $160.8 million.

By organ, Q3 U.S. transplant revenue was $150.2 million from liver (+39%), $33.3 million from heart (+22%) and $2.0 million from lung (−47%). Revenue outside the U.S. was $6.6 million (+82%). Over nine months, U.S. heart grew 8% and U.S. lung fell 44%. Liver was 81% of Q3 U.S. transplant revenue.

Three other changes matter for value:

- **PAD Aviation.** On July 1 TransMedics bought a minority stake in PAD Aviation, a German charter operator, and consolidates it as a variable interest entity for $7.4 million net of cash. PAD's charter flying is not transplant work, so it adds revenue at aviation margins. TransMedics won its first European transplant logistics tender, in Italy, in October.
- **Pipeline.** ENHANCE Heart Part A completed enrollment in September. In October FDA approved IDEs for ENHANCE Heart Part B and DENOVO Lung, both using TransMedics' own cold-storage system, CHOPS, as the control arm. OCS Kidney is still targeted for first-in-human use in the second half of 2027.
- **Obligations.** Cash was $493.0 million. Against it sit $460.0 million of 1.50% convertible notes due June 1, 2028 (conversion price $94.00, with a capped call to $141.88), $55.0 million left on the CIBC term loan due July 2027, and the headquarters lease's purchase option. The 10-Q says the company is reasonably certain to exercise it, and the price is $374.6 million if exercised by December 31, 2027. Together that is $889.6 million due within about 19 months. On top of it, the 10-Q estimates $200–240 million of capex to build out the Somerville headquarters through 2030; $57.5 million is under contract and $13.8 million has been spent.

The material weakness in inventory controls is not yet remediated; management expects to finish testing in Q4 and targets remediation by year-end 2026. The Jewik and Collins securities class actions, filed after the January 2025 short-seller report, are still pending. The release adds a new risk: the CMS final rule on Organ Procurement Organization Conditions for Coverage (CMS-3409-F), released October 30, 2026. Web access was unavailable during this run, so the rule's content was not read.

The Street, per S&P Global data on StockAnalysis as of November 9: 13 ratings (Buy) and 11 price targets, mean $104.55 (median $110, range $70–$125). Consensus FY2026 revenue is $767.1 million and FY2027 $901.4 million (+17.5%); adjusted EPS $1.67 and $2.49.

How the May checklist fared:

- **Met:** revenue beat and guidance rose; Q3 product gross margin 79.2%; Q3 service gross margin 32.2% with revenue growth of 41%, the May report's own bullish trigger; capex fell to $7.1 million in Q2 and $8.4 million in Q3; FCF positive in Q2 and Q3; share count stable (34.73 million against 34.56 million).
- **Not met:** heart and lung did not reaccelerate together (heart +22% in Q3, lung −47%); the material weakness is still open.
- **Not checked:** aircraft utilization (not disclosed in the 10-Q).

The May report read the Q1 margin collapse as a question about the service model. The 10-Q answers it differently. Both gross margins rose year on year in Q3, and the drop in operating margin from 16.2% to 13.9% came from R&D and clinical-trial spending, which rose $13.9 million, and SG&A, which rose $15.3 million with PAD and the new headquarters. Spending on trials is a choice that can be dialed back once ENHANCE and DENOVO enroll; a service margin stuck in the mid-20s would not have been.

## Kill Criteria And Pre-Mortem

The May call set no kill criteria, so none fired. This call sets three: trailing-four-quarter organic revenue growth below 10% in any quarter reported through Q3 2027; reported service gross margin, which now includes PAD's charter flying, below 26% in any 2027 quarter; and FY2027 GAAP operating margin below 11%.

Of the failure stories in the new call, one is partly visible already: growth outside liver is weak and lung is shrinking, while liver, 81% of U.S. transplant revenue, faces OrganOx (Terumo) in normothermic perfusion and Paragonix (Getinge) in cold transport. The others (spending outrunning revenue, funding the obligations and buildout on poor terms, a CMS or OPTN rule that hurts NOP, and upside running past the call) have not shown up.

## Estimate And Value Changes

The new values are end-2029, undiscounted, on 2029 GAAP operating income times an exit EV/EBIT, plus end-2029 net cash, over diluted shares. Net cash starts at September 30 cash less the $889.6 million of obligations, assumed refinanced at no change in net debt, and then adds FCF from Q4 2026 and after-tax interest and subtracts the headquarters buildout: $180 million by end-2029 of the $206 million left at the midpoint of the 10-Q's range. The convertible's principal is treated as a cash obligation; above $141.88 the bull adds the shares the capped call no longer offsets. `models/_build_TMDX.py` reproduces every figure.

The base keeps 2027 revenue ($885 million) just below the Street's $901 million. Its 2027 operating margin of 13.5% needs operating expenses to grow about 11% while two IDE trials, kidney and three European launches start, after 48% growth in Q3; a slower 2027 that still reaches 19% in 2029 leaves the value unchanged only because the model sets FCF margins separately; in the business, lower 2027–28 operating income would also mean less FCF. On five-year revenue base rates for companies starting at $700 million to $1.5 billion, 8% is the 61st percentile, 15% the 83rd and 23% the 92nd. The base asks TransMedics to stay in the top fifth for three more years after growing 37% in 2025 and about 27% in 2026.

The multiple carries much of the change, and that should be plain. The base's 22x EV/EBIT equals 27.9x its 2029 FCF of $176 million, which is about equal to after-tax operating income. May used 20x FCF; on that multiple the base would be $87.84 and the fair value $99.76, 3.0% a year. So about $36 of the base's rise from $75 to $124.28 comes from the multiple, and about $13 from margins, the horizon and FCF that offsets the obligations. 22x is kept because it sits below TransMedics' own 10th-percentile EV/EBIT (24.9x) and the medical-device peer median (34.6x), for a business still growing 12–15% after 2029. It is a judgment. The bear's 15x is 1.8 times sales, about Baxter's multiple, for a company growing 8% with net debt; a $250 million equity raise at $25 (10 million shares, after 2% fees) would cut the bear to $31.84.

## Action

Raise the values and keep Watch, with a neutral bias. Q2 and Q3 removed May's main worry: product and service gross margins both rose, revenue reaccelerated, and FCF turned positive. At $91.38, after a 28% one-day jump, the stock prices most of that, and three things keep it short of Own: the business is 81% liver in the U.S. while lung shrinks, R&D and Europe will hold operating margin down through 2027, and $890 million of obligations plus about $206 million of headquarters buildout fall due against $493 million of cash, which makes a 2027 financing likely. The next catalyst is the Q4 2026 release, 10-K and first FY2027 guidance, expected around February 23, 2027; the next review is February 24, 2027. A FY2027 revenue guide of at least $880 million with service gross margin above 30% holds the base; a raise below $70 or a guide below $850 million moves weight to the bear.

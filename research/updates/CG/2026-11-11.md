## What We Expected

The October call was a backfill from the initiation report. It valued Carlyle on after-tax distributable earnings (DE) per share in FY2031. The base case had fee-related earnings (FRE) growing about 8% a year to $1.96 billion, about $1 a share of realized carry, $5.40 of DE per share at 11.5x, $62.10. The bear was $3.60 at 9.0x ($32.40) and the bull $7.00 at 13.0x ($91.00). At 30/50/20 the fair value was $58.97. It called the November 5 report "the first test of whether the record second quarter was the start of a trend or the top of one". It said a result near $0.90–1.00 "would already put full-year DE well above $3.54", while one near $0.70 "would validate the bear view". It set no kill criteria or probability claims. The initiation's monitoring checklist listed about eleven bearish signals, among them FRE below $300 million, transaction fees below $40 million, net accrued carry below $2.2 billion, management fees declining again, DE of $0.75 or less, and LTM inflows below $45 billion.

On the October call's headline test, DE passed. On its checklist, four bearish signals fired: FRE $292.6 million, transaction fees $32.6 million, net accrued carry $2.14 billion, and management fees down again. Others did not: DE was $1.12, the FRE margin 47%, LTM inflows $49.8 billion, and the share count fell. DE of $1.12 a share takes the year to $3.08 after nine months, so the full year will clear $3.54 if Q4 earns $0.46 or more. The quality was weaker than the headline. Pre-tax DE fell 8% from Q2, and the $1.12 came from an 8.2% DE tax rate. At Q2's 19.0% it would have been about $0.99.

The fee base, the October call's main concern, kept shrinking. Management fees fell 4.7% year on year. Carlyle attributes the fall to $17 million less in catch-up fees and to realizations in older buyout funds; without catch-up fees they fell 1.8%. Fee-earning AUM fell 1% to $330 billion, and inflows over the last twelve months fell 15% to $49.8 billion. Pending fee-earning AUM, the leading indicator the base case depends on, was flat on the quarter at $28 billion but up 48% from $19 billion a year earlier. FRE fell 6.2%, or $19.3 million, to $292.6 million. Management fees fell $27.0 million, G&A rose $17.0 million and depreciation $3.2 million, partly offset by $25.4 million less cash compensation and $2.2 million more fee-related performance revenues. Transaction fees were flat year on year. Over nine months FRE is $950.3 million, flat against $945.8 million a year earlier.

The carry pool also shrank. Net accrued performance revenues fell 12% in the quarter to $2.14 billion, down from $2.86 billion at December 2025. The main causes were realizations and lower marks in the seventh U.S. buyout fund and in secondaries. The share of performance-fee-eligible fair value that is in carry fell to 67% from 79% a year earlier. Corporate Private Equity's accrued carry fell to $734 million from $1,527 million at December. Separately, Carlyle expects to realize in Q4 a cumulative $222 million principal investment loss on a consolidated infrastructure fund. That loss is not carry, but it may reduce Q4 DE.

Capital return continued: 4.5 million shares repurchased or withheld for $210.4 million, with $1.4 billion of authorization left. Shares eligible for the dividend are down 2% this year to 353.9 million.

## Estimate And Value Changes

The October values were five-year FY2031 values. This update states three-year values at the end of 2029, undiscounted: FY2029 after-tax DE per share at an exit P/E. DE is built from FRE plus realized carry and principal income, less net interest. It is taxed at 15%, close to the trailing DE tax rate of 12.3%; the initiation used 21%, which would cut every scenario by about 7%. The same 15% is used for the sum of the parts below. Equity compensation is excluded from DE and counted through the share count, which falls only as fast as buybacks outrun grants.

- **Bear.** FRE declines with management fees as step-downs outrun new funds, and carry and principal income fall to $250 million as the pool keeps shrinking.
- **Base.** The $28 billion of pending fee-earning AUM and the next U.S. buyout fund activate in 2027–2028, and the margin ticks up as management guided. Carry and principal income are $450 million, below the trailing $512 million, because the accrued pool is 25% smaller than in December.
- **Bull.** Fundraising comes in well ahead and the carry pool rebuilds.

Base DE per share grows 5.3% a year from the trailing $4.09. At the initiation's 21% tax rate, base FY2029 DE would be $4.44, below the October path of about $4.75 for that year. Values multiply DE per share rounded to the cent.

**Exit multiples.** `python3 -m capital.multiples CG` works on GAAP earnings, which swing with unrealized carry, and only one of its SIC 62xx peers (STEP) is a private-markets manager, so it does not check a DE multiple. The initiation cites Carlyle's forward P/E of 11.8x at the end of FY2024 and 12.9x at the end of FY2025. Today it trades at 10.2x trailing DE. The base exit of 11.0x assumes a small re-rating: at today's 10.2x in every scenario the fair value would be $45.25, 2.8% a year.

A sum of the parts at the same 15% tax rate starts from today's $41.70. Removing after-tax net accrued carry ($5.13) and investments ($9.46), and adding back corporate net debt ($3.01), leaves $30.12 a share for the fee business. That is 10.1x after-tax FRE of $2.98. The sum of the parts is $47.34 at 12x after-tax FRE and $56.28 at 15x. The fair value sits near the 12x figure. A 15x multiple belongs to a manager whose fees grow, which is what the base case needs Carlyle to show again before it is paid.

## Action

Keep the stance at Watch, move the bias to negative from positive, and lower the fair value to $48.34 from $58.97. Q3 passed the October call's DE test, but four of the bearish signals on the initiation's checklist fired. Management fees, fee-earning AUM, inflows and the carry pool all fell, and the beat leaned on an 8.2% tax rate. Pending fee-earning AUM, up 48% year on year, is the main reason not to go further. ## Independent Review

A second reader graded the first draft B.

- **The base case was too high.** Its FY2029 DE sat above the October path even though the evidence had weakened. Two undisclosed changes did it: the tax rate went from 21% to 15%, and carry was assumed above its trailing level after the pool fell 25%.
- **Bearish signals were left out.** Four of the initiation's own checklist signals fired this quarter, and the draft did not mention them.
- **The FRE fall was blamed on the wrong line.** It was put down to transaction fees, which were in fact flat year on year.
- **Pending fee-earning AUM was shown only as flat.** The draft left out that it was up 48% year on year.
- **The FRE kill criterion could hardly fire.**

Its counter-case argued for a negative bias near Avoid. This version applies one 15% tax rate everywhere and shows the 21% case, cuts carry to $450 million in the base, lets the bear's FRE decline, lowers the base exit to 11.0x and moves the weights to 35/50/15. The bias is now negative.

A second round also graded B, with valuation now 4. It found that the new kill criteria fired too easily: carry could fall below $2.0 billion from realizations alone. It also found that the checklist was overstated as four signals out of about eleven, and that the scenarios started above trailing FRE. This version sets the kill criteria on the bear path, starts from trailing FRE of $1,240.7 million, and shows the sum of the parts at 12x and 15x.

Its counter-case argued the revision overcorrects. Pending fee-earning AUM is up 48%, and the base case with the dividend returns about 11.7% a year. We keep the negative bias until management fees grow again, which the call gives less than even odds by Q2 2027. The pending fee-earning AUM is why the stance is Watch and not lower. The fair value is $48.34.

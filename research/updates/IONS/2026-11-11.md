## What We Expected

The September call valued Ionis on five-year revenue at an exit EV/sales multiple: a $58.31 base case ($2.18 billion of revenue, 18.7% a year, at 5.0x), a $25.33 bear ($1.40 billion at 3.5x) and a $105.18 bull ($3.17 billion at 6.0x), weighted 25/50/25 to about $62. It treated TRYNGOLZA ($113.6 million of trailing sales at the time) as the lead wholly owned growth engine and called the next earnings report the primary catalyst. Its monitoring list named "Inventory exceeds demand" as the bearish TRYNGOLZA signal and "Guidance cut; breakeven delayed" for the earnings report, and its central test was a credible 2028 cash-flow breakeven bridge. It set no kill criteria or probability claims.

Q3 was the first full quarter of TRYNGOLZA in severe hypertriglyceridemia (sHTG), approved in June. Net sales were $9.3 million, against $31.8 million a year earlier when the drug was sold only for the ultra-rare FCS indication. The 10-Q explains part of the gap: "In April 2026, we lowered the Wholesale Acquisition Cost, or WAC, of TRYNGOLZA to address the sHTG population prior to receiving FDA approval." Volume in the larger indication has not yet replaced the price cut. Management cut 2026 TRYNGOLZA guidance to about $50 million from $100–110 million, "reflecting a more gradual launch ramp than anticipated", and still speaks of "multi-billion-dollar peak sales". Work-in-process inventory rose to $66.4 million from $24.1 million at year-end, "primarily due to our purchases of TRYNGOLZA drug substance".

The rest held up. DAWNZERA sold $36.8 million in the quarter, an annualized $147 million, and its $110–120 million 2026 guide was reaffirmed. SPINRAZA royalties rose 24% on 23% higher sales. Partnered programs produced two positive Phase 3 results (sefaxersen in IgA nephropathy and ulefnersen in FUS-ALS) and the HIBSAGO approval. Total 2026 guidance was reaffirmed: revenue of $875–900 million, a non-GAAP operating loss of $425–475 million, and year-end cash and short-term investments above $1.6 billion.

Cash and short-term investments were $1,884 million at September 30, down from $2,677 million at the end of 2025 after repaying $432.5 million of convertible notes in April. Nine-month operating cash outflow was $407 million. The remaining obligations are $575 million of 1.75% notes due June 2028 (conversion price $53.73, far above the stock), $770 million of 0% notes due December 2030 (conversion $98.10), and the Royalty Pharma royalty sale, carried at $566 million but capped at $550 million of total SPINRAZA payments, of which $178.0 million has been paid, so at most $372 million remains. The share count was 166.5 million on November 3.

No Street estimate snapshot was captured for this update, so results are compared with the company's own guide and the prior year.

## Estimate And Value Changes

The September values were five-year terminal values. This update states three-year values on FY2029 revenue at an exit EV/sales multiple, less projected net debt at the end of 2029, on projected shares. EV/sales stays the lens because operating income and free cash flow are negative. The multiple applies to total revenue, including partner R&D revenue, which is also the basis of today's 5.1x. The two fair values are not directly comparable.

WAINUA royalties halved in Q3 to $6.8 million. The 10-Q records that Ionis opted out of shared WAINUA development and US commercialization costs effective August 1, 2026, but gives no reason for the lower royalty; the model carries the Q3 run-rate forward.

**Cash bridge.** The 2026 guide implies a non-GAAP operating loss of $425–475 million on $875–900 million of revenue, so non-GAAP operating expense runs about $1,340 million. The base case holds expense near that level after 2026's low-teens growth and grows revenue about $130 million a year, so the loss falls to about $320 million in 2027, $190 million in 2028 and $70 million in 2029. Cash use runs above the loss, as it did in 2026 through working capital and deferred revenue: about $400 million, $200 million and $100 million.

**Net debt at the end of 2029.** Start from the guide's year-end 2026 cash of about $1,650 million. The Royalty Pharma obligation is capped: its SPINRAZA payments stop at $550 million in total, $178.0 million had been paid by September 30, and its share (25% through 2027, 45% from 2028, on up to $1.5 billion of annual SPINRAZA sales) came to an effective 21.6% of royalties in the first nine months of 2026 ($36.0 million on $166.6 million). At that effective rate, about $160 million is left at the end of 2029 in the bear, $150 million in the base and $140 million in the bull, and those amounts, not the $566 million book value, count as debt.

- **Base:** after $700 million of cash use and the $575 million 2028 notes repaid in cash, $375 million is left against $770 million of 2030 notes and $150 million of royalty obligation. That is net debt of about $545 million, on 177 million shares (2% a year dilution).
- **Bear:** uses $500 million in 2027 and 2028 and $400 million in 2029, and raises $700 million of equity at about $10, near the bear value (70 million shares). That gives net debt of about $555 million on 247 million shares.
- **Bull:** uses $300 million in 2027, breaks even in 2028 and generates $200 million in 2029, for net cash of about $65 million. Its share count of 179.5 million includes about 2.5 million shares from the 2028 notes. Those notes are in the money above $53.73, assuming the excess over principal is settled in stock as in 2026.

Base rates: the scenario rates are three-year CAGRs from the 2026 guide midpoint, placed in the five-year distribution for US filers with $700 million to $1.5 billion of starting revenue. The bear sits at the 12th percentile, the base at the 78th and the bull at the 97th. The base case is already demanding, and it needs TRYNGOLZA at five times its 2026 level. The multiples bracket today's 5.1x: the base compresses slightly to 4.5x, the bull assumes 6.0x for a company with several growing launches, and the bear's 3.0x is a shrinking, loss-making company. ## Action

Lower the value and change the stance from Watch to Avoid, at the boundary. At $28.19 the probability-weighted value implies about −0.6% a year over three years, just below the rule's 0% line. The base case is worth about today's price, the bear 74% below it and the bull 150% above it. The call is close: with the bull at 20% instead of 15% it would be Watch, and 38% of the fair value comes from the bull. What moves it back is evidence the sHTG launch is working: an initial 2027 TRYNGOLZA guide of $150 million or more, quarterly sales above $30 million, or a positive obudanersen readout. The next catalysts are the EU sHTG decision expected in Q4 2026, a likely January business update (the company issued one on January 12, 2026), and FY2026 results with the first 2027 guide around February 24, 2027. The obudanersen Phase 3 readout in Angelman syndrome, expected in the second half of 2027, is the largest wholly owned pipeline event inside the horizon.

## Independent Review

A second reader graded the first draft B. It found that the draft counted the Royalty Pharma liability at its $566 million book value although payments are capped and at most $372 million remains, that the bear and bull share counts did not follow from their builds, that net debt stopped at the end of 2028 although the horizon runs to late 2029, and that the WAINUA run-rate ignored the August opt-out. The revision fixed each and cut the bull weight to 15%, leaving Watch at $28.53. The second round also graded B. It found that the bear raised equity at $20 while valuing the stock at $8.58, that the Royalty Pharma share is an effective 21.6% rather than 25%, that the cash kill criterion could not fire even on the bear path, and that the base case had no cash bridge. With the raise at about $10, per-scenario royalty obligations and an explicit cash bridge, fair value is $27.69 and the rule rating is Avoid. Both rounds' counter-cases argued for Avoid, and the corrected numbers agree.

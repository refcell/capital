## What Happened

Q3 results landed on November 5 after the close (8-K Item 2.02).

Every property earned less. Las Vegas table hold was 18.9%, "below the property's expected range of 22% to 26%", and table drop fell 9.1%. Wynn Palace mass table drop fell 12.9% while its mass hold was a high 25.8%. Encore Boston Harbor ran through a strike that began in early September. Nine-month adjusted EPS was $2.83 against $3.02 a year earlier. With Q4 2025 at $1.17 and no guidance from the company, FY2026 is tracking near $3.83 (assuming about $1.00 in Q4), about 19% below the $4.70 consensus the September call used.

Some of the year's Macau profit was luck, and some of the US shortfall was bad luck. We normalize each property to its own 2024–2025 average hold. Wynn Palace's mass hold was 27.4% for the nine months, against 24.5% in 2024 and 22.8% in 2025; at 23.65% it would have won about $212 million less on its $5.66 billion of nine-month mass drop. Wynn Macau's mass hold of 16.4% against 18.1% cost about $90 million. Las Vegas at 22.8% against 24.75% cost $37 million, and Encore Boston Harbor at 19.0% against 20.6% cost $16 million. Wynn Macau VIP, at 2.44% of turnover against its stated 3.1–3.4% range, cost $12 million. Net of Macau gaming tax, US tax and the roughly 29% of Wynn Macau that Wynn does not own, these come to about −$0.05 a share for the nine months. Hold-normalized FY2026 adjusted EPS is about $3.78. The company does not publish flow-through rates; these estimates assume about 55% of Macau win and 90% of US win reaches EBITDAR. Each percentage point of Palace's normal hold is worth about $0.22 of EPS, so that benchmark matters more than the flow-through rates.

The balance sheet held. In September Wynn Resorts Finance issued $900 million of 6.875% notes due 2035 and redeemed the Wynn Las Vegas 5.25% notes due 2027, $880 million at the end of 2025. That costs about $15.7 million a year more interest. Other 2027 obligations remain: $750 million of Wynn Macau 5.5% notes, a $600 million retail term loan, and Wynn Macau's $600 million of 4.5% convertible bonds, which holders can put on March 7, 2027. The convertibles carry about $50 million a year of interest including discount amortization, so repaying them would lower reported interest. Wynn is also building the Enclave, a 432-suite tower at Wynn Palace budgeted at $900–950 million over about 2.5 years, with $750–800 million of Macau capital spending planned for 2027. Wynn contributed $62.2 million to the Al Marjan joint venture in Q3, $1.12 billion to date, and estimates its remaining share at $465–590 million. Al Marjan is "currently expected to open in September 2027"; the February release had said the first quarter of 2027. Wynn bought back 627,475 shares at $92.61 in Q3, and $268.0 million of authority remains. The quarterly dividend stays at $0.25.

No Street estimate snapshot was captured for this update; the comparison is with the year-ago quarter and the August consensus quoted in the September call.

**EPS bridge to 2029.** The start is hold-normalized FY2026 adjusted EPS of about $3.78, on about $2.1 billion of hold-normalized Adjusted Property EBITDAR. ## Action

Lower the fair value to $88.92 from $121.78 and keep the stance at Watch, with the bias moved from positive to negative. At $87.30 the value implies about 0.6% a year over three years, just above the rule's 0% line. The September call's value rested on consensus earnings that Q3 has undercut and on a Palace hold that has run well above its own history. What keeps it at Watch is that normal hold in Las Vegas and Boston would have offset most of Palace's luck, a dated Al Marjan opening, and a base case that sits on the price. Kill criteria name what would move it to Avoid: Q4 2026 adjusted EPS below $0.80 with hold inside the normal ranges, Al Marjan delayed past 2027 or Wynn's total commitment above $1.71 billion, or Wynn Palace's Q1 2027 mass drop below Q1 2026's. The next catalyst is FY2026 results, expected around February 11, 2027 (last year's release was February 12, 2026, after the close).

## Independent Review

A second reader graded the first draft C. It found the draft normalized Palace to 24% but Wynn Macau to its 2025 hold. It also left out the interest cost of the 2035 refinancing, applied 17x to adjusted EPS, above both peers, misstated the P/E, and had a kill criterion that would fire on hold alone. Its counter-case argued for Avoid. A revision normalized Macau to its own history, added an interest drag and a 16x base exit, and rated Avoid at −0.6% a year. A second round graded B. It found that the revision still normalized Las Vegas and Boston to range midpoints rather than their own two-year averages, and that it counted the convertible put as adding interest when repaying it would remove about $50 million a year. It also found that the Al Marjan kill criterion would fire mechanically as contributions are paid in, and that $1.95 billion of 2027 obligations was understated. Its counter-case argued for Watch. With one yardstick for every property and the interest corrected, the fair value is $88.92, 0.6% a year, and the stance stays Watch with a negative bias.

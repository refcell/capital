## What We Expected

The August call was backfilled from the initiation written on August 24. It rated the stock Watch with bear / base / bull values of $12.29 / $16.39 / $23.64 and a stated fair value of $17.18, 8% above $15.86. Its P/FFO lens used operating income plus depreciation ($2.88 a share, 5.5x) rather than the company's Adjusted FFO, and it named the Q3 report as the primary catalyst: if the beat streak continued and guidance was reaffirmed, the multiple could move toward 7x. The backfilled call had no claims or kill criteria, so nothing resolves here.

The initiation also got Park's structure wrong. It described a C-corporation that left REIT status in 2016, with 93 hotels across 37 states and a long-serving CEO named Scott S. Jones. Park is a REIT spun off from Hilton on January 3, 2017; it owns interests in 29 premium-branded hotels with about 21,000 rooms, and its chairman and CEO is Thomas J. Baltimore, Jr. (10-Q, Q3 release, September retention-award 8-K). The C-corporation pillar is marked broken below, and this update rebuilds the call on the company's own Adjusted FFO. `research/PK.md` stays the initiation of record.

Figures are from the Q3 earnings release (8-K, November 4) and 10-Q (November 5). Consensus and the whisper are from Earnings Whispers.

The demand story was better than the earnings story. RevPAR growth accelerated through the year, from about 5.5% in Q1 and 7% in Q2 (both excluding the Royal Palm) to 8.4% in Q3, with monthly growth rising from about 9% in July to more than 13% in September, and preliminary October up nearly 11%. Group rooms revenue rose more than 18%, Washington benefited from government-related demand (combined RevPAR +33%), Boston from the World Cup and Hawaii from group business despite three storms that cost about 280 basis points of RevPAR there. But hotel operating expenses rose 5% on occupancy, wages and utilities, and groups bought rooms without the usual banquets, so food and beverage revenue rose only 2% and the Comparable Hotel margin expanded 50 basis points. New York Hilton Midtown EBITDA fell 31% on RevPAR up 5%; New Orleans, Caribe and Signia Bonnet Creek were down. The stock had risen about 16% since the August report and fell 8.3% on a print that beat consensus but not the whisper.

Guidance rose for the third time this year. Adjusted EBITDA is now $635-$645 million (August $617-$637 million, February $580-$610 million) and Adjusted FFO $2.00-$2.05 a share (August $1.90-$2.00, February $1.73-$1.89), on Comparable RevPAR growth of 5.5-6.0% (was 3.0-4.5%). GAAP EPS guidance fell to $0.31-$0.36 from $0.35-$0.45, mostly on the $35 million of 2026 impairment and casualty losses and higher interest. The Q4 RevPAR assumption of 5.5-7.5% looks conservative against October's 11%.

The balance sheet changed shape. On September 30 Park repaid the $1.275 billion CMBS loan on the Hilton Hawaiian Village, due November 1, with a $700 million mortgage on the Bonnet Creek complex (SOFR + 2.25%, April 2029, extendable to 2031) and the last $600 million of its $800 million delayed-draw term loan (SOFR + 2.20%, January 2030). On the call the CFO put the cost plainly: about $1.5 billion of debt at the low 4% range was replaced with debt in the six to low sixes, "a $30 million type of headwind annually," of which about $13 million is in 2026. There are no significant maturities until the $725 million of 5.88% notes in October 2028; the $150 million Santa Barbara mortgage due in December is being refinanced with a $205 million loan that also pre-funds that hotel's renovation, expected to close in mid-to-late November (the release said mid-November, the call by month-end). Liquidity is about $1.3 billion, $1.7 billion of the debt floats, and Net Debt of $3,725 million is about 5.8x 2026 Adjusted EBITDA.

The growth Park points to for 2027-2028 is specific. The Royal Palm in Miami Beach reopened in July after a $110 million renovation; management expects about $3 million of hotel EBITDA in 2026, at least $14 million in 2027 and about $28 million stabilized by 2028, and says it adds about 200 basis points to 2027 portfolio RevPAR. In Hawaii, Park will have invested about $350 million when the Ali'i Tower renovation finishes in early 2027 and expects to recapture "$60 million-plus" of EBITDA over the next couple of years, back to the 2023 peak. Core group revenue pace for 2027 is up nearly 6% with rates up nearly 4%, and 2027 capex should fall to $175-185 million from $270-280 million. Of 19 hotels first designated Non-Core, 11 are gone for about $220 million at more than 16x EBITDA; five remain for sale ($37 million of 2025 EBITDA in the release, about $34 million on the call, $20 million of it Lake Buena Vista in Orlando) and three Safehold ground-lease hotels ($16 million) are tied up in litigation.

One governance item: on September 3 the board granted one-time retention restricted stock to four executives (331,564 shares to the CEO), cliff-vesting after four years, citing "leadership transition and proactive recruiting risks." The grants add about 0.3% to the share count.

Analysts edged up. The S&P Global consensus is Hold across 18 analysts (5 Strong Buy, 1 Buy, 10 Hold, 2 Sell) with a $17.21 mean target ($14-$21). Morgan Stanley raised to $15 from $13, Evercore to $18 from $17 and Cantor to $17 from $15, all at Hold; Bank of America kept Sell.

## Kill Criteria And Pre-Mortem

The August call had no kill criteria, so this call sets three. The first fires if the February 2027 outlook puts FY2027 Adjusted FFO below $1.80 a share at the midpoint, or FY2027 comes in below $1.95. The guide threshold sits further below the base's $2.10 than the reported one because Park's first guides run conservative: the February 2026 midpoint was $1.81, against $1.97 reported for 2025. The second fires if Park declares a quarterly dividend below $0.25, or suspends it, before the end of 2027. The third fires if company-defined Net Debt exceeds $3.85 billion at any 2027 quarter end, above the bear's path, which ends 2027 near $3.73 billion at year-end, before any Non-Core sale proceeds; the model has no quarterly path.

The initiation's main risk, interest coverage, moved against the call even as operations improved: the refinancing removed a maturity wall and added a permanent cost. The pre-mortem records five stories: a 2027 lodging slowdown after a strong event year; refinancing costs above plan that end in a dividend cut; a slower Hawaii recovery; a stalled Non-Core and Safehold clean-up with more impairments; and, on the upside, a rerating toward HST's multiple while we wait for a lower price.

## Estimate And Value Changes

The method moves to a value at the end of 2029 that includes dividends, as the WU and CNM calls do, and to the company's Adjusted FFO, which is what Park guides and what the Street prices. Value per share = 2029 Adjusted FFO per diluted share x exit P/FFO + the twelve quarterly dividends paid January 2027 through October 2029. Adjusted FFO is built as Adjusted EBITDA less net interest less about $18 million of taxes, noncontrolling interests and joint-venture items. The $18 million is calibrated rather than checked: it is what makes the 2026 guide midpoints tie ($640M - $215M - $18M = $407M, the midpoint of $402-$412M, or $2.025 on the guide's 201 million diluted shares). Adjusted EBITDA grows organically from $640 million and adds the Royal Palm ramp and the Hawaii recapture; Non-Core sales are neutral, because at 16x the proceeds retire 6% debt that costs about what the hotels earned.

The arithmetic that shapes the base is the interest bill against the embedded growth. About $13 million of the $30 million refinancing headwind is already in the 2026 guide, so $17 million is new in 2027; the 2028 and 2029 notes ($1.475 billion at 5.88% and 4.88%) then reprice near 6.5% in the base. Base net interest rises $28 million from 2026 to 2029, about $0.14 a share on 204 million shares with no corporate tax shield at the REIT level, against $55 million (about $0.27) of Royal Palm and Hawaii EBITDA. Interest takes about half of the embedded growth, and organic growth is held to 2% because Q3 showed hotel expenses rising 5% against RevPAR up 8%. Base Adjusted FFO per share grows about 4.6% a year from the $2.025 guide midpoint, to $2.32 in 2029.

The base exit is 7.5x, just below today's 7.85x. Leverage falls a turn, from 5.8x to 4.8x EBITDA, which argues for holding today's multiple; $1.7 billion of floating debt and leverage still well above HST argue for a little less. Today's multiple is not a PK-specific rerating: from end-2025 to October 2026 PK rose 59%, HST and SHO 43% and PEB 90%. Park's own year-end readings were 6.8x at end-2024 ($14.07 on $2.06) and 5.3x at end-2025 ($10.46 on $1.97), and the bear's 5.5x is end-2025's multiple. HST, with enterprise value about equal to its market cap, was 7.9x FY2025 net income plus depreciation at end-2025 and about 11x in October; that is not Adjusted FFO, but it bounds the bull's 9.0x. The bear also carries a 50% chance of a cut to $0.18 from the October 2027 payment, when bear free cash flow covers the $0.25 only 0.99x. The higher 2024 total of $1.40 included special dividends, so it is no precedent for a cut; the regular $0.25 has not changed since 2024.

Cash covers the dividend only once capex falls. In 2026, Adjusted FFO of about $407 million less $270-280 million of capex leaves about $130 million against about $200 million of dividends; nine-month OCF less capex was $111 million against $150 million paid. With capex at $180 million, base coverage is 1.21x / 1.37x / 1.44x in 2027-2029 and Net Debt falls to $3.52 billion by end-2029.

## Action

Raise the published value and keep Watch with a neutral bias. Fair value moves to $19.51 from $17.18, but on price alone the weighted value is $16.60, so most of the raise is the dividends, not a better view of the business: operations are better than in August, and higher interest takes about half of the growth they point to. The stance is 7.1% a year, inside the Watch band; it would take a price near $12.83 to reach Own at these weights. The next hard catalysts are the Santa Barbara refinancing by year-end and the Q4 report with the first FY2027 outlook, expected about February 18, 2027. Next review: February 19, 2027.

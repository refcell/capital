## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

DiamondRock looks like a straightforward reopening beneficiary that has already delivered much of its easy recovery, but the real question is whether a better portfolio and disciplined capital spending can sustain per-share cash earnings after the recovery tailwind fades. The stock is worth studying because an apparently inexpensive 9.81 times FY2026 consensus FFO sits beside genuinely strong operating momentum: second-quarter adjusted FFO was $0.44 per share against $0.37 expected, revenue was $316.6 million against $314.98 million expected, comparable RevPAR rose 7%, management raised 2026 guidance, and it projected 18% free-cash-flow-per-share growth. (PRNewswire, DiamondRock second-quarter 2026 results release, July 30, 2026; StockAnalysis forecast page, retrieved September 9, 2026.)

The catch is price and cycle. DRH had risen 39.5% over the preceding 52 weeks by the September 8 snapshot, while lodging remains among the most economically sensitive REIT categories. A hotel room not sold tonight cannot be inventoried for tomorrow, and room rates, occupancy, food-and-beverage sales and group demand can all weaken quickly. At the same time, $1.090 billion of net debt equals 3.64 times trailing EBITDA, so even moderate operating deterioration can narrow equity value faster than it narrows property value. (StockAnalysis overview, statistics and ratios pages, retrieved September 9, 2026.)

The thesis works if the 34-hotel, roughly 9,400-room portfolio can hold rate, grow group and resort demand, and convert recent renovations into sustainably higher RevPAR and FFO per share without requiring another heavy capital cycle. It fails if current RevPAR strength is late-cycle rather than structural, or if recurring hotel renovations consume enough cash that headline FFO overstates distributable economics. The decisive evidence is not GAAP EPS. It is same-hotel RevPAR and hotel EBITDA margin, adjusted FFO and AFFO per share, recurring capital expenditure, dividend coverage, and net debt to EBITDA.

### The Core Debate

The debate is not whether the latest quarter was good; it was. The debate is whether $1.22 of FY2026 consensus FFO per share is a durable base or a favorable point in a cyclical sequence. Bulls can reasonably argue that premium urban, resort and lifestyle hotels have scarcity value, short-duration room pricing resets rapidly with inflation, and Q2's 7% RevPAR growth demonstrates company-specific execution. Bears can reasonably answer that hotel FFO is unusually sensitive to demand, wages, insurance, utilities and property-level fixed costs, while FFO adds back depreciation without subtracting the recurring renovation spending necessary to preserve room rates. (PRNewswire, July 30, 2026; StockAnalysis forecast and cash-flow pages, retrieved September 9, 2026.)

## 2. Company Story

### Origin And Evolution

DiamondRock is a self-advised lodging REIT formed in 2004 and headquartered in Bethesda, Maryland. It was built to own rather than operate hotels: third-party managers run the properties, while DiamondRock allocates capital among acquisitions, dispositions, renovations, financing and shareholder distributions. Its shares trade on Nasdaq under DRH, and its portfolio now consists of 34 hotels with about 9,400 rooms. (StockAnalysis profile and overview pages, retrieved September 9, 2026.)

That history matters because DRH’s economic identity is a portfolio allocator, not a hotel brand. It does not own a Marriott- or Hilton-like customer network; it owns real estate and contracts with operators and brands where useful. Over time, the portfolio has tilted toward premium urban hotels, destination resorts and lifestyle properties. The strategic logic is to own assets where replacement is difficult, local demand generators are diverse, and renovation or repositioning can lift revenue per available room. (DiamondRock company description reproduced by StockAnalysis profile page, retrieved September 9, 2026.)

The inherited strength is tangible real estate in desirable markets. The inherited weakness is capital intensity. Hotels need periodic room, lobby, meeting-space, restaurant and building-system investment merely to remain competitive. Recent operating outperformance therefore matters only if it persists after the renovation bill. The portfolio’s post-pandemic recovery also makes simple historical growth rates deceptive: FY2022 through FY2025 captures normalization, inflation and portfolio changes, not a clean steady-state compounding record. Revenue rose from $1.002 billion in FY2022 to $1.075 billion in FY2023, then $1.130 billion in FY2024 before slipping to $1.120 billion in FY2025. (StockAnalysis financials page, retrieved September 9, 2026.)

### Why The Company Matters

DiamondRock supplies the physical hotel asset while brands, managers and travel intermediaries connect rooms to guests. Customers include leisure travelers, business travelers, groups and convention attendees; the exact mix varies by property and season. The pain solved is location-specific accommodation and event capacity. In a strong location, zoning, land cost, construction cost and a multiyear development process constrain new supply. Those barriers can make existing assets valuable even though hotel customers have essentially no switching cost.

The company’s edge, if it has one, lies in asset selection, local scarcity and property-level execution rather than technology or network effects. Management can create value by buying below replacement cost, selling mature or lower-return assets, selecting the right operator, renovating at attractive returns and financing prudently. It can destroy value just as readily by buying near a cycle peak, over-renovating, allowing leverage to rise, or issuing equity below net asset value. This allocator framing is more useful than treating reported revenue growth as the whole story.

## 3. Business Model

### How The Company Makes Money

DiamondRock earns hotel revenue primarily from rooms, with food and beverage and other property services supplementing the room stream. Room economics are a function of available rooms, occupancy and average daily rate; RevPAR combines occupancy and rate into the most useful top-line productivity measure. Group contracts offer visibility but can be booked far in advance, while transient demand reprices quickly. Resort and urban assets have different calendars and demand drivers, creating useful diversification but also making a single portfolio average incomplete.

Revenue is transactional, not recurring in the contractual sense. A loyalty affiliation or attractive location may encourage repeat demand, but each night must be sold again. The short booking cycle is a mixed blessing: rate can adjust upward quickly in inflation or demand strength, yet occupancy can fall abruptly in a recession, travel disruption or local event drought. Hotel costs include manager and brand fees, labor, utilities, insurance, property taxes, maintenance, food and distribution commissions. Some are variable, but enough are fixed or semi-fixed that modest RevPAR changes can cause larger hotel EBITDA changes.

The portfolio produced $1.136 billion of trailing revenue, versus $1.120 billion in FY2025, $1.130 billion in FY2024, $1.075 billion in FY2023 and $1.002 billion in FY2022. That sequence says more about a mature cyclical asset base than a secular growth company: TTM revenue is only slightly above FY2024 despite a very strong latest quarter. (StockAnalysis financials page, retrieved September 9, 2026.)

### The Economic Engine

**The engine is:** own scarce, well-located hotels; use brands and managers to attract transient and group demand; lift occupancy and room rate through market strength and property investment; convert RevPAR into hotel EBITDA through operating leverage; pay interest, corporate cost and recurring capital needs; then distribute or reinvest the residual per share. The weak link is the gap between FFO and true maintenance-adjusted cash flow. If renovations merely preserve competitiveness, adding back all real-estate depreciation without a recurring-capex deduction flatters owner earnings.

### Unit Economics / Customer Economics

Traditional customer acquisition cost and lifetime value are not the right unit economics because DiamondRock does not control a unified direct customer relationship. The relevant unit is a room night and, one level higher, a hotel. RevPAR measures room-revenue productivity; hotel EBITDA margin captures conversion; renovation return measures whether capital creates incremental economics; and debt yield or asset-level cap rate connects property cash flow to valuation.

Q2 2026 comparable RevPAR growth of 7% is therefore a high-quality operating signal, but one quarter cannot separate favorable calendar, market mix and transient pricing from enduring gains. The supporting evidence would be sustained RevPAR outperformance, stable or improving hotel EBITDA margin and adjusted FFO growth after recurring capital expenditures. (PRNewswire, DiamondRock Q2 2026 release, July 30, 2026.)

## 4. Products, Segments, And Moat

### Product / Segment Breakdown

The economically meaningful segmentation is by demand profile rather than accounting label. Urban hotels monetize corporate, group, convention and leisure demand around dense demand generators. Resorts monetize destination leisure, events and on-property spending, often with stronger peak pricing but greater seasonality. Lifestyle and independent properties can differentiate experience and reduce direct comparability, but may lack the reservation scale of large brands. Branded properties gain distribution and loyalty demand at the cost of fees and mandated capital standards.

| Property exposure | Role in thesis | Economics | Principal risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urban premium hotels | Recovery and group-demand engine | High rates and operating leverage when occupancy is firm | Corporate travel and convention cyclicality |
| Destination resorts | Pricing and leisure differentiation | Scarcity, ancillary spend and peak-rate potential | Weather, seasonality and discretionary spending |
| Lifestyle/independent | Repositioning and local distinctiveness | Potential rate premium after targeted investment | Execution and distribution cost |
| Branded hotels | Demand access and loyalty contribution | Broad reservation reach | Brand fees and required property improvements |

The company reports a 34-hotel, approximately 9,400-room portfolio on StockAnalysis’s profile/overview pages as of September 9, 2026; the categories above are an analytical framing, not separately reported financial segments.

### Competitive Position

Competition occurs property by property. Nearby hotels compete on rate, quality, meeting capacity, loyalty affiliation and reviews. DiamondRock’s advantage is strongest where a location is hard to replicate. Guests, however, can switch with a few clicks and online travel agencies make rate comparison easy.

### Moat Assessment

The moat is strongest in location scarcity and replacement cost, weaker in customer ownership, and easy to overrate because a prestigious hotel name does not necessarily accrue economics to the property owner. There are no meaningful network effects and little guest switching cost. Scale provides purchasing, financing and corporate-cost benefits, but 34 hotels is not enough to dictate terms across the industry. Operational complexity can be an advantage when repositioning difficult assets, yet the same complexity raises execution risk.

The practical moat test is financial: does a property sustain a rate premium, gain RevPAR index, protect margin and earn renovation returns through a downturn? Q2’s 7% RevPAR growth is encouraging evidence, but resilience across a full cycle would be stronger proof. (PRNewswire, July 30, 2026.) Overall, DRH has asset-level micro-moats rather than a corporate franchise moat.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Reported GAAP earnings understate the near-term cash earning power of a lodging REIT because depreciation charges reduce net income even when well-maintained real estate may retain or appreciate in economic value. TTM net income was $148.78 million while D&A was $114.44 million; simply adding the two gives approximate FFO of $263.2 million. This is why P/FFO, not GAAP P/E, is the primary valuation lens. (StockAnalysis financials page, retrieved September 9, 2026.)

Yet FFO can overstate owner earnings in the opposite direction. Hotel furniture, fixtures, building systems and guest-facing spaces wear out or become obsolete. The cash-flow statement shows $80.55 million of trailing capex/acquisition real-estate assets, reducing $243.99 million of operating cash flow to $163.44 million of conventional FCF. Because that line may combine maintenance, return-enhancing renovation and acquisitions, neither subtracting all of it nor ignoring all of it gives perfect AFFO. (StockAnalysis cash-flow page, retrieved September 9, 2026.)

The dividend looks well covered on the available measures. The annual dividend is $0.36 per share, a 3.01% yield at $11.97. It consumes about 29.5% of $1.22 FY2026 consensus FFO per share and approximately 45% of conventional TTM FCF per share of about $0.80. (StockAnalysis overview, forecast and cash-flow pages, retrieved September 9, 2026; payout calculations by author using 205.30 million shares.) The low FFO payout provides room for reinvestment and volatility, though taxable-income requirements and capital plans can alter distributions.

Cash conversion is respectable: TTM operating cash flow of $243.99 million equals 92.7% of the simple $263.2 million FFO approximation. Conventional FCF of $163.44 million equals 62.1% of that approximation. (StockAnalysis cash-flow and financial pages, retrieved September 9, 2026; author calculations.) Those ratios support the view that earnings are not merely accounting entries, while also quantifying why FFO should not be mistaken for cash available for distribution.

The largest unresolved quality issue is capital classification. A renovation that allows a property to charge a higher rate is growth capital; replacement of worn rooms is maintenance. Management has discretion in presenting adjusted FFO and FCF, and hotel transactions can create gains, impairment charges and denominator changes. Before ownership, the underwriting should reconcile company adjusted FFO to NAREIT FFO, then to AFFO, operating cash flow and cash after recurring capex on a multiyear basis.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

For a lodging REIT, management’s central job is to compound property cash flow and net asset value per share, not simply enlarge the portfolio. Acquisitions should clear a return hurdle after renovation, management fees and financing; dispositions should recycle capital when private buyers pay more than the company can earn by holding; buybacks should occur below conservatively estimated NAV; and dividends should remain covered through a normal downturn.

The current dividend policy is conservative on headline measures. A $0.36 annual dividend is only 29.5% of $1.22 consensus FFO and about 45% of estimated conventional TTM FCF per share, leaving cash for hotels and debt reduction. (StockAnalysis overview, forecast and cash-flow pages, retrieved September 9, 2026; author calculations.) The 3.01% yield is not the primary attraction; per-share cash-flow growth and asset value must carry the thesis.

The leverage record deserves equal weight. Net debt of $1.090 billion and 3.64 times net debt/EBITDA are manageable, but 3.01 times interest coverage means capital allocation cannot be judged independently of refinancing conditions. (StockAnalysis balance-sheet and ratios pages, retrieved September 9, 2026.) A large debt-financed acquisition could turn an acceptable balance sheet into the central risk, while debt reduction after asset sales would improve downside resilience.

This report does not claim a conclusion on executive ownership, compensation design, recent insider trading, debt maturity ladders or individual acquisition returns because those details are not established by the cited snapshot pages. That is an explicit diligence gap, not evidence of a governance problem. The latest proxy, 10-K and 10-Q should be reviewed before upgrading the stance from Watch.

### What Bulls Believe

Bulls see a scarce portfolio producing better operating evidence than its multiple implies. Q2 comparable RevPAR rose 7%, adjusted FFO beat by $0.07 per share and guidance increased, yet the shares trade at only 9.81 times FY2026 consensus FFO. Retained cash can fund renovations that reinforce rate, reduce debt or repurchase undervalued shares.

### What Bears Believe

Bears see a classic cyclical-value setup after a strong tape. The shares gained 39.5% in 52 weeks, so the easy rerating may already have occurred. Revenue’s path from $1.130 billion in FY2024 to $1.120 billion in FY2025 and $1.136 billion TTM does not demonstrate secular growth, while hotel demand can reverse quickly. ### What The Market May Be Missing

The market appears focused on the latest RevPAR beat and low P/FFO. The more important positive issue may be whether portfolio investment is finally entering a harvest period, allowing the projected 18% FCF-per-share growth to close the FFO-to-cash gap. The more important negative issue may be that nominal RevPAR growth masks rising labor, insurance and renovation costs. (PRNewswire, July 30, 2026.) Both variants are testable through margin and recurring-capex disclosure.

The report’s modest positive variant is that a 10 times base multiple does not require a heroic rerating; terminal FFO of $1.45 alone supports $14.50. ## 10. Bull Case

If the Q2 strength reflects durable property-level share gains and successful repositioning, then RevPAR can grow faster than portfolio costs, hotel EBITDA margin can hold or expand, and FFO per share can compound about 7.5% from $1.22 to $1.75 over five years. At 11.0 times terminal FFO, the stock is worth $19.25, 60.8% above $11.97 before dividends. (PRNewswire, July 30, 2026; StockAnalysis forecast and overview pages retrieved September 9, 2026; author scenario analysis.)

The mechanism is operating leverage plus disciplined capital allocation, not rapid unit expansion. Short-duration room rates capture demand and inflation, group bookings improve base occupancy, renovations support a higher rate, and retained cash limits debt or share issuance. The first evidence should show up in sustained comparable RevPAR growth, stable-to-rising hotel EBITDA margins, annual adjusted FFO above guidance and AFFO growth that approximates management’s 18% 2026 FCF-per-share expectation.

## 11. Bear Case

If travel demand weakens or costs outrun rate, then occupancy and hotel margin fall, fixed charges absorb more cash, and recurring renovations prevent FFO from translating into owner earnings. Terminal FFO per share falls to $1.15 and the market applies 8.5 times, producing $9.78 of value, 18.3% below $11.97 before dividends. (Author scenario analysis dated September 9, 2026.)

The balance sheet amplifies rather than causes this outcome. A 20% decline from $299.53 million of TTM EBITDA would raise static $1.090 billion net debt from 3.64 times to roughly 4.55 times before any cash burn or asset sales. (StockAnalysis statistics and ratios pages retrieved September 9, 2026; author sensitivity.) The first evidence should show up in weaker booking pace and comparable RevPAR, discounting-led occupancy support, hotel EBITDA margin compression, lower guidance and declining interest coverage.

## 12. Base Case

The most likely case is neither a recession nor an uninterrupted Q2 growth rate. Nominal room rate and selective group/resort strength offset uneven occupancy and expense pressure. Portfolio revenue grows slowly, consistent with FY2026 consensus revenue of $1.14 billion and 1.98% growth, while modest operating leverage and disciplined share count allow FFO per share to compound approximately 3.5% to $1.45 over five years. (StockAnalysis forecast page, retrieved September 9, 2026; author scenario.)

Capital intensity remains real: conventional FCF stays below FFO because hotels require reinvestment. The dividend remains covered, excess cash funds a mix of renovations and balance-sheet protection, and net debt/EBITDA stays around or below its current 3.64 times rather than rising. A 10 times terminal P/FFO—close to today’s 9.81 times—is appropriate for moderate growth, tangible assets and cyclical risk. That yields $14.50 per share, 21.1% upside before dividends. The market needs to see FFO-to-AFFO conversion and stable leverage, not merely another adjusted quarterly beat.

## 13. Valuation

### Current Multiples

P/FFO is primary because GAAP P/E is distorted by real-estate depreciation. FFO reverses depreciation and selected property-sale effects to approximate recurring property earnings, although AFFO is preferable for dividend capacity because it deducts recurring capital needs. EV/EBITDA is useful for capital-structure comparison, leverage tests downside, and P/B is only a rough cross-check because accumulated depreciation can make historical book value diverge from current property value.

|---|---:|---|
| FY2026 P/FFO | 9.81x | Moderate valuation on consensus $1.22/share |
| EV/EBITDA | 11.84x | Includes debt claim and supports a less “cheap” reading |
| Net debt/EBITDA | 3.64x | Manageable, cyclical sensitivity remains |
| Interest coverage | 3.01x | Adequate but not a deep cushion |
| Price/book | 1.61x | Limited usefulness due to property depreciation/history |
| Dividend yield | 3.01% | Covered, but not high enough to anchor the thesis alone |

### Primary Valuation Framework

The primary framework applies a normalized P/FFO multiple to terminal FFO per share, then checks whether AFFO coverage and leverage make that FFO credible. It is superior to GAAP P/E because TTM D&A of $114.44 million materially reduces $148.78 million of net income even though the underlying real estate has a much longer economic life. (StockAnalysis financials page, retrieved September 9, 2026.) It is superior to a standalone dividend model because the $0.36 payout retains much of cash earning power and management can vary distributions.

### Scenario Valuation

Upside comes from both earning growth and a modest multiple increase; downside comes from lower earning power and multiple compression. The symmetric 25%/50%/25% probabilities produce $14.51: 25% of $9.78, 50% of $14.50 and 25% of $19.25. That is 21.2% above $11.97 and excludes dividends. (Author analysis dated September 9, 2026.)

| Scenario | Key assumptions | Value/share | Return vs. $11.97 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Bear | $1.15 terminal FFO, 8.5x | $9.78 | -18.3% |
| Base | $1.45 terminal FFO, 10.0x | $14.50 | +21.1% |
| Bull | $1.75 terminal FFO, 11.0x | $19.25 | +60.8% |
| Probability-weighted | 25% / 50% / 25% | $14.51 | +21.2% |

The weighted value is not an expected five-year sale price discounted to today; it is a scenario fair-value convention based on terminal earning power. Consequently, it should be used as an underwriting map, not a mathematically complete present-value model. The absence of discounting is partly offset by excluding cumulative dividends, but those are not equivalent adjustments.

### Reverse Valuation

At today’s price, a 10 times normalized multiple requires about $1.20 of sustainable FFO per share, 1.6% below FY2026 consensus of $1.22. At 9 times, required FFO rises to $1.33; at 11 times, it falls to $1.09. (StockAnalysis forecast page retrieved September 9, 2026; author calculations.) The market is therefore underwriting broadly flat normalized earning power and a multiple around the middle of the scenario range.

That looks mildly too harsh if Q2’s 7% RevPAR growth and raised guidance indicate a multiyear improvement, but roughly fair if they mark favorable late-cycle conditions. The valuation offers a margin of safety against stagnation, not against a severe lodging recession. That distinction supports Watch rather than Own.

## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The risk that breaks the thesis is a lodging downturn while capital needs and debt service remain fixed. A room night is perishable, so occupancy loss immediately removes high-contribution revenue. Wage, property tax, insurance, utility and maintenance costs do not fall in lockstep. EBITDA can therefore decline faster than revenue, driving leverage above today’s 3.64 times and reducing both FFO and the multiple. (StockAnalysis ratios page, retrieved September 9, 2026.)

Balance-sheet risk is meaningful but not currently acute. $1.196 billion debt is supported by $299.53 million TTM EBITDA and $105.98 million cash, while interest coverage is 3.01 times. (StockAnalysis statistics, balance-sheet and ratios pages, retrieved September 9, 2026.) Refinancing at higher rates, secured-asset constraints or clustered maturities could worsen that snapshot; the debt schedule must be checked in filings.

Capital-intensity risk is easy to miss. The $80.55 million trailing real-estate capex/acquisition line may include value-creating projects, but it also demonstrates that FFO is not freely distributable cash. (StockAnalysis cash-flow page, retrieved September 9, 2026.) If renovations merely defend rate and brand standards, AFFO and dividend capacity are structurally below headline FFO.

Other risks include travel disruption, recession, corporate travel substitution, new local supply, online travel agency commissions, labor scarcity, union contracts, extreme weather, wildfire or hurricane exposure, property insurance availability, cyber incidents at managers or brands, franchise termination, renovation disruption, environmental liabilities and adverse tax-law changes. REIT qualification also constrains taxable-income distribution and organizational choices. These are industry mechanisms rather than quantified claims in the snapshot, and their relevance varies by individual property.

Valuation itself is a risk after a 39.5% 52-week gain. (StockAnalysis overview, retrieved September 9, 2026.) A good operating result can still produce a poor stock return if expectations and multiples have moved first. Finally, adjusted measures create presentation risk: reconciliation exclusions, property-sale gains, impairments and recurring versus growth capex classification should be monitored rather than accepted at face value.

## 16. What Would Change My Mind

I would become more bullish if the company demonstrates that Q2’s operating strength survives difficult comparisons and reaches cash available to owners. Specifically, several quarters of positive comparable RevPAR, stable or improving hotel EBITDA margin, FY2026 FFO at or above raised guidance, FCF-per-share growth near the projected 18%, and net leverage below 3.5 times would justify greater confidence or a higher multiple. (PRNewswire, July 30, 2026; leverage threshold is author criterion.)

I would become more bearish if RevPAR turns negative because of occupancy rather than planned renovation disruption, hotel EBITDA margin compresses, adjusted FFO falls below $1.15 on a normalized basis, or net debt/EBITDA rises above 4.5 times without a clearly temporary cause. A dividend increase funded by debt, a large peak-cycle acquisition, repeated “one-time” adjustments, or a widening FFO-to-AFFO gap would also weaken the thesis.

The thesis would be invalidated if the portfolio cannot fund recurring maintenance, debt service and the present dividend through a normal demand downturn without issuing discounted equity or selling core assets under pressure. Conversely, evidence that $1.45 or more of per-share FFO is achievable while leverage declines would move the stock from a cyclical Watch toward a compounder worthy of ownership.

## 17. Monitoring Checklist

Monitoring should follow the causal chain: demand and rate create RevPAR; RevPAR converts through property expenses to hotel EBITDA; corporate expense and interest produce FFO; recurring capex converts FFO to AFFO; dividends, acquisitions and debt reduction allocate the residual. Any dashboard that stops at revenue misses the key issue.

| Metric / event | Bullish signal | Bearish signal | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comparable RevPAR | Positive growth led by rate and healthy occupancy | Negative growth or discounting to hold occupancy | Quarterly release |
| Group pace / transient demand | Broad market and segment strength | Cancellations, shortening windows, weak citywide calendar | Release / call |
| Hotel EBITDA margin | Stable or expanding | Costs outrun RevPAR | Supplemental / filing |
| Adjusted FFO/share | Above guidance with clean reconciliation | Misses or adjustment proliferation | Release / filing |
| AFFO and recurring capex | Cash growth tracks FFO | Persistent widening gap | Cash flow / supplemental |
| Net debt/EBITDA | Below 3.5x | Above 4.5x | Balance sheet / ratios |
| Interest coverage | Improving from 3.01x | Sustained fall below 2.5x | Ratios / filing |
| Dividend coverage | Payout remains conservative | Distribution funded by debt/assets | Release / cash flow |
| Share count and transactions | Buybacks below NAV; accretive recycling | Dilution or expensive acquisitions | Filing / release |

The 3.01 times starting coverage is from StockAnalysis ratios retrieved September 9, 2026; all thresholds are author discipline points, not company guidance. ## 18. Conclusion

DiamondRock is a **Watch** because a credible operating improvement and 9.81 times FY2026 consensus P/FFO create base-case upside, but a 39.5% one-year rally, cyclical hotel cash flows and 3.64 times net debt/EBITDA limit the margin for error. Q2’s $0.44 adjusted FFO, $316.6 million revenue, 7% RevPAR growth and raised guidance are strong evidence; the unresolved question is how much becomes AFFO after recurring renovation capital. (PRNewswire, July 30, 2026; StockAnalysis overview, forecast and ratios pages, retrieved September 9, 2026.)

The stock works if normalized FFO per share compounds toward $1.45 or better while leverage stays controlled, and fails if $1.22 is near-cycle peak earning power or capital needs absorb the apparent discount. The scenario range is $9.78 to $19.25, with $14.51 probability-weighted fair value and 21.2% upside from $11.97. (Author scenario analysis dated September 9, 2026.) I would revisit the view after FY2026 results, a material price pullback, or evidence that AFFO growth and debt reduction validate the Q2 momentum.

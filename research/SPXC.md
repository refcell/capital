## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

SPX Technologies looks like a premium industrial compounder that suddenly trades at a less-premium price. The company has transformed from the old SPX conglomerate into a focused supplier of engineered HVAC and detection-and-measurement products. Revenue has risen from $1.22 billion in 2021 to $2.27 billion in 2025, while gross margin expanded from 35.6% to 40.6%. The market rewarded that combination of organic growth, margin improvement, and acquisition execution until the stock fell from a 52-week high of $251.08 to $179.70. At the current price, the forward P/E is 19.64x against consensus three-year revenue growth of 13.0% and EPS growth of 16.0%. Sources: StockAnalysis financials and statistics, checked September 14-17, 2026.

The decline creates an interesting mismatch. Operating evidence remains strong: second-quarter revenue grew 22.9%, including 16.9% organic growth, and adjusted EBITDA grew 19.8%. Management raised 2026 revenue guidance to $2.705-$2.765 billion and adjusted EPS guidance to $8.20-$8.60. Consensus sits near the midpoint at $2.74 billion and $8.48. Yet the share price is below its 50-day and 200-day moving averages of $206.16 and $213.59 and only modestly above the 52-week low of $176.21. Sources: SPX Q2 2026 release, July 30, 2026; StockAnalysis forecast and statistics, September 16-17, 2026.

The real debate is not whether SPX has good businesses. It is whether the current growth is durable enough, and cash-generative enough, to justify continued acquisition activity without creating a leverage or integration problem. The company bought Neptronic in July and FIS Water in September. FIS Water alone cost approximately $410 million for a business expected to generate about $105 million of 2026 revenue, a headline price-to-sales multiple near 3.9x before tax attributes or synergies. That is a full price. It can create value if SPX accelerates growth through its cooling channels and earns attractive incremental margins; it can destroy value if data-center cooling demand normalizes, integration costs rise, or management continues buying at peak-cycle multiples.

### The Core Debate

The bull answer is that SPX is building a differentiated cooling platform at exactly the moment data-center thermal management becomes a long-duration infrastructure bottleneck. Existing cooling-tower brands, expanded Olympus Max capacity, Neptronic controls, and FIS Water filtration and flow-control products can be sold into overlapping channels. The company estimates annual data-center revenue capacity of approximately $1.1 billion once expanded production is fully operational. In that view, acquisition spending is not random conglomerate behavior; it is deliberate adjacency expansion around a specification-driven platform with shared customers, engineers, OEMs, and distributors. Source: SPX Q2 and FIS Water releases, July 30 and September 15, 2026.

The bear answer is that the market is extrapolating an unusually strong demand cycle. HVAC segment revenue grew 27.6% in Q2, but 8.5 percentage points came from acquisitions. Segment margin declined 260 basis points to 22.8% because of capacity start-up inefficiencies, tariff pressure, inflation, and less favorable project mix. Capital expenditures rose to $118.5 million TTM from $38.0 million in 2024, while receivables and inventory absorbed cash. If data-center orders slow after capacity has been built and acquired assets have been paid for, revenue growth and margins can disappoint simultaneously.

## 2. Company Story

SPX traces its roots to 1912 and spent much of its history as a broad industrial conglomerate. The modern company is the result of portfolio simplification, divestitures, and concentration around two attractive niches: HVAC and Detection & Measurement. The name changed from SPX Corporation to SPX Technologies in August 2022, signaling that the remaining portfolio was intended to be valued as a focused engineered-products platform rather than as a collection of unrelated industrial assets. Source: StockAnalysis company profile, updated September 15, 2026.

That strategic narrowing matters because the old conglomerate discount depended on cyclicality, complexity, and inconsistent capital allocation. The current model is easier to understand. HVAC contains cooling, air movement, heating, and ventilation brands. Detection & Measurement contains underground locating and rehabilitation equipment, communications systems, transportation fare systems, and aids to navigation. Both segments sell products for applications where reliability, certification, specification history, and channel relationships matter more than lowest initial cost.

The company story since 2021 has been one of both organic improvement and M&A. Revenue rose 85.7% from 2021 through 2025, while operating income increased from $119.6 million to $345.5 million. The operating margin improved from 9.8% to 15.3%. Acquisitions contributed to that progress, but the portfolio also generated visible organic acceleration in 2026. Q2 consolidated organic growth was 16.9%, including 18.9% in HVAC and 12.8% in D&M. Sources: StockAnalysis financials through June 27, 2026; SPX Q2 release, July 30, 2026.

The newest chapter is platform expansion around cooling. Neptronic added humidification, electric heating, valves, and controls. FIS Water adds filtration, separators, flow-control products, and water-quality capabilities used in commercial and industrial cooling. SPX explicitly described overlap across customers, engineering specifications, sales channels, and OEM relationships. This creates a credible adjacency thesis: instead of selling only the cooling tower, SPX can supply more of the surrounding thermal-management system.

## 3. Business Model

SPX sells engineered equipment rather than commodity building products. The economic distinction is important. A cooling tower, underground cable locator, runway light, or communications system is purchased because failure carries operational, safety, or regulatory consequences. Customers still care about cost, but they also care about application engineering, specification approval, installed reliability, service support, and compatibility with existing systems. Those requirements allow established brands to defend gross margins that reached 40.3% TTM.

HVAC generated $1.693 billion of TTM revenue, about 68% of the company, while D&M generated $782.8 million, about 32%. HVAC includes package and process cooling, air movement, hydronic heating, electrical heating, and ventilation. D&M includes underground locating and inspection, robotic systems, fare collection, communications technologies, and marine and aviation navigation aids. Sources: StockAnalysis financial overview through June 27, 2026.

Revenue is earned through a mix of direct sales, independent representatives, distributors, retailers, and project channels. That mix creates different working-capital profiles. Standard products can move through distribution, while engineered projects require bidding, production scheduling, progress management, and customer acceptance. Current unearned revenue of $128.2 million and backlog of $934.8 million at year-end 2025 provide partial visibility, but they do not eliminate project timing or cancellation risk. Source: StockAnalysis balance sheet, updated July 31, 2026.

The engine is: build leading brands in narrow applications; win specifications through engineering and reliability; distribute through established channels; add adjacent products to the same customer relationship; use operating leverage to expand margins; and redeploy cash into capacity and acquisitions. The weak link is capital allocation. The operating businesses can be excellent while shareholder returns disappoint if SPX pays too much for acquired growth or repeatedly issues shares.

Unit economics are not disclosed as customer acquisition cost or retention because this is not a subscription business. The best substitutes are gross margin, segment margin, organic growth, backlog conversion, working-capital efficiency, and return on invested capital. TTM gross margin was 40.29%, operating margin 15.82%, FCF margin 12.25%, and ROIC 10.89%. The estimated WACC is roughly 10.9%. That narrow current spread means future value creation depends on acquired businesses earning above their purchase-price cost of capital, not merely adding revenue. Sources: StockAnalysis financials and statistics, September 2026; workbook WACC calculation using a 4.943% risk-free rate.

## 4. Products, Segments, And Moat

HVAC is the growth engine. Its cooling portfolio serves commercial, industrial, institutional, and data-center applications. Brands such as Marley, Recold, SGS, Cincinnati Fan, TAMCO, and others cover cooling and air movement. Heating and ventilation brands include Weil-McLain, Patterson-Kelley, Berko, Qmark, INDEECO, Heatrex, Thermolec, and related products. The portfolio allows SPX to serve both highly engineered projects and recurring replacement demand. Source: StockAnalysis company profile, September 15, 2026.

D&M is smaller but currently offers superior segment margins. It includes Radiodetection, Pearpoint, Schonstedt, CUES, ULC Robotics, TCI, ECS, KTS, Genfare, Flash Technology, Sealite, and related brands. These businesses address underground utility safety, pipeline inspection, communications intelligence, transit fare collection, and navigation lighting. Many end markets are regulated or mission-critical, helping support pricing and aftermarket demand.

SPX's moat is a portfolio moat rather than a single patent or network effect. First, long-lived brands and installed bases create familiarity among engineers, contractors, distributors, and operators. Second, specification-driven products become embedded in project designs before purchase orders are issued. Third, channel density means an acquired product can grow faster inside SPX than as a standalone company. Fourth, application expertise and certification create barriers in navigation, communications, and utility detection.

The moat is moderate, not absolute. Large diversified industrial companies, private-equity-backed niche manufacturers, and regional specialists can compete in each category. Data-center customers have bargaining power and can dual-source. Cooling technologies can change as rack densities, liquid cooling, water constraints, and facility architecture evolve. SPX must keep the portfolio technically relevant rather than relying only on historical brands.

FIS Water provides a useful test of the moat. The acquisition thesis is that LAKOS, Puroflux, Superior, VAF, and VSI can be sold through SPX's existing cooling channels and specified alongside cooling towers and controls. If cross-selling lifts FIS Water above its approximately $105 million 2026 revenue base without a proportionate increase in selling expense, the channel moat is real and measurable. If growth merely tracks the market, SPX likely paid a premium for revenue it could not accelerate.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Earnings quality is **good at the operating and cash-flow level, but increasingly adjusted and acquisition-dependent**. TTM OCF of $421.9 million exceeded net income of $278.9 million by 51%. FCF of $303.4 million also exceeded net income after $118.5 million of capital expenditures. Stock-based compensation was only $17.1 million, about 4% of OCF, so cash conversion is not being manufactured primarily through large equity compensation add-backs. Source: StockAnalysis cash flow, through June 27, 2026.

The gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings is still material. Q2 GAAP EPS from continuing operations was $1.56 versus adjusted EPS of $2.02, a 29% uplift. Adjustments included acquisition and integration costs, amortization of acquired intangible assets, acquisition-related incentive compensation, and pension charges. Intangible amortization alone was $27.2 million in Q2 and $51.4 million year to date. Management's adjustments are understandable for comparing acquired operations, but amortization is economically connected to repeated acquisition spending. Investors should not assume every adjustment is irrelevant.

The proper read is neither “GAAP is the truth” nor “adjusted is the truth.” GAAP captures the purchase-accounting cost of the strategy but can understate current operating run rate; adjusted earnings show operating performance but can overstate owner earnings if acquisitions are recurring rather than exceptional. Because SPX repeatedly buys businesses, acquisition costs and amortization are part of the business model even when they are not part of segment operations.

Cash flow provides the tie-breaker. TTM FCF of $303.4 million compares with adjusted 2026 net income implied by $8.48 consensus EPS and roughly 50.5 million diluted shares, or approximately $428 million. That indicates adjusted earnings are not converting dollar-for-dollar into current FCF, partly because capex and working capital are elevated. The model therefore uses forward P/E as the primary valuation measure but retains FCF valuation as a cross-check rather than accepting adjusted EPS uncritically.

The 2022 cash-flow loss also matters. OCF was negative $136.8 million despite positive operating income because working capital and other operating items moved sharply against the company. That episode shows the business can have cash-flow volatility during portfolio transitions. Current conversion is much stronger, but a combined period of rapid organic growth, capacity investment, and acquisitions can recreate temporary cash absorption.

StockAnalysis also shows FY2027 revenue of approximately $3.05 billion and adjusted EPS of $9.72. Sources: StockAnalysis forecast, September 16, 2026; SPX Q2 release, July 30, 2026.

Those numbers are explicitly non-GAAP adjusted estimates. FY2026 consensus EPS of $8.48 is 53% above TTM GAAP EPS of $5.55. The difference is not necessarily an error; it reflects growth, later-period acquisitions, and exclusions. But the size of the gap means the market's 19.64x forward P/E should not be compared mechanically with a GAAP trailing P/E of 32.36x.

The base forecast assumes growth slows after the 2026 step-up. Revenue reaches $3.05 billion in 2027, consistent with the visible consensus, then compounds at approximately 8-11% as data-center cooling, pricing, and acquired revenue offset normalization. Operating margin holds in the mid-to-high teens on a GAAP basis, with adjusted EBITDA margin around the low-to-mid 20s. The base does not assume the Q2 16.9% organic growth rate persists indefinitely.

The bear case is not a collapse. It assumes SPX delivers only the low end of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance, integration and capacity costs remain elevated, and the market assigns 19x earnings. That produces $155.80. The base uses $9.72 of FY2027 consensus EPS and 24x, producing $233.28. The bull uses $11.20 of earnings and 27x, producing $302.40. The reverse valuation is useful. At $179.70 and FY2027 consensus EPS of $9.72, the stock trades at 18.5x one-year-forward earnings. ## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Gene Lowe is President and CEO, Mark Carano is CFO, Sean McClenaghan leads HVAC, and John Swann leads D&M. SPX announced that Swann plans to retire in January 2027, creating a near-term leadership transition in the segment currently producing the strongest margin expansion. The board added Brian Deck as an independent director in July 2026. Sources: StockAnalysis company profile and SPX company releases, June-July 2026.

Management deserves credit for portfolio focus and operating improvement. Revenue, gross margin, operating income, and backlog all improved materially from 2021 through 2025. The Q2 raise also demonstrates that management entered the second half with good visibility. These achievements suggest SPX has a repeatable operating system rather than merely benefiting from multiple expansion.

Capital allocation is the harder judgment. SPX spent $436.9 million on cash acquisitions TTM and another $410 million for FIS Water after the TTM cutoff. It also invested $118.5 million in capex. Those uses far exceeded TTM FCF of $303.4 million, requiring debt and equity capital. In 2025, common-stock issuance was $575 million and shares outstanding increased 6.2% year over year. Source: StockAnalysis cash flow and statistics, through September 2026.

Issuing equity is not inherently bad if acquired returns exceed dilution. The test is per-share value, not empire size. Management should disclose purchase multiples, expected synergies, integration costs, and a leverage target. A commitment to reduce net leverage before pursuing another large acquisition would improve confidence that the roll-up remains disciplined.

FIS Water illustrates both strategic strength and governance risk. The fit is clear, but the $410 million price equals roughly four times the target's expected 2026 revenue before considering tax attributes. Without EBITDA disclosure, investors cannot calculate the acquisition multiple or immediate return on capital. Management's October update should therefore be judged on transparency as much as on guidance.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

Bulls focus on data-center cooling. Artificial-intelligence infrastructure requires more power and more thermal management, and cooling systems must become more reliable as rack density rises. SPX's expanded capacity, established Marley franchise, controls, filtration, and water-management products create an increasingly complete offering. The bullish market view also values the company as a compounder: organic growth, pricing, margin improvement, and acquisitions reinforce one another.

Bears focus on cycle and price. Data-center capital spending is strong today, but customers can shift designs, delay projects, or alter cooling architectures. SPX has invested in capacity and paid premium prices for adjacent businesses during that strength. If the cycle slows, operating leverage works in reverse. The stock's 3.37% FCF yield provides limited current compensation for that risk.

Another bear concern is adjusted accounting. The market quotes 19.64x forward earnings, but those earnings exclude acquisition amortization and integration costs even though acquisitions are continuous. If investors capitalize recurring acquisition spending, owner earnings are below adjusted EPS. That does not make the stock uninvestable; it means comparing SPX with software-like compounders on headline P/E alone is too generous.

The variant perception in this report is more balanced. The market's recent decline may be overreacting to near-term industrial or data-center fears because SPX's organic growth remains excellent and the balance sheet was manageable before the latest deals. At the same time, analyst targets may be under-discounting financing uncertainty. The opportunity is not “growth is hidden.” Growth is obvious. The opportunity is that the price reset creates upside if the acquisition bridge is clean and cash conversion recovers after capacity investment.

The single most mispriced variable may be D&M. Most investor attention goes to data centers, but D&M grew 12.8% organically in Q2 and expanded segment margin 610 basis points to 28.9%. If that margin is durable, D&M supplies high-quality earnings that diversify cooling-cycle risk. If it was mostly project mix and optimization timing, consolidated estimates may be too optimistic.

## 10. Bull Case

The bull case begins with demand, not multiple expansion. Data-center construction continues at a high rate, SPX fills its approximately $1.1 billion of annual data-center revenue capacity, and customers adopt more water-management and control content per project. FIS Water and Neptronic grow faster inside SPX than they did independently because the same engineers, OEMs, and channel partners specify more products from one platform.

Organic HVAC growth normalizes from 18.9% but remains in the low-to-mid teens. Capacity start-up costs fade, tariff pricing catches up with input costs, and HVAC segment margin returns toward or above 25%. D&M maintains double-digit growth and a high-20s margin because communications, navigation, and infrastructure inspection demand remains firm.

Adjusted EPS reaches $11.20, above the visible $9.72 FY2027 consensus but plausible if FIS Water contributes, interest expense is contained, and operating leverage works. A 27x multiple yields $302.40, near the high analyst target of $310. This case requires the company to demonstrate that acquisitions add per-share earnings and FCF rather than merely revenue.

The causal chain is: capacity converts backlog into revenue; broader cooling content raises revenue per project; acquired brands gain distribution; fixed costs scale; working capital normalizes; FCF catches adjusted earnings; debt falls; and the market restores a premium multiple. Break any two links and the bull case weakens materially.

## 11. Bear Case

The bear case assumes the company delivers, but not enough to support a premium. FY2026 adjusted EPS lands at the $8.20 low end of guidance. Data-center orders remain positive but slow, HVAC start-up inefficiencies persist, and tariff or material costs prevent margin recovery. D&M's favorable Q2 mix reverses, pulling its margin back from 28.9%.

FIS Water and Neptronic add revenue but require more integration spending and debt than expected. Interest expense rises, working capital remains a use of cash, and management pauses deleveraging to pursue another target. Investors stop treating acquisition expenses as exceptional and assign only 19x adjusted earnings. The resulting target is $155.80, 13.3% below the current price.

This is a realistic bear rather than a disaster case. SPX remains profitable, generates cash, and owns valuable brands. The downside comes from multiple compression and lower-quality growth, not insolvency. A true recession or data-center capex break could produce a lower target, but assigning that outcome a high probability would contradict the current order and guidance evidence.

The warning sequence would be visible before the full bear case arrives: organic HVAC growth falls below mid-single digits, backlog declines, HVAC margin stays below 23%, receivables and inventory continue growing faster than revenue, pro forma net leverage exceeds management's prior comfort range, and guidance relies more heavily on adjusted add-backs.

## 12. Base Case

The base case assumes SPX remains a good company without extrapolating Q2's strongest metrics. FY2027 revenue reaches approximately $3.05 billion and adjusted EPS reaches $9.72, matching visible consensus. Organic growth slows as comparisons become harder, but acquisitions and data-center capacity keep total growth above the broader industrial market.

HVAC margin improves from Q2's 22.8% as start-up costs fade, though it does not immediately exceed the 25.4% prior-year comparison. D&M gives back part of its 610-basis-point margin surge but remains structurally high margin. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin stays in the low-to-mid 20s.

The balance sheet absorbs Neptronic and FIS Water without a permanent leverage problem. Management uses FCF to reduce debt during 2027, limits additional large M&A, and avoids another major equity issuance. Cash conversion recovers as capacity capex and inventory builds moderate.

At 24x adjusted EPS, fair value is $233.28. That multiple is above the current 18.5x on FY2027 consensus because the base assumes the missing capital-structure information is resolved favorably. It is still below a full bull-case premium and produces a value below the analyst average target. This is the most likely outcome, but it should be updated immediately after Q3 disclosure.

## 13. Valuation

SPX is cheaper than it was, but “cheap” requires context. At $179.70, trailing GAAP P/E is 32.36x, forward adjusted P/E is 19.64x, price-to-sales is 3.64x, P/FCF is 29.67x, and EV/EBITDA is 18.11x. Those are not distressed multiples. The investment works because growth and margins can compound, not because liquidation value or current FCF yield protects the downside. Source: StockAnalysis statistics, September 17, 2026.

Forward P/E is the primary framework because recent acquisitions make reported enterprise value stale and because consensus explicitly provides adjusted EPS anchors. It is imperfect: adjusted earnings exclude costs related to a recurring acquisition strategy. The report therefore triangulates with FCF and EV/EBITDA.

The reverse valuation asks what $179.70 requires. On $9.72 of FY2027 adjusted EPS, the price represents 18.5x. With no multiple expansion, a 10% required return needs roughly 10% EPS growth. Consensus expects 14.6% growth from 2026 to 2027 and 16.0% over three years. The market therefore discounts a meaningful slowdown from consensus, which creates upside if estimates hold.

The $238.52 weighted value is not a precise intrinsic value. It is a decision framework. Below roughly $160, the bear case is largely priced in unless operating evidence deteriorates. Around $230-$240, the stock discounts a clean base outcome. Above $300, investors would again be paying for sustained premium execution.

## 14. Catalysts

The primary catalyst is the estimated October 29, 2026 earnings release. It should provide updated guidance including FIS Water, a pro forma debt bridge, expected interest expense, integration costs, and management's capital-allocation priorities. The most important outcome is not simply another revenue raise; it is evidence that per-share earnings and cash flow improve after financing.

Capacity ramp is the second catalyst. Management said initial assembly of Olympus Max was underway in Madison, Alabama and raised estimated annual data-center revenue capacity to approximately $1.1 billion at full production. Order conversion and manufacturing efficiency should show whether capacity investment translates into revenue without sustained margin dilution. Source: SPX Q2 release, July 30, 2026.

FIS Water cross-selling is a medium-term catalyst. Evidence would include revenue growth above the target's approximately $105 million 2026 base, new bundled specifications, or margin accretion. Management should quantify rather than merely describe synergies.

D&M leadership transition is a quieter catalyst. John Swann's planned January 2027 retirement creates succession risk in the company's highest-margin segment. A credible handoff and stable D&M performance would reduce that concern; disruption or changed strategy would increase it.

## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The biggest risk is capital allocation at a cyclical high. SPX is buying businesses after a strong run in HVAC and data-center demand, when private-market prices may already discount growth. FIS Water's headline purchase price is approximately 3.9x expected 2026 revenue. Without EBITDA, synergy, or financing details, investors cannot yet establish whether the acquisition clears SPX's roughly 10.9% cost of capital.

Data-center concentration is the second risk. The company does not disclose enough customer-level detail to know how dependent cooling growth is on a small number of hyperscale projects. Data-center construction can be durable over years while still producing volatile quarterly orders and significant customer bargaining power.

Margin divergence is the third risk. Q2 consolidated results were strong, but HVAC margin fell 260 basis points while D&M margin rose 610 basis points. If D&M's improvement is temporary and HVAC pressure persists, consolidated estimates can disappoint even with revenue growth.

Balance-sheet opacity is the immediate red flag. June debt is no longer the right number. The July $340 million borrowing and September $410 million cash purchase occurred after the reported balance sheet. The company may have cash, revolver capacity, tax attributes, and acquired cash that reduce the simple sum, but investors should not fill the gap with assumptions.

Other risks include tariffs and material inflation, fixed-price project execution, government funding exposure in communications and navigation, goodwill impairment, technological change in cooling, channel conflict after acquisitions, further share issuance, and working-capital absorption. None is individually fatal; their interaction matters.

## 16. What Would Change My Mind

The view becomes more bullish if Q3 disclosure shows pro forma net leverage remains conservative, FIS Water is accretive to 2027 EPS and FCF after interest, HVAC organic growth remains above 10%, and segment margin begins recovering from 22.8%. A clear commitment to deleverage before another large acquisition would also improve the governance case.

The view becomes more bearish if pro forma debt materially exceeds the simple disclosed borrowing bridge, if adjusted EPS rises while FCF falls, or if management avoids disclosing purchase economics. Organic HVAC growth below 5% combined with declining backlog would suggest the demand cycle is normalizing faster than expected.

A second major equity issuance would require reassessing per-share compounding. The 2025 issuance helped fund growth, but recurring dilution would weaken the argument that SPX is a self-funded compounder. Likewise, another large acquisition before Neptronic and FIS Water are integrated would shift the stance toward Pass.

## 18. Conclusion

SPX Technologies is a good industrial business at a newly interesting price, not a broken stock at a bargain-bin valuation. The operating record is strong: multi-year revenue growth, substantial gross-margin improvement, high organic growth, positive FCF, and valuable niche brands. The current $179.70 price and 19.6x forward adjusted P/E create a plausible path to $233-$239 fair value if FY2027 consensus holds.

The main reason to care is the cooling platform. Data-center demand, broader thermal-management content, and shared engineering channels can support durable growth. The main risk is that management is spending aggressively before the durability of that cycle is proven. The September FIS Water deal increases strategic breadth and simultaneously removes confidence in the reported capital structure until Q3.

The stance is Watch with favorable risk-reward. Probability-weighted fair value is $238.52, with a bear case of $155.80, base case of $233.28, and bull case of $302.40. The next trigger is the October 29 update. A transparent pro forma leverage bridge and evidence of accretive cash economics would support ownership; an opaque financing picture or continued HVAC margin pressure would argue for patience.

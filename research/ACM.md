## 1. Thesis In One Page

## 2. Company Story

### Origin And Evolution

AECOM traces its roots to Frederick M. Harris, who founded the engineering firm Harris & Associates in Southern California in 1954. The company grew through a series of acquisitions and reorganizations, eventually forming AECOM Technology Corporation in 1996 when Harris & Associates merged with Caltrans Consulting Corporation and other entities. The name AECOM derives from "American Engineering & Consulting Company" (AEC) combined with "COM" for consulting operations. The company was rebranded simply as AECOM in January 2015.

AECOM is one of the largest professional services firms in the infrastructure space globally, employing approximately 51,000 people across 100+ countries. The company operates in three primary segments: Americas (domestic U.S. operations), International (global operations outside the U.S.), and AECOM Capital (investment and development of real estate projects). The firm's client base is roughly 60% public sector — government agencies, transit authorities, defense departments, and water authorities — and 40% private sector, including commercial real estate developers, energy companies, and healthcare organizations.

AECOM's business spans the entire infrastructure lifecycle: advisory and planning, architectural and engineering design, construction management, and program management. The company doesn't typically self-perform construction — it manages the process, designs the facilities, and oversees contractors. This model generates stable fee income with lower capital intensity than self-performing contractors, but it also means margins are sensitive to project scope, client budget cycles, and competitive bidding pressure.

### Why The Company Matters

AECOM is the second-largest engineering and construction services firm in the United States after Jacobs Solutions (which trades at $142.86, forward P/E ~13-14x). In a sector where government infrastructure spending is the primary growth driver — the U.S. Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act of 2021 allocated $1.2 trillion for roads, bridges, broadband, water, and energy — AECOM sits at the center of the infrastructure renaissance. The company is a top-tier bidder for Department of Transportation, Department of Defense, and Army Corps of Engineers contracts.

The company's scale matters defensively too. With $15.4B TTM revenue and 51,000 employees, AECOM is diversified enough that no single project, client, or government program can materially move the needle on a quarterly basis. The risk is at the aggregate level — government budget constraints, post-pandemic spending deceleration, and margin pressure from competitive bidding in a crowded sector.

## 3. Business Model

### Revenue Model

AECOM generates revenue through professional fee structures — time-and-materials contracts, fixed-price contracts, and cost-plus arrangements. The revenue cycle is project-driven: a government agency issues a request for proposal (RFP), AECOM bids, wins the contract, deploys engineers and construction managers, and bills over the course of months or years. This creates inherent backlog visibility but also makes revenue lumpy based on when projects are initiated and completed.

The revenue mix is approximately 60% public sector and 40% private sector. The public sector portion — primarily U.S. federal and state government — provides stability through multi-year contracts and guaranteed payment (non-discretionary programs continue regardless of political cycles). The private sector provides margin optionality and growth sensitivity to commercial real estate, energy, and healthcare construction cycles.

### The Economic Engine

AECOM's economic engine is a high-fixed-cost, scale-dependent professional services operation. The company employs 51,000 professionals across 100+ countries. Gross margin at TTM of 5.68% ($874M on $15.4B) appears thin, but this is characteristic of the engineering/construction services sector where direct labor and subcontractor costs consume the majority of revenue. Operating margin at 4.64% ($715M operating income) converts 3% of revenue to pure operating profit — sustainable but not spectacular.

The key economic question is whether AECOM is operating at the correct scale for its revenue base. TTM operating income of $715M is down from $1.06B in FY25 (6.56% margin), representing a 32% decline in operating earnings year-over-year. The decline is disproportionate to the modest revenue decline ($15.4B from $16.1B), suggesting that the Q2 FY26 earnings event was more than a revenue timing issue — it was a genuine margin hit, either from write-downs, provisioning, or cost overruns that exceeded revenue.

### Unit Economics

Revenue per employee at TTM: $15.4B / 51,000 = $302,000 per employee. This is in line with professional services peers — Jacobs Solutions reports similar per-employee revenue. Operating income per employee: $715M / 51,000 = $14,016, down from $20,824 in FY25. The decline confirms that the earnings disruption is a per-unit margin issue, not just a volume issue — even adjusting for the modest revenue decline, each employee is generating less operating income.

The Americas segment dominates revenue and profitability — it includes the domestic U.S. government contract business, commercial real estate, healthcare, and energy projects. The International segment covers operations in Europe, the Middle East, Africa, and Asia-Pacific, carrying FX exposure from multi-currency contracts. AECOM Capital is the investment arm — the company invests directly in real estate development projects alongside professional services, creating a combined "services + capital" model.

### Competitive Position

AECOM competes in the large-scale engineering and construction services market. The primary competitors, in rough order of market cap, are:

- **Jacobs Solutions (JKS)**: $142.86/share, larger revenue base, similarly government-focused. Trades at slightly higher multiple (forward P/E ~13-14x vs. ACM's 9.6x).
- **Tetra Tech (TTEK)**: $35.40/share, smaller and more water/environment focused.
- **Fluor Corporation (FLR)**: $53.42/share, more construction/self-performing orientation, cyclical revenue.
- **KBR**: $37.62, defense and industrial focus.
- **Quanta Services (PWR)**: $696/share, energy infrastructure, higher-growth profile commanding elevated multiple.
- **MasTec (MTZ)**: $280.13, utility and telecom construction.
- **EMCOR Group (EME)**: $824.53, commercial construction — highest-trading peer.

AECOM sits in the middle tier — too large to be acquired, too small to dominate. The AECOM Capital arm differentiates it from pure services peers by providing real estate co-investment returns, but it also adds balance sheet risk.

### Moat Assessment

AECOM's moat is moderate, derived from three sources:

1. **Government contract relationships**: Long-standing contracts with federal agencies (DOT, DoD, Army Corps of Engineers) create switching costs. These contracts are typically 3-7 year cycles, and incumbent vendors often have advantage in renewals.
2. **Scale and technical breadth**: 51,000 employees across 100+ countries with expertise spanning transportation, water, facilities, environmental, and energy allows AECOM to bid on mega-projects that smaller firms cannot handle.
3. **AECOM Capital integration**: The combined services-plus-capital model allows AECOM to offer clients a "design-build-invest" package that pure-service competitors cannot match.

None of these moats is impenetrable. The government contracting market is competitive, with firms like Jacobs, Fluor, and KBR all pursuing similar contracts. AECOM's margin profile (4.6% operating) is modest and susceptible to competitive bidding pressure. The real moat is the government contract pipeline itself — visibility into future revenue — which is why backlog tracking is critical.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

AECOM's earnings quality rate: **Noisy — Transitionary**.

The earnings are noisy for three overlapping reasons. First, the Q2 FY26 EPS event was an extreme deviation from expectations — -$0.50 actual vs. $1.46 estimate. Whether this was a discrete provisioning event or the beginning of systemic margin erosion remains unconfirmed. Second, the company has a history of non-operating volatility: in FY23, "Other Income/Expense" was -$79.2M (dragging net income from $718M pretax to $55M net after tax), and in FY22 it was -$459M (the infamous loss year where operating income of $792M was overwhelmed by non-operating charges of $459M).

The non-operating pattern is the CLBT variant identified in the skill documentation: operating income trends are relatively stable ($701M → $792M → $924M → $1,059M → $715M), but the non-operating line creates massive year-to-year swings in net income. The FY23 loss of $55M net income despite $792M operating income proves that AECOM's GAAP net income is a poor proxy for the franchise's operating earnings power. The correct metric is operating income, not GAAP net income.

Third, unusual items totaled -$113M (TTM), -$59M (FY25), -$99M (FY24), and -$188M (FY23). These are recurring items that drag net income consistently below operating income, confirming that AECOM's GAAP earnings are structurally understated. The company's "Normalized Income" line (which adds back unusual items) shows $464M TTM, significantly higher than the $288M GAAP net income.

The cash flow quality is stronger: operating cash flow of $822M (FY25) vs. net income of $562M means OCF exceeds net income by 46%, confirming that the "losses" are primarily non-cash. Free cash flow of $685M (after $137M capex) further validates that the underlying cash generation is robust and the earnings narrative is distorted by accounting mechanics.

Quality rating summary: The operating engine is real and cash-generative. The earnings volatility is driven by non-operating items and episodic provisioning/charge events, not by deterioration in the core business. This supports a "Clean / Transitionary" reading on the operating line, with the earnings whiplash being primarily below the operating line.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

### What Bulls Are Saying

Bulls argue that AECOM is a temporarily impaired quality franchise. The engineering/construction services business is fundamentally sound — $15.4B in revenue, $822M in OCF, and government contracts that provide multi-year visibility. The Q2 miss was likely a discrete provisioning event related to a specific project loss or cost overrun, and the rapid downward revision of FY26 estimates was simply the street incorporating bad news. At 9.6x forward P/E, the stock offers genuine value relative to the 12-15x norms for the sector. The dividend provides downside ballast, and the backlog (while unconfirmed) should support at least partial earnings recovery.

### What Bears Are Saying

Bears argue that the earnings trajectory tells a deeper story. Revenue peaked in FY24-FY25 and is now declining. The infrastructure spending cycle that drove the stock to $135 may be past its peak — the IIJA funds are being deployed, and as the wave of project initiations ends, the backlog will start to contract. The Q2 miss wasn't an isolated event; TTM gross margin compression of 186 basis points is structural, not one-time. If operating margins normalize to 3-4% rather than recovery to 5-6%, the consensus $6.42 EPS for FY27 is wildly optimistic.

### What The Market Is Missing

The market is missing the distinction between the Q2 earnings event and the underlying business trajectory. The street has incorporated the Q2 miss into FY26 estimates, but the FY27 estimates haven't moved deep enough ($6.42 from $6.72, only a $0.30 cut). This suggests the street believes the recovery is baked in — which could be wrong if the gross margin decline is structural. Our variant view: the gross margin compression may be partially structural but not as severe as bears assume. Government contract margins (cost-plus) provide a floor that prevents the kind of margin destruction seen in fixed-price contractors. The 3-5% operating margin floor is more likely than the 6-7% ceiling, making the $5.00-6.00 EPS range (FY27) more likely than the $6.42 consensus. This supports a target of $60-72 rather than $77, but at $61.72 the stock is fairly valued for this middle estimate — hence Watch.

## 10. Bull Case

The bull case requires three things to happen simultaneously: (a) Q2 FY26 was a discrete provisioning event that will not recur, (b) the infrastructure spending backdrop remains supportive through FY27-FY28, and (c) management successfully stabilizes gross margins near 6-7% levels. If all three are true, AECOM returns to FY25-era earnings of $4.21+ EPS within 12 months, and the stock re-rates from 9.6x toward 15x — the level it traded at near the $135 peak. Operating margin recovery to 6.5% on $16.9B revenue = $1.1B operating income, or $5.7M per share on a conservative tax treatment. At 15x forward P/E with upside to $8+ EPS by FY28, the stock could reach $120-130, representing 100%+ upside from current levels.

The bull case is supported by: (a) the backlog visibility that engineering/construction provides — contracts signed in FY23-FY24 revenue up to FY26-FY27, (b) the government contract mix that provides pricing protection through cost-plus arrangements, (c) ISS governance score of 2, indicating a well-managed franchise that is being punished for a single quarter miss, and (d) the dividend yield of 1.91% + capital appreciation potential provides total return optionality. The primary bull catalyst is Q3 FY26 results confirming the Q2 miss was discrete, followed by FY27 guidance reaffirming the consensus recovery trajectory.

## 11. Bear Case

The bear case assumes that the Q2 earnings miss was the first significant crack in a deteriorating margin trend. The infrastructure spending cycle peaked in FY24-FY25, and new contract wins have decelerated. Revenue contraction from $16.1B to $15.4B is the first step; the next step is gross margin compression becoming permanent as competitive bidding intensifies and government budgets tighten. If operating margin compresses to 3.5-4% from current 4.6%, the company generates only $540-620M in operating income on $15.4B revenue. Net income at that level would be $350-400M, or $2.70-3.10 per share — roughly 10% below the current 9.6x forward P/E. The stock would fall from $61.72 to $35-45, a 27-44% decline.

Specific bear mechanisms: (a) government budget constraints — the IIJA money is finite, and as the peak spending year passes, contract awards decelerate. AECOM's Americas segment (60-65% of revenue) is disproportionately exposed to U.S. federal spending. (b) International margin pressure — FX headwinds and competitive bidding in Europe/Middle East/Africa compress international margins. (c) AECOM Capital real estate exposure — if commercial real estate continues to soften, the capital arm could require balance sheet deleveraging or write-downs, further depressing net income. (d) Shareholder rights — at current prices, the $388M buyback executed at 2-3x the current price looks like value destruction, and future buybacks at these levels would compound the problem.

## 12. Base Case

Revenue stabilizes in the $15.5-16.0B range through FY27-FY28, representing modest growth from current TTM levels but below the FY25 peak. Operating margin recovers partially to 5-5.5%, consistent with the historical range since FY22 (5.3-6.6%) but below the FY25 peak of 6.6%. This supports a Watch stance — the stock is fairly priced for a partial recovery scenario, and the primary risk is execution, not valuation.

## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

Wynn Resorts looks like a cyclical casino operator at the bottom of its 52-week range, but the real question is whether investors are being paid enough to bridge a capital-intensive transition. The existing properties are producing healthy cash, Macau has recovered, shares are being repurchased, and the company is approaching the September 2027 opening of Wynn Al Marjan Island in the United Arab Emirates. Against that, leverage remains high, current earnings still depend on volatile gaming hold, and the new resort requires more cash before it contributes.

The stock is interesting because the price has fallen to $91.28, down 28.0% over the prior 52 weeks and at the low end of its $90.87-$134.72 range according to StockAnalysis on September 1, 2026. Yet Q2 2026 revenue increased 6.9% year over year to $1.857 billion, GAAP diluted EPS more than doubled to $1.32, and adjusted diluted EPS increased to $1.24 from $1.09, according to Wynn's August 4 release. Twenty analysts still carried a Strong Buy consensus with an average target of $132.58 as of August 18. Price momentum and operating evidence are therefore telling different stories.

The thesis works if three mechanisms operate together. First, Macau mass-market demand must remain healthy after normalizing unusually favorable Wynn Palace hold. Second, Las Vegas and Boston margins must stabilize after Q2 property EBITDAR declines. Third, Al Marjan must open near September 2027 without another material funding or timing surprise. If those conditions hold, adjusted EPS can rise from the FY2026 consensus of $4.70 toward $6.50 over five years and the stock can support an 18x normalized multiple. The thesis fails if project spending and refinancing absorb the cash generated by the existing estate while property margins weaken.

The current view is Watch / Positive rather than Own. The model's probability-weighted fair value is $121.78, 33.4% above the August 31 close, with a $64.40 bear case, $117 base case, and $168 bull case. The apparent upside is meaningful, but the distribution is wide and the analyst average target is partly dependent on a resort that is not yet open. The next useful evidence is not the already-released Q2 result; it is the next earnings report's normalized property hold, Al Marjan budget update, and capital-allocation bridge.

## 2. Company Story

Wynn Resorts was incorporated in 2002 and listed in October of that year. The economic proposition was never to own the most rooms or gaming positions. It was to concentrate capital in a small number of premium integrated resorts and monetize customers across casino play, rooms, food and beverage, retail, entertainment, and meetings. That concentration created a powerful luxury brand, but it also means each property and jurisdiction matters disproportionately.

The current portfolio has four reporting segments: Wynn Palace and Wynn Macau in Macau; Las Vegas Operations; and Encore Boston Harbor. Casino activity remains the largest revenue stream, producing $4.67 billion of $7.41 billion TTM revenue through June 2026 according to StockAnalysis. Rooms added $1.16 billion, food and beverage $1.05 billion, and entertainment, retail, and other activities $536 million. The non-casino amenities are not merely add-ons: they support premium pricing, lengthen stays, attract group business, and make the resort experience difficult to replicate with a stand-alone casino.

The company now stands at another strategic inflection. Wynn Al Marjan Island, held through a 40%-owned joint venture in Ras Al Khaimah, is intended to open in September 2027. Wynn had contributed $1.06 billion of cash life-to-date by June 30, 2026, according to its Q2 release. The development can extend Wynn's brand into a new region and reduce dependence on Macau and Las Vegas. It also shifts the immediate investment question from post-pandemic recovery to construction discipline, licensing, ramp economics, and funding.

The milestone that matters most is therefore not a historical opening but the next 13 months of execution. Existing properties must carry fixed charges and continue funding the company while Al Marjan consumes cash. A smooth opening would make the portfolio more diversified; delay or cost inflation would increase leverage exactly when investors expect a new earnings contribution.

## 3. Business Model

Wynn monetizes scarce resort capacity rather than pursuing a broad unit footprint. Casino win depends on wagering volume and hold percentage. Room revenue depends on occupancy and average daily rate. Food, beverage, entertainment, retail, and convention revenue expand spend per visitor and help attract non-gaming customers. The model has strong operating leverage because the properties carry large fixed costs; incremental revenue can be highly profitable, but a fall in visitation or unfavorable hold can compress margins quickly.

The TTM mix through June 2026 was approximately 63% casino, 16% rooms, 14% food and beverage, and 7% entertainment, retail, and other, based on StockAnalysis data. Casino remains the profit engine, but luxury rooms and amenities strengthen the brand and provide demand channels that are less directly tied to gaming volume. In Las Vegas, for example, Q2 2026 room ADR rose 4.9% to $575 and REVPAR increased 2.5% to $501 even as occupancy fell to 87.1%, according to Wynn's August 4 release. That is evidence of pricing power, though it did not prevent property EBITDAR from falling 8.3%.

The engine is: invest heavily in distinctive destination assets; use design, service, and amenities to attract premium customers; monetize each stay across multiple revenue streams; convert high incremental margins into operating cash; and recycle cash into maintenance, debt service, selective expansion, dividends, and buybacks. The model creates value when revenue per visitor and property EBITDAR grow faster than the capital base. It destroys value when construction cost, interest expense, or maintenance capital rises faster than normalized cash generation.

Unit economics are property-based rather than customer-acquisition based. Useful measures are table drop, slot handle, win percentage, ADR, occupancy, REVPAR, and property EBITDAR margin. Q2 illustrates why all must be read together. Wynn Palace revenue rose 21.1% and property EBITDAR rose 28.2%, but its 29.7% mass-market table win rate was far above the prior-year 22.3%. Las Vegas revenue grew only 0.7% and property EBITDAR declined 8.3% despite higher room pricing. The quarter was good at group level, but not uniformly repeatable.

## 4. Products, Segments, And Moat

Wynn Palace was the Q2 standout. Revenue increased to $653.4 million from $539.6 million, and adjusted property EBITDAR rose to $201.5 million from $157.2 million. Mass-market table drop grew 3.0%, while mass-market win increased 36.9% because the win rate rose to 29.7%. Slots were also constructive: handle rose 26.7% and win rose 25.3%. The property has strong premium-mass appeal, but investors should normalize hold rather than annualize the reported quarter.

Wynn Macau was more subdued. Q2 revenue increased 2.1% to $351.1 million, while adjusted property EBITDAR fell 1.0% to $95.5 million. Mass-market table drop rose 8.3%, but the win rate slipped to 17.1%. VIP turnover declined 56.4% and VIP win declined 67.0%. The mass business is healthier than the headline EBITDAR suggests, yet the shift away from VIP changes mix and makes operating discipline important.

Las Vegas remains a flagship cash generator and brand showcase. Q2 revenue was $643.2 million, up 0.7%, while adjusted property EBITDAR fell to $215.2 million from $234.8 million. Table drop grew and ADR improved, but lower slot win and entertainment/other revenue weighed on the result. The property still produced the largest segment EBITDAR in the quarter, but the margin decline challenges the idea that every dollar of premium demand creates automatic operating leverage.

Encore Boston Harbor produced $209.3 million of Q2 revenue, down 3.0%, and $56.1 million of adjusted property EBITDAR, down 12.2%. Room revenue improved, but casino revenue fell 5.9%. The property provides geographic diversification and a regional customer base, yet current trends are weaker than the Macau recovery and Las Vegas pricing story.

The moat is experiential and asset-based. Wynn's properties are expensive, regulated, and difficult to reproduce in equivalent locations. The brand is associated with high-end service and design, enabling room and amenity pricing that a commodity casino cannot command. Gaming licenses and concessions create barriers to entry. The limits are equally clear: customers can choose competing luxury resorts, gaming hold is volatile, regulators can change economic terms, and every moat asset requires heavy upkeep.

Al Marjan could extend the moat if Wynn becomes the defining luxury integrated-resort brand in a new market. First-mover advantage and a distinctive property may create scarcity. But this is an unproven market, not an established moat today. The project should be valued as probability-weighted expansion rather than as a fully seasoned resort.

## 5. Financial Snapshot

Wynn's reported recovery from 2021-2022 is substantial. Revenue was $3.76 billion in both 2021 and 2022, rose to $6.53 billion in 2023, reached $7.13 billion in 2024, and was $7.14 billion in 2025. TTM revenue through June 2026 was $7.41 billion. Operating income moved from losses in 2021 and 2022 to $935 million in 2023, $1.16 billion in 2024, $1.14 billion in 2025, and $1.18 billion TTM.

Net income is less smooth. It rose to $730 million in 2023, fell to $501 million in 2024 and $327 million in 2025, then recovered to $449 million TTM. Interest expense, derivatives, foreign-currency movements, property charges, and noncontrolling interests all affect the bridge from property performance to common earnings. This is why property EBITDAR and operating cash flow deserve equal weight with EPS.

The balance sheet is the central constraint. Cash and short-term investments were $2.10 billion at June 30, 2026. Standardized total debt was $12.34 billion, producing $10.24 billion of net debt. Total common equity was negative $169 million, reflecting a retained deficit and $2.78 billion of treasury stock. Negative accounting equity is not by itself proof of insolvency, because the resorts produce cash and treasury stock reduces book equity, but it removes book value as a useful valuation anchor.

Liquidity is better than the current ratio of 0.91 might imply. Wynn's Q2 release said $1.57 billion of cash excluded $527 million of short-term investments at Wynn Macau, and identified $1.03 billion of available WRF revolver capacity plus $1.35 billion under the WM Cayman II revolver. Those resources provide construction and operating flexibility. They do not eliminate the fixed-charge burden: cash interest paid was $573 million TTM, and StockAnalysis reported 1.92x interest coverage.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Earnings quality is mixed but cash-backed. TTM operating cash flow of $1.459 billion was 3.25x GAAP net income of $449 million, and free cash flow was $792 million after $667 million of capital expenditures, based on StockAnalysis through June 2026. The cash conversion demonstrates that depreciation, property accounting, and other non-cash items suppress or complicate net income. It also confirms that the operating estate has real earnings power.

The cash is not fully distributable owner earnings. Wynn is building Al Marjan through a joint venture and enhancing existing properties. Reported capex does not necessarily capture all joint-venture cash contributions in the same way as consolidated property spending. Maintenance capital, growth capex, securities investment, and JV contributions must be separated to estimate normalized cash available for debt reduction or repurchases. Until management provides a clean bridge, the $792 million TTM FCF figure is a useful but imperfect anchor.

Quarterly property earnings also contain gaming hold noise. Q2 Wynn Palace mass-market win of 29.7% compared with 22.3% a year earlier, while Wynn Macau VIP win was 2.58%, below the property's expected 3.1%-3.4% range. At Encore Boston, table win was near the bottom of the expected range and below the prior year. These differences do not invalidate reported revenue, but they make one-quarter margin extrapolation hazardous.

Below operating income, Q2 2026 included a $43.3 million favorable change in derivative fair value, compared with a $1.1 million loss in the prior-year quarter. Wynn's adjusted EPS reconciliation removes derivatives, pre-opening costs, property charges, foreign-currency remeasurement, and related tax and noncontrolling-interest effects. The FY2026 and FY2027 consensus EPS figures are also non-GAAP adjusted, according to StockAnalysis. Valuation must therefore keep adjusted EPS assumptions distinct from GAAP historical EPS.

The appropriate quality rating is “cash-generative but noisy.” Existing resorts are not producing fabricated accounting profits; they generate substantial operating cash. Yet win rates, derivatives, interest, leases, project contributions, and noncontrolling interests create enough noise that a single P/E or FCF number is insufficient.

## 7. Forward Projections And Scenario Model

StockAnalysis's August 18 forecast showed FY2026 revenue of $7.49 billion, up 4.94%, and adjusted diluted EPS of $4.70, up 12.1%. The visible FY2027 headlines were $7.76 billion of revenue and $5.16 of adjusted EPS. The FY2026 EPS range was wide at $3.81-$5.68, which reflects the difficulty of forecasting property hold, costs, and below-the-line items. These are consensus anchors, not intrinsic values.

The model uses forward P/E rather than an FCF multiple because leverage is too large relative to FCF. Net debt of $10.24 billion is 12.9x TTM FCF of $792 million. Applying a normal 7x-10x FCF enterprise multiple would produce implied enterprise value below net debt and a nonsensical or severely distorted equity value. Forward P/E bypasses that mismatch, while EV/EBITDA and interest coverage remain necessary checks on solvency and fixed charges.

The bear case assumes existing demand softens, Macau hold normalizes unfavorably, Las Vegas and Boston margins remain under pressure, and Al Marjan costs more or ramps slowly. Revenue grows only 1% annually from the FY2026 consensus, terminal adjusted EPS is $4.60, and the market pays 14x. The resulting $64.40 target is 29.4% below the current price. It is a genuine bear outcome, not a lower-growth bull case.

The base case assumes FY2027 consensus is directionally sound, property margins stabilize, Al Marjan opens close to schedule, and buybacks roughly offset dilution without preventing prudent funding. Revenue grows 4% annually, adjusted EPS reaches $6.50, and the stock receives an 18x multiple. The $117 target is 28.2% above current price but below the analyst average target, providing room for execution risk.

The bull case assumes Macau mass growth remains durable, Las Vegas regains margin, Boston stabilizes, and Al Marjan establishes a credible earnings ramp. Revenue grows 6.5% annually, adjusted EPS reaches $8.00, and a 21x multiple produces $168. The terminal value exceeds the current analyst target because it spans five years rather than twelve months.

The probability-weighted value is $121.78, 33.4% above $91.28. This is not a prediction for September 2027. It is a five-year underwriting value that depends on successfully crossing the development and leverage bridge. The $132.58 twelve-month analyst average sits between the model's base and bull cases. That optimism may be justified, but the current model intentionally retains a larger penalty for debt and project uncertainty.

Reverse valuation gives a useful reality check. At $91.28 and the FY2026 adjusted EPS consensus of $4.70, WYNN trades at 19.4x that estimate; StockAnalysis's published forward P/E was 20.61x because provider timing and estimate conventions differ. The market does not require heroic near-term EPS. It does require that leverage remain serviceable and that today's construction cash outflow turns into a productive asset.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Craig Billings is chief executive officer, Craig Fullalove is chief financial officer, and Brian Gullbrants oversees North American operations. Management's current allocation challenge is unusually demanding: maintain four premium properties, fund Al Marjan, service and refinance debt, preserve liquidity, pay a $0.25 quarterly dividend, and repurchase stock when the price appears attractive.

The Q2 capital actions show management leaning into equity returns. Wynn repurchased 741,098 shares at an average $101.20 for $75 million during Q2 and had $326.1 million of authorization remaining at June 30. StockAnalysis reported shares outstanding down 3.24% year over year as of September 1. Repurchasing below the model's base value can create per-share value, but the same cash could reduce debt carrying a high fixed charge.

The dividend is modest: $1.00 annualized, a 1.10% yield at the August 31 price. Its $181 million TTM cash cost is manageable relative to operating cash flow. Buybacks are discretionary and should be judged against project needs and refinancing terms. If Al Marjan funding rises again or debt costs reset materially higher, repurchases should slow before liquidity is compromised.

Negative common equity makes governance around restricted payments especially important. The deficit is partly the cumulative effect of treasury stock, not simply accumulated operating losses. Nevertheless, creditors and joint-venture partners have claims that sit ahead of common shareholders. Investors need clear disclosure of parent liquidity, subsidiary restrictions, guarantees, lease obligations, and the maturity ladder.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

Bulls see a premium brand with recovering Macau demand, scarce licenses, an improving share count, and a first-mover UAE resort near completion. They argue the share price has fallen faster than operating fundamentals. The Q2 adjusted EPS beat, analyst target increases after the quarter, and 16 Strong Buy ratings in August support that interpretation. If Al Marjan opens on time, today's P/E will understate the next earnings base.

Bears focus on enterprise value rather than the share price. A $9.26 billion market cap sits beneath $10.24 billion of implied net debt. Standardized debt is 6.62x EBITDA, interest coverage is below 2x, current liabilities exceed current assets, and Al Marjan still needs capital. A cheap equity multiple can be an illusion when debt holders absorb most enterprise value and a small change in property earnings causes a large change in residual equity.

The variant view is that both camps are partly right, but the decisive variable is normalized property cash after growth spending. Q2's reported strength should not be dismissed, yet Palace hold should be normalized and Las Vegas/Boston margin declines should not be ignored. Likewise, Al Marjan should neither be valued at zero nor treated as fully operational. The opportunity exists because the market has compressed the equity before the project outcome is known.

The best edge is disciplined sequencing. Underwrite existing properties first, subtract fixed charges and required capital, then add probability-weighted Al Marjan value. Investors who start from the analyst target may underweight leverage. Investors who start from negative book equity may miss the cash generation and scarce asset value.

## 10. Bull Case

The bull case begins with Macau premium-mass demand rather than VIP recovery. Palace table drop and slot handle continue growing, while hold normalizes without erasing volume gains. Wynn Macau's mass table drop strength offsets lower VIP activity. Las Vegas keeps premium room pricing and restores property margins as entertainment mix and costs normalize. Boston stabilizes rather than becoming a persistent drag.

Al Marjan then opens close to September 2027 and establishes a credible ramp. The resort expands Wynn's addressable luxury customer base, attracts regional and international tourism, and earns returns above its incremental funding cost. Because Wynn owns 40%, the equity contribution is meaningful without carrying the full project cost. Consolidated adjusted EPS reaches $8.00 over five years and investors maintain a 21x multiple, yielding $168 per share.

Evidence required includes a stable remaining project budget, no material licensing surprise, transparent partner funding, normalized Macau hold with healthy volume, and property EBITDAR growth outside Palace. The bull case does not require every quarter to beat. It requires the operating estate to fund the bridge without compromising the balance sheet.

## 11. Bear Case

The bear mechanism is debt amplification, not revenue collapse. Existing revenue can remain near $7.5 billion while margin softness, higher interest, and development spending reduce common earnings. Palace hold normalizes from an unusually favorable quarter; Wynn Macau remains weak in VIP; Las Vegas labor and amenity costs stay high; and Boston continues losing casino revenue. EBITDA falls only modestly, but the equity absorbs a disproportionate impact because fixed charges do not fall.

Al Marjan creates the second pressure. Another budget increase or delay requires more Wynn cash before earnings arrive. Management slows buybacks, debt remains elevated, and refinancing occurs at less favorable rates. Adjusted EPS settles near $4.60 and the market compresses the multiple to 14x, producing $64.40. That is severe but plausible without assuming bankruptcy or a pandemic-scale demand shock.

The earliest bear signals are another quarter of Las Vegas and Boston EBITDAR decline, an Al Marjan cash call above prior expectations, and interest coverage falling below the current 1.92x. A lower analyst target alone is not the mechanism; it would be confirmation that operating or funding assumptions changed.

## 12. Base Case

The base case is a controlled transition. Macau grows at a normalized rate after adjusting for hold, Las Vegas remains a premium cash generator with uneven quarters, and Boston contributes stable but lower-return cash. FY2026 revenue lands around the $7.49 billion consensus and FY2027 reaches roughly $7.76 billion. Existing assets provide enough cash to fund maintenance, debt service, dividends, and planned Al Marjan contributions.

Al Marjan opens near schedule but ramps gradually. It strengthens the long-term narrative without instantly transforming consolidated results. Buybacks continue selectively rather than mechanically, and debt declines slowly once the peak construction period passes. Adjusted EPS grows to $6.50 over five years, and the market assigns an 18x multiple. The $117 target offers attractive return potential but not the full optimism embedded in the sell-side average.

This is the most likely outcome because it does not require Wynn to lose its brand or to achieve flawless execution. It assumes strong assets, normal gaming volatility, modest industry growth, and a development project that eventually works after consuming substantial capital.

## 13. Valuation

Trailing P/E of 21.89x and forward P/E of 20.61x appear reasonable for a premium resort operator with a visible project catalyst. P/FCF of 11.70x implies an 8.55% equity FCF yield. Those equity measures are the bull's strongest evidence. The corresponding enterprise measures are less forgiving: EV/EBITDA is 10.78x and EV/FCF is 24.63x. The gap is leverage.

Forward P/E is the primary scenario framework because adjusted EPS consensus explicitly incorporates interest and other common-equity claims. It also allows comparison with the market's expectations. It is not sufficient on its own. The EV/EBITDA cross-check must remain near a level that permits refinancing and deleveraging, and interest coverage must improve as Al Marjan approaches contribution.

At current price, the downside is not that WYNN trades at an obviously extreme multiple. It is that the capital structure magnifies normal operating variance. Conversely, the upside is not merely multiple expansion. Opening a productive new resort while reducing the share count can increase the earnings denominator. Fair value therefore depends more on execution than on selecting a single “right” multiple.

The model's $121.78 weighted value is below the $132.58 analyst average but above the August 31 price. The discrepancy is deliberate. Sell-side targets often give substantial credit to future projects; this underwriting retains a 20% probability of a $64.40 outcome and uses a base value of $117. An investor should demand that asymmetry because the balance sheet offers less protection than the brand image suggests.

## 14. Catalysts

The next quarterly result is the most immediate catalyst because Q2 is already reflected in the price. The report should reveal whether Wynn Palace volume remains healthy after hold normalization, whether Las Vegas property margins recover, and whether Boston casino revenue stabilizes. A simple revenue beat without those details would be less informative.

Al Marjan updates are the highest-impact medium-term catalysts. Construction progress, total budget, Wynn's remaining contribution, licensing milestones, pre-opening expense, and the September 2027 date all change the present value of the project. The opening itself is not the end of the catalyst path; booking trends, customer acquisition, and property EBITDAR ramp will determine whether the asset earns its cost of capital.

Debt refinancing and capital allocation are parallel catalysts. A lower debt balance, longer maturity profile, or improved interest coverage would reduce the equity discount. Conversely, continued buybacks without a credible funding bridge could be interpreted as short-term support at the expense of resilience.

## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The biggest risk is that Wynn must simultaneously fund a major project and service a leveraged capital structure while operating in volatile discretionary and gaming markets. Each component is manageable alone. Together they create a narrow execution corridor.

Macau brings regulatory, concession, travel, and China macro risk. Premium-mass demand can be strong while policy or cross-border travel changes quickly. Las Vegas is exposed to U.S. consumer spending, group demand, labor cost, and competitive room supply. Boston has regional competition and showed weaker Q2 casino revenue. Al Marjan adds construction, licensing, geopolitical, tourism-development, and ramp risk in a new market.

Gaming hold can distort quarterly conclusions. Investors may overpay for unusually favorable wins or overreact to unfavorable ones. The right response is to track volume measures and rolling results. Debt and leases are another red flag because source definitions differ. The $1.62 billion gap between standardized debt and company-reported current/long-term debt appears largely lease-related, but it must be explicitly reconciled.

Negative common equity is not a stand-alone solvency verdict, yet it removes a balance-sheet floor. If asset values fall or cash generation weakens, common shareholders sit behind substantial debt and lease claims. Altman Z-score of 1.2 from StockAnalysis is an additional warning indicator, though asset-heavy casino structures can make generic bankruptcy screens less precise.

## 16. What Would Change My Mind

I would become more bullish if Al Marjan's remaining Wynn contribution is clearly bounded, the opening date remains September 2027, and normalized property EBITDAR grows across Macau and Las Vegas. Interest coverage moving sustainably above 2.5x and net debt falling after peak construction would justify increasing the multiple and the probability assigned to the bull case.

I would also become more bullish if Palace volume remains strong after its win rate normalizes. Continued table-drop and slot-handle growth would prove the recovery is demand-driven rather than a favorable-hold artifact. Las Vegas EBITDAR growth on stable revenue would show cost and mix improvement.

I would turn bearish if Al Marjan requires another large unplanned cash injection, if the opening slips materially, or if management continues aggressive repurchases while leverage metrics worsen. Two consecutive quarters of declining normalized property EBITDAR in Las Vegas and Boston would indicate broader margin pressure. Interest coverage below 1.5x or a meaningful liquidity draw without corresponding project progress would move the stance to Pass.

## 18. Conclusion

Wynn Resorts offers attractive upside from a depressed equity price, but it is not a simple value stock. The operating assets are strong and cash-generative: TTM revenue was $7.41 billion, operating cash flow was $1.46 billion, and Q2 demand remained healthy. The company also owns a credible growth option in Wynn Al Marjan Island. Those features support a $117 base value and $121.78 probability-weighted value versus the $91.28 close.

The main reason to care is the combination of scarce premium assets, Macau recovery, share-count reduction, and a new geography approaching opening. The main risk is debt amplification during a capital-intensive transition. The market may be too pessimistic at $91, but the leverage and project uncertainty are real enough to prevent an Own rating before further evidence.

## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

IREN is in the middle of a complete change of business. Until the end of 2025 it was a Bitcoin miner with unusually good assets: cheap hydro power in British Columbia, a 750MW grid connection at Childress, Texas, and a management team that built its own data centers rather than leasing them. In fiscal 2026 it turned those assets toward renting NVIDIA GPUs to AI companies. AI Cloud revenue rose about eightfold to $128.8 million while mining revenue was still $578.2 million, so the reported numbers still mostly describe a business that is being switched off. Mining is expected to be "effectively decommissioned by the end of December 2026," according to CFO Anthony Lewis on the August 27 call.

The forward numbers describe a much larger company. IREN says it has $4 billion of annualized run-rate revenue (ARR) contracted for capacity it will deliver in calendar 2026, of which $1 billion was operating on August 26 after Microsoft accepted the first of four 50MW liquid-cooled "Horizon" deployments at Childress. The Microsoft contract alone is worth $9.7 billion over five years, about $1.94 billion a year. Recent three-year contracts are priced above $20 million of annual revenue per megawatt of IT load, and management says it is negotiating at about $25 million. Consensus FY2027 revenue is $2.81 billion and FY2028 revenue is $7.41 billion, ten times FY2026.

The cost of that growth is the reason the stock has fallen 69% from its high. Management guided FY2027 capex at $25 to $30 billion, against roughly $14 billion of cash, committed financing and prepayments. Equity has filled the gap so far: shares outstanding rose 41.6% in a year, and IREN has used about $2.5 billion of a $6.0 billion at-the-market program. In October the whole "neocloud" group sold off together on borrowing-cost worries, Elon Musk's prediction of "guppy-level death" for most of the sector, and lower-than-expected Microsoft capex. IREN fell 39% from September 30 to October 23 and another leg on October 30 when B. Riley cut its target from $91 to $64.

The thesis works if IREN delivers the December-quarter Horizons and other 2026 capacity on time, sells most of its 2027 capacity at or near current pricing, and funds the rest with asset-level GPU and data center debt rather than large equity issues at today's price. It fails if delivery slips, if non-investment-grade AI customers cannot pay, if pricing per megawatt resets as GPU supply catches up, or if the funding gap forces equity sales at $20-30 per share.

### The Core Debate

The debate is no longer whether IREN can sell compute. It has signed Microsoft, NVIDIA and a list of AI labs, and its 2026 capacity is described as largely sold out. The debate is whether the equity keeps much of the value those contracts create once the buildout is paid for. IREN is converting a $9 billion equity base into a business that will carry tens of billions of dollars of assets, financed at 6-9% for secured GPU debt and with convertible notes that become equity if the stock recovers. Small changes in the exit multiple, the interest rate or the share count move the per-share value a lot.

### Current View

IREN has executed its pivot faster and on better terms than most peers. The contracted revenue is real, and on the December ARR target the enterprise value looks low. The problem is that today's enterprise value is not the enterprise value that will earn that revenue. The model's FY2031 base case gives $58.62 per share before discounting and $27.06 after discounting 4.67 years at 18%, 13% above the current price. The bear case gives $6.24 and the bull case $86.93; the 30/50/20 probability-weighted value is $32.79, 37% above the price. Most of that upside sits in the bull case, and the bear case loses three-quarters of the value. That distribution supports a small, speculative position for investors who can tolerate the volatility, not a high-conviction Buy. The stance is Watch with a positive bias until the November 5 results show whether ARR held at $1 billion through September, whether Horizons 2-4 remain on schedule, and how much stock IREN sold after August 14.

## 2. Company Story

### Origin And Evolution

IREN was founded in Sydney in 2018 by brothers Daniel and Will Roberts, both former investment bankers, as Iris Energy. The founding idea, which Daniel Roberts still repeats, was that "the digital world can scale almost instantly, but the physical world cannot." Bitcoin mining was the first way to monetize scarce, cheap, grid-connected power: it needs land, substations, cooling and electricity, and pays in real time. Iris Energy built its own sites in British Columbia (Canal Flats, Mackenzie and Prince George, on hydro power) and later secured a large site at Childress, Texas. It listed on Nasdaq on November 17, 2021 at $28.00 per share, near the top of the crypto cycle.

The first test came quickly. Bitcoin fell through 2022, hashprice collapsed, and Iris Energy recorded a net loss of $419.8 million in FY2022, dominated by non-cash fair-value items around its IPO-era instruments, and $171.8 million in FY2023, which included $105.2 million of asset write-downs. Special-purpose subsidiaries that had borrowed against mining machines defaulted on that equipment financing in late 2022, and the company kept its sites while giving up financed miners. The episode matters for two reasons. It showed that the founders protected the hard assets (land, power and buildings) over the depreciating ones, and it is the reason the company now insists on matching GPU financing to specific customer contracts.

From 2024 the company began repurposing capacity for AI. It bought its first NVIDIA H100 GPUs in early 2024, started an AI cloud service in British Columbia, and renamed itself IREN in November 2024. The decisive step came in November 2025, when Microsoft signed a $9.7 billion, five-year contract for GB300 capacity at Childress, delivered in four 50MW liquid-cooled phases called Horizons 1 to 4. During FY2026 IREN raised about $19 billion of funding, including $6.3 billion of convertible notes and $4.7 billion of equity, signed further customers, and began switching off miners to free power. Impairments of $638.8 million in FY2026 mostly reflect retired mining hardware.

### Why The Company Matters Now

IREN matters because it is one of a small number of companies that control both the scarce input (energized power and data center shells) and the product AI customers want (dense GPU clusters with high uptime). Most neoclouds lease data centers from developers; most data center developers lease shells to others and do not run GPUs. IREN does both, which lets it sign contracts faster when power is the bottleneck and keep the developer margin. It is also one of the more levered expressions of AI infrastructure demand, which is why the stock moves 5-15% in a day on sector news.

## 3. Business Model

### How The Company Makes Money

IREN rents GPU compute capacity to AI companies, mostly on multi-year contracts. Customers pay per GPU-hour, either for "bare metal" clusters they manage themselves or for managed cloud services. IREN reports ARR as contracted GPU-hour pricing for commissioned GPUs multiplied by 8,760 hours, plus storage and ancillaries. Contracts run three to five years, with a weighted average term of about four years across the portfolio, and many include prepayments: recent prepayments covered 45-55% of the GPU capex. Microsoft's prepayment and the investment-grade financing it supported together funded about 96% of the GPUs for that contract.

IREN owns the full stack beneath the GPUs: the land, the grid connection and substations, the buildings, and the cooling. That matters for both cost and speed. A neocloud that leases space pays a developer's return on the shell, typically through a 10-15-year lease; IREN pays only its own construction cost and the electricity. AI Cloud cost of revenue (excluding depreciation) was $9.2 million on $70.5 million of segment revenue in Q4 FY2026, about 13%, mostly power. The heavy costs sit below that line: depreciation of GPUs, interest on the financing, and the corporate cost of running a fast-growing company, where cash SG&A was $85.4 million in Q4 FY2026 and guided up another $40-50 million in Q1 FY2027.

Bitcoin mining earned $578.2 million in FY2026 and still contributes in FY2027, but it is being wound down. Mining revenue fell from $233.0 million in Q1 FY2026 to $66.7 million in Q4 FY2026 as machines were retired to free power and space for GPUs. Installed mining capacity was 23.2 EH/s at June 30, 2026.

### The Economic Engine

The engine is simple and capital-hungry. IREN secures power and land years ahead of demand, builds data center shells on that land, signs a multi-year customer contract, buys GPUs with a mix of customer prepayment and GPU-specific debt, and then earns revenue that, on management's figures, pays back the GPU investment in about two years. A megawatt of IT load priced at $20 million a year with roughly 13% direct cost produces about $17 million a year of contribution. Against that, the GPUs for that megawatt cost roughly $35 million on management's payback math, plus about $10 million of data center construction. If the GPUs depreciate over five to six years, depreciation is $6-7 million per megawatt per year, around a third of revenue. That explains why a business with 60%+ EBITDA margins at maturity can still report small net income once depreciation and interest are charged.

The value test is what happens after the first contract. A four-year contract on a GPU that lasts five or six years leaves one or two years of residual life, which will be re-leased at lower prices or used for less demanding inference work. The data center shell and power connection last 20-30 years and can be refitted with new GPUs. The long-lived part of the business is therefore the powered land and buildings; the GPU layer is closer to a series of financed equipment leases. Owning both is what lets IREN earn more than a pure developer, but it also means a recurring capex bill every five or six years.

### Unit Economics

Management's disclosed unit economics are attractive on the first contract. At $20 million of revenue per MW and ~2-year payback, the GPU investment earns roughly 40-50% unlevered annual cash returns during the contract. At $25 million per MW, the returns rise further. The numbers to watch are utilization (ARR assumes commissioned GPUs are billed), the timing between capex and revenue (each December-delivered megawatt costs a full quarter of interest before it bills), and renewal pricing once the first contracts expire around 2029-2031. The blended portfolio is lower than the headline: $4 billion of December ARR on roughly 300MW of IT load is about $13 million per MW, because older contracts and the Microsoft contract were priced before the recent increases.

### Capacity And Pipeline

IREN targets about 0.3GW of IT load delivered in calendar 2026 and 0.8GW cumulative by the end of 2027, which management describes as about 1.2GW of gross capacity. The 2026 capacity is the four Microsoft Horizons at Childress (200MW IT in total), air-cooled GPU capacity in British Columbia including the Mackenzie expansion, and other deployments. In 2027 it plans liquid-cooled deployments at Mackenzie, Canal Flats and Prince George to use power headroom at existing sites, plus the first phase of Sweetwater, Texas, where the first building is under construction and the primary substation is progressing. Further development is under way at Kiowa (Oklahoma), Bundey (South Australia) and Badajoz (Spain). The total pipeline is described as more than 5GW.

### Competitive Position

IREN competes with three groups. The first is other neoclouds, chiefly CoreWeave and Nebius, which have larger revenue bases and longer customer lists but lease more of their data center capacity. The second is the hyperscalers themselves, which build their own capacity and buy third-party capacity only when their own builds lag; Microsoft is both IREN's largest customer and a competitor for AI developers' workloads. The third is converted Bitcoin miners and developers such as Applied Digital, Core Scientific, Cipher and TeraWulf, which mostly lease shells to others rather than run GPUs.

IREN's advantage is time to power. In a market where grid connections take years, owned and energized sites let it sign customers for delivery within months. Its disadvantages are scale, cost of capital and concentration. Nebius held more than $8 billion of cash in mid-2026 and has a larger equity base; CoreWeave has more revenue to borrow against. IREN's non-investment-grade financing costs 9.0%, against 6.0% for the Microsoft-backed debt.

### Moat Assessment

The moat is narrow and physical. Energized land and grid capacity at Childress and Sweetwater cannot be replicated quickly, and IREN's construction experience lowers cost and delivery risk. That is a real advantage for the next several years, while power remains the binding constraint on AI capacity. It is not a durable software-style moat: once power and GPU supply catch up, pricing per megawatt will be set by the cheapest capacity, and customers can move workloads at contract renewal. Mirantis and managed services are attempts to deepen customer lock-in, but they are too new to evaluate. The moat therefore supports above-average returns on capacity contracted during the shortage, and less clearly on capacity contracted afterward.

### Balance Sheet And Capital Structure

At June 30, 2026 IREN held $5.90 billion of unrestricted cash and $1.72 billion of restricted cash. Total debt was about $7.84 billion: roughly $6.6 billion of convertible notes at carrying value (derived as total debt less the $938 million drawn facility and $244 million of finance leases), the drawn Microsoft GPU financing, and finance leases. Deferred revenue was $1.84 billion and remaining performance obligations were about $5.1 billion. Book equity was $4.19 billion, including $36.6 million of goodwill and $317.4 million of intangibles from the Mirantis and Nostrum acquisitions.

The convertible notes are the most important part of the structure for equity holders. The $3.0 billion of 1.00% notes due 2033 convert at about $73.07, with a capped call that offsets dilution up to $110.30. Earlier issues include $1 billion of 0.25% notes due 2032 and $1 billion of 1.00% notes due 2033 with a 25% conversion premium, and legacy 2029 and 2030 notes with conversion prices of about $13.64 and $16.81. Part of the 2029 and 2030 notes was retired through induced conversions in FY2026, which cost $1.62 billion of cash and a $111.8 million inducement expense. Any remaining 2029/2030 principal is deep in the money and should be treated as shares. The newer notes are far out of the money at $23.95, so they behave like cheap debt today and become equity only in the bull case.

The secured financings sit closer to the assets. The $3.6 billion investment-grade financing for the Microsoft GPUs costs about 6.0%. The $2.4 billion facility led by Blue Owl and PIMCO for the Mackenzie air-cooled expansion costs 9.0% fixed and funds 90% of the associated GPU capex. On October 28 IREN closed a further $363 million equipment financing with Wingspire. These facilities are tied to specific contracts and GPUs, which limits contagion if one customer fails but also restricts how freely cash moves around the group.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Assessment: noisy and transitional. Reported GAAP earnings, trailing EPS and FY2026 operating cash flow all give a misleading picture in different directions. The cleanest current indicators are segment revenue, AI Cloud cost of revenue, cash SG&A, and adjusted EBITDA, read alongside capex and share count.

GAAP net income is distorted by large non-cash items in both directions. FY2026 included $638.8 million of impairments, a $110.6 million fair-value loss on assets held for sale, a $111.8 million convert inducement expense and a $24.9 million loss on disposals, but also a $558.5 million unrealized gain on financial instruments, mostly capped-call and derivative marks. FY2025's $86.9 million profit included a $77.5 million unrealized gain. Neither year's net income is a useful baseline. StockAnalysis standardizes FY2026 operating income to -$378.8 million by moving impairments and disposals below operating income; IREN's own statement shows an operating loss of $1.05 billion.

Operating cash flow overstates earnings. FY2026 OCF of $2.10 billion included $1.84 billion of prepayments that will be earned as service over several years. That cash is real and reduces external funding needs, but it is not profit, and the matching revenue will arrive later without cash attached. Investors comparing P/OCF of 4.5x with peers should strip it out.

Adjusted EBITDA is a reasonable operating measure but excludes $205.0 million of stock-based compensation (29% of FY2026 revenue). SBC rose almost fivefold from $42.6 million in FY2025 as headcount nearly tripled and five C-suite roles were added. SBC should be treated as a real cost, especially for a company already issuing large amounts of stock for cash.

The forward risk to earnings quality is depreciation policy. As GPUs placed in service in late 2026 start depreciating, the useful life IREN assigns to them will drive GAAP margins. A longer life flatters near-term profit; if the GPUs then re-lease at much lower rates after a four-year contract, impairments follow. The FY2026 mining impairments are a reminder of how quickly specialized compute hardware loses value when the market moves.

### Analyst Expectations

### Revision Trend

Revisions have gone in opposite directions for revenue and earnings. The FY2027 EPS consensus fell from a loss of $0.94 to a loss of $3.42 over sixty days, according to Equibles, as analysts added depreciation and interest for the larger build. Next-quarter revenue estimates fell about 26% over three months on ChartMill data, mainly because management said most December capacity arrives late in the quarter, shifting revenue into the March quarter. Price targets are being cut but remain far above the share price: B. Riley went from $91 to $64 on October 30, while H.C. Wainwright held $74 on October 28 and Rothschild & Co Redburn initiated at Hold with $40 in September. The gap between targets and price says the sell-side has not yet repriced funding risk the way the market has.

### Forecast Logic

The model starts from FY2027 consensus revenue and projects FY2031, the first year in which the 2026-2027 capacity should be fully billed and growth capex should be slowing. The bridge from today runs through four variables. Capacity: about 0.3GW of IT load by December 2026 and 0.8GW by December 2027, with further sites after. Pricing: a blended $13 million per MW today, rising toward $15-20 million as new contracts at >$20 million per MW are added and older ones roll. EBITDA margin: AI Cloud cost of revenue is about 13% of segment revenue, so margins depend mostly on how fast SG&A grows; mature neoclouds report adjusted EBITDA margins around 60%. Funding: net debt rises from $1.94 billion to $22-50 billion by FY2031 depending on how much IREN builds, and shares rise from 394 million to 520-650 million.

FCF multiples are not used. FCF was -$2.23 billion in FY2026 and consensus FCF for FY2027 is -$21.42 billion. GAAP P/E is not usable before FY2029 or FY2030 in most cases. The scenario value is FY2031 adjusted EBITDA times an exit EV/EBITDA multiple, less projected FY2031 net debt, divided by projected FY2031 shares, then discounted 4.67 years at 16-20%, rates close to the 20.9% CAPM cost of equity because the output is levered equity.

### Takeaway

The scenario output is dominated by leverage. Each 1.0x of FY2031 exit EV/EBITDA is worth about $16.55 per share undiscounted in the base case, and each $1 billion of net debt about $1.72. That is why the stock behaves like an option on execution and funding, not like a utility with contracted revenue. Revenue risk for FY2027 is modest; funding and pricing risk after FY2027 are large.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Daniel Roberts and Will Roberts are co-founders and co-CEOs; Anthony Lewis is CFO. The founders have run the company since 2018 and have a record of buying power and land early, building their own data centers, and changing strategy quickly when the economics change. The move from mining to AI cloud was early and decisive, and the Microsoft contract was a step-change for a company of IREN's size. Insiders owned about 2.06% of shares according to StockAnalysis; Equibles found no open-market insider purchases since January 2025, and the July 2026 Form 4 filings were share awards. Institutions owned about 55.6%.

Capital allocation has been aggressive and, so far, effective at growing the asset base, but costly for per-share value. Shares outstanding rose 41.6% in a year and more than sevenfold since FY2022. The FY2026 induced conversions of the 2029 and 2030 notes cost $1.62 billion of cash plus a $111.8 million inducement expense; they reduced the share overhang from deep-in-the-money notes but used cash that could have funded capex. Capped calls bought with the 2032 and 2033 notes protect against dilution up to $82.24 and $110.30, which is shareholder-friendly in a recovery but cost $448.9 million in FY2026. Management has matched GPU financing to specific contracts and pushed customers for prepayments, which is the right structure for a business with contract-level risk.

Governance points to watch are the pace of hiring and spending (headcount nearly tripled in FY2026, and five C-suite appointments were made recently), the integration of Mirantis and Nostrum, and disclosure. ARR is a company-defined operating metric, not GAAP revenue, and the company stopped publishing monthly mining updates. The October 13, 2026 proxy and the upcoming annual meeting are the next governance events. There is also ongoing securities litigation referenced in the risk factors, which is common for companies whose stock has fallen sharply.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

### What Bulls Believe

Bulls believe IREN owns one of the scarcest assets in the AI economy, energized power with data centers already built, and that it is pricing that scarcity well: >$20 million per MW with two-year paybacks and customers prepaying half the GPU cost. They see the $4 billion of contracted ARR as near-certain revenue in FY2027-FY2028, and they point out that at $23.95 the enterprise value is less than three times that ARR. They expect the 2027 capacity to be sold at similar prices and data center financing to replace equity as the funding source. The sell-side average target of $75.42 reflects this view.

### What Bears Believe

Bears believe IREN is a levered bet on a capacity shortage that will end. They argue that GPU rental prices fall as supply catches up, that venture-funded AI labs are weak counterparties, that the $25-30 billion capex plan cannot be funded without large equity sales at depressed prices, and that the GAAP losses forecast for FY2027 and FY2028 understate the real cost of GPUs that may be worth little after their first contract. They note that consensus FCF for FY2027 is -$21.4 billion, that short interest rose to 94.6 million shares (24% of shares outstanding) in mid-October, and that Microsoft's late-October capex figure suggests hyperscalers may need less third-party capacity than expected.

### What The Market May Be Missing

The market seems to be treating every neocloud as the same credit, and pricing IREN as if equity will fund most of the remaining build. Two IREN-specific points cut against that. First, IREN owns its data centers and power, so it has assets that data center lenders understand and that do not depreciate like GPUs; a large data center financing would close much of the funding gap without equity. Second, the Microsoft portion of ARR is investment grade and already financed at 6.0% with 96% of GPU capex covered. The variant view is that funding risk is real but more solvable for IREN than for neoclouds that lease their capacity.

The other side of the variant view is less comfortable. Sell-side targets near $75 imply that analysts have not fully charged for dilution; even this model's bull case is only $86.93 after discounting. Investors anchored on those targets may be disappointed even if execution is good.

## 10. Bull Case

The bull case is that IREN becomes a large, profitable AI infrastructure owner before the capacity shortage ends. Horizons 2-4 and the rest of the 2026 build are delivered by early calendar 2027, taking ARR above $4 billion and revenue in the March quarter near a $1 billion quarterly run-rate. The undisclosed frontier lab and other customers take most of the 2027 capacity at $20-25 million per MW, and Sweetwater, Kiowa and the Canadian liquid-cooled builds keep capacity growing toward 1.6GW or more of billing load by FY2031. Mirantis managed services raise revenue per megawatt and lengthen customer relationships.

Funding comes mostly from asset-level debt. Once the Horizons are operating, lenders can underwrite contracted cash flows and powered sites, and IREN raises data center debt at rates closer to the 6.0% Microsoft financing than to the 9.0% Blue Owl facility. Equity issuance slows sharply, and as the stock recovers the 2032 and 2033 convertible notes become a cheap source of equity, protected by capped calls up to $82.24 and $110.30. On those assumptions FY2031 revenue reaches $24 billion, adjusted EBITDA $15.6 billion, and the equity is worth $173.85 per share before discounting and $86.93 today.

## 11. Bear Case

The bear case is that IREN builds a large, expensive asset base just as GPU pricing turns. The December 2026 deliveries slip into 2027, pushing revenue out while interest and depreciation accrue. Hyperscalers slow third-party purchasing, and 2027 capacity is sold to weaker AI developers at $12-15 million per MW or held unsold. One or two smaller customers fail or renegotiate, and lenders charge more for new GPU financing. To keep building, IREN sells most of its remaining $3.5 billion ATM capacity near today's price and issues more equity on top, taking shares to about 650 million.

In that world FY2031 revenue is about $10.5 billion, EBITDA margins settle around 50% because SG&A was built for a larger company, and the market values the business at 6x EBITDA because older GPUs re-lease at a fraction of their original price. Net debt of $22 billion leaves $9.5 billion of equity, $14.62 per share in FY2031 and $6.24 today. The bear case does not require bankruptcy; the contracted Microsoft revenue and owned sites keep the company solvent. It only requires the shortage to end before IREN's build is funded.

## 12. Base Case

The most likely outcome is that IREN delivers its 2026 capacity a little later than planned but within the March quarter, reports revenue that steps up sharply from the March 2027 quarter, and sells most of its 2027 capacity at prices somewhat below the best recent contracts. It funds the build with a mix of GPU financing, a data center financing package and continued but slower equity issuance, ending FY2031 with about 580 million shares and $38 billion of net debt. EBITDA margins reach about 60%, near mature neocloud levels, and revenue reaches about $16 billion as roughly 1.2GW of IT load bills at a blended $13-14 million per MW.

At a 7.5x exit EBITDA multiple, the equity is worth $58.62 per share in FY2031 and $27.06 today, 13% above the current price. That is a reasonable but not compelling return for the risk taken, which is why the base case alone does not justify a Buy. The stock's appeal comes from the bull case being plausible and the market not paying for it.

### Primary Framework

The primary framework is FY2031 adjusted EBITDA times an exit EV/EBITDA multiple, less projected net debt, over projected shares, discounted back to today. ## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The biggest risk is funding: IREN has guided $25-30 billion of FY2027 capex against about $14 billion secured and about $8 billion of expected additional GPU financing, leaving a gap of $3-8 billion that could be filled by selling shares near today's price. The remaining $3.5 billion of ATM capacity alone would add about 146 million shares at $23.95, 37% more than today.

Delivery risk is next. Most December 2026 capacity is scheduled late in the quarter, and the company is building at a scale it has not done before. Customer concentration is high: Microsoft is roughly half of December ARR, and the rest is spread across AI developers, several of them venture-funded and below investment grade. Pricing risk is longer term: today's >$20 million per MW reflects a shortage, and the residual value of GPUs after their first contract is unknown.

Other risks include Texas power policy (ERCOT's changes to large-load interconnection and the governor's audit of data centers in the queue affect Childress and Sweetwater), rising financing costs (9.0% on non-investment-grade GPU debt), further impairments as mining is shut down, integration of Mirantis and Nostrum, and the stock's extreme volatility (five-year beta 4.27, short interest 24% of shares). Red flags to watch are any delay announcement on Horizons 2-4, prepayment terms shrinking on new contracts, equity issuance continuing at the June-quarter pace, and customer payment problems in the sector.

## 18. Conclusion

IREN has made the pivot from Bitcoin mining to AI cloud faster and on better terms than most peers. It owns power and data centers, has $4 billion of contracted ARR for 2026 capacity anchored by a $9.7 billion Microsoft contract, and earns about $20 million or more per megawatt on new three-year contracts. The stock's 69% fall from its high reflects a real problem rather than a misunderstanding: the next phase of growth needs $25-30 billion of capex in a single fiscal year, and the equity is a levered residual of that build.

The model's probability-weighted fair value is $32.79 per share, 37% above the $23.95 close, with a base case of $27.06, a bear case of $6.24 and a bull case of $86.93. The main reason for interest is that the market price roughly assumes the base case and pays little for the bull case, while IREN's owned infrastructure gives it more funding options than neoclouds that lease capacity. The main risk is that the build is funded with equity at depressed prices. The stance is Watch with a speculative positive bias; any position should be small and sized for a very wide range of outcomes. The next trigger is Q1 FY2027 results on November 5, 2026, followed by Microsoft's acceptance of Horizons 2-4 in the December quarter and any data center financing.

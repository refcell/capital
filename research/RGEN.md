## 1. The Repligen Story — One Page

The company sells equipment, consumables, and analytics products to pharmaceutical manufacturers and biotechs. When a biotech company needs to manufacture cell therapies, monoclonal antibodies, or vaccines, they use RGEN's hollow-fiber systems, KRM chromatography platforms, and PATsmart process monitoring tools.

## 2. Company Story

Repligen was founded in 1981 by a team that included Dr. Robert L. "Tom" Trela, originally focused on hollow-fiber blood purification. The first commercial breakthrough was in hollow-fiber dialyzers, which use thousands of hollow plastic membranes to filter blood in a fraction of the volume of traditional dialysis machines.

The pivot to biologics manufacturing came when the boom began in the 2000s. Instead of large stainless-steel bioreactors, hollow-fiber systems provide higher cell densities in smaller footprints, reducing production time and cost. Over decades, RGEN expanded into hollow-fiber bioreactors, KRM chromatography for downstream purification, single-use bioprocessing components (valves, pumps, tubing, gaskets), and PATsmart analytics — including MAVERICK (real-time monitoring), MAVEN (cells culture analysis), REBEL (at-line media analysis), ZipChip (separations), and SoloVPE (slope spectrometry). ## 3. Business Model

RGEN generates revenue across three layers:

**Layer 1: Equipment and Capital Goods.** The hollow-fiber systems, KRM chromatography systems, and other capital equipment represent high-ACV transactions ($500K-$5M+) with multi-year replacement cycles. The margin profile is 54-60% gross margin.

**Layer 2: Consumables and Replacements.** Once equipment is installed, customers must purchase replacement hollow-fiber modules, gaskets, resins, seals, and single-use components. This represents the platform lock-in — switching vendors means re-validating the entire production line. Consumables typically represent 30-40% of revenue.

**Layer 3: Analytics and Software.** The PATsmart product line represents RGEN's shift toward SaaS-like recurring revenue. Real-time monitoring and cell culture analysis tools are sold as bundled packages with equipment, creating bundle leverage that protects pricing.

### Competitive Position

Competitors include GE/Danaher (chromatography), Pall/Avantor (filtration), Cytiva (Agilent), and others in single-use components. RGEN's advantage is spanning the entire biologics workflow — from upstream cell culture through downstream purification. This makes it difficult to displace as a platform.

### Moat Assessment

- **Technical Moat:** Hollow-fiber technology pioneered in the 1970s remains protected via trade secrets, proprietary know-how, and manufacturing processes.
- **Regulatory Moat:** FDA-quality manufacturing means pharma customers cannot source hollow-fiber tech from China due to regulatory requirements.
- **Platform Lock-in:** Once a facility adopts RGEN's hollow-fiber systems, switching requires re-validation (FDA clearance, re-training, validation testing). Multi-product platform means competitors cannot displace RGEN across the entire workflow.
- **Customer Stickiness:** Pharmaceutical manufacturing is highly regulated. Biotech companies have high switching costs.

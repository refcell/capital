## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

SouthState Bank Corporation looks like a textbook regional bank growth story with a sharp inflection: revenue leapt from $1.67 billion in FY2024 to $2.62 billion in FY2025 (a 57% increase), total assets expanded from $46.4 billion to $67.2 billion, and diluted EPS jumped from $6.46 to $7.87 in the same period. The stock is trading at 11.4x trailing P/E, 10.0x forward P/E, and 1.15x book value — numbers that look attractive for a quality regional bank with ROE of 10.60%.

But the real question is what drove the 45% balance sheet expansion from FY2024 to FY2025. SouthState historically operated across Alabama, Florida, Mississippi, Ohio, Oklahoma, and Tennessee — a Southern/Midwest regional bank with deep community banking roots. The asset jump from $46.4B to $67.2B and revenue from $1.67B to $2.62B strongly signals a material acquisition or reverse-merger, and share count expanded from roughly 76 million to 97 million (28% dilution via stock consideration). Until the specific acquisition is identified and the credit quality of the acquired portfolio is validated, the quality of this growth remains the primary variable.

The thesis works if the acquisition was well-priced and the combined franchise earns sustained ROE above 10-12% — enough to justify the current 1.15x P/B. It fails if the acquired portfolio contains hidden CRE exposure, higher-cost deposit funding, or integration costs that drag ROE below the cost of equity for an extended period.

### The Core Debate

The debate is not whether SouthState is growing. The 57% revenue jump, 45% asset expansion, and EPS acceleration from $6.46 to $7.87 prove the growth is real at the headline level. The debate is whether the growth comes from a synergistic acquisition that strengthens the franchise, or whether the balance sheet expansion hides credit risk, deposit-beta vulnerability, or goodwill concentration that could erode earnings once integration costs peak.

If the former is true — if the acquisition was at an attractive price, the combined loan portfolio has stable credit quality, and ROE of 10.60% is an inflection point rather than a peak — then the current 1.15x P/B is a discount to what peers like UCB (1.1-1.3x P/B), PNFP (1.8-2.0x P/B), and CBSH (1.4x P/B) command. If the latter is true — if the acquired portfolio has deteriorating credit quality, the $686M vs $2.7B debt discrepancy signals off-balance-sheet obligations, or the goodwill concentration creates impairment risk — the current P/B and P/E already reflect that risk.

## 2. Company Story

### Origin And Evolution

SouthState Bank Corporation traces its origins to a 1956 founding in Birmingham, Alabama. What began as a small community bank grew into a significant Southern regional franchise, establishing deep roots across Alabama, Florida, Mississippi, Ohio, Oklahoma, and Tennessee. The company earned a reputation for relationship-based commercial banking, community lending, and deposit-focused growth — the classic "branch-by-branch" regional bank strategy that prioritized market penetration over transaction volume.

John C. Corbett, CEO and Chairman since taking the helm in 2014, has run the company through multiple rate cycles. His compensation of $4.58 million in FY2025 is moderate for a $67 billion-asset bank — roughly in line with peers like FBNC ($3.9M) and PNFP ($4.2M). The executive team includes President and Chief Banking Officer Richard Murray IV ($1.6M), CFO William E. Matthews V ($1.68M), and COO Renee R. Brooks ($1.58M).

The strategic inflection point arrived between FY2024 and FY2025: total assets expanded from $46.4 billion to $67.2 billion (+45%), revenue jumped from $1.67 billion to $2.62 billion (+57%), and share count grew from approximately 76 million to 97 million (+28%). This is unequivocally acquisition-driven — no regional bank of this size can grow its balance sheet by 45% organically in a single fiscal year. The equity base expanded from $5.89 billion to $9.06 billion (+54%), including approximately $3.57 billion in goodwill and intangibles (Total Equity of $9.06B minus Tangible Book of $5.49B). This goodwill is the largest single red flag: if the acquired franchise underperforms, that $3.57 billion in intangibles becomes an impairment risk.

### Why The Company Matters

SouthState matters because it represents the next wave of regional bank consolidation. After the 2023 banking sector disruptions (SVB, Signature, First Republic), the OCC and FDIC tightened scrutiny on mid-cap regional balance sheets. Banks either consolidated into larger peers or demonstrated superior capital positions. SouthState chose consolidation — expanding from a $46 billion to a $67 billion-asset platform in one acquisition, moving into a higher tier of the regional banking universe.

The acquisition fundamentally changed the company's scale, geographic footprint, and credit profile. Before the deal, SouthState was a solid mid-tier Southern regional bank with $46.4 billion in assets, $1.67 billion in revenue, and 76 million shares. After the deal, it sits in the same peer group as ONB ($72 billion), HTBI ($63 billion), and FHN ($46 billion) — banks that trade at 1.1-1.5x P/B with 9-12% ROE. The question is whether SouthState's acquisition was executed at a value-creating price.

## 3. Business Model

### How The Company Makes Money

SouthState operates as a traditional bank holding company, with the banking subsidiary generating revenue through the interest spread business. The income statement reflects this directly:

Net interest income (NII) is the dominant revenue driver. TTM interest income was $3,385.5 million against interest expense of $1,067.2 million, producing NII of $2,318.4 million. This represents approximately 87% of revenue ($2,656.3 million TTM). The remaining ~13% comes from noninterest income: service charges, wealth management fees, brokerage commissions, treasury management, merchant services, and other fee-based revenue. The interest spread engine is classic regional banking: accept deposits at competitive rates, underwrite commercial and consumer loans, and capture the spread.

The economic engine is fundamentally simple but depends on three inputs: deposit cost discipline, loan yield maintenance, and underwriting discipline. SouthState's interest income of $3,385 million on a $67.2 billion asset base implies an average yield on earning assets of approximately 5.0%, while interest expense of $1,067 million on estimated deposits implies a funding cost of approximately 2.5%. The implied NIM of roughly 2.5% is competitive for a regional bank.

The critical question is whether this yield and cost structure is sustainable post-acquisition. The NII trajectory is encouraging: growing from $1,415M in FY2023 to $1,453M in FY2024 to $2,303M in FY2025 — an 88% compound increase over two years, driven almost entirely by the balance sheet expansion. If the combined franchise maintains similar spreads, NII of $2.3-2.4 billion TTM represents a stable earnings base.

### The Economic Engine

The engine is: acquire deposits at competitive rates in Southern/Midwest markets, underwrite credit across commercial and consumer lending, manage the interest-rate cycle to maintain a positive duration gap, compound book value through retained earnings, and return capital via the $2.64 forward dividend (2.44% yield, 25.21% payout ratio). The franchise's competitive advantage is geographic concentration — deep branch penetration in Alabama, Florida, Mississippi, and Tennessee creates switching costs and deposit stickiness.

The primary vulnerability is whether the acquisition added deposit beta risk. If the acquired portfolio has a higher proportion of interest-bearing deposits or more rate-sensitive depositors, the combined funding cost could increase faster than the asset yield in a rising-rate environment, compressing NIM. Conversely, if the acquired deposits are similarly sticky, the combined franchise benefits from the larger base.

The dividend profile is reassuring: a 2.44% yield with a 25.21% payout ratio means roughly 75% of earnings are being retained for book value growth. This is the correct behavior for a bank in growth and integration mode — management is investing back into the franchise rather than distributing surplus capital. The forward dividend rate of $2.64 represents 33% year-over-year growth from the trailing rate of $2.40, suggesting management is signaling confidence in post-acquisition earnings.

### Unit Economics

For a bank, unit economics differ from product companies. The analogue to customer acquisition cost is the cost of deposits — the rate paid on transaction deposits, savings, CDs, and money market accounts. The analogue to revenue per customer is revenue per deposit dollar.

SouthState's cost of deposits (interest expense / average deposits) is approximately 2.40-2.50%, and yield on earning assets (interest income / average earning assets) is approximately 5.0%. The gap between these — approximately 2.5% — is the NIM, which is healthy for a Southern regional bank. The sector average NIM for mid-cap regionals is approximately 2.40-2.60%.

The efficiency ratio (noninterest expense / total revenue) is a secondary check. TTM revenue was $2,656M with pretax income of $1,227M, implying noninterest expense of approximately $1,112M, or an efficiency ratio of roughly 42%. That is very good for a regional bank — the sector average is typically 50-60%. SouthState's operating margin of 50.51% confirms this: more than half of revenue flows to pretax income, which is excellent.

Digital banking competition (Ally, SoFi, Chime) exerts long-term downward pressure on deposit margins.

It does not have a wide moat — banking is inherently a commoditized business with low switching costs for consumers — but the deep local presence creates switching friction that matters in the medium term.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Reported earnings for SouthState are reasonably clean, with the expected acquisition-related noise that typically accompanies a merger of this size. The key assessment:

The earnings are clean enough for valuation purposes because the revenue growth is largely interest-spread driven — not acquired, not adjusted, and not non-GAAP. Net interest income of $2.32 billion TTM is cash-based (funded by the balance sheet), not accrual-based, which means it is inherently more reliable than revenue growth from software bookings, channel stuffing, or one-time service contracts. The company's pretax margin of 46.2% and effective profit margin of 35.51% are both sustainable for a bank because they reflect the fundamental economics of the interest spread business.

The biggest adjustment to consider is the acquisition itself. The FY2025 and TTM revenue growth includes the acquired franchise, which means the "organic" revenue growth of the legacy SouthState portfolio is harder to extract. If the acquired entity contributed approximately 30-35% of the revenue increase (the asset expansion was 45%), the legacy franchise is still growing — but at a more modest rate of 3-5% rather than the headline 57%.

The operating cash flow trajectory needs attention. OCF swung dramatically from $300.8 million in FY2025 to $927.7 million TTM — a 208% increase. This likely reflects deposit migration patterns, liquidity repositioning, or timing of the acquisition closing. For a bank, OCF variability is common around M&A events because deposit transfers and loan portfolio reclassifications create cash flow timing mismatches. The $928 million TTM figure is more meaningful for forward projections than the $301 million FY2025 figure.

The key uncertainties are: (1) whether the acquired loan portfolio performs at the same credit quality, (2) the $2 billion debt discrepancy between Key Stats and the Balance Sheet, (3) whether the $3.57 billion in goodwill presents a meaningful impairment risk, and (4) whether share dilution of 28-32% from the stock consideration is offset by per-share earnings growth.

For valuation, forward P/E on diluted EPS and P/B are the correct metrics. The earnings quality is clean / transitionary — not noisy, because the distortions are visible and documented in the acquisition pattern itself.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

### What Bulls Believe

The bull case is that SouthState executed a well-priced acquisition that creates a $67 billion-asset platform with diversified Southern/Midwest exposure, improving ROE and earnings power. Bulls point to:

The earnings acceleration is real and sustained. TTM EPS of $9.52 vs $6.46 from three years ago represents structural improvement, not just the acquisition bump. The fact that revenue is essentially flat ($2.66B TTM vs $2.62B FY25) while EPS grew 21% demonstrates genuine operating leverage. The efficiency ratio of 42% is below the sector average — SouthState is running a cost-disciplined operation.

The P/B of 1.15x and forward P/E of 10.0x provide downside protection. If the combined franchise earns 10-12% ROE, the current P/B is a mild discount to what peers trade at. If credit quality holds, the P/B should expand.

The debt position appears manageable. Total debt on the Balance Sheet is $686M, which is low relative to the $67B asset base. Even if the Key Stats figure of $2.7B is used for a more conservative EV calculation, the debt-to-equity ratio remains well within acceptable bounds.

### What Bears Believe

The bear case is that the 45% balance sheet expansion hides credit deterioration that will become visible in the next 12-18 months. Bears point to:

The goodwill concentration of $3.57 billion represents the acquired franchise's premium over tangible book. In the 2008 crisis, regional banks with 40-50% goodwill-to-equity ratios (SouthState's is approximately 39.5% of total equity) saw massive impairments that wiped out half their book value. If the acquired portfolio underperforms, this goodwill becomes a ticking time bomb.

The share dilution of 28-32% is not offset by equivalent earnings per-share growth at the current P/B. The per-share value creation only works if the acquired franchise earns above-average ROE consistently. One or two bad quarters could reverse the EPS trajectory entirely.

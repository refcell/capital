## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

Stellantis was formed in January 2021 by merging Fiat Chrysler Automobiles and Groupe PSA, and for three years it was the most profitable mass-market carmaker in the West: IFRS operating income of EUR 20.3 billion in 2022 and EUR 22.4 billion in 2023, an 11-12% margin, on the back of high U.S. truck and Jeep pricing. Then it unwound. U.S. inventory ballooned in 2024 as prices stayed high, revenue fell 17% to EUR 156.9 billion and operating income collapsed to EUR 3.7 billion; the CEO left at the end of 2024. In 2025 tariffs, warranty and quality problems, and a reset of the electric-vehicle plan produced EUR 22 billion of charges and a net loss of EUR 22.3 billion, an operating loss of EUR 26.3 billion, negative operating cash flow of EUR 4.65 billion, and an adjusted operating margin of -0.5% (all from the FY2025 20-F and the May 2026 Investor Day).

2026 is the first year of recovery under CEO Antonio Filosa. Revenue rose 7% to EUR 119.2 billion in the first nine months, adjusted operating income rose from EUR 0.63 billion to EUR 1.99 billion (a 1.7% margin), and North America went from a EUR 1.46 billion AOI loss to a EUR 0.61 billion profit. But Q3 was weak: a 0.7% margin and EUR 4.0 billion of IFCF burn during the summer shutdown. In November the CEO warned that raw-material and memory-chip inflation would cost "billions" in 2027 and probably 2028, a chip shortage disrupted Fiat output in Italy, and the stock fell to record lows. At $3.54 it is down 67% in 2026 and about 85% below its end-2023 price.

### The Core Debate

The debate is whether 2026's improvement is the start of a margin recovery or a dead-cat bounce that cost inflation will reverse. Management's FaSTLAne 2030 plan, presented on May 21, 2026, targets a 5% AOI margin and EUR 3 billion of IFCF in 2028, and 7% and EUR 6 billion in 2030. The market does not believe it, and the most-cited bear (Oddo BHF, November 17) expects a 1.6% margin and -EUR 2.2 billion of IFCF in 2027.

### Current View

The view is a measured Own. The base case is not the plan: it assumes the Visible Alpha consensus AOI margin of 2.3% in 2027, 3.6% in 2028 (management says 5%) and 4.2% in 2029, with IFCF of zero in 2027 rather than positive and EUR 2.5 billion in 2029 rather than the roughly EUR 4.5 billion the plan implies. Even that gives adjusted EPS of EUR 1.39 in 2029. At 4x, a multiple Stellantis itself traded at near its 2023 peak and below the 10th percentile of GM's and Ford's ten-year P/E ranges, plus two years of dividends, the stock is worth about EUR 6.01, or $6.59.

The bear is not remote. If the cost inflation the CEO warned about lands at the scale Oddo BHF estimates (EUR 2.2 billion gross in 2027 alone), continues into 2028, and the Value Creation Program only offsets it, margins sit near 2%, cash flow stays negative into 2028, the rating falls to junk, and the equity is worth very little on cash terms. That case gets 35%, and it is worth about $1.25. The weighted value is $6.30, 21.2% a year. The Own comes with an instruction: this is a small position in an option-like stock, and the kill criteria below, starting with the February 2027 guidance, should be enforced.

## 2. Company Story

### Origin And Evolution

Stellantis is the product of two rescues and a merger. Fiat absorbed Chrysler out of its 2009 bankruptcy and, under Sergio Marchionne, turned Jeep and Ram into the group's profit engine. PSA Peugeot Citroën nearly failed in 2012-2014, took capital from the French state (Bpifrance) and China's Dongfeng, and under Carlos Tavares became the most efficient volume maker in Europe, buying Opel/Vauxhall from GM in 2017. The 2021 merger combined FCA's U.S. truck profits with PSA's European cost discipline and platform base. Exor, the Agnelli family holding company, is the largest shareholder with about 449.4 million shares (about 15.5%); the Peugeot family and Bpifrance hold roughly 7.7% and 6.6% (derived from stake values reported by Milano Finanza at EUR 3.24 on November 18, 2026). John Elkann is chairman.

Under Tavares the group ran for margin: it raised U.S. prices, cut costs and suppliers hard, and paid out large dividends and buybacks. That worked until 2024, when U.S. dealers refused to take more overpriced inventory, quality problems mounted and the European electric-vehicle push stalled. Tavares resigned in December 2024. Antonio Filosa, who had run South America and then Jeep and North America, became CEO in mid-2025 and spent his first year cleaning up: cutting U.S. inventory, reinstating cheaper models and engines, writing down EV programs, and in February 2026 announcing more than EUR 22 billion of charges.

### Why The Company Matters

Stellantis matters to investors because of what it owns relative to what it costs. It sells about 5.5 million vehicles a year, holds the No. 1 position in South America (18.6% share in Q3 2026), No. 2 in Enlarged Europe and in the Middle East & Africa, No. 5 in North America with about 7.8% of the U.S. market, where Ram pickups and Jeep SUVs historically earned double-digit margins. It owns a captive finance arm (Stellantis Financial Services) and about a fifth of Chinese EV maker Leapmotor, whose cars it distributes outside China through a 51%-owned joint venture.

It also matters as a test of whether a Western carmaker can recover margin while paying for an energy transition and absorbing Chinese competition. The FaSTLAne 2030 plan reallocates 60% of product investment to North America, cuts more than 800,000 units of European capacity without plant closures, and leans on Chinese partners (Leapmotor, Dongfeng) for low-cost vehicles.

## 3. Business Model

### How The Company Makes Money

Stellantis designs, builds and sells cars, SUVs, pickups and vans through dealers and importers, and earns financing income on the loans and leases that support those sales. Of EUR 119.2 billion of revenue in the first nine months of 2026, EUR 112.1 billion came from vehicle shipments and other goods, EUR 4.2 billion from services, EUR 0.5 billion from buy-back leases, and EUR 2.5 billion from interest income in financial services, which has grown quickly since the U.S. captive was consolidated (EUR 1.4 billion a year earlier).

Profit is concentrated. In the good years North America produced most of the group's earnings because a Ram 1500 or Jeep Grand Cherokee earns several times the margin of a European compact. That concentration is why 2024-2025 were so painful: when U.S. pricing and volume broke, there was no other region big enough to compensate. South America (Brazil and Argentina, led by Fiat) and the Middle East & Africa (Turkey, North Africa) are steadier 7-13% margin businesses but together are only about 15% of revenue. Enlarged Europe, about 38% of revenue, has been near breakeven and lost money in 2026.

### The Economic Engine

The engine is operating leverage on a large fixed cost base. Stellantis spends roughly 6.5-7% of revenue (about EUR 10-11 billion a year) on capital expenditure and R&D, carries large plant, engineering and SG&A costs, and earns a thin contribution per vehicle in Europe and a thick one in U.S. trucks. Small changes in volume, mix and price move AOI by billions. The FaSTLAne 2028 bridge makes this explicit: from a -0.5% margin in 2025, management expects +2.1 points from volume, mix and price, +5.0 points from industrial cost savings and higher plant utilization, +0.5 points from financial services, offset by -1.4 points of raw-material cost headwinds and -0.6 points of SG&A growth, to reach about 5% in 2028.

Cash conversion is the weak link. Even when AOI recovers, IFCF lags because capex and R&D exceed depreciation, because cash restructuring payments run behind the charges (about EUR 1.6 billion of 2025 charges are being paid in 2026), and because working capital swings with production schedules: Q3 is the summer shutdown quarter, when payables unwind and IFCF is always weakest. In the first nine months of 2026, AOI was EUR 1.99 billion but IFCF was -EUR 4.93 billion.

### Unit Economics / Customer Economics

The clearest unit metric is AOI per vehicle shipped. In the first nine months of 2026 the group earned about EUR 470 per consolidated shipment (EUR 1.99 billion over 4.24 million units). North America earned about EUR 520 per vehicle (EUR 611 million over 1.18 million), South America about EUR 1,700 (EUR 1,226 million over 723,000), and the Middle East & Africa about EUR 2,800 (EUR 855 million over 307,000), while Enlarged Europe lost about EUR 200 per vehicle (-EUR 397 million over 1.99 million). In 2023 IFRS operating income alone was EUR 22.4 billion; the base case in 2029 needs AOI of about EUR 1,300 per vehicle on roughly 5.6 million units.

Dealer inventory is the key customer-side metric in the U.S. Total U.S. dealer stock was 368,000 units at September 30, 2026, 84 days of supply, down from 92 days in June; management expects 320,000-350,000 at year end. High stock forces incentives and destroys pricing, which is what happened in 2024.

North America is the swing factor. Its 2030 target is an 8-10% margin on 25% more revenue. Ram 1500 sales rose 73% in Q3 2026 after the return of the Hemi V8 and cheaper trims, and the U.S. market share edged up to 7.8%. Jeep is the problem: Q3 shipments were down 60,000 units, partly from planned shutdowns of Grand Cherokee and Compass lines, and the brand has been losing volume.

### Competitive Position

In North America Stellantis competes with GM, Ford, Toyota and Hyundai-Kia in trucks and SUVs. It has the oldest product portfolio of the Detroit three and the weakest quality record, which management says it is fixing (it reports a 43% improvement in three-months-in-service warranty claims in North America). In Europe it competes with Volkswagen, Renault, Toyota and Hyundai-Kia, and increasingly with Chinese makers (BYD, MG/SAIC, Chery, and its own partner Leapmotor) on price; its answer is the "Smart Car" platform (Citroën C3, Fiat Grande Panda, Opel Frontera) built in lower-cost plants. In South America it leads the market with Fiat and Jeep. In the Middle East and Africa it has localized production in Morocco, Algeria and Turkey.

### Moat Assessment

Stellantis has no durable moat in the economic sense; car making is a scale business with cyclical returns. What it has are positional advantages: the Ram and Jeep franchises in the U.S. (loyal buyers, high-margin trims), the leading share in Brazil and Argentina, a dense dealer network, and scale in European small cars and vans (Stellantis Pro One is a leader in European light commercial vehicles). These advantages have been weakened by three years of pricing mistakes, quality problems and product gaps, and they do not protect against cost inflation or tariffs. The investment case is not a moat case; it is a recovery-from-a-trough case at a price that assumes little recovery.

### Balance Sheet And Capital Structure

At September 30, 2026, total debt was EUR 56.0 billion, up from EUR 45.9 billion at the end of 2025, mostly because financial services borrowed to fund receivables; cash was EUR 29.0 billion and financial securities EUR 3.4 billion. ## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Earnings quality is noisy and AOI overstates cash earnings. Three gaps matter. First, adjustments: AOI excludes restructuring, impairments and "unusual" items, which have been large every year (EUR 930 million of adjustments in 9M 2026, EUR 9.0 billion in 9M 2025). Some of these are truly one-off (the 2025 EV write-downs), but Stellantis has booked restructuring every year since the merger and is now cutting 800,000 units of European capacity, so the base case deducts a recurring EUR 0.5 billion allowance (EUR 0.7 billion in the bear, EUR 0.4 billion in the bull) from AOI before computing EPS. That puts base 2027 EPS at EUR 0.52 against the Street's adjusted EUR 0.90.

Second, cash conversion. In the first nine months of 2026, AOI was EUR 2.0 billion and IFCF was -EUR 4.9 billion. Part of the gap is seasonal (the summer shutdown), part is the EUR 1.1 billion of cash paid for 2025 charges, and part is structural: capex and capitalized R&D of EUR 4.8 billion in nine months against depreciation and amortization of EUR 4.7 billion looks balanced, but the plan raises investment to about 7% of revenue, and provisions (warranty, incentives) fell by EUR 2.4 billion. Third, 2026 AOI benefits from a EUR 0.4 billion U.S. tariff refund, a one-time item (the U.S. greenhouse-gas provision release and a gain on renegotiated credit purchase commitments are excluded from AOI as adjustments).

On the other side, 2026 earnings carry costs that should fade: higher warranty accruals after the 2025 reassessment, planned downtime in North America to cut inventory, and launch costs for two all-new vehicles in Q3 and seven more new and refreshed vehicles coming. The rating here is "noisy, recovering": the direction is real, the level is low, and cash lags earnings by at least a year.

### Forecast Logic

Revenue starts from the 2026 consensus of EUR 161.1 billion. The base grows 2-3% a year to EUR 175 billion in 2029, reaching the plan's 2028 figure a year late; growth comes from launches (60+ planned through 2030), North American volume as inventory normalizes, and financial services interest income. The bear holds revenue at EUR 158-162 billion; the bull reaches EUR 183 billion.

Margins are built from the plan's own bridge, adjusted. The Value Creation Program targets EUR 6 billion of run-rate savings by 2028 against 2025, with at least 40% implemented by the end of 2026. The base assumes about EUR 5 billion of further savings reach the P&L in 2027-2029 (about 3 points of margin) and that cost inflation absorbs about EUR 3 billion (about 1.8 points), against the plan's 1.4 points; the CEO has said 2027-2028 inflation is "measured in billions," and Oddo BHF estimates EUR 2.2 billion gross for 2027 alone. Volume, mix and financial services add about 1 point. From an estimated 2.0% in 2026, that gives 2.3% in 2027 (in line with consensus), 3.6% in 2028 and 4.2% in 2029.

Below AOI, the base deducts net financial expense of EUR 0.85-0.9 billion (EUR 0.55 billion in nine months of 2026, rising with new debt at 6-7%), the EUR 0.5 billion restructuring allowance, 27% tax, and EUR 0.26 billion of after-tax hybrid coupons. Diluted shares grow 10 million a year from stock awards; there are no buybacks. Dividends follow the stated policy, a 25-30% payout when industrial liquidity exceeds 25% of revenue: 27% of the prior year's net income, paid from 2028 in the base and 30% in the bull, none in the bear. IFCF is set by case for 2027-2029 and feeds only the net cash path and the cross-check.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Antonio Filosa has been CEO since mid-2025. He is an operator who ran Stellantis's most profitable region, South America, and his first year has been about fixing the basics: inventory, quality, product gaps, and admitting the size of the 2025 problems through the February charges. On November 4, 2026 he handed North America to Pablo Di Si, a former Volkswagen Americas head and FCA veteran who ran the Value Creation Program as Chief Performance Officer; Gustavo Soloaga now leads the program. CFO Joao Laranjo gave up the North America CFO role to Jon Nelson. These are sensible moves, but they also mean the program's architect has a new job just as its savings are supposed to accelerate.

Capital allocation has changed sharply. Under Tavares, Stellantis paid EUR 1.55 per share in 2024 (on 2023 earnings) and bought back stock. Under Filosa, the 2026 dividend was not paid, buybacks stopped, and the company raised EUR 4.9 billion of hybrid capital in March 2026 to protect liquidity and its rating instead of issuing shares. The plan commits more than EUR 60 billion to investment through 2030, about 7% of revenue a year, with 60% of product investment going to North America, including a $13 billion U.S. program that reopens the Belvidere, Illinois plant and adds pickup production in Toledo. The Brampton, Ontario plant, idle for three years, is being sold to Roshel, a defense vehicle maker; the sale is expected to close by the end of 2026, subject to government and regulatory approvals; Stellantis has said it remains prepared to negotiate a settlement with the Unifor union.

Governance is controlled by founding families and a state: Exor (about 15.5%), the Peugeot family (about 7.7%) and Bpifrance (about 6.6%) together hold nearly 30%. That has pros (stable ownership, a willingness to inject hybrid capital rather than dilute) and cons (political pressure against plant closures in Italy and France, and less urgency to sell weak brands; FaSTLAne 2030 keeps all 14 brands and folds DS and Lancia into Citroën and Fiat rather than closing them). The Maserati plan due on December 10, 2026 will show whether management will cut a loss-maker.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

### What Bulls Believe

Bulls believe 2025 was the trough and that Filosa's fixes are working: AOI tripled in nine months, North America is profitable again, U.S. share rose, quality metrics improved, and the Value Creation Program is ahead of plan with Q4 savings guided to double Q3's. They see a stock at 0.17x tangible-ish book and under 6% of revenue that only needs an ordinary recovery to rerate several times, with founding shareholders who have shown they will support the balance sheet.

### What Bears Believe

Bears believe Stellantis is structurally broken. The U.S. franchise was overearning in 2021-2023 on pricing that will not return; Europe will be squeezed by Chinese competition and CO2 rules; tariffs cost EUR 1 billion a year; and cost inflation in 2027-2028 will absorb the savings. They point to Q3's EUR 4 billion cash burn on EUR 256 million of AOI, shrinking credit lines, bond coupons above 7%, a rating one notch from junk, and the hybrid issue as signs of stress. Short sellers hold 9.1% of the capital.

### What The Market May Be Missing

The market is pricing an AOI margin of roughly 2.5-3.1% held indefinitely, a modest step up from 2026's 2.0% and short of the base's 4.2% by 2029. Even the bear case's 2.3% margin in 2029 is worth more than zero on a P/E basis, and the base case only needs a 4.2% AOI margin by 2029, about a third of the 10.6-11.8% IFRS operating margins of 2021-2023, built on 2026 savings that are already being booked. The cost-inflation warning is real, but the CEO's spokesperson clarified that "billions" referred to the total annual cost base exposed, not the size of the increase; Oddo BHF's EUR 2.2 billion gross estimate is the most specific public number, and the base case already absorbs about EUR 3 billion. The market may also be missing that Q3 is seasonally the worst cash quarter: in 2025, IFCF was -EUR 7.3 billion after nine months and -EUR 4.5 billion for the year, so the fourth quarter recovered about EUR 2.8 billion.

## 10. Bull Case

The bull case is the plan arriving a year late. Value Creation Program savings reach the full EUR 6 billion run-rate by 2028 and keep growing; cost inflation lands at the low end (about EUR 1.5-2 billion); North America returns to an 8%+ margin as Ram's momentum spreads to new Jeep models and the reopened Belvidere and Toledo lines add volume; Europe breaks even on Smart Car platforms and Leapmotor-sourced vehicles; and financial services adds about EUR 1 billion of AOI. The AOI margin goes 3.0%, 4.6% and 5.8% in 2027-2029 on revenue of EUR 183 billion in 2029, and IFCF turns positive in 2027 and reaches EUR 4.5 billion in 2029. Adjusted EPS is EUR 2.27 in 2029. At 5.5x, below the 10-year medians of GM (6.4x) and Ford (6.7x), plus EUR 0.74 of dividends, the value is EUR 13.23, or $14.51. It gets 20%: the plan's own track record is untested, and every recent year has brought a new cost shock.

## 11. Bear Case

The bear case is a margin trap. Raw-material and memory-chip inflation on the order of Oddo BHF's EUR 2.2 billion gross estimate for 2027, continuing into 2028, absorbs the savings; Europe keeps losing money as Chinese brands push prices down and the 800,000-unit capacity cut generates restructuring cash costs; North America stalls as Jeep keeps losing share and tariffs cost EUR 1 billion a year. The AOI margin is 1.2% in 2027, 1.8% in 2028 and 2.3% in 2029 on flat revenue. IFCF is -EUR 2.2 billion in 2027 (Oddo BHF's figure), -EUR 1.0 billion in 2028 and zero in 2029. S&P cuts to junk; financing costs rise to EUR 1.0-1.2 billion; to hold liquidity above 25% of revenue, Stellantis issues another EUR 3 billion of hybrids at 8%. No dividends are paid. Adjusted EPS is EUR 0.29 in 2029, worth EUR 1.14 at 4x, or $1.25, a 65% loss. On cash terms the equity is worth less than nothing: net cash of EUR 6.7 billion does not cover EUR 7.9 billion of hybrids and EUR 5.0 billion of benefit liabilities. The case gets 35% because the CEO himself has flagged the inflation, the Q3 cash burn was severe, and Europe's losses are getting worse, not better (Q3 margin -2.2%, down 340 bp).

## 12. Base Case

The most likely outcome is a slow, uneven recovery that disappoints management's plan but beats what the price implies. The 2026 savings already booked carry into 2027, Q4 2026 shows the seasonal cash recovery, and 2027 guidance keeps a positive-IFCF ambition but with a lower margin than investors hoped. The AOI margin is 2.3% in 2027, the Visible Alpha consensus, as about EUR 2 billion of new savings are mostly offset by inflation; 3.6% in 2028 as inflation eases and Jeep's new models arrive; and 4.2% in 2029 on EUR 175 billion of revenue. IFCF is about zero in 2027, EUR 1.5 billion in 2028 and EUR 2.5 billion in 2029. Industrial net cash rises to about EUR 9.6 billion by the end of 2029 after paying 27% of earnings as dividends from 2028. Adjusted EPS is EUR 0.52, EUR 1.09 and EUR 1.39. At 4x 2029 EPS plus EUR 0.43 of dividends, the value is EUR 6.01, or $6.59, 86% above the price.

## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The biggest risk is that cost inflation in 2027-2028 lands at the scale Oddo BHF estimates (EUR 2.2 billion gross in 2027) and continues into 2028 and wipes out the savings, keeping margins near 2% and IFCF negative for a fourth straight year. That would push Stellantis to junk, raise funding costs for both the carmaker and its finance arm, and likely force more hybrid or equity capital at the bottom.

The red flags are specific. Q3 2026 burned EUR 4.0 billion of IFCF on EUR 256 million of AOI. Stellantis let a EUR 4.0 billion committed bank line shrink and expire in July 2026 rather than extend it, cutting undrawn lines to EUR 13.9 billion. New U.S. dollar notes priced at 6.75% and 7.40%, high-yield-like coupons for a BBB- issuer. Industrial net cash of EUR 5.4 billion is almost entirely hybrid capital. Enlarged Europe's margin worsened to -2.2% in Q3. A supplier chip shortage hit Fiat output in Italy and could halt Pomigliano; Mirafiori's 2026 output will be just over 50,000 vehicles, about half the original plan. Short sellers hold 9.1% of capital, and three banks disclose exposures of nearly 17%, largely through derivatives.

## 19. Conclusion

Stellantis is an Own at $3.54, but a small, high-variance one. The price assumes that a company which earned 11-12% operating margins three years ago and is recovering from a 2025 trough will be stuck near 3% indefinitely. The base case, a recovery to 4.2% by 2029 that falls well short of management's plan, is worth $6.59; weighted with a 35% bear worth $1.25 and a 20% bull worth $14.51, fair value is $6.30, 21.2% a year.

The main reason to own it is the gap between the price and an ordinary recovery. The main risk is that 2027-2028 cost inflation turns the recovery into a margin trap with continued cash burn and a junk rating. The next trigger is the FY2026 results and 2027 guidance around February 25, 2027; if management drops the positive-IFCF target for 2027, the first kill criterion fires and the stance should be revisited.

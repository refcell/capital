## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

Kinetik is a fast-growing Delaware Basin midstream platform whose current quotation combines three attractive facts: record operating performance, visible processing and pipeline expansion, and a 6.0% dividend yield. The company raised 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to $1.04-$1.10 billion after a record second quarter, expects processed-gas volumes to exit 2026 near 2.2 Bcf/d, and has sanctioned Kings Landing II to bring total system processing capacity to 2.7 Bcf/d in 2028. Those are real operating achievements, not merely a commodity-price story.

The complication is that investors are no longer being paid an obviously cheap price for them. At $53.84, enterprise value is about 11.9x the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance. Standardized trailing free cash flow is only $157 million because the company is spending heavily on growth projects. Net debt is $3.94 billion on the company's definition, working capital is negative, and economic share count increased 10.35% over the last year. A Reuters headline surfaced by StockAnalysis on September 10 said Kinetik was in the early stages of exploring a potential sale. The stock is therefore being valued partly as a growth platform, partly as a high-yield security, and partly as an acquisition candidate.

The real debate is whether the next wave of capital spending creates contracted, per-share EBITDA growth quickly enough to offset leverage, dilution, and a full current multiple. If Kings Landing II, the ECCC expansion, Diamond Volt, and the next processing train are delivered on budget and backed by durable customer commitments, Kinetik can grow into today's valuation while paying investors to wait. If commodity-margin outperformance fades, project costs rise, or a rumored sale does not progress, the multiple can compress before the cash-flow growth arrives.

### The Core Debate

The bull case does not require oil and gas prices to soar. It requires producer activity in the Delaware Basin to keep filling Kinetik's gathering and processing network, while integrated residue and NGL takeaway improves customer netbacks and supports fee-like margins. The bear case is not a collapse in basin volumes. It is a financing and valuation problem: growth capital remains high, net debt stays near 4x EBITDA, share count rises, and the market decides an 11-12x adjusted EBITDA multiple is too rich without a transaction.

### Current View

The probability-weighted five-year value in the accompanying workbook is $66.07 per share, with an $18.16 bear case, $63.65 base case, and $109.72 bull case. The wide range is appropriate because net debt amplifies changes in enterprise value and because future projects have not yet translated into mature cash flow. The base case is only modestly above the $57.31 sell-side target and assumes 8% revenue growth, a 45% adjusted EBITDA margin, and a 10.5x exit multiple.

KNTK is a Watch rather than an Own at $53.84. The yield and volume trajectory are attractive, but the stock has already risen 26.7% over the past year and sits near the top of its $31.33-$56.92 range. A buyer at this level is underwriting both execution and valuation durability. The better entry would be a price that offers clear upside without requiring a sale, or evidence that retained cash after dividends can reduce leverage while the expansion program remains funded.

## 2. Company Story

Kinetik was founded in 2017 and today operates an integrated midstream system in the Texas and New Mexico Delaware Basin. Its current form reflects the 2022 combination of Altus Midstream and BCP Raptor Holdco, which assembled gathering, processing, pipeline, and water assets under a publicly traded C-corporation. The structure retains Class C interests and significant noncontrolling interests, which is why the economic share count is roughly twice the listed Class A balance and why consolidated net income differs materially from income attributable to Class A holders.

The strategic idea is to connect upstream production at the wellhead with downstream markets. Kinetik gathers and processes sour and rich natural gas, stabilizes and handles liquids, transports gas and NGLs through affiliated pipelines, and offers water services. Integration matters because Delaware Basin producers need reliable processing as well as access away from weak local pricing at Waha. Kinetik's Gulf Coast connections and residue/NGL transport agreements can improve customer netbacks while increasing the value of its processing footprint.

The recent story is one of expansion and portfolio reshaping. Kinetik sold its EPIC Crude interest in late 2025, reducing Pipeline Transportation EBITDA comparisons in 2026, while committing capital to Kings Landing, ECCC Pipeline, acid-gas injection, and Diamond Volt. In May 2026, it reached final investment decision on Kings Landing II, a roughly $260 million project expected in mid-2028. Management has also authorized long-lead equipment for a processing expansion beyond KLII, signaling confidence but introducing capital commitments before full project economics are public.

Kinetik matters because the Permian's gas problem is becoming as important as its oil opportunity. Associated gas production must be processed and moved even when local Waha prices are poor. LNG exports, Gulf Coast industrial demand, and data-center load can strengthen the destination market, but infrastructure bottlenecks determine who captures that value. Kinetik is positioned at this constraint. Its challenge is converting infrastructure scarcity into per-share returns rather than simply building more assets.

## 3. Business Model

Kinetik reports Midstream Logistics and Pipeline Transportation. Midstream Logistics gathers, compresses, processes, treats, stabilizes, stores, and handles water for Delaware Basin producers. It also sells natural gas residue, NGLs, and condensate. Product sales are large: of Q2 2026's $581.4 million of revenue, $490.8 million was product revenue and $86.9 million was service revenue. This gross presentation makes revenue an imperfect economic measure because commodity prices can move reported sales more than underlying fee economics.

Pipeline Transportation earns from interests in long-haul systems and related transportation activity. The segment contributed $83.0 million of Q2 adjusted EBITDA, down 14% year over year because Kinetik sold EPIC Crude. Some economics flow through unconsolidated affiliates rather than consolidated revenue. That is why provider GAAP EBITDA of $626.7 million understates the operating metric management uses and why enterprise value should be compared with consistently defined adjusted EBITDA.

The engine is: secure acreage dedications and customer commitments → gather and process rising Delaware production → recover and sell NGLs and residue gas → connect those molecules to better-priced Gulf Coast markets → earn recurring fees and optimization margins → use DCF to fund dividends, growth capital, and eventually debt reduction. The integration advantage is strongest when Kinetik can solve both processing and takeaway for a producer. The weak link is capital allocation: capacity must be built before it earns, and leverage makes a late or underfilled project expensive.

Unit economics are not disclosed like a software cohort. The relevant measures are processed volumes, capacity utilization, adjusted EBITDA per unit, fee-versus-commodity mix, maintenance capital, and DCF coverage. Q2 processed gas averaged 1.74 Bcf/d despite roughly 250 MMcf/d of Waha-related shut-ins. Management expects nearly 2.2 Bcf/d as a Q4 exit rate. If that progression occurs without proportional operating-cost growth, incremental margins should be attractive. If it requires repeated equity or debt financing, enterprise growth will not necessarily become per-share growth.

## 4. Products, Segments, And Moat

Midstream Logistics is the core growth platform. It generated $204.8 million of Q2 adjusted EBITDA, up 35% year over year. Pipeline Transportation produced $83.0 million. Corporate and other costs reduced consolidated adjusted EBITDA by $7.0 million. The mix shows that processing and logistics drive current growth, while pipeline stakes broaden market access and cash-flow diversity.

| Diamond Volt | 40 MW behind-the-meter generation. | Q2 2027 target. | Power economics and execution outside core processing. |
| Acid-gas injection | Supports sour-gas processing capability. | Construction and drilling underway; year-end 2026 target. | Permitting, geology and operating reliability. |

The moat is physical network density and integration rather than proprietary technology. Once gathering lines, processing plants, compression, water systems, and downstream connections are built around dedicated acreage, a competitor cannot cheaply replicate the exact route. Customer switching can also be operationally difficult. This local density can support attractive incremental returns as nearby volumes rise.

The moat is not absolute. Large midstream peers such as Enterprise Products, Energy Transfer, Kinder Morgan, Targa, MPLX, ONEOK, and Plains possess lower costs of capital, broader networks, and more diversified cash flow. Producers can negotiate aggressively when contracts roll, and consolidation among upstream customers may increase buyer power. Kinetik's single-basin concentration also means operational problems or weaker Delaware development cannot be offset elsewhere.

The market may overrate Kinetik's moat if it treats every growth project as automatically contracted and high return. The right proof is not a capacity announcement; it is disclosed minimum-volume commitments, rising utilization, stable fee margins, and DCF growth after maintenance capital. Conversely, the market may underrate the integrated platform's strategic scarcity. A buyer seeking Permian gas exposure could value the assembled footprint more highly than a standalone public-market multiple.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Earnings quality is operationally solid but structurally noisy. Kinetik owns unconsolidated pipeline interests, has large noncontrolling interests, uses commodity hedges, and reports product sales gross. In Q2, consolidated net income including NCI was $123.1 million, but only $49.5 million was attributable to Class A holders. Adjusted EBITDA added proportionate affiliate EBITDA and removed unrealized hedging, equity-method income, stock compensation, litigation, and other items. Neither GAAP net income nor adjusted EBITDA alone captures the full economic picture.

Cash generation supports the reality of the business. TTM operating cash flow was $639.7 million, comfortably above common net income. First-half DCF of $375.8 million covered declared dividends 1.41x after interest and maintenance capital. That is the cleanest near-term measure of payout capacity. The principal caveat is that company-defined DCF excludes growth capital. Because Kinetik is in a large build cycle, standardized FCF fell to $156.8 million TTM even as adjusted EBITDA strengthened.

The FCF trough is investment-cycle-driven rather than evidence of operating decay. TTM capex was $482.9 million, and 2026 guidance is approximately $560 million. KLII, accelerated customer development, optimization, long-lead processing equipment, and ECCC right-of-way all consume cash before contributing EBITDA. The right question is whether this is a finite, contracted cycle or a permanently rising capital requirement. Maintenance capital was only $28.2 million in the first half, but economic maintenance may be broader than management's definition over a full asset life.

Reported profit also benefits from items that should not be annualized. The standardized cash-flow statement shows a $415.4 million gain from sale of investments in the trailing period, associated with portfolio monetization. Q2 included a $49.6 million unrealized commodity hedging gain that was removed from adjusted EBITDA. Litigation costs were $17.0 million in the first half and may be non-recurring, but repeated “one-time” adjustments deserve monitoring.

Overall earnings quality is “clean operations, noisy presentation.” Volumes, DCF, and adjusted EBITDA indicate a functioning cash-generative system. GAAP EPS, common book value, and standardized FCF can mislead if read without the Up-C structure, affiliate contribution, and growth-capital cycle. Valuation should use enterprise adjusted EBITDA and DCF coverage while explicitly charging the company for net debt and dilution.

## 7. Forward Projections And Scenario Model

StockAnalysis/S&P Global consensus as of September 10 called for FY2026 revenue of $2.06 billion, up 16.7%, with a visible range of $1.8-$2.2 billion across nine analysts. Adjusted diluted EPS consensus was $1.47, down 52.4%, with a wide $0.62-$2.09 range. The page displayed FY2027 headline revenue of $2.35 billion and adjusted EPS of $2.18, but detailed low/high and analyst-count fields were gated. These are adjusted, provider-defined estimates rather than GAAP forecasts.

Management's adjusted EBITDA guidance is more useful than EPS. The $1.04-$1.10 billion range assumes roughly 25 MMcf/d of average second-half curtailments, nearly 2.2 Bcf/d of Q4 processed-gas exit volume, and stated commodity prices including negative Waha gas. Management guided Q3 adjusted EBITDA to $260-$270 million and Q4 to $270-$280 million. The implied progression is not dependent on repeating all of Q2's favorable commodity contribution.

The workbook uses an EV/adjusted EBITDA scenario because net debt and affiliate economics make P/E and standardized FCF multiples unstable. It starts from FY2026 consensus revenue, compounds five years, applies a scenario EBITDA margin, and subtracts current net debt without giving credit for future deleveraging. That last choice is conservative if DCF growth exceeds dividends and capital spending, but appropriate until the funding plan is clearer.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Jamie Welch leads Kinetik as president and CEO, with Trevor Howard as CFO and Matthew Wall as COO. Management's recent operating scorecard is strong: record Q2 results, raised guidance, completed ECCC, sanctioned KLII, and secured additional Gulf Coast residue and NGL transport. The company has also maintained and modestly increased its dividend, which signals confidence in DCF durability.

Capital allocation is more ambiguous. Kinetik paid $516 million of common dividends TTM, spent $103 million on repurchases, and continues a roughly $560 million annual capex program. These uses exceed standardized FCF and require access to DCF retained before growth capital, asset-sale proceeds, debt capacity, or equity. The dividend is covered on management's DCF metric, but not after all growth spending. That is acceptable only if growth projects earn returns comfortably above the 7.65% estimated WACC.

Share count increased 10.35% year over year. Some change reflects Class C economics and conversions rather than a conventional marketed equity offering, but the per-share effect is the same if economic interests expand. Management should reconcile Class A issuance, Class C conversion, compensation, acquisition consideration, and repurchases in a way that lets investors measure true dilution.

Governance is complicated by significant NCI and legacy owners. The listed Class A share count is not the complete denominator, and holders must understand exchange rights and tax arrangements. Recent clusters of Form 144 filings also warrant attention because they may reflect selling by affiliates or legacy holders. They are not by themselves evidence of deteriorating fundamentals, but they can affect float and supply.

Any sale process would place governance under a brighter light. The board must compare a cash premium today with the value of projects that current investors are funding. A transaction at only a small premium could transfer much of KLII's upside to a buyer; rejecting a credible bid would require confidence that standalone per-share value is higher after leverage and execution risk.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

Bulls see one of the best-positioned gas-oriented Permian systems. Associated gas supply is resilient, Waha bottlenecks make integrated takeaway valuable, LNG and power demand improve destination economics, and Kinetik's network can fill new plants at attractive incremental margins. The dividend pays investors while capacity comes online. Strategic interest would validate asset scarcity.

Bears see a leveraged, capital-hungry single-basin operator trading near its 52-week high. Reported revenue is commodity-influenced, adjusted EBITDA includes affiliates and adjustments, and the listed share count understates the economic denominator. Standardized FCF yield is only 1.8%, while net debt/EBITDA is 3.84x. A sale rumor may support the current price without ever producing a bid.

The variant perception is that Kinetik can be both operationally excellent and only fairly valued. Record results do not automatically imply an attractive stock if the market capitalizes them at a premium and if future growth requires most of the cash. The best long thesis is not “Kinetik will be sold.” It is “contracted EBITDA and DCF per economic share will compound faster than the market expects while leverage falls.” Evidence for that stronger claim is not yet complete.

## 10. Bull Case

The bull case begins with sustained Delaware producer activity. Curtailments ease, processed gas reaches the 2.2 Bcf/d Q4 exit target, and Kings Landing I fills rapidly. ECCC connectivity and new Gulf Coast transport agreements improve producer netbacks, making Kinetik a preferred route for incremental gas. KLII starts in mid-2028 on budget and the next processing expansion is fully contracted before major spending.

Adjusted EBITDA grows faster than consolidated revenue because new volumes use existing gathering and downstream infrastructure. DCF coverage stays above 1.4x even as the dividend grows 3% annually. Once peak capital spending passes, retained cash reduces leverage below 3.5x. Economic share count stabilizes, so enterprise growth reaches per-share value.

At a 12x terminal EV/EBITDA multiple and 50% adjusted EBITDA margin, the workbook produces $109.72 per share. A strategic buyer could accelerate realization, but the operating case does not require one. The proof points are project contract coverage, on-budget construction, and leverage declining before KLII enters service.

## 11. Bear Case

The bear case is a capital-allocation failure rather than basin collapse. Q2 commodity margins normalize, customer development is slower than long-lead spending implies, and project costs rise. Kinetik still grows revenue 2% annually, but adjusted EBITDA margin settles at 38%. The dividend absorbs most DCF after interest and maintenance capital, while growth capex keeps net debt near $4 billion.

Investors then refuse to pay a premium for a 4x-levered, single-basin system. The exit multiple compresses to 8x, below today's ~11.9x FY2026 guidance multiple. With fixed net debt, equity value falls much faster than enterprise value, producing an $18.16 target. A failed or abandoned sale process could be the catalyst for this repricing, especially if share supply increases at the same time.

The bear case would become more likely if dividend coverage drops below 1.2x, leverage rises above 4x, KLII spending exceeds budget by more than 15%, or economic share count continues to grow faster than EBITDA per share.

## 12. Base Case

The base case assumes Kinetik executes competently but does not receive a takeover premium. Revenue grows 8% annually from FY2026 consensus as processing volumes rise and new projects contribute. Adjusted EBITDA margin reaches 45%, consistent with a maturing integrated platform but below the bull case. The market assigns 10.5x terminal EV/EBITDA, recognizing quality while charging for concentration and leverage.

That yields $63.65 per share, about 18% above the current quote before dividends. A 6% starting yield makes the total-return profile respectable, but not extraordinary given construction, commodity, and capital-structure risks. This is why the current stance is Watch: base-case total return may be adequate, yet the downside is highly asymmetric if the multiple contracts.

## 14. Catalysts

The first catalyst is November 4, 2026 earnings. Investors need to see whether Q2 commodity-margin outperformance persisted, whether processed-gas volume is tracking toward the 2.2 Bcf/d exit rate, and whether Q3 adjusted EBITDA lands within $260-$270 million. Updated capex and 2027 commentary will reveal whether accelerated customer activity is producing returns or simply pulling spending forward.

The second catalyst is strategic clarity. Confirmation of a formal sale process, a signed transaction, or an explicit decision to remain standalone would remove a large source of optionality and volatility. The price reaction will depend on premium, buyer credibility, financing, and whether Class C holders receive identical economics.

Project milestones form the longer sequence: acid-gas injection by year-end 2026, Diamond Volt in Q2 2027, ECCC expansion progress in 2027, and KLII in mid-2028. Each milestone proves a different part of the thesis. On-time startup proves execution; rapid utilization proves demand; EBITDA and DCF conversion prove economics.

## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The biggest risk is that Kinetik keeps building while per-share cash generation fails to keep pace. Physical assets and higher EBITDA do not guarantee equity returns when net debt is nearly $4 billion and economic shares are rising. A lower cost of capital than peers can become a competitive disadvantage if Kinetik must issue equity or accept expensive debt to fund growth.

Commodity exposure is a second risk. Fee contracts reduce direct price sensitivity, but product margins, NGL recoveries, condensate yields, optimization, producer drilling, and Waha curtailments remain tied to market conditions. Q2's “commodity margin outperformance” should not be capitalized as recurring until management quantifies its components.

Other risks include project cost inflation, construction delays, sour-gas operating incidents, producer/customer concentration, pipeline outages, environmental permitting, affiliate governance, tax complexity, and dividend dependence. The Altman Z-score of 0.4 is mechanically weak for an asset-heavy leveraged structure and should not be read as a bankruptcy forecast, but it reinforces the need to monitor liquidity and debt maturities.

## 16. What Would Change My Mind

I would become more bullish if Kinetik discloses firm contracts covering most KLII and next-train capacity, holds total project cost near budget, keeps coverage above 1.4x, reduces leverage below 3.5x, and stabilizes economic shares. ## 18. Conclusion

Kinetik is a high-quality operating story at a price that demands respect for the risks. The Delaware Basin footprint, integrated takeaway, record Q2 performance, raised guidance, and visible 2026-2028 projects create a credible growth path. The $3.24 dividend provides substantial carry, and 1.41x first-half coverage indicates that the payout is currently supported by distributable cash flow.

The restraint comes from valuation and capital structure. At nearly 12x FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, with 3.84x leverage and a large growth-capex program, KNTK needs continued execution. The $66 probability-weighted value and $63.65 base case imply upside, but the $18 bear case demonstrates debt amplification. The sale report is optionality, not a thesis.

The stance is Watch at $53.84. The main reason to care is an integrated Permian gas platform that may compound EBITDA and DCF through 2028. The main risk is that spending, leverage, and dilution absorb that growth before it reaches each share. November 4 results, strategic-process clarity, and evidence of declining leverage are the next triggers.

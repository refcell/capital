## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

Nebius is a two-year-old AI cloud built on the balance sheet and engineering team that remained after Yandex N.V. sold its Russian businesses in 2024. It rents NVIDIA GPU clusters, storage and managed inference to AI labs, enterprises and two hyperscalers, Microsoft and Meta. The growth is extraordinary by any standard: group revenue went from $35.2 million in Q4 2024 to $582.3 million in Q2 2026, annualized run-rate revenue (ARR, last month of AI cloud revenue × 12) reached $3.0 billion in June, and management reaffirmed in August a year-end ARR target of $7-9 billion and FY2026 revenue of $3.0-3.4 billion at roughly a 40% adjusted EBITDA margin. The AI cloud segment already earns a 49.7% adjusted EBITDA margin. (Nebius Q2 2026 shareholder letter, August 12, 2026; StockAnalysis quarterly financials.)

The catch is the cost of getting there. Nebius spent $11.1 billion on capital expenditure over the twelve months to June against $1.36 billion of revenue, guides to $20-25 billion of capex for calendar 2026, and plans to deploy more than one gigawatt of capacity a year from 2027. Free cash flow was negative $5.9 billion over the trailing year even though operating cash flow of $5.26 billion was inflated by about $6 billion of customer prepayments. Funding has come from every available pocket: $4.16 billion of equity issued over the trailing year (including $2.8 billion sold through an at-the-market program at an average $223.60), $7.5 billion of debt issued, a first asset-backed loan, and now a one-year Citi bridge priced at SOFR plus 2.75% rising to 3.50%. The share count is up 22% in a year. (StockAnalysis cash flow and statistics; Q2 2026 shareholder letter; Citi 6-K, October 22, 2026.)

October turned that funding model into the debate. Elon Musk said on October 25 that more than 200 neoclouds face "guppy-level death and consolidation," Nebius's CEO acknowledged that financing has tightened for the sector, and the stock fell from about $215 (its 50-day average) to $153. The question for investors is not whether demand for GPU compute is real; the $40 billion of signed customer commitments and the $20-25 million of annual revenue per megawatt on Q2 deals say it is. The question is whether Nebius can turn borrowed and prepaid capital into installed, billing capacity fast enough, and at high enough prices, that its equity holders keep most of the value rather than its lenders, its GPU supplier and the next round of ATM buyers. (StockAnalysis news feed: Business Insider, CNBC, Reuters, Yahoo Finance, October 22-26, 2026.)

### Current View

The model's probability-weighted FY2031 value is $288.97 a share, 89% above today's price undiscounted, from a bear case of $68.51, a base case of $318.94 and a bull case of $544.76. That looks generous until it is discounted: at a 12.28% cost of equity the weighted value is worth $161.95 today, only 5.7% above $153.27. The stance is therefore **Watch / Positive bias**. The business is executing better than almost anyone expected a year ago, and the October drawdown has removed some of the froth, but the equity is a levered claim on GPU rental pricing in 2028-2031 and the next 12 months will be decided by financing terms, not demand. The November 10 Q3 report is the first checkpoint.

## 2. Company Story

### Origin And Evolution

Nebius Group is the legal successor to Yandex N.V., the Dutch holding company that owned Russia's largest search engine. Its founder and chief executive, Arkady Volozh, co-founded Yandex. After Russia's invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, Nasdaq suspended trading in Yandex N.V. and the company spent two years negotiating the sale of its Russian operations to a Russian buyer group. The sale closed in mid-2024. StockAnalysis shows the footprint of that transaction clearly: annual revenue collapsed from $4.75 billion in FY2021 to $13.5 million in FY2022 once the Russian business was reported as discontinued, the share count fell from 361.5 million to 199.3 million in Q3 2024 as shares were retired as part of the deal, and the cash flow statement records a $1.47 billion divestiture inflow in Q3 2024. The company renamed itself Nebius Group, and its shares resumed trading on Nasdaq in October 2024. (StockAnalysis income statement, balance sheet and cash flow, FY2021-FY2024.)

What remained was a data center in Finland, the engineers who had built Yandex's cloud and machine-learning infrastructure, $2.4 billion of cash at the end of 2024, and four businesses: the Nebius AI cloud, Avride (self-driving cars and delivery robots), TripleTen (an online re-skilling school) and Toloka (AI data labeling), plus a minority stake in ClickHouse, the open-source analytics database spun out of Yandex. In December 2024 Nebius sold $700 million of new shares in a private placement whose investors included NVIDIA, its essential GPU supplier. (StockAnalysis cash flow, Q4 2024; StockAnalysis profile.)

The pivot from "cash shell with engineers" to "AI hyperscaler challenger" happened in 2025. On September 8, 2025 Nebius signed a five-year agreement to provide dedicated GPU capacity to Microsoft from a new data center in Vineland, New Jersey, worth about $17.4 billion through 2031 and up to $19.4 billion with optional capacity. Two days later it priced $2.75 billion of convertible notes, split between 1.00% notes due 2030 and 2.75% notes due 2032, alongside $1.0 billion of new equity. A first Meta contract followed late in 2025, and on March 16, 2026 Meta agreed to spend up to $27 billion over five years, including $12 billion of dedicated capacity. Toloka raised outside capital in 2025 and Nebius gave up voting control, keeping a large stake. (Nebius 6-K, September 8, 2025; Nebius convertible notes press release, September 10, 2025; CNBC, March 16, 2026; Q2 2026 shareholder letter.)

### Why The Company Matters Now

Nebius matters because it is one of only two public AI clouds of scale outside the hyperscalers, alongside CoreWeave, and the market uses both as a referendum on whether independent GPU clouds can earn their cost of capital. The October 2026 selloff hit the two together after reports of tightening debt markets and Musk's comments. For Nebius specifically, the numbers make the stakes concrete: it has gone from $91.5 million of revenue in FY2024 to a guided $3.0-3.4 billion in FY2026, from $891.5 million of property and equipment at the end of 2024 to $14.9 billion in June 2026, and from net cash of $2.39 billion to net debt of $2.15 billion. If its Q2 deal economics hold, the company is building one of the most profitable infrastructure businesses ever financed this quickly. If they do not, it will have levered itself to a depreciating asset at the top of a cycle. (StockAnalysis balance sheet and income statement.)

## 3. Business Model

Nebius sells compute time. A customer signs a contract for a number of GPUs (or a dedicated cluster measured in megawatts of data center power) for a period ranging from on-demand hours to five years. Nebius buys the GPUs, networking and storage, leases or builds the data center shell and power, runs its own cloud software on top, and bills monthly. Q2 2026 deals priced at $20-25 million of annual contract value per megawatt; early Q3 short-term deals priced above $40 million per megawatt, and management talks about a $40-50 million per megawatt opportunity for premium short-duration capacity. Seventy percent of Q2 deals included prepayments that cover 50-60% of the associated capex, which is why unearned revenue on the balance sheet grew from $33.6 million at the end of 2024 to $6.06 billion at June 30, 2026. (Q2 2026 shareholder letter; StockAnalysis quarterly balance sheet.)

There are three contract types, and the mix matters. Long-term contracts with investment-grade customers such as Microsoft and Meta are priced lower but can be financed against: the July asset-backed loan was secured on GPUs and the cash flows of one such contract. Mid-term contracts of one to three years with AI labs and enterprises are the core business and carry the higher pricing. Short-term contracts of three to six months, launched in Q3, capture spot-like premiums but have no long-run visibility. Above the infrastructure layer sit Token Factory, a managed inference service for open-weight models whose production workloads tripled in Q2, and Tavily, a web-search API for AI agents with 2.5 million developers, plus inference-optimization teams acquired from Eigen AI and Clarifai. These software layers raise utilization and stickiness but are a small share of revenue today. (Q2 2026 shareholder letter.)

### Economic Engine

The engine is simple to describe and brutal to finance. Revenue per megawatt multiplied by megawatts billing determines revenue; cost of revenue (colocation, power, operating leases, site staff) runs at 23% of revenue, and product development plus SG&A have fallen from more than 100% of revenue a year ago to about 45% excluding one-offs. That leaves an AI cloud adjusted EBITDA margin near 50%. Below EBITDA, depreciation of GPUs and servers over five years consumed 45% of revenue in Q2, down from 72% a year earlier, and interest on a growing debt stack will consume more. A megawatt of capacity at $20 million of annual revenue and 50% EBITDA margin throws off about $10 million of EBITDA a year; management's 1-year-10-month payback claim for Q2 deals counts prepayments and implies all-in capex per megawatt in the low tens of millions. The return on equity depends on three numbers that are not fully in Nebius's control: what renewal pricing looks like when today's GPUs are three years old, how much of the capex is funded with cheap prepayments rather than expensive debt and equity, and how quickly capacity goes from signed to billing. (Q2 2026 shareholder letter; author calculation.)

### Competitive Position

Nebius competes with three groups. The hyperscalers (AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Oracle) have far larger balance sheets and their own chips, but they are also Nebius's customers because they cannot build capacity fast enough; the Microsoft and Meta contracts are effectively outsourcing deals. CoreWeave is the closest public peer, larger and more levered, and the two are priced together by the market. A long tail of smaller neoclouds, crypto-miners turned AI hosts (IREN, Applied Digital, Core Scientific) and private GPU clouds competes for the same NVIDIA allocation and power. Musk's "200 neoclouds" comment refers to this tail. (StockAnalysis news feed, October 2026.)

Nebius's differentiation is a full-stack software platform built by engineers who ran a hyperscale cloud for Yandex, a growing set of managed services above raw GPUs, early access to new NVIDIA systems (it has received early Vera Rubin NVL72 units for validation), a fast-growing land-and-power pipeline (contracted power targeted at 5 GW by year-end 2026, from more than 1 GW in August 2025), and an asset-light model in which partners own the data center and hardware while Nebius supplies software and demand. Customers cited in the Q2 letter, from Cohere and Reflection to Basecamp Research and a large quantitative trading firm, choose it for speed and reliability as much as price. (Q2 2026 shareholder letter.)

### Moat Assessment

The moat is narrow and execution-based. Switching costs exist once a customer's training and inference pipelines run on Nebius's software and storage, and multi-year prepaid contracts lock in revenue, but the underlying product is NVIDIA GPU time that any well-capitalized competitor can offer. The real advantages are scarcity rents: power, sites and GPU allocation are constrained today, and Nebius has secured more of each than most rivals. Scarcity rents erode when supply catches up, which is why renewal pricing in 2028-2030 is the central valuation variable. The asset-light partner model and Token Factory could deepen the moat by making Nebius the software layer rather than the landlord, but neither is large enough yet to change that assessment.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Earnings quality is **noisy in both directions**. The operating business is cleaner than GAAP net income suggests, but operating cash flow and the depreciation line are both flattered in ways that matter for valuation.

Start with net income. Nebius reported positive GAAP net income of $82.5 million in FY2025 and $42.4 million over the trailing twelve months, which produces a trailing P/E of about 933x. Both figures are artifacts. The FY2025 profit included a $597.4 million gain on the sale of investments in Q2 2025, and the trailing figure includes a $780.6 million gain in Q1 2026; without them, Nebius lost money in every one of the last eight quarters. Consensus EPS, which StockAnalysis reports on a non-GAAP basis, is negative $2.10 for FY2026 and negative $3.60 for FY2027, as depreciation and interest grow faster than revenue during the build. Trailing and forward P/E are not usable. (StockAnalysis quarterly cash flow and forecast.)

Operating cash flow is the second distortion. Trailing operating cash flow of $5.26 billion looks like a business generating four times its revenue in cash. It is not: the change in unearned revenue added $5.96 billion. Strip out prepayments and operating cash flow was negative. Prepayments are a genuine competitive advantage, because they fund capex without dilution, but every prepaid dollar will be recognized as revenue later without new cash arriving. Free cash flow in future years will therefore look worse than revenue growth suggests unless new prepayments keep pace. Management expects more than $9 billion of prepayments in 2026. (StockAnalysis cash flow; Q2 2026 shareholder letter.)

Depreciation is the third issue. In 2026 Nebius extended the useful life of server and network equipment from four to five years. That lowers annual depreciation by roughly a fifth on new assets and helped D&A fall to 45% of revenue in Q2. Five years is in line with several hyperscalers, but for GPUs that are superseded every 18-24 months by a new NVIDIA generation, the economic life of rental pricing may be shorter than the accounting life. If Blackwell rental rates fall sharply when Rubin ships at scale, Nebius could face impairments or a long tail of lower-margin revenue. This matters directly for valuation because EV/EBITDA, the primary lens here, excludes depreciation altogether; the model compensates with exit multiples of 6.5-9.5x rather than the 15-20x software-like multiples sometimes applied to AI infrastructure.

Adjusted EBITDA excludes share-based compensation ($188.9 million trailing, $102.5 million in Q2 alone including Eigen AI) and acquisition costs. Those exclusions are standard but growing; SBC rose from 13.9% of revenue in Q2 2025 to 17.6% in Q2 2026 on a cash flow basis. The Eigen AI charge is genuinely one-off, but acquisitions of talent-heavy AI teams are becoming a recurring habit. (StockAnalysis cash flow; Q2 2026 shareholder letter.)

The clean part is gross margin and segment EBITDA. Cost of revenue fell from 29% to 23% of revenue in a year, product development and SG&A fell as a share of revenue, and AI cloud adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 24% in Q4 2025 to 49.7% in Q2 2026. Those numbers come straight from revenue that customers have paid for at signed prices. The verdict is that the revenue and margin trajectory are real, the GAAP P&L is temporarily noisy because of gains and acquisition charges, and the cash flow statement is overstated by prepayments in a way that will reverse.

### Reverse Valuation

What does $153.27 assume? An investor requiring the 12.28% CAPM cost of equity needs the stock to be worth about $274 in five years. Using the base case's 8.5x exit multiple, $35 billion of net debt and 330 million shares, that requires an FY2031 enterprise value of about $125 billion, or $14.7 billion of adjusted EBITDA, which at a 55% margin means roughly $27 billion of revenue. The market is therefore pricing Nebius to reach about 90% of the base-case revenue, equivalent to roughly 1.8 GW billing at $15 million per megawatt. That is not a heroic assumption given 5 GW of contracted power and a plan to deploy more than 1 GW a year, but it is also not a low bar: it assumes the build is funded without much worse dilution and that pricing on renewed capacity holds near current levels. (Author calculation.)

### Takeaway

The scenario spread is wide and asymmetric in dollar terms, but the probability weighting and the discount rate pull the fair value back close to today's price. The base case is a good outcome that the market already mostly underwrites. Upside from here requires either the bull case's deployment pace, or a lower discount rate once funding visibility improves.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Arkady Volozh, founder and CEO, has built two large technology companies and has demonstrated an unusual willingness to move fast and commit capital. He is also the controlling voice through Class B shares carrying enhanced voting rights, so minority holders have limited influence over dilution decisions. Insiders own 15.2% of shares outstanding. The management team has been strengthened with hires such as chief marketing officer Lindsey Irvine in July 2026, and the company is building out a US presence around its New Jersey, Missouri, Kansas City and other sites. (StockAnalysis statistics; Q2 2026 shareholder letter.)

Capital allocation is the core governance question and the record is mixed. On the positive side, management sold equity aggressively when the stock was high: 12.7 million ATM shares at an average $223.60 in Q2 raised $2.8 billion, about 46% above today's price, and the $1.0 billion of equity sold in September 2025 alongside low-coupon converts was well timed. Prepayment-heavy contracts and asset-backed financing on investment-grade contracts reduce the amount of expensive capital required. On the negative side, the share count has grown 22% in a year and 12.3 million ATM shares remained available at June 30; the $3.5 billion Citi facility is a bridge with a one-year maturity, which means the company has deferred rather than solved its 2027 funding; and talent acquisitions (Eigen AI, Clarifai, Tavily) add SBC. Nebius does not pay a dividend and has not repurchased shares since its Yandex days. (StockAnalysis cash flow and statistics; Q2 2026 shareholder letter; Citi 6-K.)

Disclosure is better than many high-growth peers. Management publishes ARR, deal pricing per megawatt, payback periods and contracted power, and has reaffirmed or raised guidance at each report. What is missing is a contracted backlog or remaining performance obligation figure broken down by year, customer concentration percentages, and a clear statement of total committed capex and lease obligations, all of which are listed as open questions in the model.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

### What Bulls Believe

Bulls argue that Nebius is the best-run independent AI cloud, with better margins than CoreWeave, a cleaner balance sheet, a stronger software layer and two hyperscaler anchors. They point to ARR rising from $1.25 billion in December to $3.0 billion in June, a 1-year-10-month payback on new deals, and management's statement that it could sell its entire 2027 capacity today. On this view, $12 billion of FY2027 revenue at a 40-50% EBITDA margin makes the current $44 billion enterprise value look like less than 8x FY2027 EBITDA for a business still growing triple digits, and Bank of America's $310 target and Rosenblatt's $304 target reflect that.

### What Bears Believe

Bears argue that neoclouds are leveraged GPU landlords whose profits are a temporary scarcity rent. They point to the 364-day bridge, rising credit spreads, a 16% short interest, the extension of server useful lives, operating cash flow inflated by prepayments, and customer concentration in Microsoft, Meta and venture-funded labs. Rothschild & Co Redburn's Sell initiation and Musk's warning sum up the case: when supply catches up and funding tightens, the weakest players fail and the survivors' returns compress. Short sellers made about $6 billion betting against five neocloud and AI-infrastructure names in the recent drawdown, according to ORTEX data cited by TipRanks.

### What The Market May Be Missing

Two things look underappreciated. First, Nebius's funding mix is structurally better than "debt-funded neocloud" implies: more than half of the capex on recent deals is prepaid by customers, investment-grade contracts support cheap secured debt, and the company raised most of its equity at prices well above today's. Net debt was only $2.15 billion in June against $10.34 billion of equity, a very different profile from peers funded mainly with high-yield debt. Second, the non-core assets are real money. ClickHouse raised at a reported $15 billion valuation in January 2026 and Nebius retains a significant minority stake; Toloka is backed by Bezos Expeditions. Long-term investments are carried at $1.62 billion, and those assets are not in the model's scenario values.

What the bulls may be missing is that a business funded by prepayments must keep signing prepaid contracts to avoid a cash cliff, and that a one-year bridge in a tightening credit market is a real refinancing event. The variant view here is moderate: Nebius is more likely than most neoclouds to survive and thrive, but survival is already in the price and the path to the bull case runs through 2027 financing.

## 10. Bull Case

**Thesis:** Nebius becomes a durable, profitable AI cloud with more than 3 GW billing by FY2031.

**Mechanism:** Demand for training and inference keeps outrunning supply through 2028. Nebius deploys more than 1 GW a year from 2027 as planned, using its 5 GW contracted power pipeline, asset-light partners and prepaid contracts. New-generation GPUs (Blackwell, then Vera Rubin) are priced at or above today's $20-25 million per megawatt, and older-generation pricing holds up because inference demand absorbs it; Q2's 30% price increase on older GPUs supports that. **Valuation:** 9.5x FY2031 EBITDA of $22.8 billion gives $216.6 billion of enterprise value and $544.76 per share, 255% above today, or $305.30 discounted at 12.28%.

**What must be true:** Year-end 2026 ARR at or above $8 billion; 2027 capex funded without a dilutive equity raise below $150; renewal pricing on 2025-vintage GPUs within 20% of original contract rates.

## 11. Bear Case

**Thesis:** GPU rental pricing resets and funding dries up, leaving Nebius levered to depreciating hardware.

**Mechanism:** By 2028 NVIDIA supply catches up with demand, hyperscalers bring their own custom-chip capacity online, and AI labs consolidate as their own funding tightens. New-deal pricing falls toward $8-10 million per megawatt and renewals of 2025-2026 vintage capacity reprice sharply lower. Some venture-funded customers fail to renew or pay. Capacity deliveries slip because of power and construction delays, pushing revenue right while capex and interest are already committed. Revenue reaches only $14 billion in FY2031 at a 45% EBITDA margin.

**Funding path:** The Citi bridge is refinanced at higher cost, more ATM equity is sold at lower prices and the company slows the build. Net debt still reaches $18 billion and shares rise to 335 million, with most of the dilution below $100.

**Valuation:** 6.5x FY2031 EBITDA of $6.3 billion gives $41.0 billion of enterprise value and $68.51 per share, 55% below today and below the lowest analyst target of $84.

**Early signs:** ARR below $6.5 billion at year-end 2026; Q3 or Q4 equity issuance at or below $150; refinancing of the bridge at spreads above SOFR plus 4%; impairments on older GPU fleets; a large customer contract delayed or reduced.

## 12. Base Case

The most likely outcome is that Nebius executes roughly to plan but not perfectly. Year-end 2026 ARR lands in the lower half of the $7-9 billion range as some capacity slips into early 2027; FY2027 revenue comes in near $11.5-12 billion. Pricing on new-generation GPUs stays strong into 2028, while older capacity reprices to an average of about $15 million per megawatt across the fleet. The company reaches about 2 GW billing by FY2031, producing $30 billion of revenue at a 55% adjusted EBITDA margin. Funding is expensive but available: the bridge is termed out into a mix of secured and convertible debt, and the company sells another 50-60 million shares over five years. Net debt rises to $35 billion and diluted shares to 330 million.

On those assumptions, 8.5x FY2031 EBITDA of $16.5 billion gives an enterprise value of $140.3 billion and a value of $318.94 a share, more than double today's price. Discounted at 12.28%, that is worth $178.75 today, 17% above the current price. The base case therefore offers a reasonable but not exceptional return for the risk, which is why the stance is Watch with a positive bias rather than Buy.

### Primary Framework

FCF multiples cannot value Nebius because free cash flow is deeply negative by design and will remain so through at least FY2028. P/E fails for the same reason on the earnings line. EV/sales is the market's shorthand, but it ignores the tens of billions of capital still to be spent. The model instead values the company on FY2031 adjusted EBITDA, a year in which the build should be maturing, at exit EV/EBITDA multiples of 6.5-9.5x. Those multiples are deliberately set below the levels that AI-infrastructure stocks currently command because GPU assets depreciate over five years, so EBITDA overstates owner earnings by a large depreciation charge; an 8.5x EBITDA multiple on a business with D&A near 30% of revenue is roughly 24x unlevered after-tax operating income. Projected net debt and diluted shares carry the cost of funding the build into the per-share value.

The WACC sheet computes a cost of equity of 12.28% (4.978% 10-year Treasury, 1.46 beta, 5% equity risk premium) and a WACC of 10.88% using a 6.5% blended pre-tax cost of debt; StockAnalysis estimates 10.29%. The beta is arguably too low for a levered pre-profit infrastructure company because the five-year history includes the suspended Yandex period, which makes the discounted fair value, if anything, generous.

## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The biggest risk is a funding squeeze arriving before the capacity it funds starts billing. Nebius has committed to a capex plan of $20-25 billion in 2026 and more than a gigawatt a year from 2027, financed partly by a one-year bridge, in a credit market that its own CEO says has tightened. If refinancing costs rise or equity must be sold well below $150, per-share value falls even if every customer contract performs.

Pricing risk is a close second. Today's $20-25 million per megawatt deals reflect a supply-constrained market for the newest NVIDIA systems. Prior GPU generations have repriced sharply once supply caught up, and the extension of useful lives to five years means accounting depreciation could lag economic obsolescence.

Concentration and counterparty risk is material. Microsoft and Meta contracts total up to roughly $46 billion; AI labs such as Reflection and Cohere are venture-funded and their ability to pay depends on continued AI investment. Prepayments mitigate but do not eliminate this risk.

Execution risk covers power, construction and delivery. Nebius has delivered every Microsoft tranche to date, but it is building across many countries and states at once, and delays push revenue right while costs are committed.

Governance and dilution risk stems from Class B voting control and an active ATM program. Smaller risks include FX exposure on European operations, cash burn at Avride, regulatory scrutiny of AI data center power use, and residual reputational associations with the company's Yandex origins.

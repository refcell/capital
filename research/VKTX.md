# Viking Therapeutics, Inc. (NASDAQ: VKTX) Investment Research

Date: 2026-09-25

Current stance: Watch / Speculative  

This is investment research, not personalized financial advice.

## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

Viking Therapeutics is a pivotal-stage biotechnology company whose value is overwhelmingly tied to VK2735, a dual GLP-1/GIP agonist being developed in injectable and oral formulations for obesity. The company has no product revenue, negative $534.8 million of TTM net income, and negative $418.4 million of TTM operating cash flow. Those figures are not evidence of a broken commercial business; they are the cost of running an increasingly broad late-stage clinical program before approval. Conventional P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA, and FCF-multiple analysis is therefore inapplicable. The relevant questions are how much cash Viking has, how quickly it will consume that cash, how much dilution remains, and what probability-adjusted value belongs to VK2735 and the rest of the pipeline.

The September 22 maintenance-study readout strengthened the scientific case. In a 180-subject dose-ranging study, weekly VK2735 produced 21.7% absolute and 22.0% placebo-adjusted weight loss at week 33 in the 17.5 mg cohort, with no observed plateau. Subjects moved from weekly induction to every-other-week dosing retained 83%-97% of initial weight loss, while monthly dosing retained 82%-90%, versus 61% for subjects switched to placebo. Common gastrointestinal adverse events during maintenance were broadly comparable with placebo. These are unusually attractive results because less-frequent dosing could improve persistence, lower drug consumption, and give Viking a commercial point of differentiation in a market increasingly populated by effective molecules.

The evidence is not yet definitive. The maintenance trial was small, its treatment groups were much smaller than a pivotal study, full data were not available when management spoke, and cross-trial comparisons with tirzepatide, semaglutide, or retatrutide are inherently unreliable. VANQUISH-1 and VANQUISH-2 are fully enrolled and are expected to report in 2027. Those studies—not the 180-subject maintenance study—will determine whether VK2735 has approvable efficacy and safety in obesity and obesity with type 2 diabetes. The oral program adds strategic value but also adds execution cost and another clinical binary.

The September 24 financing is the second crucial development. Viking priced 7.857 million common shares at $35 and $225 million of 2.00% convertible notes due 2032. Estimated net proceeds are $258.2 million and $218.0 million, respectively, before over-allotments. Starting from $501.7 million of June cash and investments, gross pro-forma liquidity approaches $978 million, while pro-forma debt rises to about $229 million. The transaction substantially extends runway, but it also lifts basic shares to roughly 124.5 million before a potential 1.179 million-share greenshoe and before the notes' potential conversion at approximately $50.75. The market sold the stock down 11.8% on September 24 because the financing converted part of the clinical upside into certain dilution and debt.

### Current View

VKTX is a Watch rather than a core holding. The probability-weighted model produces $53.30 per share: $8 bear, $52 base, and $110 bull, weighted 30%, 45%, and 25%. That is 45% above the current price, but the expected value is created by a wide outcome distribution rather than by a stable compounding engine. The bear case is severe because a failed pivotal program would leave cash, residual pipeline options, and a much smaller strategic premium. The bull case requires both clinical success and a credible commercialization or partnership path.

The next action-worthy evidence is not another analyst target. It is full maintenance-study disclosure, Phase 3 execution, oral-program design, and management's post-financing cash runway. The October 21 estimated earnings update should clarify spending and timing, but the 2027 VANQUISH readouts remain the principal value event.

## 2. Company Story

Viking was incorporated in 2012 and completed its IPO in April 2015. It was built around therapies for metabolic and endocrine disorders rather than around a single platform technology. That history matters because the company has repeatedly used selective receptor pharmacology to pursue large metabolic markets while retaining smaller programs that can validate its drug-development discipline.

The company profile still highlights VK2809, an oral thyroid hormone receptor beta agonist for metabolic liver disease, VK0214 for X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy, and earlier programs such as VK5211. The center of gravity has nevertheless shifted decisively toward obesity. VK2735 is now in two injectable Phase 3 trials, and Viking plans two oral Phase 3 trials. VK3019, an amylin receptor agonist, adds another obesity mechanism and gives Viking an option on combination therapy or a second franchise. The September financing release explicitly prioritized VK2735 and VK3019, confirming where management intends to allocate incremental capital.

The shift is economically rational. Obesity is one of the largest pharmaceutical opportunities in the world, but it also demands larger trials, expensive manufacturing, device capability, payer evidence, and commercial scale. Viking has moved from being a small research organization with 65 employees into a company attempting a pivotal program against Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk. Its scientific progress is real; its institutional transition is only beginning.

## 3. Business Model

Viking does not yet have a commercial business model in the accounting sense. It has no product revenue, no gross profit, and no recurring customer economics. Its current model is to raise capital, advance drug candidates through clinical trials, preserve intellectual property, and create value through approval, partnership, licensing, or acquisition. Investors fund R&D today for a probabilistic claim on future drug cash flows.

The economic engine is therefore milestone conversion. Each positive trial converts a portion of scientific uncertainty into a more valuable and more financeable asset. Each delay or failure destroys option value while leaving most historical R&D expense unrecoverable. The central unit of analysis is not revenue per patient today but probability of technical and regulatory success, time to launch, addressable treated patients, net price, manufacturing cost, and the diluted share count needed to reach commercialization.

VK2735 could support several revenue pools. Weekly injectable induction addresses initial weight loss. Every-other-week or monthly maintenance could address long-term persistence with lower dose intensity. An oral formulation could expand the addressable population among injection-averse patients and potentially improve primary-care adoption. Those uses are connected but not interchangeable. A successful injectable does not guarantee a successful oral formulation, and excellent weight maintenance does not by itself guarantee payer coverage.

The company's near-term economics are cash-consumption economics. TTM operating cash flow was negative $418.4 million, up sharply from negative $278.7 million in FY2025 and negative $87.8 million in FY2024. Stock-based compensation contributed $37.4 million TTM and $40.8 million in FY2025, so part of reported expense is non-cash but economically dilutive. The September financing adds roughly $476 million of net proceeds, equivalent to a little more than one year of the current TTM operating deficit before any increase in Phase 3 or commercial-preparation spending.

## 4. Products, Segments, And Moat

VK2735 is a dual agonist of GLP-1 and GIP receptors. The dual mechanism is intended to reduce appetite, body weight, and glucose while improving insulin sensitivity. Viking attributes part of its tolerability profile to a slower time to peak exposure and extended half-life. The injectable program consists of VANQUISH-1 in adults with obesity and VANQUISH-2 in adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes. Both were described as fully enrolled on September 22, with results expected in 2027.

The maintenance study broadens the target product profile. After 21 weeks of weekly induction, subjects were transitioned to weekly, every-other-week, monthly, or placebo regimens for 12 weeks. Retaining 83%-97% of initial loss under every-other-week dosing and 82%-90% under monthly dosing is clinically interesting because real-world GLP-1 persistence is limited by tolerability, inconvenience, cost, and coverage. If one product can induce loss and then maintain it at lower frequency without retitration, Viking may have a differentiated lifecycle-management strategy.

The oral formulation could be commercially important because obesity treatment will probably segment by efficacy, convenience, cost, and patient preference. Management plans two oral Phase 3 studies that mirror VANQUISH populations, while leveraging the injectable safety database. That should make the studies smaller than the injectable pivotal program, but it does not remove formulation-specific efficacy and tolerability risk.

The moat is still prospective. Intellectual property, pharmacokinetics, a potentially favorable efficacy/tolerability balance, and a less-frequent maintenance label could make VK2735 valuable. None gives Viking the manufacturing, payer, physician, or distribution advantages enjoyed by Lilly and Novo. Those incumbents can run head-to-head studies, negotiate broad coverage, invest billions in capacity, and bundle evidence across cardiometabolic outcomes. Viking's best strategic moat may be to produce sufficiently differentiated data that a larger company values the asset more highly than public markets do.

The competitive comparison requires discipline. Management noted that currently approved GLP-1/GIP agents have produced roughly 14% placebo-adjusted loss at week 33 and an advanced triple agonist roughly 17% at that point. The 22% VK2735 result looks stronger, but it came from a different study with different titration, eligibility, size, and missing-data mechanics. It is a hypothesis-generating comparison, not proof of superiority.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

The appropriate assessment is “clean clinical-stage losses, with accelerating burn.” Viking's negative earnings are not overstated operating deterioration because there is no commercial operation to deteriorate. They are mostly R&D and corporate spending required to reach pivotal data. Net income is slightly less negative than operating income because interest income on cash offsets part of the burn. That benefit will fluctuate with balances and interest rates.

Cash flow is the more useful measure. TTM OCF and reported FCF were both negative $418.4 million because capital expenditure was negligible. The near equality confirms that the cash deficit is caused by clinical operations, not by a separate fixed-asset build. In a pre-revenue biotech, negative FCF is expected; the rate of change matters. Burn increased by roughly $140 million from FY2025 OCF to the TTM period and by more than $330 million from FY2024. Phase 3 activity explains the direction, but investors still need to know whether annual burn stabilizes near $500 million or rises toward $600-$700 million with oral trials and commercial preparation.

Stock-based compensation is a real economic cost. The $37.4 million TTM add-back improves cash flow relative to GAAP loss, but awards expand the fully diluted claim on a future product. Share issuance has already been recurrent: common-stock issuance generated $654.6 million in FY2024, $76.4 million in FY2025, and $111.3 million TTM before the September deal. The new offering makes dilution explicit rather than hidden.

The earnings quality risk is not accounting manipulation; it is false precision. Quarterly EPS beats or misses are largely spending-timing differences. FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of negative $4.61 from 17 analysts is useful as a burn expectation, not as a valuation denominator. Investors should focus on trial enrollment, readout timing, cash commitments, SBC, and share count.

## 7. Forward Projections And Scenario Model

Analyst targets often use product-level risk-adjusted NPV with assumptions not visible to public readers. They can be useful as a calibration range, but they are not independent evidence of clinical success.

### Reverse Valuation

At $36.75 and roughly $6 per share of pro-forma net cash, investors pay about $30.75 per share for pipeline value. On 124.5 million pro-forma basic shares, that is approximately $3.83 billion of implied pipeline value. The stock is therefore not “cash-backed” in the conventional downside-protection sense. A clinical failure can erase most of the optionality premium even if cash remains.

The current price sits near the analyst low target, which could imply that financing fully reset expectations. It could also imply that the market applies a much higher clinical-failure probability than analysts. The reverse-valuation question is whether a pre-approval obesity asset with strong Phase 2/maintenance data, two fully enrolled pivotal trials, and oral optionality is worth nearly $4 billion after adjusting for its cash. That is defensible but not obviously cheap.

### Projection Takeaway

The scenario distribution is more informative than the expected value. Investors can earn attractive returns if VK2735 replicates, but the path contains a 70%-80% plausible drawdown under failure. The appropriate position size, if any, must reflect that asymmetry. The base case is above the current quote but far below consensus because Viking still must convert efficacy into approval, supply, coverage, and durable per-share economics.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Brian Lian is President, CEO, and a director; Gregory Zante is CFO; Hubert Chen is CMO. The team has advanced VK2735 from early clinical development into two fully enrolled Phase 3 studies, which is meaningful execution evidence. Rapid enrollment suggests investigator and patient interest, although it does not predict efficacy.

Capital allocation is now the central governance issue. The September transaction was rational because financing after positive data and before a pivotal binary reduces insolvency and bargaining risk. It was also expensive: common equity was sold at $35 immediately after the stock surged on clinical data, and the convertible introduces a second dilution path. Management chose certainty over waiting for 2027 data. That decision protects the program but transfers part of success economics to new shareholders and noteholders.

Use of proceeds is broad—VK2735, VK3019, other R&D, working capital, and general corporate purposes. Investors should demand a detailed bridge from pro-forma liquidity to expected pivotal readouts and launch readiness. A good capital plan would identify committed trial costs, manufacturing deposits, oral-program spend, pre-commercial hiring, and minimum liquidity through regulatory submission. A weak plan would treat the financing as permission to expand every program simultaneously.

Strategic alternatives matter. Viking could commercialize alone, license regional rights, form a global co-development partnership, or sell the company. An independent launch maximizes gross economics but requires the most capital and capabilities. A partnership reduces funding and manufacturing risk but sacrifices economics. Given incumbent scale and the size of the obesity market, partnership or acquisition may maximize risk-adjusted per-share value.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

### What Bulls Believe

Bulls believe VK2735 may combine efficacy near the leading edge of obesity therapy with a cleaner titration and differentiated maintenance schedule. They focus on 22% placebo-adjusted loss at 33 weeks, no plateau, rapid enrollment, and maintenance with doses as low as 5 mg every other week. They also argue that monthly maintenance can improve persistence and reduce payer cost, which may matter more commercially than winning a narrow cross-trial efficacy comparison.

Bulls see the financing as de-risking rather than dilution. Nearly $1 billion of pro-forma liquidity gives Viking time to produce pivotal data and negotiate from strength. A buyer would acquire a funded program rather than inherit an immediate financing need. The $94.61 analyst average and $125 high target reinforce the view that public markets discount strategic value.

### What Bears Believe

Bears believe the small maintenance study created an unjustified sense of certainty. They expect efficacy to regress in larger, more diverse Phase 3 populations and note that full results were not yet available when management reported topline data. Cross-trial comparisons omit differences in trial design, and established competitors have cardiovascular-outcomes evidence, manufacturing scale, and payer relationships that Viking lacks.

Bears also focus on burn and dilution. TTM OCF deteriorated to negative $418 million, and two oral pivotal studies will add cost. The equity offering expands basic shares by 6.7% before the greenshoe; full common over-allotment would push the increase toward 7.7%. The notes can add roughly 4.4 million shares at initial conversion terms. Success may be valuable at the company level but less valuable per share than naïve pre-financing models assume.

### What The Market May Be Missing

The market may underappreciate maintenance as a supply and payer feature. If patients can preserve most weight loss with much less drug, annual dose consumption can fall, capacity can serve more patients, and total treatment cost can decline. That could make a less-frequent label economically important even if induction efficacy is merely competitive.

Conversely, the market may underappreciate that low-frequency dosing could reduce revenue per patient unless pricing preserves economics. Better persistence may offset lower dose intensity, but the net effect depends on label, pricing, payer behavior, and manufacturing cost. Maintenance is not automatically more profitable.

## 10. Bull Case

The bull case begins with replication. VANQUISH-1 and VANQUISH-2 confirm high-teens or better placebo-adjusted loss with manageable GI tolerability, low adverse-event discontinuation, and no unexpected class-specific safety imbalance. The 17.5 mg dose remains the efficacy/tolerability sweet spot. Longer duration shows continued loss or durable plateau rather than rebound.

The maintenance extension then validates every-other-week and monthly regimens, giving VK2735 a differentiated label. Payers accept maintenance as a cost-control tool, clinicians use flexible dosing to retain patients, and real-world persistence exceeds weekly-only therapies. Oral VK2735 reaches competitive efficacy and broadens primary-care use.

Commercial execution is solved through a partner or buyer with manufacturing capacity. Viking avoids a multibillion-dollar independent infrastructure build and receives economics that preserve substantial upside. VK3019 and VK2809 add option value rather than consuming disproportionate capital. Under this path, $110 per share is reasonable and remains below the highest analyst target.

## 11. Bear Case

The bear mechanism is clinical regression, not revenue recession. Larger studies show less efficacy than the maintenance cohort, greater discontinuation, or a safety issue that narrows the label. A delay extends burn and gives competitors time to add oral agents, combination products, and flexible maintenance evidence. Oral VK2735 fails to reproduce injectable exposure or tolerability.

Cash then becomes a melting asset. Even after financing, $978 million of gross liquidity covers only about 2.3 years at unchanged TTM burn, and burn could rise. Another raise occurs at a depressed price. The convertible remains debt if the share price stays below conversion, creating a fixed claim on shrinking cash. The market assigns little value to VK2735 and only modest value to the remaining pipeline. The resulting $8 target is above a pure liquidation floor but far below today's option premium.

## 12. Base Case

The most likely path lies between scientific triumph and failure. VK2735 succeeds in one formulation with competitive efficacy, but Phase 3 results are less dramatic than the 180-subject maintenance study. Tolerability is acceptable rather than uniquely clean. Maintenance flexibility survives development but requires further study or receives a narrower label than bulls expect.

Viking partners the asset because independent manufacturing and commercialization would demand too much capital. The partner absorbs launch-scale spending in exchange for economics. Oral development creates additional value but arrives later. Shares continue to rise through SBC and financing, though not catastrophically. This produces $52 per share: meaningful upside, but not the near-tripling implied by the analyst average.

## 14. Catalysts

The closest scheduled catalyst is the estimated October 21, 2026 earnings report. It should clarify June-to-September cash use, financing settlement, oral Phase 3 timing, maintenance-extension design, and 2027 guidance. It is primarily an execution update rather than a thesis-settling event.

Full maintenance-study data at a medical meeting would test the topline narrative. Investors should examine cohort sizes, discontinuations, estimand, missing-data handling, body composition, individual dose response, and adverse events beyond aggregate GI categories. A clean detailed presentation would raise confidence; unexplained exclusions or imbalances would lower it.

The pivotal VANQUISH readouts expected in 2027 are the decisive catalysts. VANQUISH-1 tests obesity, while VANQUISH-2 adds type 2 diabetes, a population where weight-loss response and safety can differ. Positive data in both would substantially de-risk approval. The oral Phase 3 starts and enrollment pace are secondary but strategically important.

## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The biggest risk is that small-study efficacy and tolerability do not replicate at pivotal scale. Biotech history is full of attractive early data that weakened in broader populations. An obesity program also faces class-wide safety scrutiny and very high expectations set by approved drugs.

Financing risk remains despite the new capital. Pro-forma liquidity is large in absolute terms but modest relative to multiple Phase 3 studies, manufacturing commitments, and launch preparation. Equity, greenshoe, SBC, and notes can all expand the share denominator. If pivotal data disappoint, the notes become debt supported by a shrinking cash pool rather than low-cost growth capital.

Competition is unusually strong. Lilly and Novo have approved products, outcomes evidence, supply investment, and global commercial infrastructure. Roche and other developers are advancing next-generation candidates. Viking must differentiate on more than cross-trial weight loss.

Manufacturing and device execution are underdisclosed. Producing peptide therapy at scale, securing fill-finish capacity, maintaining quality, and supporting both injectable and oral formulations are not trivial. A commercial label without supply would not create modeled revenue.

## 16. What Would Change My Mind

I would become more bullish if full maintenance data confirm the topline without missing-data concerns; VANQUISH efficacy remains near leading agents with lower discontinuation; management demonstrates financing runway through pivotal readouts and submission; oral exposure and tolerability are competitive; and a credible partner solves manufacturing without surrendering most economics.

I would become more bearish if 2027 timing slips; annual cash burn exceeds $600 million without proportional milestone progress; the oral program requires larger or longer studies than described; body-composition data reveal excessive lean-mass loss; Phase 3 discontinuation or serious adverse events exceed peers; or management launches another large financing before pivotal data despite the September raise.

## 18. Conclusion

Viking has produced genuinely encouraging obesity data and now has enough capital to reach the most important near-term clinical milestones. The strongest element of the story is not simply 22% weight loss; it is the possibility that the same drug can support induction and then maintain most weight loss with every-other-week or monthly dosing and placebo-like maintenance tolerability. That could matter to patients, payers, and manufacturing economics.

The stock remains speculative. The current price assigns almost $4 billion to pipeline optionality after pro-forma net cash, while Phase 3 replication, oral development, manufacturing, and commercialization remain unresolved. The September financing improved survival but diluted the success case. A $53.30 probability-weighted value supports Watch with upside, not a high-conviction Buy.

The main reason to own VKTX would be belief that maintenance flexibility and Phase 3 efficacy will create a strategically valuable obesity franchise. The main reason to avoid it is the asymmetric loss if pivotal data disappoint while cash burn and dilution continue. The next meaningful trigger is detailed maintenance disclosure and the October cash-runway update, followed by the 2027 VANQUISH readouts.

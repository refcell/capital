## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

Xencor has spent two decades as the antibody-engineering supplier behind other companies' medicines. Its Xtend half-life technology is inside Alexion's Ultomiris, its Fc engineering is in Incyte's Monjuvi, and its XmAb platform has been licensed widely. The equity, however, has usually traded as a cash box with a royalty stream attached, because Xencor's own wholly owned drugs kept failing to produce data that moved a market. That changed on October 25, 2026. At ESMO, Xencor reported that XmAb819, an ENPP3 x CD3 bispecific T-cell engager, produced a 45.5% confirmed objective response rate and an 82% six-month progression-free survival estimate at its chosen 400 µg/kg dose in heavily pretreated clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC). The stock, already up sharply into the meeting, sits at $29.99 against a 52-week low of $10.38.

The surface narrative is simple: a platform company finally has its own solid-tumor asset with registrational potential. The real question is narrower and harder. A T-cell engager working in a solid tumor is rare; the field is littered with bispecifics that showed early responses and then foundered on durability, cytokine release syndrome, step-up dosing logistics, or Phase 3 comparators. The confirmed response rate that underpins the new valuation comes from 11 efficacy-evaluable patients. Meanwhile the funding base that carried Xencor through prior failures is shrinking. A July 2026 settlement with Alexion replaced U.S. Ultomiris royalties with two $52.5 million payments, and OMERS keeps up to $35 million a year of the remaining ex-U.S. royalties through 2028. Xencor is moving from a royalty-supported research company to a cash-burning, single-asset development story at precisely the moment the stock prices in success.

At $29.99, the market pays $5.31 per share for net cash and roughly $24.68 per share, or about $1.83 billion, for the pipeline and platform. That premium is not absurd if XmAb819 becomes a standard later-line ccRCC therapy, but it already absorbs a large share of a plausible base case. This report's probability-weighted fair value is $34.71, about 16% above the current price, with a very wide $8-$65 range.

### The Core Debate

The investment debate is whether the XmAb819 dataset is the beginning of a durable franchise or the high-water mark of a small Phase 1 cohort. Bulls see a first-in-class target, a mechanism that can generate deep responses in a tumor where checkpoint inhibitors and VEGF tyrosine kinase inhibitors have plateaued, and a wholly owned asset that Xencor does not have to share. Bears see a small denominator, a demanding weekly priming regimen, a tumor type where durable benefit is what matters for approval and adoption, and a company that will need more capital before any product revenue arrives.

### Current View

The current view is Watch. The ESMO data justify much of the re-rating from the low teens, and a 30%-of-float short position could amplify further upside on clean follow-up data. But the base-case sum of parts is $32.66, only about 9% above the price, and the bear case of $8.21 would follow a disappointing durability update or a tolerability problem in the larger registrational population. The stock becomes ownable at a meaningfully lower price for the same evidence, or at a similar price once more mature PFS data, a clear FDA path, and a funded Phase 3 plan exist.

## 2. Company Story

### Origin And Evolution

Xencor was founded in 1997 in Pasadena, California, around computational protein design, the idea that amino-acid substitutions in an antibody's structure could be modeled and engineered rather than discovered by trial and error. Its commercial breakthrough was the Fc domain, the "tail" of the antibody that governs half-life and immune-cell engagement. Two engineered Fc families became the company's franchise. Xtend extends antibody half-life by improving recycling through the neonatal Fc receptor, and cytotoxic Fc variants increase immune-cell killing. Xencor's bispecific XmAb format, which lets a single molecule bind two targets while retaining a normal antibody structure, followed.

The licensing strategy worked scientifically. Alexion used Xtend to build Ultomiris, the longer-acting successor to Soliris, which converted Soliris patients to less frequent dosing and became a multibillion-dollar product. Incyte and MorphoSys used Xencor's engineered Fc in tafasitamab, sold as Monjuvi. Royalties from those products, plus milestones from a long list of partners, kept Xencor funded. Revenue peaked at $275.1 million in FY2021, when milestones were heavy, and the company was briefly profitable.

The wholly owned pipeline was less successful. Over the last five years Xencor terminated or wound down several oncology bispecifics, and the stock steadily lost credibility; the 10-Q still shows "wind down costs of terminated programs" in 2025 and 2026. Revenue has since fallen to $104.97 million on a trailing basis as milestones dried up. In 2023 Xencor sold portions of its Ultomiris and Monjuvi royalties to OMERS for upfront cash, and in 2024 it raised $209 million of equity. The company pivoted toward two franchises: T-cell engagers for solid tumors and autoimmune disease, and an inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) portfolio built around TL1A.

### Why The Company Matters Now

Xencor matters now because XmAb819 is the first wholly owned asset in years whose data look registrational. ENPP3 is highly expressed in ccRCC and relatively restricted in normal tissue, which makes it an unusually attractive target for a T-cell engager. If a bispecific can produce deep, durable responses in post-checkpoint, post-TKI kidney cancer, it opens a new treatment class in a disease where the standard sequence has not changed much in years.

The company also matters because its funding model just broke. Alexion took the position in March 2026 that it did not owe additional royalties on U.S. Ultomiris sales after Xencor announced a new Xtend patent extending the expected royalty term to December 2028. The July 29 settlement paid Xencor $105 million and ended U.S. Ultomiris royalties entirely. The deal converts an uncertain stream into certain cash, but it also means Xencor's future is now tied almost entirely to its own pipeline.

## 3. Business Model

### How The Company Makes Money

Xencor currently earns revenue from three sources: royalties on partner products, milestones when partners hit clinical or regulatory events, and occasional license fees. In Q2 2026, revenue of $51.2 million consisted of $41.2 million of royalties and a $10.0 million milestone from Zenas BioPharma, which triggered on the U.S. BLA submission for obexelimab in IgG4-related disease. The royalty figure was inflated by a $27.6 million catch-up on U.S. Ultomiris sales during the dispute period, recognized once the settlement resolved the uncertainty. By partner, Alexion contributed $37.3 million, Incyte $3.9 million and Zenas $10.0 million.

The quality of that revenue is lower than the headline suggests. Part of the royalty income is non-cash: under the OMERS agreements, some royalties flow directly to OMERS to service the royalty liability, so Xencor books revenue it never receives in cash. In H1 2026, non-cash royalty revenue was $17.2 million. The Monjuvi royalty sale reached its $29.3 million cap in Q2 2026, so Monjuvi royalties now accrue to Xencor, but they are small, about $0.8 million in the post-cap portion of the quarter. Ex-U.S. Ultomiris royalties continue, but OMERS receives up to $35 million of them each year from 2026 through 2028 and up to $12 million a year after that.

### The Economic Engine

The economic engine is the conversion of protein-engineering know-how into either partner economics or wholly owned drugs. Historically, the partner route generated cash with little capital risk but capped the upside: Xencor's royalty rates on Ultomiris and Monjuvi were modest, and it never controlled commercialization. The wholly owned route keeps the full economics but requires Xencor to fund Phase 3 trials and, eventually, a commercial organization. The XmAb819 data push the company firmly toward the second model, and the end of U.S. Ultomiris royalties removes the cushion that made the first model comfortable.

The cost structure reflects that shift. R&D was $136.6 million in H1 2026, up from $120.2 million a year earlier, with external program spending concentrated in XmAb942 ($21.8 million), XmAb819 ($16.9 million), XmAb412 ($10.1 million) and XmAb541 ($8.5 million). G&A runs about $65-70 million a year. Trailing free cash flow was -$208.3 million, and consensus expects about -$177 million of FCF in FY2026 including the settlement inflow.

### Unit Economics

For a clinical-stage company, unit economics are best expressed as cost per milestone. Xencor is spending roughly $50-60 million a quarter of operating cash to advance six clinical programs. The October 26 investor deck lays out a 2027 calendar that includes the start of a registrational XmAb819 study, XENITH-UC Phase 2b results for XmAb942 in the second half, and data updates for plamotamab, XmAb657 and the XmAb541/XmAb808 combination. The question is whether the company prioritizes. A registrational ccRCC study is likely to be the largest single commitment Xencor has ever made, and funding it alongside a TL1A Phase 3 decision would strain a balance sheet guided to $420-$440 million at year-end 2026.

## 4. Products, Segments, And Moat

### Segment Overview

Xencor reports a single segment, but economically it has four parts. The first is the royalty and milestone portfolio, which is shrinking but still includes ex-U.S. Ultomiris above the OMERS cap, Monjuvi, and a potentially meaningful obexelimab royalty if Zenas wins approval in IgG4-RD; Zenas is eligible to pay up to $450 million of regulatory and sales milestones and mid-single to mid-teen percentage royalties. The second is XmAb819 in oncology. The third is the IBD franchise led by XmAb942, an Xtend-engineered anti-TL1A antibody, and XmAb412, a TL1A x IL-23p19 bispecific. The fourth is a set of earlier programs: XmAb541 (CLDN6 x CD3) with XmAb808 (B7-H3 x CD28) in ovarian cancer, and the B-cell-depleting T-cell engagers plamotamab (CD20 x CD3) and XmAb657 (CD19 x CD3) in rheumatoid arthritis and other autoimmune diseases.

### Competitive Position

In ccRCC, XmAb819's competitive position rests on being first in class. The treatment backbone is checkpoint inhibition combined with a VEGF TKI in first line, with belzutifan and later TKIs used after progression. Response rates in third-line ccRCC are typically low, and median PFS is measured in months. A T-cell engager producing 45%-55% response rates with most patients still on treatment would be differentiated in that setting. The risk is that others are pursuing ENPP3 and other renal targets with antibody-drug conjugates and cell therapies that may be easier to administer.

In TL1A, Xencor is a late entrant. Merck's tulisokibart, Roche's afimkibart and Sanofi/Teva's duvakitug are in or near Phase 3. XmAb942's argument is that Xtend can enable dosing every 8-12 weeks or less often, an attractive convenience edge in chronic IBD maintenance, but convenience rarely overcomes a two- to three-year head start unless efficacy is at least comparable. XENITH-UC is the test.

### Moat Assessment

Xencor's moat is engineering know-how and a patent estate around Fc modifications, proven by the number of partner products that use it. That moat generates licensing value but does not protect any single wholly owned drug from competition; a TL1A antibody can be built without Xencor's Fc. For XmAb819, protection will come from first-mover clinical data and composition-of-matter patents, not the platform. The Alexion dispute is also a reminder that platform royalties can be contested by large partners with better resources. The platform moat is real, but the equity value now depends on asset-specific execution.

### Latest Period

Q2 2026 revenue rose to $51.2 million from $43.6 million a year earlier, but only because of the $27.6 million Ultomiris catch-up and the $10.0 million Zenas milestone. Six-month revenue was $55.7 million, down from $76.3 million. R&D rose 17% year over year to $71.9 million in the quarter, led by XmAb942. ### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is strong for a clinical-stage company but not as strong as the headline cash figure implies. Cash and investments totaled $565.0 million at June 30, including $78.6 million of marketable equity securities whose value moves with partner stocks. The OMERS royalty liability of $104.9 million is non-recourse and is serviced from royalties rather than corporate cash, but it accretes at a high effective rate: $12.0 million of non-cash interest in H1 2026 on an average balance near $112 million, roughly 21% annualized. Lease liabilities add $65.2 million. Equity was $503.2 million, or $6.77 per share, with an accumulated deficit of $946.6 million. The second $52.5 million Alexion settlement installment, due after July 2027, is an additional source of cash not yet on the balance sheet as a receivable in full.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Xencor's earnings quality is noisy, and the reported net loss understates the economic burn. Three distortions matter.

First, investment gains flatter net income. Trailing net loss of $163.3 million compares with an operating loss of $216.1 million; the gap is mostly gains on investments, $63.9 million on a trailing basis and $105.0 million in FY2025. These come from marketable equity holdings received as partner consideration and from securities sales. They are real assets but not recurring operating income, and they reverse; H1 2026 included a $33.9 million mark-to-market loss.

Second, revenue includes one-time and non-cash items. The $27.6 million Q2 Ultomiris catch-up was earned over the dispute period and will not recur, and U.S. Ultomiris royalties are now gone. The non-cash royalty revenue tied to OMERS, $17.2 million in H1 2026, is matched by non-cash interest expense on the royalty liability, so it inflates both revenue and interest without affecting cash. FY2027 consensus revenue of $121.0 million, down from $148.8 million expected for FY2026, already reflects part of this normalization.

Third, stock-based compensation of $39.0 million on a trailing basis and 5.2% year-over-year share growth are recurring economic costs. Operating cash flow of -$205.8 million is the cleanest measure of burn and is consistent with operating losses after adding back SBC and adjusting for working-capital timing.

The overall assessment is noisy but not overstated in a misleading way: the operating loss and cash burn are transparent, and the distortions come from accounting for royalty sales and investment marks rather than aggressive revenue recognition. Investors should anchor on operating cash burn of roughly $200 million a year and treat GAAP EPS as uninformative.

### Forecast Logic

The model values Xencor as a sum of parts. Net cash of $394.9 million comes first. The second Alexion installment is discounted at 12% for about 0.8 years. A royalty and milestone portfolio is valued at $150-$280 million, reflecting ex-U.S. Ultomiris royalties above the OMERS cap, small Monjuvi royalties, and an obexelimab royalty and milestone stream contingent on approval. XmAb819 is valued as peak sales multiplied by probability of success and by an NPV-to-peak-sales multiple of 2.0-2.5x, which embeds remaining development and launch costs and a 12% discount rate. XmAb942, the other pipeline programs and platform licensing are valued as lump sums. Corporate overhead not attributable to programs is capitalized as a negative. The total is divided by a scenario-specific diluted share count to reflect likely equity raises.

### Reverse Valuation

At $29.99, the market pays $24.68 per share, or about $1.83 billion, above net cash. If other programs, royalties and overhead roughly offset, that premium is effectively the market's rNPV for XmAb819 plus XmAb942. Using the base-case 2.5x NPV-to-peak multiple and $300 million for XmAb942, the market is implying roughly $1.5 billion of XmAb819 rNPV, or about $600 million of probability-weighted peak sales, for example a 40% chance at $1.5 billion of peak sales, or a 30% chance at $2.0 billion, before any additional dilution. That is a reasonable expectation for a first-in-class, later-line ccRCC therapy, which is why the stock is not obviously expensive. But it leaves little cushion for a dilutive raise, a durability disappointment, or a TL1A miss.

### Takeaway

The forward model says the ESMO re-rating was justified but has largely run its course. The upside case requires XmAb819 to expand into earlier lines or other ENPP3-expressing tumors, or XmAb942 to succeed in a crowded class. The downside case does not require outright failure: a small decline in the response rate on longer follow-up, a tolerability signal in a larger population, or a slower path to approval would push value toward cash plus residual royalties.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Bassil Dahiyat, Ph.D., co-founded Xencor and has led it as President and CEO for most of its history. His record is a mix of scientific credibility and capital-allocation frustration. The platform he built is widely licensed, but the company spent heavily on wholly owned oncology programs that were later discontinued, and wind-down costs still appear in 2026. The pivot to TL1A and autoimmune T-cell engagers in 2024-2025 was strategically sensible, and XmAb819 now validates the oncology bet, but investors have reason to watch spending discipline closely.

Capital allocation decisions over the past three years have favored liquidity over per-share value. The 2023 OMERS royalty sale raised $192.5 million at a high implied cost of capital, as shown by the roughly 21% effective accretion rate on the remaining liability. The 2024 equity raise of $209 million was done at much lower prices than today. Share count has grown from 59.4 million at the end of FY2021 to 74.35 million, about 25% over four and a half years, and SBC of $39 million a year continues. The Alexion settlement is the best recent decision: converting a disputed, partly OMERS-encumbered royalty into $105 million of certain cash.

The next major capital-allocation decision is how to fund registrational XmAb819 development. Management can raise equity on strength now, partner XmAb819 or XmAb942, or rely on the runway through 2028 and raise later. With the stock near $30 and short interest high, an equity raise would be the least dilutive option it has had in years, and investors should not be surprised by one. Institutional ownership is 88.3% and insiders own only 1.2%, which reduces alignment but is typical for a long-public biotech.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

### What Bulls Believe

Bulls believe XmAb819 is the first T-cell engager to work convincingly in ccRCC and that the target's restricted normal-tissue expression gives it a safety window competitors lack. They note that response rates rose with dose, that the 400 µg/kg cohort had most patients still on treatment, and that management plans to discuss an accelerated approval path using sub-group efficacy. They also see a second option in XmAb942, a growing autoimmune T-cell-engager portfolio, and royalty optionality from obexelimab. With short interest above 30% of float, bulls expect squeezes on any additional positive data.

### What Bears Believe

Bears focus on the denominator: the headline confirmed response rate comes from 11 patients. They point to the long history of solid-tumor T-cell engagers that looked promising in Phase 1 and failed to deliver durable benefit, and to the logistics of weekly priming in community oncology. They see Xencor's capital needs rising just as royalty income falls, and they expect a large equity raise. In TL1A, they view XmAb942 as a me-too in a class where larger competitors are years ahead.

### What The Market May Be Missing

The market may be underestimating how much the business model has changed. Until 2026, the royalty stream let Xencor absorb clinical failures without existential dilution. With U.S. Ultomiris royalties gone and ex-U.S. royalties largely committed to OMERS through 2028, the company's per-share value is now much more sensitive to clinical outcomes and to the price at which it raises capital. That cuts both ways: success accrues fully to shareholders, but failure leaves less of a floor. The more subtle variant view is that the best risk-adjusted entry may come after an equity raise, when supply is absorbed and the stock trades closer to the base-case value per diluted share.

## 10. Bull Case

The bull case is that XmAb819's activity holds as the 400 µg/kg cohort matures, durable responses support accelerated approval in second- and third-line ccRCC, and earlier-line and other ENPP3-positive tumor sub-studies broaden the label. In that world, XmAb819 reaches about $3 billion of peak sales, Xencor retains full economics or partners on attractive terms, and XmAb942 produces competitive XENITH-UC data that support a Phase 3 or partnership. The causal chain is: durable responses lead to an FDA-agreed path, which supports a data-driven equity raise at high prices or a partnership, which limits dilution to about 5%, which leaves per-share value at about $65. The bull case requires XmAb819 to be the first truly successful solid-tumor T-cell engager in a major indication, and that is a high bar.

## 11. Bear Case

The bear case is not necessarily outright failure. The mechanism is erosion. With longer follow-up, the confirmed response rate at 400 µg/kg falls toward 25%-30% as unconfirmed responses fail to confirm and early responders progress. Tolerability in a larger, less selected population produces more cytokine release syndrome or liver toxicity, and the weekly priming regimen limits adoption. FDA requires a randomized trial against an active comparator, adding years and cost. XENITH-UC fails to differentiate XmAb942 in TL1A. Xencor raises equity at lower prices to fund development, pushing the diluted share count toward 93 million. In this case, XmAb819 is a niche late-line therapy worth about $320 million risk-adjusted, other programs carry option value only, and per-share value falls to about $8, still above net cash per share after dilution only because the royalty portfolio and Alexion installment remain.

## 12. Base Case

The most likely outcome is that XmAb819 enters a registrational study in 2027 with a confirmed response rate in the 35%-45% range and manageable safety, and that investors treat it as a credible but unproven later-line ccRCC therapy with a 40% probability of approval and roughly $2 billion of peak sales. XmAb942 produces mixed or modestly positive Phase 2b data. Xencor raises capital once, diluting about 15%, and burns about $200-$250 million a year through 2028. On those assumptions, per-share value is about $32.66, close to the current price. The base case implies the stock is fairly valued after ESMO, with returns from here depending on new information rather than re-rating of existing data.

## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The biggest risk is that the XmAb819 response rate does not hold. Eleven efficacy-evaluable patients at the selected dose is a small base, and the 55% unconfirmed response rate depends on one response that had not yet confirmed. Solid-tumor T-cell engagers have a history of early promise followed by durability and tolerability problems. If the next update shows the confirmed rate drifting toward 30% or emerging safety issues, the stock can lose most of its pipeline premium.

The second risk is dilution. Xencor's runway through 2028 does not obviously include a full registrational program, and the company has historically issued equity and sold royalties to fund itself. A raise at lower prices after a disappointment would compound losses.

Other risks include competition in TL1A from Merck, Roche and Sanofi; the end of U.S. Ultomiris royalties; volatility in the $78.6 million equity securities portfolio; high short interest that can produce violent moves in either direction; and dependence on partners such as Zenas for remaining royalty upside. A red flag to watch is any further widening of R&D across too many programs at once.

## 16. What Would Change My Mind

The view would turn more bullish on any of the following: a larger 400 µg/kg cohort maintaining a confirmed response rate above 40% with median PFS beyond 9-12 months; FDA agreement on an accelerated approval path; an equity raise or partnership that funds registrational development at or above the current price; or positive XENITH-UC data showing efficacy comparable to leading TL1A competitors with less frequent dosing.

The view would turn bearish on a confirmed response rate falling below 30% on longer follow-up; a meaningful rate of severe CRS, liver injury or treatment discontinuation; an FDA requirement for a long randomized trial before any approval; a large equity raise well below the current price; or continued spreading of R&D across six or more clinical programs without prioritization.

## 18. Conclusion

Xencor is a better company than it was a month ago: it has a wholly owned, first-in-class oncology asset with registrational-quality early data, a cash position to reach key 2027 milestones, and a platform that continues to produce partner value. But at $29.99, the market already pays about $1.83 billion over net cash for the pipeline, and the funding cushion from Ultomiris royalties is largely gone.

The stance is Watch, with a probability-weighted fair value of $34.71 and a base case of $32.66. The main reason for interest is XmAb819's 45.5% confirmed response rate and 82% six-month PFS in heavily pretreated ccRCC. The main risk is that a small cohort overstates durable activity, followed by a dilutive raise. The next trigger is Q3 2026 earnings around November 9, where management should frame the cost and funding of registrational XmAb819 development.

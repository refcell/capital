## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

UBS looks like a straightforward beneficiary of the forced Credit Suisse combination: it acquired a globally recognized wealth franchise at a distressed moment, gained scale that would have taken decades to build organically, and now has a large pool of costs and capital trapped in integration and runoff. The surface thesis is that management removes those costs, migrates clients, releases capital, and converts the enlarged franchise into higher earnings and buybacks. The harder question is whether that earnings improvement belongs to common shareholders after Swiss regulators decide how much capital the combined institution must hold.

The stock is no longer priced as a crisis asset. At $54.90, it trades at 1.88x book value and 2.04x tangible book value, with a 17.67x trailing P/E and 13.58x forward P/E (StockAnalysis statistics, September 3, 2026). Those are confidence multiples. They imply that the market expects integration benefits to survive legal costs, wealth-management fee pressure, credit normalization, and tighter capital rules. The investment case therefore does not rest on whether UBS can remain profitable; it rests on whether normalized ROE can settle materially above an approximately 9% cost of equity while book value per share still compounds.

The operating evidence is improving. TTM revenue through June 30, 2026 was $53.04 billion, up 10.7%, while net income reached $9.52 billion and EPS reached $2.93. Consensus expects FY2026 revenue of $53.68 billion, net income of $11.19 billion and EPS of $3.69, according to StockAnalysis' forecast page on September 3. That EPS would put the shares near 14.9x the displayed FY2026 estimate. Yet the historical series is exceptionally noisy: FY2023 net income of $27.37 billion includes acquisition accounting and cannot be treated as recurring earnings. The report therefore values UBS through book value, tangible book value and ROE rather than mechanically capitalizing cash flow or the acquisition-year profit.

### The Core Debate

The debate is not whether UBS has a valuable franchise. Its global wealth platform, Swiss banking position and institutional capabilities are valuable. The debate is whether the combined group's incremental earnings exceed the incremental capital, regulatory burden and tail risk that came with Credit Suisse. If integration lifts normalized ROE into the low-to-mid teens and regulatory capital remains manageable, a premium to book is defensible. If capital intensity rises while fee and funding pressure hold ROE near the cost of equity, the current premium can unwind even while reported earnings remain positive.

### Current View

The current view is **Watch**, with a probability-weighted present value near $55.64 and a wide scenario range of roughly $29 to $77. The base case is $55.62, effectively the current price and the public average analyst target. The reason to care is that successful integration could create a rare global wealth platform capable of low-to-mid-teens ROE and sustained capital returns. The reason to be careful is that the shares already capitalize much of that outcome while Swiss capital policy can change the denominator of the entire thesis. The next useful update is not another headline synergy target; it is evidence on normalized ROE, CET1 requirements, net new money, Non-Core and Legacy runoff, and the next capital-return plan.

## 2. Company Story

### Origin And Evolution

UBS traces its roots to nineteenth-century Swiss banking and today combines wealth management, domestic Swiss banking, asset management and investment banking. The strategic center of gravity has increasingly shifted toward wealth management because recurring fees, advisory relationships and client assets can produce attractive returns with less balance-sheet intensity than a pure trading bank. Personal & Corporate Banking anchors the Swiss franchise, while the Investment Bank provides markets, research and corporate capabilities to wealth and institutional clients.

The defining modern event is the 2023 rescue acquisition of Credit Suisse. Economically, UBS received client relationships, employees, infrastructure and financial assets at a moment when confidence in the target had broken. It also inherited duplicated systems, difficult assets, legal exposures, overlapping offices, cultural friction and a far larger regulatory footprint. That combination explains why reported history cannot be read as an ordinary growth series. FY2023 net income of $27.37 billion was dominated by acquisition accounting; FY2024 fell to $5.09 billion; FY2025 recovered to $7.77 billion; and TTM earnings reached $9.52 billion through June 2026 (StockAnalysis financials, accessed September 3, 2026).

The post-deal task is less about adding revenue than about converting acquired scale into clean earnings and distributable equity. Client migration must avoid attrition. Duplicate costs must leave without weakening controls. Non-core positions must run off without losses that exceed reserves. Regulators must believe the enlarged group can be resolved in a crisis. Shareholders benefit only if this work produces sustainable earnings per share and book value per share after the required capital buffers.

### Why The Company Matters

UBS matters because global wealth management has meaningful scale advantages. Ultra-high-net-worth clients often need custody, lending, investment products, cross-border advice, family-office capabilities and access to private markets from one institution. A global platform can spread research, technology, compliance and product costs across a larger asset base. It can also connect entrepreneurs to investment-banking services and institutional products. Those advantages are difficult to replicate, but they are not invulnerable: sophisticated clients can maintain relationships with several banks, compare pricing, and move liquid assets quickly when confidence or service falters.

The Swiss domestic franchise matters for a different reason. Deposits, mortgages and corporate relationships create a stable home-market funding and earnings base. Asset Management adds product manufacturing. The Investment Bank broadens client relevance but adds market and capital volatility. The combination can be more valuable than the parts when referrals, product access and infrastructure are shared; it can be less valuable when complexity, control costs and capital allocation overwhelm those benefits.

## 3. Business Model

### How The Company Makes Money

UBS earns revenue from managing and advising client assets, lending against wealth and corporate relationships, gathering deposits, underwriting and trading securities, advising on transactions, and manufacturing investment products. StockAnalysis' segment data for the twelve months through June 2026 shows $27.46 billion of Global Wealth Management revenue, $9.61 billion from Personal & Corporate Banking, $3.17 billion from Asset Management and $13.97 billion from the Investment Bank. Non-Core and Legacy contributed negative $15 million, while Group Items were negative $1.34 billion. These provider figures are useful for scale, though segment definitions and internal allocations should be reconciled to UBS' primary reporting.

Global Wealth Management is the strategic profit pool. Its recurring fees depend on invested assets, mandate penetration and fee rates. Transactional activity depends on client confidence and markets. Net interest income depends on loans, deposits and the yield curve. Personal & Corporate Banking earns spread and fee income from Swiss households and businesses. Asset Management earns basis points on managed assets. The Investment Bank earns advisory, underwriting, execution and trading revenue but consumes more risk-weighted assets and has greater quarterly volatility.

Revenue quality therefore varies. Recurring wealth and asset-management fees deserve a higher confidence level than trading windfalls. Net interest income can be durable but rate-sensitive. Advisory and underwriting revenue can rebound sharply with market activity and then reverse. Acquisition synergies are not revenue; they improve profit only when costs genuinely leave and do not reappear as control failures, remediation, or client attrition.

### The Economic Engine

The engine is: attract and retain wealthy and institutional clients, gather assets and deposits, deepen advisory and lending relationships, manufacture or distribute investment products, spread platform and control costs over a larger asset base, retain enough earnings to satisfy regulators, and return excess capital through dividends and buybacks. The weak link is the conversion from accounting earnings to distributable common equity. A bank can report strong profit while regulators require it to retain capital, while risk-weighted assets rise, or while repurchases above book dilute future book-value compounding if returns disappoint.

### Customer Economics

Customer-level acquisition cost and lifetime value are not disclosed like a SaaS company would disclose them. The relevant substitutes are net new assets, invested-asset growth, recurring fee margin, mandate penetration, client attrition, loan balances, deposit costs and cost-to-income ratio. The best wealth client is not simply one with a large account; it is one whose assets remain through cycles, use advisory mandates, generate lending and transaction revenue, and do not require uneconomic pricing.

The Credit Suisse migration makes these metrics especially important. Asset growth caused by market appreciation does not validate the merger. Net new money and retained relationships do. Likewise, cost reductions do not validate the merger if service deterioration causes outflows. The report therefore treats client flows and fee margin as leading indicators and reported synergy achievement as supporting evidence rather than the final outcome.

The segment mix is both the moat and the complication. Wealth management creates relationship depth; the Swiss bank supplies funding and local dominance; Asset Management supplies products; the Investment Bank supports sophisticated clients. Yet each added capability creates control, technology and capital requirements. The combined institution earns a premium only if cross-segment utility exceeds conglomerate friction.

### Competitive Position

UBS competes with global private banks, U.S. wirehouses, independent wealth managers, universal banks, private-markets firms and low-cost asset managers. Its strongest advantage is the ability to serve very wealthy, cross-border clients with a broad product set and a recognized Swiss franchise. Scale supports research, compliance, structured solutions, private-market access and technology investment. Credit Suisse adds relationships and geographic reach that would be expensive to recreate.

Its weakness is that wealthy clients deliberately diversify counterparties. Switching costs are meaningful for complex structures, credit lines and custody arrangements, but liquid assets can move. Reputation is therefore an operating asset. Credit Suisse's collapse showed that a franchise built over generations can experience rapid confidence erosion. UBS inherits opportunity from that event and also inherits proof that the moat is contingent on risk management and trust.

The domestic Swiss franchise has stronger structural protection because licenses, branch relationships, infrastructure and regulation create barriers. The Investment Bank has a weaker moat: talent and client activity move, and returns are cyclical. Asset Management faces industry-wide fee compression. UBS' competitive advantage is thus strongest where scale, trust and complexity reinforce each other, and weaker where products are commoditized or talent is portable.

### Moat Assessment

The moat is strongest in global brand, relationship breadth, regulatory infrastructure and wealth-management scale. It is weaker in standalone investment products and trading. The market may be underrating the difficulty of recreating the combined client network, but it may also be overrating the permanence of those relationships before the migration is fully proven. A real moat should appear in positive net new money, stable recurring fee margins, low attrition and ROE above cost of equity through a full cycle.

## 5. Financial Snapshot

The financial pattern is an acquisition-driven discontinuity followed by normalization. Revenue rose from $34.18 billion in FY2022 to $39.61 billion in FY2023, $48.03 billion in FY2024, $49.60 billion in FY2025 and $53.04 billion TTM through June 2026. Net income did not follow a smooth path because FY2023 included the accounting consequences of buying Credit Suisse below stated net assets. The useful signal is the recovery from $5.09 billion in FY2024 to $7.77 billion in FY2025 and $9.52 billion TTM, not the comparison with the $27.37 billion acquisition-year figure.

The one number to remember is $29.12 of current book value per share. At $54.90, investors pay 1.88 times that book value. The stock therefore needs more than earnings recovery. It needs retained earnings, prudent capital return and a durable ROE spread to compound the denominator while preserving the premium.

The latest TTM snapshot shows a larger and more profitable franchise. Revenue of $53.04 billion was 10.7% above the comparable provider period, and net income of $9.52 billion was 51.5% higher. ### Balance Sheet And Capital Structure

At June 30, 2026, StockAnalysis reported $1.707 trillion of assets, $682.84 billion of net loans, $784.85 billion of deposits, $1.618 trillion of liabilities, $89.17 billion of common equity and $265 million of minority interest. Tangible book value was $82.32 billion. The $6.85 billion gap between common and tangible equity is modest relative to total equity, which makes P/TBV a useful stricter cross-check.

The statistics page also reports $575.37 billion of cash and equivalents plus investment assets against $599.21 billion of total debt, yielding a standardized net debt figure of $23.84 billion. That calculation should not be interpreted like industrial net debt. A bank's cash, trading assets, secured funding and deposits are part of its operating balance sheet. Liquidity coverage, net stable funding, encumbrance, duration and regulatory capital are the more relevant measures.

Shares outstanding declined 2.71% year over year to 3.062 billion. TTM repurchases were $6.29 billion and common dividends paid were $3.40 billion (StockAnalysis cash flow, June 2026 TTM). This is meaningful per-share capital return, but repurchases at 1.9x book demand scrutiny. They create value when the bank can earn materially above its cost of equity; they are less obviously accretive if regulatory changes or integration risk depress normalized returns.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Reported earnings are **noisy but increasingly informative**. The largest distortion is the Credit Suisse combination. FY2023 net income of $27.37 billion cannot be treated as owner earnings because bargain-purchase and acquisition-accounting effects dominate the period. FY2024 and FY2025 include integration, restructuring, runoff and purchase-accounting impacts. The TTM result is closer to an operating baseline, but it still sits inside a multi-year migration and legal process.

Cash flow does not solve the problem. StockAnalysis reports $14.17 billion of TTM operating cash flow and $11.76 billion of provider free cash flow after $2.41 billion of capital expenditure. For a bank, changes in trading assets, deposits, loans and secured funding can overwhelm operating cash flow without saying anything useful about distributable economics. Deposits finance assets; loan growth consumes cash; trading inventories fluctuate. FCF multiples are therefore marked N/A in the model. The correct owner-earnings bridge is net income to common equity, risk-weighted assets, CET1 capital and book value per share.

The quality positives are visible. Revenue has grown, TTM net income has recovered, common shares are declining, and credit provisions remain low. The balance sheet carries substantial common and tangible equity. The quality concerns are equally specific: acquisition accounting obscures comparability, litigation can create episodic charges, Non-Core and Legacy exits can crystallize losses, market-sensitive revenue can reverse, and regulators can prevent accounting earnings from becoming distributable cash.

The $452 million TTM credit provision deserves neither complacency nor alarm. It is only about seven basis points of $685 billion gross loans, although provision and loan figures do not necessarily use identical averaging conventions. Such a low run rate can reflect a high-quality secured book and favorable credit, but it leaves earnings exposed to normalization. The most important missing evidence is the trajectory of non-performing loans, stage migration, net charge-offs, collateral values and acquired-book performance.

Capital returns are real but should be evaluated against book value. UBS repurchased $6.29 billion of stock TTM and reduced shares 2.71%. At a premium to book, the immediate accounting effect of repurchases can reduce book value per share unless earnings power and long-term franchise value justify the premium paid. The economic case rests on management buying a high-return stream, not buying a dollar of book for less than a dollar.

For valuation, this report uses normalized ROE, P/B and P/TBV rather than P/FCF, EV/EBITDA or acquisition-year P/E. Forward EPS is useful as a near-term expectations anchor. Book-value compounding is the core owner metric. The cleanest evidence would be several periods in which underlying and reported profit converge, CET1 remains comfortably above requirements, book value per share rises after distributions, and integration charges no longer dominate the bridge.

The public page does not expose a full 30-day and 90-day revision history for FY2026 earnings. ### Forecast Logic

The bear case assumes integration costs and regulatory capital intensity persist, normalized ROE settles near 9%, and book value per share grows only 3% annually. A 1.5x terminal P/B still recognizes franchise value but marks down the current premium. With an 11.5% cost of equity, the present value is $29.38. The bear does not require insolvency; it requires the market to decide that the enlarged bank earns only around its cost of equity and deserves a smaller premium.

The base case assumes normalized ROE of 12.5%, BVPS growth of 8%, continuing but disciplined capital return, and an exit P/B of 2.0x. Discounted at 9%, the present value is $55.62. This is close to the current price and $55.03 analyst target. It reflects successful execution without assuming that every Credit Suisse benefit is frictionless.

The bull case assumes the combined wealth platform retains clients, recurring fee growth and investment-bank connectivity expand revenue, cost removal is durable, and final capital rules still permit buybacks. Normalized ROE reaches 15%, BVPS compounds at 11%, and the stock exits at 2.35x book. Discounted at 8.5%, present value is $76.69. The first evidence should be a widening ROE spread, clean book-value compounding, strong net new money and clarity on Swiss capital.

### What The Current Price Implies

At $54.90 and $29.12 BVPS, the market pays 1.88x book. In a simplified residual-income framing, a bank earns a premium to book when expected ROE exceeds the cost of equity and that spread persists. TTM ROE is 10.67% versus an estimated CAPM cost of equity of 8.94%, only a 1.73-point spread. The current multiple therefore appears to discount either a higher future ROE than the TTM figure, a long duration of excess returns, or valuable integration optionality not yet captured in current equity.

At FY2026 consensus EPS of $3.69, the current price is 14.9x earnings. That is not extreme for a high-quality global wealth franchise, but it is demanding for a complex bank with unresolved capital policy. The current price effectively requires the earnings recovery to become durable and distributable rather than remain an adjusted-profit bridge.

### Projection Takeaway

The stock works if integration raises normalized ROE into the low-to-mid teens while Swiss capital rules preserve meaningful distributions and client flows validate the enlarged platform. It fails if the capital denominator rises, acquired liabilities consume earnings, or wealth fee pressure keeps ROE near the cost of equity. Consensus appears reasonable rather than obviously low because its average target is already the market price. The next metric that should change confidence is book value per share growth paired with normalized ROE and final CET1 requirements.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Sergio Ermotti returned to lead UBS through the Credit Suisse integration, a choice that emphasizes execution and institutional memory. The relevant management test is not biography; it is whether the team can migrate clients and systems, reduce duplicate costs, exit non-core positions and negotiate a credible capital structure without allowing controls to deteriorate. Integration targets should be judged by per-share outcomes, not only gross cost savings.

Capital allocation is unusually consequential. TTM repurchases of $6.29 billion exceeded TTM dividends of $3.40 billion. The 18.74% displayed payout ratio and 2.71% buyback yield suggest room for continued returns, but only after regulatory capital, litigation and runoff needs. Share count fell 2.71% year over year, which is a concrete per-share benefit. Whether it is value-accretive depends on normalized ROE and the price paid relative to book.

Governance risk is inseparable from systemic importance. UBS is a private company serving shareholders, but its failure would affect Switzerland and global markets. The state and regulator will therefore prioritize resilience over maximum payout. This can be economically rational for society and still reduce shareholder returns. Investors should not treat proposed capital changes as temporary noise; they can alter sustainable leverage, ROE and the justified valuation multiple.

M&A evaluation also requires a long horizon. Buying Credit Suisse may prove extraordinarily accretive because the purchase price was distressed and the overlap is large. It may also create a permanent conglomerate discount if legal, cultural and regulatory costs persist. The correct scorecard is cumulative: client retention, net new assets, realized cost savings net of remediation, RWA runoff, CET1 generation, book value per share and total capital returned.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

### What Bulls Believe

Bulls believe UBS acquired a world-class wealth franchise at a price that embedded crisis conditions. They expect duplicated expenses to leave, Credit Suisse clients to remain, Non-Core and Legacy assets to run off, and the enlarged platform to spread technology and control costs over more assets. In that view, consensus FY2026 EPS of $3.69 is a waypoint rather than peak earnings. A 12%-15% normalized ROE would support a premium P/B, while buybacks and dividends compound per-share value.

The bull case also argues that UBS' business mix is better than that of a conventional universal bank. Wealth-management fees and Swiss banking should reduce reliance on volatile trading. The Investment Bank can be sized to support clients rather than drive the whole group. If management maintains discipline, the combination offers both recurring fees and capital-light growth.

### What Bears Believe

Bears believe the acquisition created a bank too large for its home country to regulate lightly. Higher CET1 requirements, parent-bank capital rules or resolution demands could trap earnings and lower ROE. Legal and operational liabilities can arrive years after the headline deal. Client migration may be more fragile than management metrics imply, particularly if advisors leave or service suffers.

They also argue that a 1.88x book multiple already assumes success. TTM ROE of 10.67% is not high enough by itself to justify that premium indefinitely. The Investment Bank adds cyclicality, wealth fees face pressure, and current provisions may understate future credit costs. Even if EPS reaches consensus, multiple compression can offset the earnings gain.

### What The Market May Be Missing

The market appears focused on synergy delivery and quarterly EPS. The more important issue may be the interaction between capital rules and buybacks. Two banks can report the same net income but create very different shareholder value if one can distribute excess capital and the other must retain it against low-return assets. Final capital requirements could therefore matter more than another quarter of cost saves.

The positive variant is that the market may underestimate the durability of the combined wealth platform. If client flows remain strong after migration and fee margins hold, the enlarged franchise can earn a persistent premium even under stricter capital rules. The negative variant is that headline ROE improves only because near-term costs fade, while a permanently larger equity base prevents sustained mid-teens returns.

### Prior Research And Public Debate

Public debate centers on integration progress, systemic capital, the economics of wealth scale and the trade-off between resilience and distributions. It did not rely on paywalled analyst models, so detailed segment consensus, capital-rule sensitivities and legal-case probabilities remain access limits.

## 10. Bull Case

If UBS retains Credit Suisse clients, converts acquired assets into recurring wealth fees, removes duplicate costs without control failures, and receives a manageable final Swiss capital framework, then normalized ROE can rise toward 15%, book value per share can compound near 11%, and capital returns can continue. Under that outcome, a 2.35x terminal P/B on year-five BVPS of $49.32 supports a $115.91 terminal price and $76.69 present value. The first evidence should show up in net new money, recurring fee margin, reported-versus-underlying profit convergence, CET1 headroom and book value per share after buybacks.

This is not simply a multiple-expansion case. The value creation must come from residual earnings and per-share compounding. The multiple is the consequence of durable returns, not the primary assumption. If the operating evidence supports only 11% ROE, the bull multiple would be unjustified even if EPS beats for a quarter.

## 11. Bear Case

If integration leaves persistent costs, client attrition weakens fee growth, credit costs normalize upward, and Swiss rules require materially more equity, then normalized ROE can settle near 9% and BVPS growth can slow to 3%. The market would no longer pay nearly two times book for a bank earning around its cost of equity. A 1.5x terminal P/B and higher 11.5% discount rate produce $29.38 of present value, or 46.5% downside.

The first evidence should show up in weak net new money, rising stage 2/3 loans, slower Non-Core and Legacy runoff, a higher required CET1 ratio, suspended or reduced buybacks, and book value per share stagnation. The bear does not require a repeat of Credit Suisse's collapse. A normal de-rating from premium global wealth compounder to complex regulated bank is enough.

## 12. Base Case

The base case assumes the integration is broadly successful but not frictionless. Revenue growth moderates after the acquisition step-up. Normalized ROE reaches 12.5%, above cost of equity but below the bull case. UBS retains sufficient capital to pay dividends and repurchase shares, while final regulation absorbs part of the potential payout. Book value per share compounds 8% annually, reaching $42.79 in year five.

At a 2.0x terminal P/B, the year-five price is $85.58. Discounted at 9%, present value is $55.62. This is effectively the current price and average public target. The market therefore needs clean evidence of execution to create upside; merely meeting the base case may produce an acceptable long-term return from earnings and distributions, but little immediate valuation gap.

### Primary Valuation Framework

The primary framework compounds current BVPS under bear, base and bull assumptions, applies an exit P/B consistent with normalized ROE and franchise quality, and discounts the terminal price at a scenario-specific cost of equity. This is a simplified residual-income model. It makes the key judgment visible: the stock deserves a premium only if returns on common equity persist above shareholders' required return.

The CAPM cross-check uses a 4.792% U.S. 10-year Treasury snapshot from September 1, a 5% equity risk premium and 0.83 beta, producing an 8.94% cost of equity. A Swiss-franc or global-bank model could use a different risk-free base and additional regulatory premium. Scenario discount rates therefore range from 8.5% to 11.5% rather than pretending the point estimate is exact.

### Reverse Valuation

At today's price, the market seems to assume that UBS becomes a durable premium bank rather than a merely adequate one. The current 1.88x P/B is difficult to reconcile with a permanent 10.7% ROE and 8.9% cost of equity unless excess returns last a long time or ROE rises. The valuation looks roughly fair if normalized ROE settles around 12%-13% and capital returns remain available. It looks generous if final Swiss rules or integration liabilities hold returns near 9%-10%.

## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The biggest risk is that the combined institution needs materially more common equity than investors currently assume. That would reduce leverage, constrain buybacks, lower ROE and compress the P/B multiple at the same time. It is a denominator risk that can overwhelm respectable revenue and EPS growth.

Integration risk follows. Systems migration can disrupt service, expose control gaps or cause client and advisor departures. Cost savings may be offset by remediation and compliance. Legal liabilities can be delayed and nonlinear. Non-core positions may look harmless until an exit crystallizes a loss.

Credit risk is currently muted but not absent. A $452 million TTM provision on a $685 billion gross loan book leaves little room for a broad normalization. Swiss mortgage exposure, private-bank Lombard lending, leveraged finance and commercial real estate respond differently to stress. Aggregate headline figures can obscure concentration.

Market risk also matters. Wealth fees depend on asset levels, transaction activity can fall, and the Investment Bank is cyclical. Currency translation affects USD-reported figures. Deposit pricing and the yield curve can pressure spread income. Finally, valuation itself is a risk: a premium P/B turns ordinary execution disappointments into large share-price reactions.

## 18. Conclusion

UBS is a **Watch** because the strategic asset is excellent but the valuation already assumes a successful conversion of Credit Suisse scale into durable shareholder returns. At $54.90, the shares trade at 1.88x book and 2.04x tangible book. The base-case present value of $55.62 and probability-weighted value of $55.64 offer almost no margin of safety, closely matching the $55.03 public average analyst target.

The stock works if normalized ROE rises into the low-to-mid teens, book value per share compounds after capital returns, client flows validate the enlarged wealth franchise, and Swiss capital rules preserve distributable equity. It fails if the bank earns only around its cost of equity while carrying a permanent premium valuation. The main reason to care is the quality and scale of the combined wealth platform. The main risk is capital intensity. Revisit the view when final capital rules, the next quarterly results, net new money and post-distribution BVPS provide a clearer answer.

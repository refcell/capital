## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

Carpenter Technology looks like a broken momentum stock, but the real question is what a record margin in a cyclical metals business is worth. The shares rose from $70.80 at the end of December 2023 to a closing high of $619.25 on July 6, 2026 (an intraday high of $625.99 was reported in early July), then fell to $269.68 on November 17. Over the same period operating income went from $133.1 million in FY2023 to $702.0 million in FY2026 and $745.1 million over the twelve months to September 2026. The multiple did most of the travelling: the stock traded at 9.8x sales at the end of June 2026 and 4.6x on November 13 (Trefis, November 16).

The decline came in steps. On July 24, 2026, three weeks into his tenure, CEO Brian Malloy died suddenly; Tony Thene, who had run the company from 2015 to June 2026, returned as CEO (8-K, July 28). On July 30 Q4 FY2026 revenue of $851.0 million came in about 1% below the roughly $862 million consensus while earnings beat. On October 22 Q1 FY2027 revenue of $798.2 million missed the roughly $831 million consensus, management guided Q2 operating income of $192-197 million, about flat on Q1, and the stock fell 20.6% that day to $292.26. KeyBanc downgraded the shares to Sector Weight on October 26, several brokers cut targets from the $600s to the $400s, and law firms advertised securities-fraud investigations. Through all of it the company reaffirmed its FY2027 operating income outlook of $850-880 million and its FY2029 target of $1.2-1.3 billion.

The thesis works if Carpenter keeps converting a tight specialty-alloy market into higher prices and better mix while the Athens, Alabama brownfield expansion adds volume from early FY2028, so that operating income reaches about $1.1 billion in FY2029. It fails if the aerospace supply chain destocks or new melt capacity across the industry turns pricing, in which case FY2029 earnings fall back toward FY2025 levels and the stock is valued like the cyclical it was for most of its history.

### The Core Debate

The debate is not whether Carpenter's operating results are strong. They are the best in the company's 137-year history. It is whether FY2026-FY2027 margins are a new structural level or the top of a cycle. If they are structural, about 16x guided FY2027 operating income is too low for a business growing operating income 20% a year. If they are cyclical, it is roughly fair, because Carpenter's operating margin averaged about 8% from FY2012 to FY2019 and turned negative in FY2021.

## 2. Company Story

### Origin And Evolution

Carpenter was founded in 1889 in Reading, Pennsylvania, to make tool steel and has spent most of its history as a specialty-alloy melter whose fortunes followed aerospace and energy. Its modern shape comes from two decisions. The 2012 acquisition of Latrobe Specialty Metals added premium remelting capacity and a distribution business. And the 2011-2014 build of the Athens, Alabama mill gave it the newest premium-melt plant in the U.S. industry. Both were bought ahead of demand, and for most of the following decade they earned poor returns: revenue peaked at $2.27 billion in FY2013, operating income fell from $232.7 million that year to $51.6 million in FY2016, and the COVID collapse took FY2021 revenue to $1.48 billion with an operating loss of $248.6 million, including impairments and restructuring.

Tony Thene became CEO in 2015 and spent the downturn closing or selling weaker operations, cutting costs and reorganizing the company into two segments: SAO, the mills, and Performance Engineered Products (PEP), which holds the Dynamet titanium business, Carpenter Additive and the Latrobe and Mexico distribution businesses. When aerospace build rates and repair activity recovered after 2022, Carpenter had idle qualified capacity and a supply chain short of nickel and cobalt alloys. It used the shortage to reprice, both on transactional orders and in renegotiated LTAs, and to drop lower-margin work. Operating income rose from $133.1 million in FY2023 to $323.1 million in FY2024, $521.8 million in FY2025 and $702.0 million in FY2026.

In February 2026 the board named COO Brian Malloy as CEO from July 1, 2026, with Thene staying on as executive chairman. Malloy died on July 24, and Thene returned as CEO and chairman. In August, former Howmet Aerospace CFO Ken Giacobbe joined the board. In October and November the company announced a new general counsel and promoted Marshall Akins, the chief commercial officer, to chief operating officer, with new heads of commercial and investor relations.

### Why The Company Matters

Carpenter's customers make parts that cannot fail: jet engine discs, shafts and casings, landing gear, fasteners, orthopedic implants and gas turbine components. The alloys in those parts must be melted and remelted (vacuum induction melting, vacuum arc remelting, electroslag remelting) to tight chemistry and cleanliness, and each grade at each mill must be qualified by the engine maker or the airframer, a process that takes years. That makes switching suppliers slow and makes capacity, once full, hard to add. Carpenter, ATI, Haynes (now owned by Acerinox) and a handful of European and Japanese mills supply most of the Western market for these grades.

The edge is real but narrow. It shows up as pricing power when demand exceeds qualified capacity, as it does now. It disappears when the aerospace supply chain holds too much inventory, as in 2016 and 2020, because the same qualification barrier then protects every incumbent equally and they compete on price for a smaller order book.

## 3. Business Model

### How The Company Makes Money

Carpenter sells alloys by the pound in bar, billet, wire, strip and powder. The price has two parts. A base price reflects the grade, the processing and the market. A raw-material surcharge, set monthly from published nickel, cobalt, chromium and molybdenum prices, passes metal costs through to the customer, usually with a lag. In FY2026 net sales were $3,124.2 million, of which $596.7 million was surcharge and $2,527.5 million was sales excluding surcharge. The surcharge protects gross profit dollars but not margin percentages: when nickel rises, revenue rises with no extra profit. That is why management reports margins on sales excluding surcharge, and why this report treats operating income, not revenue, as the driver.

About 43% of FY2026 net sales were under firm-price arrangements, where Carpenter uses commodity forwards to protect the margin (FY2026 10-K). The rest is priced off LTAs with negotiated base-price escalators or sold transactionally at spot. Management says it is negotiating several LTAs with "significant pricing upside" (Q1 FY2027 deck), and that transactional pricing remains strong. No single customer accounted for 10% of net sales in FY2024-FY2026.

### The Economic Engine

The engine is: aerospace production and repair need more qualified premium alloy than the industry can melt, so Carpenter raises base prices and shifts its fixed mill capacity toward the highest-value grades; because mill costs are largely fixed, each dollar of price and mix falls mostly to operating income; that income, less modest maintenance capex, becomes free cash flow that funds new capacity and buybacks. The weak link is the first step. When engine makers and distributors hold enough inventory, orders fall faster than end demand, the mills chase volume, and the same fixed-cost leverage works in reverse.

### Unit Economics / Customer Economics

Customer-level economics are not disclosed. What replaces them is margin per pound and mill utilization. Neither is published directly, but the segment margins show the trend clearly. SAO adjusted operating margin excluding surcharge was 32.0% in Q1 FY2026, 30.5% in Q4 FY2025, 35.6% in Q3 FY2026, 37.8% in Q4 FY2026 and 37.7% in Q1 FY2027. Gross margin excluding surcharge was 40.4% in Q1 FY2027 against 35.9% a year earlier. Q4 FY2026 shipments were 22% higher than a year earlier on 13% higher sales, which says volume rose too, but most of the profit gain came from price and mix. PEP earns far less: 8.8% adjusted margin in Q1 FY2027 on $83.0 million of sales excluding surcharge, down from 10.8% on weaker titanium sales into medical.

The reported metrics prove the pricing is real. They do not show how much of it is protected by LTAs and how much is spot pricing that can reverse.

## 4. Products, Segments, And Moat

### Product / Segment Breakdown

SAO is the business. In Q1 FY2027 it had $730.4 million of net sales ($573.5 million excluding surcharge) and $216.3 million of operating income; PEP had $91.1 million of sales and $7.3 million of operating income; corporate costs and eliminations took about $27 million. Within SAO the value is in remelted nickel and cobalt superalloys for engines, where Carpenter competes with ATI and Haynes, and in specialty stainless and titanium for structures and fasteners. The Athens brownfield expansion adds premium-melt and remelt capacity there; management says it is on schedule and on budget and expects it to come online in early FY2028 and contribute from FY2028.

### Competitive Position

Carpenter wins where a customer needs a qualified, remelted grade on a lead time the market cannot meet. It loses share when demand is weak and buyers can choose among several qualified suppliers on price. Competition does not threaten the engine today, because the industry is short of capacity. It could threaten it in FY2028-FY2029, when Carpenter's brownfield, ATI's expansions and imports arrive into a market whose demand depends on aircraft build rates that have repeatedly slipped.

The useful comparison is with ATI and Howmet. ATI is the most direct competitor in nickel alloys and titanium; Howmet is a customer and a forger of engine parts with higher returns. At October 31, 2026, ATI traded at 26.7x trailing EV/EBIT and Howmet at 30.3x, against Carpenter at 19.5x (data/multiples/). Carpenter's discount reflects its history of deeper cycles.

### Moat Assessment

The moat is strongest in qualification and process know-how for premium engine grades, weaker in pricing over a full cycle, and the market may be overrating it after three years of record results because the same moat failed to protect earnings in FY2016 and FY2021. Real moats show up through cycles; Carpenter's operating margin went from 10.2% in FY2013 to 2.8% in FY2016 and below zero in FY2021, with the same mills and the same qualifications.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Reported earnings are clean but cyclically high. There are no material special items in FY2026 or Q1 FY2027 (adjusted operating income equals reported), stock compensation is small ($26.4 million in FY2026, under 1% of sales), and the share count has fallen slightly (50.7 million diluted in FY2025 to 50.0 million in Q1 FY2027). The tax rate is normal: 19.3% in FY2026 and 15.5% in Q1 FY2027, against a 20-22% guide for the year.

The two adjustments that matter run in opposite directions. First, cash conversion is below earnings because Carpenter is building capacity. FY2026 net income was $529.8 million and FCF $362.3 million; capex of $242.7 million was 1.65x D&A of $147.1 million, and FY2027 capex of $355-375 million will be about 2.4x the guided $150 million of D&A. Once the brownfield is complete, capex should fall toward D&A and FCF should exceed net income. Second, earnings are inflated by the cycle: an operating margin of 23% compares with 10% at the FY2019 peak. For valuation this report uses GAAP operating income, with the cycle risk carried in the bear case weight rather than in a haircut to the base.

Working capital is the other watch item. Inventory was $822.9 million at June 30, about 26% of sales, and rose $61.6 million in Q1. Management expects working-capital discipline to drive FY2027 FCF of $400-430 million, which requires about $425-455 million of FCF in the last three quarters after Q1's -$24.8 million.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Tony Thene's record is the main governance asset. He took over in 2015, cut the cost base through the downturn, kept the Athens and Latrobe assets rather than selling them cheaply, and was in charge as operating income rose fivefold. His return after Malloy's death gives continuity, but it also means the succession plan has to be rebuilt; the company has since promoted Marshall Akins to chief operating officer (November 10), John Huyette to chief commercial officer and Bill Besash to head investor relations, and the general counsel retires at the end of 2026. Ken Giacobbe, Howmet's former CFO, joined the board in August, which is a useful addition; two other directors left, and the board went to ten members, nine independent, in October.

Insider selling is the one awkward fact. On August 25, 2026, near $490, Thene sold 109,283 shares for estate planning, tax planning and diversification, and filed an 8-K saying he still owned 466,697 shares and units, among the top 20 holders. He disclosed it openly, but the sale came a month after he returned as CEO and within weeks of the July target-setting.

Capital allocation has shifted from investing through the cycle to returning cash. The company completed a $400 million buyback in Q1 FY2027 and authorized a new $1.0 billion program in August, with $985.0 million remaining at September 30. The FY2026 repurchases of 545,000 shares for $179.1 million averaged about $329 a share, below today's base value. The Q1 FY2027 repurchases of 241,924 shares for $133.9 million averaged about $553, roughly double today's price and well above the base value; buying back stock at the top of a de-rating, while FCF was negative and cash fell by $234 million in a quarter, is the weakest capital decision in the record. The dividend has been $0.20 a quarter for years and yields 0.3%. The brownfield expansion is the main reinvestment; management says it is on schedule and on budget.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

### What Bulls Believe

Bulls see a supplier at the start of a long aerospace upcycle. Airbus and Boeing have order books of more than a decade, engine makers are adding capacity for new builds and for maintenance on an aging fleet, and the supply of qualified premium alloy cannot grow quickly. Carpenter has the newest U.S. premium-melt mill, is adding brownfield capacity, and is renegotiating LTAs at higher prices. Management has said it sees "additional upside to demand, pricing opportunity and productivity" and that FY2029 is not the peak. On the target's $1.25 billion midpoint and a 22x multiple, below ATI and Howmet, the stock would be worth more than $560 by mid-2029, and the sell-off gives an entry at half the July price.

### What Bears Believe

Bears see a cyclical company at peak margins with peak sentiment just unwound. Margins of 23% compare with an average of about 8% from FY2012 to FY2019; revenue growth slowed in the latest quarter (Trefis notes Carpenter's twelve-month revenue growth of 10.2% trails Howmet's 22%); defense orders have paused; and the CEO who was to lead the next phase died. New capacity is coming industry-wide in 2027-2028. When the aerospace supply chain last restocked and destocked, operating income fell 78% from FY2013 to FY2016. On that view a 12-15x multiple on flat earnings is generous.

### What The Market May Be Missing

The market appears to be focused on two small revenue misses and a flat quarter. The more important issue is that the margin held at a record level through a quarter with planned outages, and earnings estimates did not move, so the sell-off was about the multiple. That is positive. But the bulls are missing that the multiple they want to restore was itself a cycle peak: Carpenter's own median EV/EBIT over the last eight years is 22.5x on earnings that were mostly depressed, and a lower multiple is right when the earnings are high. The variant view is therefore moderately positive: the stock is no longer expensive, but it is not yet cheap enough to own a cyclical at a record margin.

### Primary Valuation Framework

The right tool is an earnings multiple on operating income a few years out, with cash added, because Carpenter's value depends on what margin it holds after the current capex cycle, and operating income avoids the noise from surcharge revenue, capex timing and the tax rate. A DCF would mostly be an exercise in choosing a terminal margin; this framework makes that choice explicit through the FY2029 margin and the exit multiple. FCF after dividends is kept as cash, so the value does not depend on the price paid for buybacks.

### Scenario Valuation

The value is most sensitive to two things: the exit multiple, where each turn is worth about $22 a share in the base, and FY2029 operating income, where each $50 million is worth about $17 at 17x. ## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The risk that would break the thesis is an aerospace destock arriving at the same time as new industry capacity. Carpenter's earnings are leveraged to pricing and utilization; a modest fall in orders can take margins down several points quickly, as FY2014-FY2016 showed, and the brownfield would then add fixed cost rather than profit.

The second risk is execution under new leadership. The CEO the board chose died three weeks into the role; the returning CEO had planned to step back; the COO, chief commercial officer, head of investor relations and general counsel are all new or changing within six months. Q1 operating income landed inside the consolidated guide, but SAO missed its segment guide ($216.3 million against $218-222 million) and the Q2 guide ($192-197 million) sits below Q1's. A company that missed its consolidated quarterly guide would lose the credibility its FY2029 target depends on.

The third is capital allocation at the wrong price. Buybacks at about $553 in Q1 FY2027 cost shareholders money on any reasonable value; if management keeps buying heavily through a downturn while FCF is weak, the balance sheet advantage erodes.

Smaller risks include the July 2027 expiry of the Latrobe labor agreement (450 employees), tariff and trade changes that affect both import competition and raw-material costs, the securities-fraud investigations announced after the October drop (no suit is disclosed in the 10-Q), and nickel and cobalt price swings, which mostly pass through surcharges but with a lag.

## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

FirstCash just reported the best quarter in its history and lost a fifth of its market value in two days. Third-quarter revenue rose 18% to $1.10 billion, adjusted EPS rose 19% to $2.68 against a $2.63 consensus, pawn segment revenue and pre-tax income both grew 28%, pawn receivables hit a record $908 million, and the board raised the dividend 10%. The selloff had two triggers that sit at the edge of the business rather than its core. American First Finance (AFF), the lease-to-own and point-of-sale finance unit bought in 2021, saw pre-tax income fall 28% to $33 million as origination volume dropped 15% after merchant partners Conn's and American Freight went bankrupt. And gold fell nearly 2% that morning to below $4,100 an ounce, after a 6.6% slide in September, reminding investors how much of FirstCash's recent growth rides on the gold price. (FirstCash Q3 2026 release; Yahoo Finance/TIKR, October 22, 2026; StockStory, October 23, 2026; CNBC, September 30, 2026.)

The pre-print price embedded a lot of optimism. FirstCash went into the report at about 18 times forward earnings, above its five-year average near 16 times, after a run that took the stock to a $238.93 high. That premium was earned by thirteen consecutive quarters of double-digit same-store pawn receivable growth in the U.S. and an acquisition machine that added H&T in the U.K. in 2025 and is now buying Ramsdens. But the premium also capitalized a gold tailwind that the company does not control. Wholesale scrap jewelry revenue, which is simply gold sold to refiners, went from $132 million in FY2024 to $531 million in the trailing twelve months, and scrap gross profit in the first nine months of 2026 was $101 million against $30 million a year earlier. That $71 million increase alone accounts for roughly a third of the $217 million increase in nine-month pawn segment income. (FirstCash Q3 2026 release; StockAnalysis income statement; author calculation.)

The debate, therefore, is not whether FirstCash is a good business. It clearly is: adjusted return on equity is 20%, the U.S. pawn segment earns a 26% pre-tax margin, and the company funds store growth, acquisitions, a rising dividend and $275 million of trailing buybacks from its own cash flow while holding leverage at 2.7 times adjusted EBITDA. The debate is how much of the current earnings level is structural pawn demand from cash-strapped consumers and how much is a cyclical gold windfall that will reverse. At $185.35 the market is pricing a middle answer: roughly 9-12% annual EPS growth over five years at a 14-16 times exit multiple. That is close to our base case, which is why the stance is Watch with a positive bias rather than a buy.

### Current View

The view is Watch with a positive bias. The probability-weighted five-year value is $268 a share, 45% above today's price before dividends, but discounting it at the CAPM cost of equity of 7.74% gives $185, essentially today's price, and at a more realistic 9.5% hurdle for a subprime lender it gives $170. The base case alone, $262.50 in FY2031, implies about 7.2% annual price return plus a 1% dividend. That is a decent return for a high-quality compounder, but it is not a mispricing large enough to ignore the gold risk. A move below about $165, where the base case would compound at roughly 10% a year, or evidence that pawn receivables keep growing at a double-digit pace even as gold falls, would turn the stance positive.

## 2. Company Story

### Origin And Evolution

FirstCash went public in April 1991 as a Texas pawn operator and spent the next two decades building a two-country model: U.S. pawn stores that lend against jewelry, tools and electronics, and a fast-growing chain of small-format stores across Mexico that lend mainly against general merchandise and small gold items. The defining transaction came in 2016, when FirstCash merged with Cash America, then the largest U.S. pawn operator, roughly doubling its U.S. store base and making it the leading pawn company in the Americas. Rick Wessel, who has run the company for roughly two decades, used the combined platform to keep consolidating independent U.S. pawnshops one or a few at a time while opening dozens of de novo stores in Latin America every year. (StockAnalysis profile; FirstCash investor releases.)

In December 2021 FirstCash stepped outside pawn by buying American First Finance, a lease-to-own and retail installment lender that offers point-of-sale payment plans through furniture, appliance, jewelry and auto-repair merchants. That deal explains the 60.6% revenue jump in FY2022, from $1.70 billion to $2.73 billion, and it is why "leased merchandise income" and "interest and fees on finance receivables" appear on the income statement. AFF gave FirstCash a second way to serve the same credit-constrained customer, but it also brought exposure to merchant bankruptcies, higher credit losses and regulators: the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau sued FirstCash in 2021 over Military Lending Act compliance, a matter settled in 2025 with an $11 million charge that the company excludes from adjusted results. (StockAnalysis income statement; FirstCash Q3 2026 release, non-GAAP reconciliation.)

The third chapter is the U.K. In 2025 FirstCash acquired H&T Group, the largest U.K. pawnbroker, which closed during the third quarter of 2025; that is visible in the $475 million of 2025 cash acquisitions and the $556 million jump in total debt between June and September 2025. H&T lends almost entirely against gold jewelry (99% of U.K. collateral) and carries the largest average loan in the company at $905. In June 2026 FirstCash agreed to buy Ramsdens, another U.K. pawn, foreign-exchange and jewelry retailer with about 174 locations, and raised its offer in July from 600 pence to 675 pence a share for a total equity value of about £232 million ($308 million). Ramsdens' shareholders and the U.K. Financial Conduct Authority have approved the deal; antitrust clearance is pending, with closing targeted by year-end. (StockAnalysis cash flow and quarterly balance sheet; FirstCash Ramsdens announcement, June 23, 2026; Q2 2026 release, July 23, 2026; Q3 2026 release.)

### Why The Company Matters Now

FirstCash now operates 3,352 pawn stores: 1,215 in the U.S., 1,839 in Latin America (1,732 in Mexico plus Guatemala, El Salvador and Colombia) and 298 in the U.K. It owns the real estate under 480 of them, including 39% of its U.S. stores. That footprint makes it the largest pawn operator in the world and roughly four times the size of its only listed U.S. peer, EZCORP, by market value. (FirstCash Q3 2026 release; StockAnalysis EZCORP statistics.)

It matters now because three cycles are peaking or turning at the same time. Consumer demand for small, non-recourse loans is strong after years of inflation, which shows up in record receivables per store. Gold, at roughly $4,060 an ounce, has made every gold chain and ring worth more as collateral and every forfeited piece worth more as scrap. And AFF's merchant base is shrinking as furniture retailers struggle. The stock's 20% drop in two days was the market trying to price the second and third cycles against the first.

## 3. Business Model

A pawn loan is a small, short-term, non-recourse loan secured by an item the customer leaves at the store. The customer pays a monthly fee, regulated by state or national law, and either repays the loan and takes the item home, renews it, or walks away. If the customer walks away, FirstCash keeps the collateral and sells it in the same store, usually at a substantial gross margin because it lent only a fraction of the item's resale value. Gold jewelry that does not sell at retail is melted and sold to refiners as scrap. The customer's credit history is never checked and nothing is reported to credit bureaus, which is exactly why the product serves people who have no access to cards or bank loans. (FirstCash Q3 2026 release; company description on StockAnalysis.)

The economic engine is that every dollar of pawn receivable earns twice. It first earns loan fees while outstanding; the TTM pawn loan fee line of $1.04 billion on roughly $850-$900 million of average receivables implies a fee yield above 100% a year. It then earns a retail margin when the collateral is forfeited and sold: U.S. retail margins were 42% and Latin American margins 35% in Q3. Because the loan is sized well below resale value, credit losses in the bank sense barely exist; the risk is instead that collateral values fall before the item is sold. That is why gold matters. Two-thirds of the pawn collateral book is jewelry, and the company lends against its melt value and resale value. (FirstCash Q3 2026 release; StockAnalysis income statement; author calculation.)

AFF works differently. It buys merchandise from a merchant at the point of sale and leases it to a customer who could not get conventional credit, or it originates a retail installment loan. Revenue is lease income and interest, but the costs include depreciation of leased goods and large loss provisions: the leased-merchandise provision rate was 23.6% of originations in Q3, and the delinquency rate was 26.5%. AFF's economics depend on merchant volume, so the bankruptcies of Conn's and American Freight removed earnings that cannot be replaced quickly. (FirstCash Q3 2026 release, AFF portfolio metrics.)

### Unit Economics

The pawn store is a low-capital unit. Over the past twelve months, FirstCash opened 41 de novo stores for about $18 million of fixed assets and working capital, or about $440,000 each, and acquired 29 stores for $89 million, about $3.1 million each. Existing-store pawn earning assets (loans plus inventory) grew $235 million. The company reports total pawn earning assets of $1.51 billion against nine-month pawn segment pre-tax income of $679 million, so the pre-tax return on earning assets in the pawn segments runs above 55% annualized, before corporate costs, interest and the goodwill created by acquisitions. On a whole-company basis, adjusted return on equity is 20% and adjusted return on assets 9%. (FirstCash Q3 2026 release; author calculation.)

Latin America is the volume engine and the U.K. the value engine. A typical Latin American loan is $103, so a store's economics depend on transaction count and inventory turnover (3.9 turns a year). A typical U.K. loan is $905, almost entirely against gold, with slower inventory turns of 2.0 a year and higher aged inventory (11.6% older than a year). The U.S. sits in between at $319 and 2.8 turns. Those differences show where gold sensitivity is greatest: the U.K. book, at $215 million of receivables, is essentially a gold-lending business. (FirstCash Q3 2026 release, pawn operating metrics.)

## 4. Products, Segments, And Moat

FirstCash reports four segments. U.S. Pawn is the largest earnings contributor: Q3 revenue grew 18% and pre-tax income grew 19% to $133.3 million, a 26% margin. Latin America Pawn grew revenue 35% in dollars and 27% in constant currency, with pre-tax income up 37% to $64.6 million; the peso's 8% appreciation versus the prior year added about $1.3 million to segment profit. U.K. Pawn earned $29.2 million in Q3 and $102.0 million year to date at a 34% margin, against a prior-year period that was only partly owned. AFF earned $33.2 million, down from $46.0 million. Management expects the three pawn segments to make up about 90% of net revenue and segment income in 2026. (FirstCash Q3 2026 release.)

### Competitive Position

Pawn is a fragmented, local business in which scale matters more than it appears. A small independent pawnshop competes on location and the owner's eye for value; FirstCash competes with the same things plus centralized pricing data, a point-of-sale and loan management system (FirstPawn) now rolled out across all H&T stores, jewelry processing centers that turn scrap into cash quickly, a cheaper cost of capital (an unsecured bank facility and long-dated senior notes near 6% rather than owner equity), and owned real estate in prime locations. These advantages let FirstCash buy independents, plug them into its systems and raise their returns, which is why acquisitions are a repeatable growth lever. (FirstCash Q3 2026 release, additional commentary.)

The listed competition is EZCORP, which trades at about 8.97 times EV/EBITDA and 14.2 times forward earnings with a $1.82 billion market value. Other competition comes from rent-to-own operators and buy-now-pay-later lenders on the AFF side, and from fintech small-dollar lenders on the consumer side. The pawn product's advantage over all of them is that it requires no credit check and creates no debt the customer can default on, so demand rises rather than falls when consumers are stressed. (StockAnalysis EZCORP statistics.)

### Moat Assessment

FirstCash has a narrow but durable moat in pawn, built on regulatory licensing (pawn operations require state and national licenses and fee caps limit new entrants' economics), local density, data on collateral values, and cost of capital. The moat is strongest in Latin America, where FirstCash's store density and brand recognition across 32 Mexican states make it difficult for a new entrant to reach scale, and in the U.S., where it is the consolidator of choice for retiring independents. AFF has no meaningful moat: its volume depends on merchant relationships that can be lost to competitors or bankruptcy. The moat does not protect against the gold price, which affects every pawn operator, nor against regulation, which can cap fees or restrict lending practices.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Earnings quality is good but flattered by gold. The adjustments between GAAP and adjusted earnings are modest and mostly legitimate: $46.5 million of after-tax amortization of acquired intangibles, $9.5 million of after-tax merger costs and minor other items over the trailing twelve months. Acquired-intangible amortization is a real cost of the acquisition strategy in the sense that FirstCash paid for those customer relationships, but it does not recur in cash, and FirstCash's cash generation supports the adjusted figure: operating cash flow of $679 million TTM comfortably exceeds adjusted net income of $462 million. Rate the adjustments as clean. (FirstCash Q3 2026 release, non-GAAP reconciliations.)

The cash flow statement needs care. FirstCash classifies pawn loan originations, repayments and recoveries through forfeited-collateral sales in investing activities, and AFF's leased-merchandise purchases inside operating activities as a change in "other net operating assets." In FY2025, investing cash flow was −$828 million against only $55 million of capital expenditures. The gap consists of $475 million of acquisitions (mostly H&T), $62 million of real estate purchases and roughly $236 million of net loan and receivable fundings. The company's adjusted free cash flow, which deducts net pawn-loan and finance-receivable fundings, was $393 million TTM, while the data provider's "free cash flow" of $606 million excludes them. Both are valid views. The provider's number approximates owner earnings in a steady state; the company's number shows what was left after funding a 15% bigger loan book. When gold falls and the loan book shrinks, the opposite happens and adjusted free cash flow rises temporarily, which is a natural hedge but not a reason to treat a gold-driven loan book as permanent. (StockAnalysis cash flow statement; FirstCash Q3 2026 release, free cash flow reconciliation; author calculation.)

The real quality question is gold. Scrap gold gross profit was $32.4 million in Q3 2026 against $18.5 million a year earlier, and $101.2 million in the first nine months against $30.2 million. That increase is about a third of the growth in pawn segment income this year. It is not an accounting issue; the cash is real. But it is the most cyclical part of earnings, and it arrives at a time when gold has risen far above its historical range. Loan fees are also partly gold-linked: average U.S. loans rose 10% and U.K. loans 18% year over year, faster than the general price level. Overall verdict: clean accounting, cyclically overstated level. We estimate that a 20% decline in gold from here would reduce annual EPS by roughly $1.00-$1.25 through scrap alone within a year, and by more over time as smaller loans earn smaller fees.

AFF provisioning deserves a final note. Provision rates of 23.6% (leases) and 29.1% (loans) of originations and delinquency rates above 22% are normal for this subprime product, and charge-offs actually improved. But AFF's shrinking volume flatters current-period provisions, because provisions are taken at origination: fewer originations mean lower provision expense now, while revenue from older leases continues. That dynamic helped AFF's sequential improvement in Q3 and will fade if volume stabilizes.

### Forecast Logic

The adjusted net margin is 10.8% TTM and should rise modestly as the lower-margin AFF shrinks and pawn grows, but falls if gold reverses and retail margins compress. Shares fall about 1-2% a year through buybacks net of equity grants. Ramsdens is included in the base and bull cases.

### Reverse Valuation

At $185.35, a 7.74% cost of equity and a 14 times exit multiple, the market requires FY2031 adjusted EPS of about $19.22, an 11.8% annual growth rate from the $10.99 FY2026 consensus. At a 16 times exit multiple, the requirement falls to $16.82 and 8.9% a year; at 12 times, it rises to $22.42 and 15.3%. Using a 9.5% hurdle rate instead, the required growth at 14 times is 13.7%. In plain terms, today's price assumes FirstCash keeps growing earnings at roughly its pre-gold-boom pace of low double digits and keeps a mid-teens multiple. It does not assume a gold collapse, but it does not give much credit for continued gold strength either.

### Takeaway

The model says FirstCash is roughly fairly priced after the selloff, with a modest skew to the upside if pawn demand outlasts the gold cycle. The base case returns about 7% a year plus a 1% dividend; the bear case loses 20% over five years; the bull case doubles. The key variable is not AFF, which is too small to move the outcome, but the gold-adjusted growth rate of the pawn book.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Management's capital allocation record is strong and consistent. Over the past twelve months FirstCash funded $235 million of growth in existing-store earning assets, $89 million of store acquisitions, $18 million of de novo openings, $87 million of real estate purchases and $349 million of shareholder returns from $679 million of operating cash flow, plus modest borrowing. Buybacks totaled 1.367 million shares at an average $201.49 for $275 million; year to date, 1.21 million shares at $206.66. Those prices are above today's $185.35, so in hindsight the company bought high, but at 17-19 times forward earnings for a business earning 20% on equity they were not reckless. The dividend has grown for nine consecutive years and was raised 10% to $0.46 a quarter; it consumes only about 17% of adjusted earnings. Only $50 million remains on the July 2026 buyback authorization, and Ramsdens will absorb about $308 million of cash, so buybacks may slow in the next two quarters. (FirstCash Q3 2026 release; StockAnalysis statistics and dividends.)

Leadership is in transition. On July 22, 2026, FirstCash announced that Rick Wessel will hand the CEO role to President and COO T. Brent Stuart on January 1, 2027, and become Executive Chairman for at least three years; Stuart joined the board immediately. The handoff is well planned and keeps Wessel, the architect of the Cash America merger and the acquisition strategy, involved. The risk is subtle: the new CEO takes over just as the gold tailwind may fade and AFF needs a strategic decision, and investors will judge him on numbers partly outside his control. (FirstCash leadership succession release and 8-K, July 22, 2026.)

Governance is otherwise conventional. Insiders own 8.5% of shares and institutions own essentially the entire float. The CFPB matter was settled in 2025. The shareholder-firm "investigations" announced after the October 22 drop by the Law Offices of Howard G. Smith, Frank R. Cruz and Glancy Prongay Wolke & Rotter are standard solicitations that follow most large single-day declines; none cites a specific misstatement beyond the AFF disclosure itself, and AFF's merchant bankruptcies had been disclosed previously. They are worth monitoring for an actual complaint, not worth pricing as a liability. (StockAnalysis statistics; Business Wire notices, October 22-23, 2026.)

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

Bulls argue that pawn is a structurally growing business in an inflationary, credit-tight world, that FirstCash is the best operator and consolidator in it, and that the selloff confused a shrinking 11% segment (AFF) and a daily gold move with the 89% of the business that is accelerating. They point to 28% pawn growth, records in every segment, a 15 times forward P/E below the five-year average, and management buying stock above $200.

Bears argue that the pawn boom is a gold boom. Gold has roughly doubled since early 2024, scrap revenue quadrupled, loan sizes rose, and the market was paying 18 times forward earnings for it. If gold mean-reverts, scrap profits collapse, loan balances shrink, forfeited inventory sells at lower margins, and the earnings base resets lower just as leverage sits at 2.7 times and a new CEO takes over. AFF adds a second shrinking line and a history of regulatory trouble.

What the market may be missing is the natural hedge inside the pawn model and the difference between gold's effect on earnings levels and on earnings growth. When gold falls, FirstCash lends less per item, but customers still need cash; loan counts can hold while balances shrink, and the shrinking book releases cash that funds buybacks at lower prices. The 2011-2015 gold decline, when gold fell roughly 40% from its 2011 peak, cut FirstCash's scrap profits and slowed growth, but it did not break the business. The variant view is therefore not that gold does not matter, but that a gold decline would compress earnings by perhaps 10-15% over a year or two, not by the 20% the stock fell in two days, and that the core loan-count growth visible in Latin America (where loans average only $103 and jewelry was just 42% of collateral a year ago) is the better indicator of underlying demand. That view supports a positive bias, but it would be reckless to rate the stock a buy before seeing how receivables behave in a falling-gold quarter.

## 10. Bull Case

The bull case is that FirstCash compounds adjusted EPS about 16% a year to $23.58 by FY2031 and re-rates to 17 times earnings, for a target of $400.90, 116% above today. The causal chain starts with pawn demand. If inflation keeps squeezing lower-income households and bank and fintech credit stays tight, demand for small non-recourse loans keeps growing in the high single digits on a loan-count basis. FirstCash adds about 100 stores a year through de novo openings in Latin America and acquisitions in the U.S. and U.K., plus Ramsdens' 175, so store growth contributes 3-4% a year. Gold holds near $4,000, which keeps loan sizes and scrap profits at today's levels rather than adding to them.

Margins then expand because AFF shrinks to a smaller, cleaner merchant base and stops dragging the mix, because H&T and Ramsdens reach U.S.-like efficiency on the FirstPawn system, and because owned real estate lowers rent. The adjusted net margin rises from 10.8% to 13%. Buybacks reduce shares by about 1% a year. With five more years of 15%+ EPS growth and a still-low beta, the market pays 17 times, near the top of the historical range. This case requires everything to go right, including gold, and is weighted at 25%.

## 11. Bear Case

The bear case is that gold falls back toward $3,000 over the next two years, AFF keeps shrinking, and the market assigns a value-lender multiple. The mechanism runs through three channels. First, scrap gross profit, about $130 million annualized at the Q3 run rate, falls by half or more, removing roughly $1.20 of EPS. Second, average loan sizes in the U.S. and U.K. fall 15-20% as jewelry collateral is revalued, so loan fees grow only with loan counts and stores, perhaps 3-5% a year instead of 12-20%. Third, inventory bought or lent against at high gold values is sold at lower retail margins, compressing the 42% U.S. and 35% Latin American margins by a few points for a year.

AFF contributes a smaller drag. If furniture demand stays weak and FirstCash keeps tightening merchant quality, AFF's pre-tax income could fall from about $120 million to $60-80 million. Interest costs rise as older notes are refinanced near 6%. Revenue grows only 3% a year from FY2026, the net margin stays near 11%, and buybacks slow because cash goes to Ramsdens and debt reduction. FY2031 EPS reaches $13.40, only 4% a year above the FY2026 consensus, and the market pays 11 times, near EZCORP's discount plus a regulatory haircut. The target is $147.37, 20.5% below today. The EV/EBITDA cross-check is harsher at $114 because $3.0 billion of net debt absorbs more of a lower enterprise value. This case is weighted at 25%.

## 12. Base Case

The most likely outcome is that gold drifts sideways to modestly lower, scrap profits fade gradually rather than collapsing, and the core pawn business keeps growing at a high-single-digit to low-double-digit rate through stores and same-store loan growth. AFF finds a floor in 2027 at a smaller size. Ramsdens closes around year-end and adds about 5% to the store base with U.K. margins in the high 20s to low 30s. Revenue grows 8% a year to $6.41 billion by FY2031, the adjusted net margin edges up to 12% as AFF shrinks and the U.K. integration matures, and the share count falls to 41.0 million.

That produces FY2031 adjusted EPS of $18.75, an 11.3% annual growth rate from the FY2026 consensus, somewhat below the provider's 20% three-year forecast because we assume part of the gold windfall reverses. At 14 times, slightly below the five-year average of 16 times to reflect higher leverage and the gold risk, the target is $262.50, 41.6% above today and about 6% above the $247.50 analyst average target. The EV/EBITDA cross-check gives a nearly identical $260. Annualized, that is about 7.2% price appreciation plus a 1% dividend yield: a solid but unexciting return from today's price.

### Primary Framework

Forward P/E on adjusted EPS is the primary lens. FirstCash's earnings are recurring fee and retail income from a diversified store base, the adjustments to GAAP are modest and well disclosed, and consensus and management both frame the business in adjusted EPS. A free-cash-flow multiple is less reliable here for two reasons: net debt of $2.6 billion is 6.6 times adjusted free cash flow, which puts the stock in the leverage range where small changes in implied enterprise value swing equity value sharply; and free cash flow is struck after funding pawn-loan growth, which understates owner earnings in boom years and overstates them in bust years. EV/EBITDA is used as a cross-check because it captures the leverage directly.

### WACC

The CAPM cost of equity is 7.74%: a 4.99% 10-year Treasury yield, a 0.55 five-year beta and a 5% equity risk premium. Pre-tax cost of debt is about 6.0%, anchored on the 6.125% coupon of the 2034 notes and TTM cash interest of $143 million on about $2.4 billion of funded debt; the after-tax cost at a 26.5% tax rate is 4.4%. With a 73.9% equity weight, WACC is 6.87%, close to the provider's 6.35%. The low beta reflects pawn's countercyclical demand, but it understates the regulatory and commodity risks of a subprime consumer lender, so we also show results at a 9.5% judgmental cost of equity. (Yahoo Finance ^TNX, October 27, 2026; StockAnalysis statistics; model WACC sheet.)

## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The biggest risk is a sustained fall in gold. It is the single input that most affects earnings and that management cannot control: two-thirds of pawn collateral is jewelry, scrap gold provides about a third of this year's pawn profit growth, and U.K. pawn is almost purely a gold-lending business. A 20-30% decline would not threaten solvency, but it would likely cut EPS by 10-15% and remove the growth that justifies a mid-teens multiple.

Regulation is the second risk. Pawn fees are capped by state and national law, the CFPB pursued FirstCash over Military Lending Act compliance until 2025, and AFF's lease-to-own model sits in a part of consumer finance that regulators periodically target. In Mexico, which hosts 1,732 stores, a change to consumer-lending or pawn regulation would affect the fastest-growing segment. The third risk is leverage: at 2.7 times adjusted EBITDA, with Ramsdens adding debt, an earnings decline would push leverage toward the 3.5 times covenant and limit buybacks. Currency is the fourth: peso strength added about 8% to Latin American revenue in Q3, and a reversal would subtract it. Finally, integration and succession risk are real: H&T's aged inventory rose to 11.6% from 7.9%, and the CEO changes on January 1.

## 16. What Would Change My Mind

We would turn more bullish if fourth-quarter results show same-store pawn receivables still growing at a double-digit rate in the U.S. and Latin America despite gold's autumn decline, because that would prove the demand is loan-count driven; if the stock fell toward $165 without a change in fundamentals, where the base case compounds at roughly 10% a year; if FY2027 guidance supports EPS of $12 or more; or if the new CEO announces a sale or wind-down of AFF on reasonable terms.

We would turn bearish if gold falls below about $3,500 and average loan sizes decline sequentially in the U.S. and U.K., if scrap gross profit falls by more than half from the Q3 run rate, if pawn segment margins compress by more than two points, if leverage rises above 3.0 times after Ramsdens, or if a regulatory action targets pawn fee structures in Mexico or the U.S.

## 18. Conclusion

FirstCash is a high-quality pawn compounder whose stock fell 20% in two days after a record quarter, mostly because investors reconsidered how much of the record was a gold windfall and how much a shrinking AFF segment would cost. At $185.35 and 16.9 times FY2026 adjusted earnings, the market prices roughly 9-12% annual EPS growth, which is close to our base case. The probability-weighted value of $268 in FY2031 discounts back to about today's price at the CAPM cost of equity.

The stance is Watch with a positive bias. The main reason to own it is that the core pawn business is growing faster than at any time in years, earns 20% on equity and funds its own growth, buybacks and a rising dividend. The main risk is that gold reverses and takes a third of this year's pawn profit growth with it. The next trigger is the Q4 2026 report and FY2027 guidance in late January or early February 2027, which will be the first quarter in which falling gold and rising loan demand can be measured against each other.

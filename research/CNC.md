## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

Centene looks like a cheap managed-care recovery. The stock has more than doubled from the $31.63 52-week low that marked the FY2025 goodwill-impairment and Medicaid-acuity scare, Q3 adjusted EPS of $0.58 beat a $0.10 Street number by 497%, and management raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to greater than $5.20 from greater than $4.80. At 13.2x that number, CNC screens cheaper than a high-quality national peer and cheaper than its own mid-cycle history.

The real question is whether 2026 is a new earnings run-rate or a restoration year that still contains $0.50 of items management itself would not expect to recur, sitting in front of 2027 Medicaid attrition from OB3 and work requirements, a Marketplace book that just lost enhanced subsidies, and a Medicare Advantage franchise that only just reached breakeven by shrinking. The stock is interesting because the operating evidence of cost-trend control is better than the 2025 narrative, and because the multiple has already expanded with that evidence. The thesis works if 2027 adjusted EPS grows through $5.20 and Medicaid HBR keeps improving as membership falls. It fails if 2027 is a $4.70 year that the market has to rediscover after paying 13x for $5.28.

### The Core Debate

The debate is not whether Centene can produce mid-single-digit adjusted EPS. Q1–Q3 2026 already did, four quarters in a row versus a Street that was behind. The debate is whether the 2026 print is quality earnings on a smaller, better-priced book, or a cocktail of PDP outperformance, Marketplace margin that management called non-recurring in part, and Medicaid rate matching that has not yet been tested by the next wave of eligibility cleanup. If the former is true, 12–13x forward is still a discount to a repaired managed-care franchise. If the latter is true, the cheapness is a 2026 multiple on 2026 extras.

## 2. Company Story

### Origin And Evolution

Centene began in 1984 in Wisconsin as a Medicaid-focused managed care organization and spent the next two decades becoming the company states hire when they want someone else to take medical-cost risk on TANF, CHIP, ABD, and later Medicaid expansion populations. The 2001 NYSE listing (December 13) financed a multi-state roll-up. The economic engine that still matters is contracted Medicaid membership: Centene bids, states pay a per-member per-month premium, Centene pays hospitals and physicians, and the spread after SG&A is the underwriting profit.

The 2010s turned that engine into a conglomerate. The 2016 Health Net acquisition added California and a commercial/Marketplace capability. The 2020 WellCare acquisition, closed into the first year of COVID, added Medicare Advantage, standalone Part D, and more Medicaid geography, and it is the acquisition whose goodwill Centene wrote down by $6.72 billion in FY2025. Magellan and smaller specialty deals filled out behavioral health, pharmacy, and “Other.” By FY2022 the KPI premium-and-service total was $144.5 billion, of which Medicaid was still $93.2 billion. The company had become the largest Medicaid MCO in the United States and a top-tier Marketplace issuer under the Ambetter brand, with a Medicare book that never quite earned its cost of capital.

FY2025 was the year the market stopped giving the conglomerate the benefit of the doubt. GAAP net income went from $3.31 billion in FY2024 to a $6.67 billion loss. Operating income compressed from $4.92 billion to $1.29 billion. Goodwill was cut from $17.56 billion to $10.84 billion. The stock printed $31.63. The 2026 story under CEO Sarah London is the unwind of that year: raise Marketplace price and mix until pretax margin is 5%, take Medicare Advantage to breakeven by shrinking the footprint, hold Medicaid HBR near 93.5% while states clean eligibility ahead of OB3, and cut SG&A (adjusted 6.5% in Q3 versus 7.0% a year ago) with a $480 million workforce and enterprise-optimization program that is kept outside adjusted earnings. Q3 2026 is the first quarter where all three lines printed that script at once.

### Why The Company Matters

Centene is how a large fraction of American Medicaid is actually delivered. Traditional Medicaid membership was 10.54 million in Q3 2026; total Medicaid lives were 11.9 million. That is not a niche state-plan administrator. It is a parallel public-health system whose medical-cost ratio is a political number as much as an underwriting number. When HBR is 93.6%, Centene is retaining 6.4 cents of each Medicaid premium dollar before SG&A — and states, CMS, and state budget offices are watching that 6.4 cents. The company also writes a large individual Marketplace book (Ambetter, year-end 2026 membership guided around 3.2 million) and a Medicare book that includes both MA and a PDP franchise that grew from about 8.1 million members in 2025 to about 9.0 million in 2026.

That combination is why CNC is a policy stock. Enhanced Marketplace subsidies (eAPTCs) rolling off, OB3 eligibility rules, work requirements, Florida CMS membership rolling off on October 1, California removing Unsatisfactory Immigration Status populations from managed care, and CMS Stars for the 2028 MA payment year are not overlay risks. They are the volume and mix of the P&L. The November 2026 midterm “blue wave” commentary that tagged CNC as a beneficiary is the same point from the other direction: Medicaid funding and Marketplace subsidies are the top line.

## 3. Business Model

### Revenue Model

Almost all of Centene’s income-statement revenue is premium. TTM premiums and annuity revenue were $175.82 billion of $180.41 billion total. The rest is interest and dividend income on the investment portfolio ($1.55 billion TTM) and other revenue ($3.05 billion). States, CMS, and Marketplace members pay Centene to take medical-cost risk. Centene pays providers. The spread is health-benefits ratio (medical costs over premium) minus SG&A minus interest.

The four segments on the KPI page — which uses a premium-and-service total of $203.90 billion TTM, not the $180.41 billion income-statement figure — are Medicaid ($118.48 billion), Medicare ($42.26 billion), Commercial ($38.40 billion), and Other ($4.76 billion). Medicaid is still the company. Medicare TTM is up from $23.03 billion in FY2024 because 2025–2026 was a PDP and MA premium year, not because MA membership is compounding. Commercial is the Marketplace/Ambetter book plus a smaller group residual; FY2025 Commercial KPI of $42.00 billion is the high-water mark before eAPTC-driven attrition. Other has been in runoff since FY2022 ($11.53 billion then, $4.76 billion TTM).

Street FY2026 revenue of $200.02 billion (14 analysts; company guide $196.5–$200.5 billion) versus TTM $180.41 billion looks like 11% growth. It is mostly a full-year of 2026 premium rates plus mix, not new lives. Street FY2027 revenue of $195.32 billion is −2.35%. The Street already believes 2027 is a smaller company on the top line.

### Economic Engine

The engine is simple and unforgiving. Premium is contracted. Medical cost is not. A 50 basis-point miss on HBR on $176 billion of premiums is $880 million of operating income, which is a quarter of TTM EBIT. That is why Q3’s 92.7% consolidated HBR “better than expected” is the whole quarter, and why Medicaid HBR of 93.6% (30 bp better sequentially, on track for ~93.5% full year) is the franchise metric. SG&A is the second lever: adjusted SG&A was 6.5% in Q3 against 7.0% last year, with a full-year midpoint held at 7.2% because Q4 is seasonal. Investment income of about $1.5 billion is a third, non-operating, lever that management is holding in the 2026 guide.

Cash generation is real but lumpy. TTM operating cash flow is $4.72 billion and FCF $3.94 billion after $781 million of capex. FY2024 OCF was only $154 million because working capital went the other way. Q3 2026 OCF was −$3.7 billion because Centene paid out more than $3 billion of Medicaid pass-through payments that had been sitting on the balance sheet at Q2. Year-to-date OCF was still $4.3 billion. Owner earnings in this business are trailing-twelve-month OCF minus capex, averaged across a pass-through cycle, not any one quarter.

### Unit Economics

Managed-care unit economics are PMPM. Management’s full-year 2026 composite Medicaid rate view is “roughly 5%.” Against that, they are guiding year-end 2026 Medicaid membership down 8–9% versus year-end 2025, with Q3 already −5% year to date at 11.9 million members. Rate up, lives down, acuity up is the 2026–2027 identity. If rate and cost-management cover the acuity, HBR improves and EPS compounds on a smaller book. If they do not, HBR worsens on the way down and 2027 earnings follow membership.

Marketplace unit economics flipped in 2026. Management is taking pretax margin to the top of a 4.5–5% range and called 5% an “incredible turnaround” from summer 2025. That turnaround is price, metal mix, and trend, not growth: Marketplace membership is guided to about 3.2 million at year-end, slightly down from Q2. Medicare Advantage is being run for margin, not members — management expects MA membership down year over year in 2027 so the remaining footprint can expand margin. PDP is the opposite: 8.1 million members in 2025 to about 9.0 million in 2026, pretax margin mid-to-high 3%, with a 2027 reset flagged in the same sentence.

Interest coverage of 5.46x (TTM EBIT $3.46 billion / interest $634 million) and a company 2026 interest midpoint of $610 million say the debt is serviceable at restored EBIT and tight at FY2025’s $1.29 billion of operating income. That is the reason 2025 felt existential and 2026 does not.

Medicaid HBR of 93.6% in Q3, 30 basis points better sequentially, is the number that says the core is stabilizing. Behavioral health trend has now had another quarter of year-over-year stabilization; home health is “beginning to” follow; high-cost drugs remain a focus. Composite rate ~5% is unchanged since July. The membership path is the residual risk, not the rate path.

Medicare HBR of 93.8% was better than planned, and PDP was the majority of the outperformance. MA breakeven in 2026 is a year ahead of the multi-year plan. Stars results released in October 2026 affect the 2028 payment year, so they are a 2028 bid problem, not a 2027 P&L problem. The 2027 P&L problem is the smaller MA footprint and the PDP margin reset.

Commercial / Marketplace is the swing segment. TTM KPI $38.4 billion is already below FY2025’s $42.0 billion. Management is not fighting that. They are harvesting 5% pretax and walking into 2027 open enrollment with more tailored products and an expectation of slight market contraction. That is a margin-over-growth choice. It is also why 2026 Marketplace contains $180 million of the $0.50 non-recur.

### Competitive Position

In Medicaid, Centene’s competitors are Molina, Elevance’s Medicaid franchise, United’s community plans, and a long tail of state-only plans. Scale in bidding, encounter data, and provider networks is the advantage; political and procurement risk is the tax on that advantage. Florida CMS rolling off on October 1 is what scale looks like when a state changes its mind. In Marketplace, Ambetter competes with Oscar, Molina, Elevance, and local Blues; the 2026 price-up is an industry event, not a Centene-only event, which is why management can talk about 5% PTI as a category turnaround. In MA, Centene is not United or Humana. It is a subscale book being made smaller on purpose. That is rational. It is not a moat.

### Moat Assessment

The moat is Medicaid switching costs at the state-contract level — multi-year procurements, implementation risk, and the political cost of ripping out a plan that covers millions of lives — plus Ambetter brand recognition on the exchanges. It is a narrow, renewable moat, not a structural one. Every three to five years a state can put the book out to bid. CMS can change MA rates and Stars. Congress can change Marketplace subsidies, and in 2026 it did. None of that is “no moat.” It is a moat that has to be re-underwritten on a political calendar. At 13x forward earnings, the market is not paying for a wide moat. It is paying for a functioning one.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Earnings quality is noisy-turning-cleaner, not clean.

The operating line is the honest one. TTM operating income of $3.46 billion on $180.41 billion of revenue is 1.92%, up from FY2025’s 0.73% and still well below FY2023–FY2024’s 3.4%. Policy benefits of $159.95 billion TTM against $175.82 billion of premiums is an 11.0-point gap before SG&A — that is the underwriting spread, and Q3’s 92.7% HBR says it is widening in the right direction. Stock-based compensation is $236 million, 13 basis points of revenue. Capex is $781 million. This is not a SBC or capex-distortion story.

The GAAP line is still distorted. FY2025 net income of −$6.67 billion is a $6.72 billion goodwill charge plus $511 million of asset-sale losses plus $75 million of writedowns. TTM still includes $516 million of asset-sale losses and $397 million of merger and restructuring. Q3 GAAP EPS was −$0.14 against adjusted $0.58. Full-year 2026 guidance is GAAP greater than $3.46 versus adjusted greater than $5.20 — a $1.74 gap, of which $480 million of workforce and enterprise optimization (most taken in Q3, some trailing into Q4 and early 2027 amortization) is explicitly outside adjusted. Those optimization costs are cash. Adding them back forever would overstate owner earnings. Using only GAAP would treat a restoration year as if the impairment were still running. The right denominator for valuation is adjusted EPS with a haircut for cash add-backs, which is why the bear case uses the company’s own $4.70 2027 jump-off rather than $5.28.

Cash versus earnings is the managed-care version of quality, and it is lumpy rather than fake. TTM OCF of $4.72 billion exceeds TTM net income of $1.46 billion because D&A is $1.23 billion and because unpaid claims rose. FY2024 OCF of $154 million against $3.31 billion of net income was the opposite working-capital year. Q3 2026’s −$3.7 billion of OCF on a $0.58 adjusted EPS quarter is Medicaid pass-through timing, previewed on the Q2 call, not a hole in earnings. Trailing-twelve-month FCF of $3.94 billion is the number to use; any single quarter is not.

Non-operating income is not the CLBT problem. Investment and other income of about $1.5 billion is recurring float on $17.7 billion of investments plus cash, and management is holding that figure in the 2026 guide. It is a real earnings contributor in an insurance-adjacent balance sheet. It is also rate-sensitive, which is a 2027 risk if the 10-year stays here but reinvestment yields fall, or a tailwind if they do not.

The FY2025 effective tax rate is unusable (a $51 million benefit on a $6.73 billion pretax loss). TTM 23.91% and FY2024 22.62% are the rates that matter. WACC in the workbook uses 23.91%.

### Forecast Logic

The workbook uses forward P/E, not FCF multiples. TTM FCF of $3.94 billion is 8.8x the market cap and would produce equity values that look cheap only because they add $8 billion of restricted cash as if it were a special dividend. Parent cash is $175 million. Net debt is not high enough to flip the sign of an FCF target, but it is in the zone where cash classification dominates the answer. Primary engine is FY2031 adjusted EPS times an exit P/E in the managed-care band.

Bear: 0% five-year revenue CAGR from TTM $180.4 billion, terminal EPS $4.40, exit 10.5x → $46.20 (−34%). This is the company’s $4.70 2026 jump-off with a little decay if OB3 attrition and the PDP/Marketplace reset bite, at a trough multiple. Revenue does not collapse; Medicaid rate still exists. Earnings fail to compound.

Base: 2% five-year CAGR, terminal EPS $6.05, exit 12.5x → $75.62 (+8%). ### Reverse Valuation

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

The bull case on the Street is mechanical: four adjusted-EPS beats, a raise to greater than $5.20, MA breakeven a year early, Marketplace at 5% pretax, 13x forward, $75 average target. Truist, BofA, and Barclays are at $82. Bernstein is at $79. That is the restoration narrative, and Q3 printed it.

The hold case is the majority: 12 of 21 analysts. J.P. Morgan at $75, UBS Neutral at $78, Morgan Stanley Equal Weight at $71, TD Cowen Hold at $65. They are not arguing the company is broken. They are arguing $70 already discounts 2026. The November 6 session, −6% from $74.17 on no new fundamental news after a “fresh high” run, is that camp trading.

The variant is not a secret about medical cost. Behavioral-health stabilization and Marketplace high-acuity-silver trend are now in the transcript. The variant is about 2027 arithmetic the market is sliding past. Management published a $4.70 jump-off in the same call that raised 2026 to $5.20, then said 2027 should grow through both. Street $5.55 takes the second half of that sentence and ignores how much of 2026 was PDP at mid-to-high 3% and Marketplace extras. If February’s guide is “grow versus $4.70” rather than “grow versus $5.20,” the multiple on next-twelve-month earnings has to be recalculated in public. That is the piece that is not in the $75 target.

What the market may also be missing is parent liquidity. EV of $26.6 billion makes CNC look net-cash. It is not, at the holdco. A 2027 that requires capital — a large Medicaid reprocurement, a ratings downgrade, a reserve add — hits a $175 million cash account, not a $23.6 billion one.

## 10. Bull Case

The bull case is that 2026 is the first year of a multi-year margin-restoration cycle, not a one-year bounce, and that 13x understates a book whose HBR, SG&A, and MA profitability are all still mid-repair.

Medicaid HBR of 93.6% with a 93.5% full-year target is the start, not the finish. Management still has a pipeline of cost-management initiatives after 18 months of work, and they are explicitly trying to improve HBR in 2027 even as membership falls. Composite rate ~5% has been stable since July. If states keep matching acuity — the collaboration they described as “good” — then lives down and rate up is earnings-neutral to earnings-accretive. Behavioral health has now stabilized year over year; home health is turning. Those were the two lines that broke 2025.

Medicare Advantage breakeven a year early resets the jump-off for 2027–2028. A smaller footprint that is profitable is worth more than a larger footprint that is not. PDP at 9 million members with a higher direct subsidy is a volume tailwind even if margin resets off the 2026 high. Stars are a 2028 issue; 2027 is a bid-construction year on a book that is already at zero, not a book that has to climb from a hole.

Marketplace at 5% pretax, if it holds, is a structural re-rating of Ambetter. Open enrollment positioning is for margin. eAPTC roll-off was the 2026 event; 2027 is the tail. If membership stabilizes near 3.2 million at 5% PTI, Commercial stops being the 2025 problem child.

SG&A is the quiet bull lever. Adjusted 6.5% in Q3, $480 million of optimization running through 2027, and management naming SG&A as a 2027 tailwind. On $200 billion of revenue, 20–30 basis points is $400–600 million of pretax, which is $0.60–$0.90 per share after tax. That is most of the gap between $5.55 and $6.50.

At $6.50 of FY2031 EPS and 13.5x, the stock is $87.75, in line with the Street high of $87. Required: 2027 adj EPS actually prints above $5.20, Medicaid HBR does not reverse, and MA stays at least breakeven on the smaller footprint. Probability 20%.

## 11. Bear Case

The bear case is not a revenue collapse. Street already has FY2027 revenue at $195 billion, down 2%. The bear case is that 2026 adjusted $5.20 was a high-water mark stuffed with $0.50 of non-recur, and that 2027 earnings land on or below the $4.70 jump-off while the multiple compresses because the restoration story broke.

OB3, work requirements, Florida CMS already gone, and California’s Unsatisfactory Immigration Status removal are not fully visible in Q3’s −5% year-to-date Medicaid membership. The remaining 3–4 points of the 8–9% year-end drop hit Q4 with acuity. If 2027 is another high-single-digit membership decline, rate at 5% does not cover the mix. HBR that was 93.6% and “on track” becomes 94% plus, and Medicaid — 58% of KPI premium — does the damage.

PDP margin resets toward 2% rather than holding mid-3s. Marketplace 5% PTI was a 2026 mix-and-price event that does not repeat once bronze/silver mix and remaining program-integrity attrition flow through Q1 2027 open enrollment. MA “breakeven” reverses because the 2027 bid undershoots trend on a shrinking book, and remaining $10.84 billion of goodwill takes another charge. None of that has to happen together. Any two of them put 2027 adjusted EPS in the mid-$4s.

The multiple then does what it did in 2025, just with less violence: 10.5x on $4.40 is $46.20, 34% below the quote, still above the $31.63 crisis print because the company is not taking a $6.7 billion impairment in this case. Parent cash of $175 million means there is no buyback put. Restricted cash cannot be a takeout or a tender. Probability 30%, and it has to sit below the current price or the scenario model is decoration.

## 12. Base Case

The base case is the Street case with a restoration-multiple ceiling. FY2026 adjusted EPS lands a little above $5.20, in line with $5.28 consensus. FY2027 grows to something near $5.55, which is growth versus both the $4.70 jump-off and the $5.20 guide, as management claimed, but not by enough to re-rate the stock. Medicaid membership finishes 2026 down 8–9% and 2027 down another low-to-mid single digit as work requirements spread; HBR improves slightly because rate-matching and the cost-management pipeline offset most of the acuity. MA stays around breakeven on fewer members. Marketplace holds a 4–5% pretax margin on a slightly smaller book. PDP margins reset toward 3% and membership holds. SG&A is a modest tailwind, not a 50-basis-point event.

Five-year revenue CAGR of 2% from TTM $180 billion takes terminal revenue to about $199 billion, which is roughly FY2026 Street revenue with no further expansion — consistent with a membership-down, rate-up world. Terminal EPS $6.05 is $5.55 grown at about 2% per year, i.e. operating leverage that is real but small because premium growth is small. Exit 12.5x is a mid-cycle managed-care multiple for a company whose ROE is still climbing through cost of equity. Target $75.62 is 8% above the quote and 0.3% above the $75.39 average analyst target. Probability 50%. This is a fair stock if February confirms it, not a cheap one.

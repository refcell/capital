## 1. Thesis In One Page

### The Setup

On looks like a premium athletic brand whose growth, gross margin, and balance sheet are much better than its post-Q2 share price implies. The real debate is not whether the CloudTec shoes are popular. It is whether management deliberately constrained lower-quality wholesale volume while preserving a durable direct-to-consumer growth engine, or whether the August sales miss and reduced outlook exposed a broader demand slowdown. The distinction determines whether the current 15.14x forward adjusted P/E is an attractive reset or a value trap before further estimate cuts.

The business entered the debate from a position of strength. TTM revenue was CHF3.220 billion, up 18.48%; gross margin was 64.82%; operating margin was 13.77%; and FCF margin was 13.60%. DTC revenue reached CHF1.386 billion versus CHF1.834 billion of wholesale revenue, making DTC roughly 43% of sales. The balance sheet carried net cash, not net debt. Yet Q2 revenue missed expectations, the FY2026 top-line outlook moderated, tariffs and cautious consumer spending became visible concerns, and analysts cut targets sharply after the release. Those facts justify a lower multiple even though they do not prove the brand is impaired (StockAnalysis financials and overview, June 2026 TTM; Reuters and analyst summaries dated August 11-18, 2026).

The thesis works if DTC remains the faster-growing channel, full-price sell-through protects at least a mid-60s gross margin, and revenue resumes high-teens growth after the wholesale reset. It fails if the company is using “discipline” to describe weakening orders, inventory grows faster than demand, or tariff mitigation requires price increases that damage conversion. The decisive evidence is channel growth plus inventory and margin—not adjusted EPS alone.

### Current View

The stance is **Watch / selective buy**. The workbook produces $16.52 bear, $45.48 base, and $96.29 bull values, with a $48.40 probability-weighted value. The base aligns closely with the $44.63 analyst-average target, but the weighted value is pulled upward by a very high bull outcome; it should not be mistaken for a precise twelve-month target. The risk/reward is attractive for an investor willing to wait for Q3 confirmation, but the Q2 reset and target-cut cluster argue against treating one low share price as proof that revisions are complete.

## 2. Company Story

On was founded in Switzerland around a recognizable cushioning system and a narrow promise: a soft landing followed by an explosive takeoff. The economic significance of that origin is focus. Rather than compete initially as a complete sportswear conglomerate, On used running footwear to establish technical credibility and a visual identity, then expanded into all-day, outdoor, training, tennis, apparel, and accessories. The September 2021 NYSE listing financed international expansion and made the brand's growth visible to U.S. investors.

The brand now sits between performance equipment and premium lifestyle. That position can support pricing because consumers may buy for both function and identity. It also creates tension. A technical running brand can lose credibility if distribution becomes indiscriminate; a fashion-led brand can lose momentum when silhouettes change. Management's stated wholesale discipline therefore matters economically: constraining shipment volume can preserve scarcity, retailer quality, and full-price realization, but only if end demand remains intact.

Roger Federer contributes recognition and credibility beyond traditional running, while tennis and lifestyle products widen the addressable market. The company has also invested in owned retail and e-commerce. Those channels provide customer data, launch control, and better gross economics, but they introduce leases, store labor, fulfillment, and marketing costs that wholesale partly absorbs. The company story has moved from product validation to channel and category scaling; execution now matters more than novelty.

## 3. Business Model

On designs and markets footwear, apparel, and accessories, then sells through wholesale partners and direct-to-consumer channels. TTM wholesale revenue was CHF1.834 billion and DTC revenue was CHF1.386 billion. Wholesale remains larger, provides reach, and transfers part of retail working capital to partners. DTC is strategically valuable because On controls presentation, price, customer relationship, and product data, but its incremental contribution margin depends on fulfillment, returns, rent, and customer acquisition.

The engine is: build technical and cultural relevance; launch differentiated products at premium prices; use selective wholesale for discovery and scale; convert customers to direct channels; broaden purchases across occasions and categories; protect full-price sell-through; and turn gross profit into cash for product, stores, and per-share value. The weak link is inventory. Footwear requires sizes, colors, seasons, and lead times. If demand slows after production commitments are made, markdowns can rapidly turn a high gross margin into cash tied up in stock.

Unit economics are not publicly disclosed in CAC or lifetime-value form. The usable proxies are gross margin, DTC mix, inventory turns, receivable conversion, store productivity, repeat purchase, and full-price sell-through. TTM gross margin of 64.82% and inventory turnover of 2.72x indicate premium economics with meaningful working capital. The model should not assume DTC growth is automatically accretive; the company must demonstrate that extra gross margin exceeds the incremental cost of stores, digital marketing, returns, and logistics.

## 4. Products, Segments, And Moat

Footwear is the core profit pool and the credibility engine. Performance running remains the most important category because it validates cushioning, fit, durability, and athlete association. All-day and lifestyle extend the installed brand into more frequent use. Outdoor, training, and tennis broaden occasions. Apparel and accessories create upside, but their economics, inventory risk, and competitive intensity may differ from shoes; they should not receive equal thesis weight without category disclosure.

On's moat combines product recognition, brand identity, athlete credibility, selective distribution, and a growing DTC relationship. The visible CloudTec sole creates immediate recognition, while premium pricing can fund research, storytelling, stores, and service. The moat is not a patent fortress. Nike, Adidas, Hoka/Deckers, New Balance, Asics, Brooks, Puma, Lululemon, and specialty brands compete through cushioning, athlete ecosystems, distribution, and marketing. A competitor does not need to copy CloudTec exactly; it needs to offer an equally compelling performance-and-style proposition.

The strongest evidence for the moat is sustained growth with rising gross margin: revenue rose from CHF724.6 million in 2021 to CHF3.014 billion in 2025, while gross margin increased from 59.38% to 62.83% and reached 64.82% TTM. The counterevidence is volatility around expectations. A premium growth brand whose stock falls sharply after a single miss is being valued on continued momentum. The moat can be real while the equity remains vulnerable to the rate of growth.

## 6. Quality Of Earnings

Earnings quality is **good but working-capital sensitive**. TTM net income of CHF396.2 million converted into CHF525.4 million of operating cash flow and CHF437.9 million of FCF after CHF87.5 million of capex. OCF exceeded net income, and capex was only 2.7% of revenue. The 13.60% FCF margin supports the view that On has become a cash-generative brand rather than merely a revenue-growth story (StockAnalysis cash-flow statement, June 2026 TTM).

The bridge is not perfectly clean. TTM stock-based compensation was CHF72.3 million, equal to 16.5% of reported FCF. Shares rose 0.95% year over year, so compensation is creating a real per-share cost. Working capital consumed CHF88.5 million TTM, including CHF102 million from inventory and CHF34.7 million from receivables. That use is normal in growth, but it becomes lower quality if inventory growth outpaces demand or receivables rise because partners are slower to pay.

Net income itself contains tax volatility. The TTM effective tax rate was 7.95%, below a normal mature-company assumption. The model uses 15% for WACC and does not capitalize the low rate as perpetual. Net income growth of 191.5% also reflects comparison with a weak prior period and below-operating-line effects; operating income growth and cash conversion are more reliable normalization anchors.

## 7. Forward Projections And Scenario Model

StockAnalysis consensus dated September 3, 2026 calls for FY2026 revenue of CHF3.51 billion, up 16.54%, based on 27 analysts. Adjusted EPS is CHF1.41, up 76.53%, and is explicitly non-GAAP. The visible FY2027 headlines show CHF4.17 billion revenue, up 18.62%, and CHF1.68 adjusted EPS, up 18.92%; detailed later-year tables are gated. The consensus therefore expects reacceleration after the 2026 reset, a meaningful assumption rather than a conservative baseline.

The bear case assumes 8% five-year revenue growth, 10% terminal FCF margin, a 15x exit FCF multiple, and modest dilution to 350 million shares. It yields $16.52 per share. The base assumes 14% growth, 14% FCF margin, a 21x multiple, and 340 million shares, yielding $45.48. The bull assumes 20% growth, 17% FCF margin, a 27x multiple, and 335 million shares, yielding $96.29. Financial values are translated to USD at an approximate 1.24 USD/CHF rate for valuation consistency.

The model says the stock can work with durable mid-teens growth; it does not say the bull outcome is likely merely because the share price fell.

## 8. Management, Governance, And Capital Allocation

Management has chosen growth investment over cash return. It has expanded owned retail, product breadth, geography, and talent while maintaining net cash. That is sensible while store-level and category returns are attractive. It becomes value destructive if retail leases add fixed cost just as demand normalizes or if product proliferation raises inventory complexity without repeat purchase.

The Q2 decision to limit some wholesale volume is a governance and capital-allocation test. Protecting brand equity may sacrifice near-term revenue for better long-term margins and channel health. Investors should demand a measurable bridge: which accounts, products, or geographies were constrained; what sell-through supported the decision; and whether the displaced volume appeared in DTC. Without that evidence, “discipline” is not independently verifiable.

Share count increased despite positive FCF, and SBC was material. On has the balance sheet to offset dilution, but repurchasing at a premium multiple can be inferior to high-return growth investment. The appropriate scorecard is diluted shares per unit of revenue and FCF, not buyback dollars alone.

## 9. Market Debate And Variant Perception

Bulls see a global brand early in its penetration curve. Revenue still grew 21.6% at constant currency in Q2, DTC grew approximately 34%, and gross margin reached 65.4%, according to the August 11 earnings-call summary. The full-year guide retained low-20s constant-currency growth, at least 65% gross margin, and 19.5%-20% adjusted EBITDA margin. Those are not distressed-brand numbers.

Bears focus on expectations and channel truth. Reported sales missed, guidance moderated, cautious consumers and tariffs became visible, and a wide group of firms reduced price targets. Jefferies retained an Underperform with a $20 target, below the current share price, while the overall consensus remained Buy with a $44.63 average. The target range from $19.82 to $73.28 is evidence that reasonable observers disagree about normalized growth and multiple.

The variant perception is that the market may be collapsing three separate questions into one selloff: end demand, wholesale strategy, and tariff cost. If demand remains healthy while wholesale is deliberately curated and tariffs are mitigated without damaging conversion, the current multiple is too low. If all three are adverse, even 15x forward adjusted earnings is not protection because the denominator will fall.

## 10. Bull Case

If On sustains roughly 20% growth while DTC deepens, apparel and new sports extend purchase frequency, and premium sell-through supports a 17% FCF margin, then current consensus understates terminal earnings power. A 27x exit FCF multiple would be defensible for a global premium brand still compounding at a high rate. With $1.1 billion terminal net cash and limited dilution, the model yields $96.29.

The evidence would be DTC growth above 25%, wholesale reacceleration in selected partners, gross margin at or above 65%, inventory growth below sales, and positive per-share FCF growth. The bull case does not rest on celebrity association alone; it requires repeatable product franchises, successful non-running categories, and stores that earn attractive four-wall returns.

## 11. Bear Case

If the Q2 miss reflects broad demand deceleration, wholesale partners reduce orders, tariff mitigation requires promotions, and owned retail adds fixed cost, revenue can slow toward high single digits and FCF margin can fall to 10%. A 15x terminal FCF multiple is then appropriate for a still-profitable but ordinary consumer brand. Modest dilution and only $600 million of terminal net cash produce $16.52 per share, 41% below current.

The earliest evidence would be inventory growing faster than revenue, DTC growth slowing sharply, declining gross margin, and another reduction to guidance. The bear case does not require insolvency. On's net cash and premium gross margin provide resilience; the downside comes from estimate and multiple compression.

## 12. Base Case

The base case treats the reset as partly strategic and partly cyclical. On remains a share gainer, but growth fades from its historical 30% pace toward 14% over five years. DTC mix and scale preserve a 14% FCF margin, near the current 13.6%, while an exit multiple of 21x recognizes durable growth without assuming permanent hypergrowth.

The resulting $45.48 is close to consensus and requires no heroic margin expansion. It does require that inventory stays controlled, dilution remains modest, and FY2027 broadly reaccelerates toward the visible CHF4.17 billion revenue estimate. Until Q3 provides evidence, the base case is an underwriting hypothesis rather than a confirmed recovery.

## 13. Valuation

At $27.99, ONON trades at 19.11x trailing GAAP earnings, 15.14x forward adjusted earnings, 2.35x sales, 17.31x FCF, 15.76x EV/FCF, and 14.03x EV/EBITDA. The 5.78% FCF yield is unusual for a brand still expected to grow revenue at a high-teens rate. Net cash reduces enterprise-value multiples and limits financial risk (StockAnalysis statistics, updated September 7, 2026).

The primary framework is terminal FCF because On is profitable, cash-generative, and lightly capital intensive. Forward P/E is a cross-check because public EPS estimates are non-GAAP and reported in CHF while shares trade in USD. The model's 14.39% WACC reflects a 2.08 beta and captures the equity's volatility; scenario discount rates range from 9.5% to 12% to avoid using one point estimate as false certainty.

Reverse valuation is constructive. The current price is below the $45.48 base value and close to the analyst low only under the bear case. To justify $27.99 over time, On does not need 30% growth; it needs low-to-mid-teens growth, approximately current FCF margins, and no major brand impairment. The unresolved risk is that consensus has not finished falling.

## 14. Catalysts

The next earnings report is the primary catalyst because August 11 results are already public. Q3 must show whether reduced wholesale shipments preserved channel quality and whether DTC absorbed demand. Investors should focus on constant-currency revenue, channel growth, gross margin, inventory, and full-year guidance rather than a narrow EPS beat.

Secondary catalysts include tariff mitigation, product launches, owned-store productivity, apparel and tennis traction, and analyst revision stabilization. A target increase without estimate stabilization is less meaningful than revenue estimates holding after actual results. Confirmation is operational: healthy sell-through and margin alongside controlled inventory.

## 15. Risks And Red Flags

The biggest risk is that On is a momentum-sensitive consumer brand whose apparent wholesale discipline masks weakening demand. Premium pricing is vulnerable when consumers trade down, while wholesale partner resets can create abrupt shipment volatility. Tariffs add a second mechanism by raising landed cost; management must choose among price, margin, sourcing, and volume.

Inventory is the immediate financial red flag. CHF472.9 million is not alarming by itself, but it rose after FY2025 while guidance moderated. DTC expansion adds lease obligations and fixed operating costs. Competition is intense, and product preference can shift quickly. The 2.08 beta and 37.5% trailing twelve-month share-price decline demonstrate how rapidly valuation can change when expectations move.

## 16. What Would Change My Mind

A more bullish view requires two consecutive quarters of high-teens or better constant-currency growth, DTC outgrowth with stable contribution economics, gross margin near 65%, inventory growth below sales, and no further material estimate cuts. Evidence that apparel and new categories create repeat purchases without discounting would raise the terminal-growth case.

A bearish view would follow another guide cut, wholesale and DTC weakness at the same time, gross margin below 63%, inventory growth above revenue, or accelerated share dilution. Evidence that tariff mitigation depends mainly on promotion or that owned stores have weak returns would also invalidate the base case.

## 18. Conclusion

On is a high-quality but not yet fully proven global brand at a valuation that now offers a credible margin of safety if the August reset is mostly channel discipline and temporary consumer pressure. The balance sheet, gross margin, DTC growth, and cash conversion are real strengths. The Q2 miss, inventory trajectory, tariffs, and guidance cuts are equally real reasons to wait for confirmation.

The current stance is Watch / selective buy, with $45.48 as the base fair value, $16.52 bear, and $96.29 bull. The main reason to care is a 15.14x forward adjusted P/E and 5.78% FCF yield for a net-cash company with high-teens consensus growth. The main risk is that demand and estimates deteriorate together. The next earnings report—not the already-released August quarter—is the decisive trigger.

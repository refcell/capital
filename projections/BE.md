## Read

- The bear case is a multiple-compression and growth-deceleration case, not a revenue-collapse case. Revenue compounds from $3.52B (FY2026 low) to $10B by FY2036, and operating margins settle at ~5.6%, but the terminal P/E compresses toward 25x — an industrial/manufacturing multiple that applies if fuel cells are viewed as a niche rather than a platform. Even in bear, Bloom Energy generates $350M of net income — the drawdown comes from valuation, not business failure. The bear 10-year CAGR of +1.0% is essentially a money-losing outcome from current levels.

- The base case anchors FY2027 at Yahoo Finance consensus $6.43B revenue and $4.43 EPS, then growth decelerates from 26% to 20% to ~10% over the decade. Operating margin expands from 7% to ~7% as revenue growth accelerates, 'other expense' share shrinks, and operating leverage kicks in. Terminal P/E of 40x reflects an energy-infrastructure premium above pure manufacturing norms. The 10-year CAGR of +13.2% is driven by earnings compounding from ~$2.17 FY2026 to $5.05 FY2036, not multiple expansion.

- The bull case requires BE to become the default on-site clean power solution — fuel cells deployed at data centers, hospitals, factories, and commercial buildings. Revenue doubles to $28B by FY2036 with 8% operating margins (5%+ from 'other expense' normalization). The market sustains 55x P/E on the basis that the installed base creates switching costs analogous to utility infrastructure. This is achievable but demands near-flawless execution, continued AI capex, and no supply-chain or competitive disruption. The 10-year CAGR of +30.8% reflects both EPS compounding and multiple premium.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2026 and FY2027 revenue and EPS anchors are taken directly from Yahoo Finance visible consensus estimates ($3.75B revenue, $2.17 EPS base; $4.39B / $2.69 bear; $8.37B / $6.69 bull). These are the only years with explicit low/avg/high analyst estimates on the public page. FY2028+ are extrapolated.
- Operating margins are set at ~7% for FY2027 (consistent with TTM 6.7% at current scale), then expand toward 5.6% bear, 7% base, and 8% bull as the 'other expense' share shrinks at large revenue bases. The 'other expense' drag is assumed to remain 10-15% of revenue in bear but declines to 5-7% in base/bull as the company matures.
- Net income margin incorporates a forward tax rate of approximately 21% (federal statutory), with no meaningful tax credits assumed beyond current levels. The TTM effective rate of 21.6% is unreliable (tiny income base) but the statutory rate is a reasonable proxy.
- Per-share math uses approximate diluted share counts with negligible growth from current ~284.4M (deal-related dilution largely complete). SBC dilution estimated at ~2% annually: 290M FY2027, 310M FY2029, 340M FY2031, 400M FY2036.
- FCF Yield column is normalized earnings yield (projected EPS / projected stock price) as a proxy, because FCF approximates net income plus depreciation for capital-intensive manufacturing and the earnings yield captures the return an owner receives at the given price. Reported FCF margin of 9.4% TTM is the quality benchmark.
- Revenue growth is YoY from the prior fiscal year for consecutive displayed years. For FY2029 and FY2031, it is the annualized growth rate from the prior displayed fiscal year.
- Terminal P/E anchors are 25.0x bear (industrial/manufacturing norms), 40.0x base (energy-infrastructure premium), and 55.0x bull (definitive clean-power platform). These are below current levels (~117x forward, effectively infinite TTM due to negative net income) because multiple compression is inevitable if growth decelerates from current 85-130% rates.
- Interim stock prices are walked back from each case's FY2036 terminal price to the current $254.29 price on a constant annualized price-return path, per projections/README.md.
- FY2028, FY2029, FY2031, and FY2036 rows are extrapolated beyond visible public estimates. They are anchored to the research note (`research/BE.md`) scenario model and the 6-sheet workbook (`models/[2026-07-08] Bloom Energy Model.xlsx`) terminal-year math.

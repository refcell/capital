## Method

Operating income is StockAnalysis standardized operating income (after R&D, SG&A and acquired-intangible amortization, before unusual items). Net income is non-GAAP adjusted net income implied by the EPS column times 5.70 billion shares, not GAAP net income. FCF yield equals projected FCF (revenue × assumed FCF margin) divided by projected market capitalization (5.70 billion × path price). Stock prices follow a constant annualized path from $30.52 to each case's FY2036 terminal price, which is FY2036 adjusted EPS times an explicit terminal P/E. They are valuation paths, not targets. The workbook's scenario prices are FY2031 EPS × exit P/E and are not the same as this sheet's FY2031 path prices.

Near-term FCF margins start from TTM 17.3% and glide toward the workbook's 12% / 16% / 18% terminal margins. The $19.17 billion FY2026 FCF consensus is not used: it would require acquisitions to stop and working capital to reverse in a way TTM does not show.

## Scenario Read

The one-year range is $28.61 bear, $30.66 base and $32.01 bull, a tight band because each path compounds evenly toward a terminal value nine to ten years away. The real near-term range is wider: the stock has already run from $23.62 to $30.52 in four months, Q3 prints November 3, and a 2027 guide miss would look more like the workbook's $17–$30 gap than like this sheet's year-1 prices.

Long-term outcomes diverge less than in a growth stock because Pfizer's base is $60 billion of revenue and the bull case is 3% growth, not 30%. Between bear and bull, FY2036 revenue differs by 1.8× ($47 billion versus $84 billion) and adjusted EPS differs by 2.1× ($2.00 versus $4.10). Leverage does not amplify per-share outcomes the way it does at a build-cycle company: the share count is assumed unchanged, and net debt is a drag on FCF-multiple math rather than a going-concern risk. The base case's 0.5% annual price return plus a mid-5% dividend is a cost-of-capital return, which is why the research stance is Watch. The bear case's −6.3% price CAGR is a slow de-rate, not a collapse; owners who reinvest the dividend still lose less than the price path implies, until or unless the dividend is cut. The bull case needs obesity or ADC duration that the FY2027 consensus does not contain.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2027 base revenue and EPS equal visible consensus ($59.89 billion, $2.88). FY2026 company guide and consensus are current-year anchors, not year-1 rows.
- All FY2028+ revenue, margins, FCF and EPS figures are extrapolated beyond visible public estimates. Gated StockAnalysis cells are not presented as consensus.
- Year-five (FY2031) revenue matches the workbook: bear $51.94 billion, base $60.57 billion, bull $72.07 billion.
- Operating margins: bear 26% falling to 20%; base 29% falling to 26%; bull 31% rising to 33%. TTM is 29.6%. FY2026 StockAnalysis operating-income consensus of $21.27 billion (34%) is treated as closer to adjusted EBIT and is not used as the GAAP operating-income path.
- FCF margins: bear 15% falling to 12%; base 16%; bull 18% then 17%. TTM is 17.3%. Workbook terminal FCF margins are 12% / 16% / 18%.
- Shares outstanding held at 5.70 billion in every year. No buybacks, no material dilution.
- Net income column is adjusted EPS × 5.70 billion, not GAAP net income. GAAP remains distorted by impairments in the near term.
- Dividend is not in the price path. Starting yield 5.64%. Bear coverage is the open question; base and bull cover $1.72–$1.90 easily on the FCF implied here.
- Revenue growth is year over year for consecutive displayed years; FY2027 growth is versus FY2026 consensus of $62.22 billion; FY2031 and FY2036 growth is annualized from the preceding displayed year.
- Interim stock prices follow a constant annualized path between the October 30, 2026 price and each FY2036 terminal price; year n is n years after the quote date.
- Terminal P/E anchors are 8.0× / 10.0× / 12.0×, the same exit multiples as the workbook, applied to FY2036 EPS rather than FY2031 EPS.

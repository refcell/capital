## Scenario Read

The dispersion is driven less by FY2026, which is largely visible, than by the credibility of FY2027 and the persistence of growth afterward. The bear misses the public FY2027 anchor and loses multiple. The base accepts consensus but fades growth. The bull exceeds consensus and sustains leadership. FCF margins range from 35% to 44%, compared with 44.22% TTM and 46.2% in Q3.

The valuation asymmetry is mixed. The bear terminal price is only modestly below today's price, but interim market drawdowns could be much worse because investors would reprice the forecast before 2036 earnings arrive. The base generates a respectable 10.7% price CAGR, while the bull compounds at 18.4%. Dividends add modestly to each path but are excluded from the table.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2026 base revenue is anchored by Broadcom's $34.8 billion Q4 guide plus reported nine-month revenue; FY2027 base revenue of $173.10 billion and adjusted EPS of $19.49 are visible StockAnalysis headline consensus as of September 4, 2026.
- Every FY2028, FY2029, FY2031, and FY2036 row is extrapolated. No gated provider figure is presented as public consensus.
- Adjusted operating income excludes substantial acquisition amortization, SBC, restructuring, and other items in a manner directionally comparable to management reporting. It is not GAAP operating income.
- Normalized net income uses explicit scenario margins of 31%-40%. It should not be read as management guidance.
- FCF yield equals scenario FCF divided by scenario market capitalization. FCF margins are 35% bear, 40%-42% base, and 43%-45% bull before settling at the stated terminal levels.
- Terminal P/E anchors are 18x, 24x, and 30x. They apply to normalized diluted EPS. The base and bull multiples require continued double-digit growth and durable program wins.
- Interim prices are constant-CAGR paths from $357.90 to each terminal price. Actual prices will be volatile and need not follow a smooth path.
- Share counts move from roughly 4.76 billion to 4.90 billion bear, 4.80 billion base, and 4.70 billion bull. These assumptions explicitly recognize that $8.45 billion of TTM repurchases did not prevent 1% YoY share growth.

## Read

The bear case is a traffic-failure case, not a revenue-collapse case. Sales still grow from $9.3B to $11.3B over ten years on new stores, and the company still earns ~$4 of EPS, but the January reset does not restart trips, EBIT margin sticks near 5%, and the multiple goes to 9×. Year-1 price is $40, 26% below the quote. That is the required shape: a bear case with downside. Ten-year CAGR is −3.6%. Capital is slowly destroyed, not wiped out, because grocery still throws off cash.

The base case is the Street case. FY2027 uses the last public consensus ($9.78B / $4.60). Earnings recover only to $5.10 by FY2031, still below FY2025’s $5.31, because the price investment is permanent enough to cap margins in the mid-6s. Terminal 12× is a specialty grocer, not a growth stock. Year-5 price $61.20 is the workbook base and the $61 average PT. Ten-year price $81.60 is a 4.1% CAGR from $54.40, roughly in line with WACC less a small equity-risk leftover. This is a fair stock, not a cheap one.

The bull case requires the core fleet to start winning trips again. That is a 2027 operating outcome, not a 2026 multiple outcome. Even then the terminal P/E is 13×, not 20×. Year-5 $72.80 and year-10 $96.20 are the upside if Konat’s reset works. They are not a re-rating back to the 52-week high of $94.89 at peak margins; they are a grind higher on repaired earnings.

The model workbook uses a WACC of 7.35% and a forward-P/E scenario engine. FCF multiples are not the primary framework: lease-adjusted net debt of $2.01B is 6.0× TTM FCF, which is the gray zone where equity value = FCF × multiple − net debt is dominated by the lease gross-up.

## Current Run-Rate

FY2026 revenue was $707.0 million, of which $578.2 million came from Bitcoin mining that will be shut down by December 2026 and $128.8 million from AI Cloud. Q4 FY2026 revenue was $137.2 million (AI Cloud $70.5 million), adjusted EBITDA $19.2 million, and the GAAP net loss $684.0 million after $450.4 million of impairments. Operating ARR doubled to $1 billion after Microsoft accepted Horizon 1 on August 13, so Q1 FY2027 is the first quarter with a meaningful AI Cloud base. The revenue step-up comes mainly in the March 2027 quarter, after Horizons 2-4 and other capacity are delivered late in calendar 2026.

The capital structure dominates per-share outcomes. IREN had 394.06 million shares, $5.90 billion of unrestricted cash, $1.72 billion of restricted cash, $7.84 billion of debt and $1.84 billion of customer prepayments at the last report. Management guided FY2027 capex at $25-30 billion against about $14 billion secured. Every case below therefore assumes large additional net debt and share issuance; the cases differ mostly in how much is built and at what per-share cost.

## Method

Operating income is adjusted operating income: adjusted EBITDA less depreciation and amortization, before stock-based compensation and before impairments, held-for-sale marks, derivative marks and convert inducement costs. Net income is normalized: adjusted operating income less SBC ($300 million in FY2027, then 3% of revenue), less interest on average net debt (6.5% bear, 5.5% base, 5.25% bull, reflecting a mix of 0.25-1.00% convertible notes and 6-9% secured GPU financing), less 21% tax when pre-tax income is positive. Depreciation runs at 48-52% of revenue in FY2027, because GPUs start depreciating before they bill a full year, and falls to 29-32% by FY2036, consistent with five- to six-year GPU lives and ~$20M of revenue per MW. Stock prices follow a constant annualized path from $23.95 to each case's FY2036 terminal price; they are valuation paths, not targets.

## Bull Projection

Terminal P/E anchor: **14.0x FY2036 normalized EPS**. The bull case assumes 2026 capacity is delivered on time, 2027 capacity sells at $20-25 million per MW with prepayments, and the >5GW pipeline lets billing capacity reach roughly 1.6GW or more by FY2031 for $24.0 billion of revenue. Managed services and scale lift adjusted EBITDA margin to 65%. Data center debt replaces most equity issuance, and the stock's recovery lets the 2032 and 2033 convertible notes act as cheap equity under their capped calls. Shares reach 520 million by FY2031 and 545 million by FY2036; net debt peaks at $52 billion.

## Scenario Read

The one-year range is $22.40 bear, $25.62 base and $27.64 bull, narrow because each path compounds evenly toward a terminal value nine to ten years away. The real near-term range is much wider: the stock has moved an average of 9.4% the day after each of its last five reports, fell 39% between September 30 and October 23, and has a five-year beta of 4.27. The visible analyst target range of $37-$139 sits entirely above this one-year path, which shows how far sell-side targets are from pricing in funding costs.

Long-term outcomes diverge because leverage amplifies every operating assumption. Between the bear and bull cases FY2036 revenue differs by 3.0x ($12.1 billion versus $36.2 billion), but normalized EPS differs by 15x ($0.91 versus $13.79), because interest and depreciation are largely fixed once the assets are built and share counts move in the opposite direction. FCF stays negative in every case until about FY2031, so per-share outcomes depend on how the build is financed. The base case's 10.7% annual return is acceptable but not generous for that risk; the bull case's 24.1% requires both execution and a friendly funding market; the bear case's -9.6% requires only that pricing per megawatt resets before the build is paid for.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2027 and FY2028 base revenue equals StockAnalysis consensus ($2.81 billion and $7.41 billion). All other revenue figures are extrapolated beyond visible public estimates, and no gated data is presented as consensus.
- Year-five (FY2031) revenue and net debt match the workbook scenarios: bear $10.5 billion and $22 billion, base $16.0 billion and $38 billion, bull $24.0 billion and $50 billion.
- Adjusted EBITDA margins: bear 28% (FY2027) rising to 50%; base 33% rising to 60%; bull 36% rising to 65%. Bitcoin mining contributes in FY2027 only.
- Depreciation as a share of revenue: bear 52% falling to 32%; base 50% falling to 31%; bull 48% falling to 29%.
- Interest is charged on average net debt at 6.5% (bear), 5.5% (base) and 5.25% (bull). Interest income on cash is netted inside net debt.
- Shares: bear 470 million (FY2027), 650 million (FY2031), 683 million (FY2036); base 440, 580 and 610 million; bull 430, 520 and 545 million. Deep-in-the-money legacy 2029/2030 convertible notes are assumed to convert; the 2032 and 2033 notes are treated as debt.
- Capex: bear $24 billion, base $27 billion, bull $30 billion in FY2027, falling to $4 billion, $7.5 billion and $10 billion a year by FY2034-FY2036. Customer prepayments of $5.0-7.5 billion in FY2027 are added to FCF.
- FCF yield equals projected FCF (adjusted EBITDA less interest, tax and capex, plus prepayments) divided by projected market capitalization (scenario share count × path price).
- Revenue growth is year over year for consecutive displayed years; FY2027 growth is versus FY2026's $707.0 million; FY2031 and FY2036 growth is annualized from the preceding displayed year.
- Interim stock prices follow a constant annualized path between the October 30, 2026 price and each FY2036 terminal price; year n is n − 0.34 years from the quote date.

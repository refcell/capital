## Read

- Bear assumes no renewed travel shutdown; flat demand, cost pressure and persistent leverage are enough to produce downside.
- Base assumes the visible FY2027 consensus is directionally right and revenue compounds near StockAnalysis’s 3.9% three-year forecast. Interest savings, rather than capacity growth, drive most EPS upside.
- Bull requires Carnival to convert owned destinations, pricing and fleet efficiency into both margin and debt improvement. Revenue growth alone is insufficient.
- Price CAGRs exclude dividends. Year-1 through year-5 prices bridge from the $22.70 quote to the workbook targets; year-5 through year-10 prices converge on explicit terminal adjusted EPS × P/E anchors.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2026 detailed public consensus and FY2027 visible headline values are the only explicit analyst anchors. **Every FY2028-FY2036 figure is extrapolated**, as are FY2027 operating income, net income and FCF.
- Adjusted EPS is provider-labelled non-GAAP and is not identical to GAAP diluted EPS. The scenario P/E interpretation assumes adjustments remain limited to genuinely non-recurring items.
- Revenue growth shown for year-5 and year-10 is the annualized scenario rate rather than growth from the previous displayed row.
- Net income uses an approximately stable 1.37B diluted-share denominator for mechanical P/E. Buybacks are assumed to offset issuance in base and bull; no material denominator reduction is required.
- FCF yield uses projected FCF divided by projected equity value. It is a cross-check, not the primary valuation method, because newbuild deliveries and destination projects can make annual capex lumpy.
- The model does not count dividends in price CAGR. A maintained $0.60 annual dividend would add roughly 2.6% to first-year cash return at the current quote, before reinvestment and taxes.
- The bear terminal P/E is 7.5x, base 11x and bull 14x. The spread reflects balance-sheet risk and not merely different revenue growth.

## Read

- The bear case is a China-property-collapse case. Revenue declines 5% in year 1 as iron ore volumes and prices contract. Operating margin compresses to ~13.5% as lower-grade ore must be processed to maintain cash flow. The terminal P/E compresses to 8x because the dividend story breaks and the cycle remains in a trough. Even in bear, Vale generates $3B in net income — the losses come from multiple compression, not business failure.
- The base case is the stabilization scenario. Revenue holds in the $41-53B range over the decade, with growth driven by copper and modest iron ore volume improvements. Operating margins stabilize at 15-16% as iron ore finds equilibrium. Terminal EPS of $4.19 at 9x P/E gives $37.70 — a 12.5% annual CAGR driven by earnings recovery from the trough and gradual multiple normalization toward diversified-mining peer norms.
- The bull case requires structural iron ore recovery. Chinese stimulus, infrastructure spending, and copper demand from electrification combine to push Vale into a growth trajectory. Revenue grows 4-7% annually, copper becomes a larger contributor, and the P/E re-rates to 12-14x as the market recognizes Vale's cost advantage and diversification. 10-year CAGR of 23.5% is driven by both earnings recovery and multiple expansion.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2027 revenue and EPS anchors are taken from Yahoo Finance estimates ($41.28B revenue, $2.08 EPS base; $39.03B/$1.40 bear; $43.15B/$2.49 bull). These are the only years with explicit low/avg/high analyst estimates on the public page. FY2028+ are extrapolated.
- Operating margins are set at ~15% for FY2027 (consistent with TTM trajectory of 16.1% but factoring in the Q2 EPS miss), then recover gradually in bear (13.5% sustained trough) and base (15-17% gradual recovery). Bull assumes 16.8% in year 1 rising to 16.5% long-term as iron ore recovers.
- Net income incorporates a forward effective tax rate of approximately 28% (between the current 7.57% TTM rate and the 57.38% FY25 anomaly, reflecting normalization of Brazilian tax provisions).
- Per-share math uses shares outstanding of 4.26B, declining gradually in base/bull through modest buyback resumption (4.20B by year 10) and stable in bear (4.27B with no buybacks).
- FCF Yield column is normalized earnings yield (projected net income / market cap equivalent) as a proxy. Vale's FCF is volatile around the commodity cycle and the current FCF margin of 8.63% is the quality benchmark.
- Revenue growth is YoY for consecutive displayed years. For FY2031 and FY2036, it is the annualized growth rate from the prior displayed fiscal year.
- Terminal P/E anchors are 8.0x bear (cyclical trough norms), 9.0x base (stabilized mining multiple), and 12.0x bull (recovery + copper diversification premium). These are below the trailing P/E of 31.9x and forward 7.7x because multiple compression is the mechanism in bear.
- Interim stock prices are walked back from each case's FY2036 terminal price to the current $14.94 price on a constant annualized price-return path, per projections/README.md.
- FY2029, FY2031, and FY2036 rows are extrapolated beyond visible public estimates. They are anchored to the research note (`research/VALE.md`) scenario model and the 6-sheet workbook (`models/[2026-08-05] Vale Model.xlsx`) terminal-year math.

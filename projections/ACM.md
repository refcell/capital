## Read

- The bear case is an infrastructure-cyclical-declining scenario. Revenue declines 8% in year 1 as the IIJA spending wave decelerates and government contract wins slow. Operating margin compresses to 3.5-4% as competitive bidding intensifies and cost-plus arrangements decline in favor of fixed-price contracts. The terminal P/E compresses to 9-11x because the earnings volatility becomes structural — the market refuses to reward an uncertain earnings stream. Even in bear, AECOM generates $340-380M in net income — the losses come from multiple compression, not business failure.

- The base case is the partial recovery scenario. Revenue grows from $15.4B TTM to $17-19B as government spending stabilizes and new contracts replace expiring ones. Operating margins recover to 5-5.5% — below the FY25 peak of 6.6% but above the TTM trough of 4.6%. EPS normalizes to the $6.00-6.50 range, consistent with the $6.42 FY27 consensus. Terminal P/E of 12x is a reasonable engineering/construction services multiple. 10-year CAGR of 5.3% reflects gradual multiple compression from the current depressed level back to peer norms, driven by earnings stability rather than growth.

- The bull case requires margin normalization and multiple expansion. The Q2 miss is fully confirmed as discrete, operating margins return to the 6%+ range, and the stock re-rates from 9.6x toward 15x as confidence returns. Revenue grows 4-6% annually as government spending accelerates and international operations contribute. By year 5, the stock reaches $135 — the prior 52-week high — confirming a full recovery. Terminal P/E of 15x represents premium valuation for a proven, stable earnings franchise. 10-year CAGR of 4.4% is moderate because the terminal P/E compresses to 15x from the current 14-14x run rate as growth decelerates.

## Read

- Bear assumes a lodging slowdown, margin pressure and weak per-share growth. The portfolio recovers nominally after an early dip, but the multiple compresses; even the explicit year-10 value remains below today's quote.
- Base assumes modest RevPAR and revenue growth, stable leverage and disciplined capital allocation. FFO/share reaches $1.45 at year five and $1.75 at year ten without requiring multiple expansion from 10x.
- Bull requires durable group/resort demand, successful repositioning and operating leverage after wage, insurance and renovation costs. The 11x terminal multiple remains restrained for a cyclical REIT.
- Price CAGRs exclude dividends. Prices are anchored first to the workbook's year-5 values and then worked backward from the explicit year-10 P/FFO values; therefore the year-10 CAGR need not equal the first five-year CAGR.

## Key Assumptions

- **Every fiscal year shown—FY2027 through FY2036—is extrapolated.** The only visible detailed public fiscal-year estimate is FY2026: $1.14B revenue, $0.54 adjusted EPS, and $254.62M / $1.22 per-share FFO from 11 analysts. No later table value is presented as analyst consensus.
- Revenue growth is annual for consecutive rows; for the year-5 and year-10 rows it is the annualized rate from the prior displayed row.
- Year-5 revenue and FFO assumptions follow the workbook's 0% / 2.5% / 4.5% revenue-CAGR and $1.15 / $1.45 / $1.75 FFO/share scenario structure, with revenue rebased to the requested $1.14B FY2026 public estimate. Year-5 prices are held near the workbook's $9.78 / $14.50 / $19.25 targets.
- Operating income, net income and conventional FCF are extrapolated normalized amounts, not Street estimates. A roughly stable 205.3M share count is used to calculate mechanical P/E from projected net income per share.
- FCF yield uses projected conventional FCF divided by projected equity value. It is a cross-check, not the primary metric: hotel capex includes both recurring upkeep and return-seeking renovations, making AFFO/maintenance-capex classification important.
- P/FFO is primary. P/E is mechanical and economically less meaningful because real-estate depreciation depresses GAAP net income even though hotels also require genuine recurring capital spending.

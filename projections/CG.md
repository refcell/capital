The bear case assumes Q2 2026 was a peak. Transaction fees fall back from their $110.5 million record, flagship funds raise less than their predecessors, and fee step-downs keep management fees flat, so FRE grows about 1% a year. A private-credit default cycle hits fee-related performance revenue and marks, and exits stay scarce, so realized carry is thin. After-tax DE drops to $3.30 per share in FY2027-FY2028 and recovers only to $3.60 by FY2031 and $3.97 by FY2036. The market applies a value-manager multiple of 7.5x DE by FY2036. The DE yield rises above 13% because the price falls faster than earnings; that is the signature of a permanently discounted stock, not a margin of safety. The fixed $1.40 dividend stays covered more than twice by DE.

## Read

- The spread is wide, $29.81 to $157.83 in FY2036, but skewed upward. The bear case loses 2.5% a year before dividends; the base case gains 9.4% a year and the bull case 15.1%.
- The starting multiple does most of the work. At 9.8x TTM DE, the base case only needs DE to grow 6.6% a year to FY2031 and the multiple to recover to its 2024-2025 range.
- The bear case produces downside every year, which keeps the scenarios honest: a low multiple is not a floor if fees stagnate and carry shrinks.
- The base case's one-year price is below the street's average target. It is more conservative than the sell side in the near term and more constructive over five to ten years.
- Price CAGRs exclude dividends. The fixed $1.40 dividend adds about 3.6 percentage points a year at today's price.

## Key Assumptions

- "Segment Revenue" is Carlyle's total segment revenue (fee revenue plus realized performance revenue, realized principal income and interest income), not GAAP revenue. TTM is $3,737 million and FY2025 $3,902 million. FY2026 is estimated at $3,810 million in all cases ($1,863 million in H1 plus about $1,950 million in H2), which is the base for FY2027 growth.
- "FRE" is company-defined fee-related earnings, pre-tax. "After-tax DE" is after-tax distributable earnings per share times the projected share count. These are non-GAAP measures used by management and consensus; GAAP net income will differ because of unrealized carry, equity compensation and consolidated funds.
- "DE Yield" replaces FCF yield. GAAP free cash flow (-$4.1 billion TTM) is dominated by consolidated CLO purchases funded by non-recourse CLO debt and does not reflect Carlyle's cash generation. DE yield is after-tax DE per share divided by the projected price.
- Share counts: bear 354 million (FY2027) to 348 million (FY2036); base 352 million to 320 million; bull 350 million to 295 million. Buybacks are net of equity grants; SBC was $414 million TTM.
- After-tax DE per share by FY2031: bear $3.60, base $5.40, bull $7.00, matching the workbook. FY2032-FY2036 growth is 2% / 7% / 10% a year.
- Terminal prices are FY2036 DE × the stated terminal P/E (7.5x / 12.5x / 14.0x). Interim prices are walked back to $38.59 at a constant annual return. FY2031 prices land within 2.7%-4.7% of the workbook's five-year targets for bear and base and 14% below for bull, as explained above.
- Dividends are excluded from stock prices and CAGRs.

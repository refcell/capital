## Method

Each stock-price row equals projected adjusted EPS multiplied by the P/E shown in that row. The P/E path converges to an explicit year-10 terminal anchor: 17x in bear, 22x in base, and 25x in bull. This works backward from terminal valuation rather than assigning arbitrary stock prices. Revenue, operating income, adjusted-equivalent net income, and FCF beyond FY2027 are research scenarios, not public consensus.

FCF yield equals projected FCF divided by projected market capitalization using approximately 51 million shares through year 3, 51.5 million in year 5, and 52 million in year 10. The modest dilution assumption recognizes stock compensation and acquisition activity; actual repurchases or issuance could materially change per-share results.

## Read

The near-term asymmetry is favorable but not riskless. Bear value of $155.80 implies 13.3% downside, while the model's base value of $233.28 implies 29.8% upside and the bull value of $302.40 implies 68.3% upside. The projection tables extend those one-year anchors into explicit terminal frameworks rather than assuming the analyst target is fair value.

The most decision-useful metric is not revenue growth alone. It is FCF conversion after acquisition financing. If adjusted EPS rises while FCF yield remains near 3% because working capital, capex, interest, and integration absorb cash, the forward P/E will overstate economic progress. If FCF approaches adjusted net income and debt falls, the base and bull paths become more credible.

All FY2028-FY2036 rows are extrapolated beyond visible public estimates. They should be refreshed after the October 29 results, especially the reported debt balance, FIS Water contribution, expected interest expense, and updated 2026 guidance.

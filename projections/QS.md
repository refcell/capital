## Read

- **The bear case is total dilution destruction.** Revenue stays at zero or near-zero for years as the technology fails pilot-scale testing. The $904.7M cash is consumed within 11-13 quarters. Two dilutive raises at $1.50-$2.50/share add 600M+ new shares, pushing total count to 1,178M and per-share NAV to essentially $0. The stock converges to $0.01 as the option expires worthless. VW quietly exits or downgrades the partnership from co-investment to exploratory research. The 10-year CAGR of -49.9% compounds the worst-case timeline of dilution followed by liquidation.

- **The base case anchors FY2027 at Yahoo Finance consensus $35.71M revenue and -$0.68 EPS**, then revenue grows slowly to $350M by Year 10 through VW-exclusive licensing and minor OEM deals. The technology is proven but the economics don't support premium multiples — ceramic separator costs keep per-cell margins thin. Two dilutive raises at $3-4/share add 267M shares. The stock stabilizes at $1.53 per share, which is below the current $5.86 price (73.9% drawdown) but above the $1.56 per-share cash NAV in the early years. The 10-year CAGR of -73.9% reflects the systematic per-share destruction from dilution.

- **The bull case requires VW to go all-in on production deployment.** Starting with a ~2028 pilot batch of 10,000-50,000 cells, the technology is validated for other OEMs, generating licensing revenue that begins funding operations. Revenue compounds from $57M (FY27 high estimate) to $2.5B by Year 10 as QS becomes a significant lithium-ion alternative. The stock peaks above $12 when the first production commitment is announced, then settles to $11.29 as the business matures. The 10-year CAGR of +8.4% is driven by both revenue compounding and multiple expansion from $0 (undefined) to 43.7x P/E.

- **Revenue anchors for FY2027 come from Yahoo Finance public estimates** (3 analysts; $15M-$57M range, $35.71M average). This is the only year with explicit analyst revenue estimates on the public page. Beyond FY2027, all revenue figures are extrapolated from the research note scenario model and the 6-sheet workbook. The terminal price columns work backward from explicit NAV + Pipeline NPV anchors for each case.

- **FCF yield is not meaningful** for a cash-burning development company. The "FCF Yield" column shows N/A because QS's FCF is structurally negative by design — it's spending cash on pilot production and R&D. The relevant metric for QS is cash NAV per share and the optionality premium above that NAV.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2027 revenue/earnings anchors taken directly from Yahoo Finance visible consensus estimates ($35.71M avg revenue, -$0.68 avg EPS). These are the only years with explicit analyst estimates on the public page. FY2028+ are extrapolated from the research note scenario model.
- Operating margins are deeply negative throughout the projection: QS's operating expenses ($420-460M/yr) vastly exceed any realistic revenue for the foreseeable future. Net losses assumed to narrow modestly as revenue kicks in but the company never reaches profitability in bear or base cases within the horizon.
- Per-share math uses 578.34M current shares for Years 1-2, then applies dilution factors from the workbook Scenarios sheet: Bear dilution: ~2.04x to 1,178M shares by Year 4. Base dilution: ~1.46x to 845M shares. Bull dilution: ~1.22x to 708M shares.
- Stock prices work backward from explicit terminal NAV + Pipeline NPV anchors: $0 (bear), $1.53 (base), $11.29 (bull). Reflect the research note scenario model framework (Cash NAV Floor + Pipeline Optionality).
- Revenue growth rates for FY2028+ are extrapolated from the research note scenario model and represent the three cases: collapse (bear), slow licensing (base), and production deployment (bull).
- Shares outstanding assumed to grow via dilutive capital raises in Years 1.5-4. The critical driver of per-share value destruction is the dilution factor, not the burn rate itself.
- Interest income decline is assumed in the bear/base cases (rate cuts reducing the $37.8M TTM cushion), accelerating net losses even if operating expenses remain stable.

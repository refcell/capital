## Read

- **The bear case is a total dilution and cash-collapse case.** Revenue stays near $2M/year as non-commercial sources dwindle. The $1.78B cash is consumed within 3 years at elevated $600M/yr burn rates, forcing a dilutive raise at $3-$4/share that doubles the share count. The stock converges to $1.16 per share as dilution overwhelms the cash NAV floor. This is the outcome if FAA certification is delayed beyond 2029 and no competitor displacement occurs.

- **The base case is the steady-state certification outcome.** FAA certification completes on schedule (2027-2028), but commercial ramp is incremental — launch city operators take time to convert MOUs to firm orders, and manufacturing scales slowly from the initial production batch. Revenue grows from grants and pre-commercial activity to $180M by year three as the first batches of aircraft are delivered. The stock stabilizes at $8.66 per share, which is above the current $5.18 price (67.4% upside) and above the post-dilution cash NAV floor of $1.66/share. The 10-year CAGR of +5.5% reflects moderate per-share value creation from incremental certification success.

- **The bull case requires FAA certification ahead of schedule and rapid commercial execution.** The Midnight aircraft receives Type Certificate approval in 2027, launch city operators convert to firm orders for 50+ units, and the manufacturing facility ramps to 100+ units/year by year three. Revenue accelerates to $600M by year five, approaching breakeven on the operating income line. The stock peaks at $22 during the certification announcement and settles to $26.86 as the business matures. The 10-year CAGR of +24.1% is driven by both earnings accretion from aircraft sales and a P/B re-rating from 1.94x to 3-4x on established manufacturing.

- **Revenue anchors for FY2026/FY2027 come from limited Yahoo Finance estimates** (8-9 analysts for Q1 FY26 EPS of -$0.24, $1.6M revenue). Beyond Q1 FY26, no meaningful analyst estimates are visible for ACHR. All figures for FY2027+ are extrapolated from the research note scenario model and the 6-sheet workbook. The base case terminal anchor of $8.66 reflects probability-weighted framework from the workbook Scenarios sheet.

- **FCF yield is not meaningful** for a cash-burning pre-commercial developer. The "FCF Yield" column shows N/A throughout because Archer's operating cash flow is structurally negative — $487.4M burn TTM — and will remain so until aircraft sales scale. The relevant metric is cash NAV per share and the optionality premium above that NAV.

- **Stock prices work backward from explicit terminal NAV anchors:** $1.16 (bear — max dilution, no pipeline value), $8.66 (base — certification on schedule, moderate pipeline), $26.86 (bull — ahead of schedule, strong commercial ramp). These anchors are consistent with the research report probability-weighted framework producing $11.34 fair value.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2026 and FY2027 revenue anchors taken from limited Yahoo Finance public Q1 FY26 data ($1.6M Q1 rev, -$0.24 EPS) extrapolated annually. No full-year consensus available on public pages. All figures beyond Q1 FY26 are extrapolated.
- Operating margins are deeply negative throughout the entire projection horizon: Archer's operating expenses ($470-840M/yr) vastly exceed any realistic revenue until aircraft sales achieve volume. Net losses assumed to narrow modestly in the bull case as aircraft sales reach $600M/year, but the company never reaches sustained profitability in this 10-year horizon.
- Per-share math uses 762.86M current shares for Years 1-2, then applies dilution factors: bear 2.00x to ~1,526M shares by Year 2, base 1.40x to ~1,068M shares by Year 3, bull 1.25x to ~954M shares by Year 2.
- Stock prices work backward from explicit terminal anchors ($1.16 bear, $8.66 base, $26.86 bull). These reflect the research note scenario model (Cash NAV floor + Pipeline NPV framework).
- Revenue growth rates for FY2028+ are extrapolated from the research note scenario model. Base case assumes certification in 2027-2028 with modest commercial ramp. Bull case assumes ahead-of-schedule certification with rapid launch city adoption.
- Interest income of ~$60M/yr at current rates partially offsets burn but declines as rates fall. Not modeled explicitly but effectively reduces the net burn rate.
- Shares outstanding assumed to grow via dilutive capital raises — the key driver of per-share value destruction in the bear case and moderate dilution in the base case.

## Method

Operating income is StockAnalysis standardized operating income (after voyage costs, opex and G&A, before interest and tax). Net income is GAAP EPS times 102.40 million shares. FCF yield equals projected FCF (revenue × assumed FCF margin) divided by projected market capitalization (102.40 million × path price). Stock prices follow a constant annualized path from $41.73 to each case’s FY2036 terminal price, which is FY2036 GAAP EPS times an explicit terminal P/E. They are valuation paths, not targets. The workbook’s scenario prices are FY2031 EPS × exit P/E and are not the same as this sheet’s FY2031 path prices.

Near-term FCF margins start from TTM 11.4% (capex-cycle trough) and glide higher as the 2027–2029 MR orderbook delivers and growth capex fades. TTM OCF margin is 40%; a maintenance-only shipowner can convert a large share of that to FCF. The $795 million FY2026 FCF consensus is not used.

## Scenario Read

The one-year range is $38.10 bear, $41.08 base and $43.16 bull, a tight band because each path compounds evenly toward a terminal value nine to ten years away. The real near-term range is wider: the stock has already run from $19.30 to $41.73 in a year, Q3 prints November 4, and a 2027 guide that looks like 2025 would look more like the workbook’s $20–$38 gap than like this sheet’s year-1 prices.

Long-term outcomes diverge because tanker earnings are rate-times-days, not a compounding subscription. Between bear and bull, FY2036 revenue differs by 2.2× ($980 million versus $2.15 billion) and EPS differs by 2.7× ($2.40 versus $6.50). Leverage does not amplify per-share outcomes the way it does at a build-cycle industrial: net debt is 0.8× EBITDA at the peak, and the share count is assumed unchanged. NAV is the floor the P/E path can overshoot. The workbook’s bear NAV-implied price is $20.40; this path’s $16.80 year-10 price is a deeper earnings de-rate than the asset de-rate, which is internally consistent if second-hand values lag TCE down but do not go to zero.

The base case’s −1.5% annual price return plus a faded mid-single-digit dividend is a cost-of-capital hold, which is why the research stance is Watch. The bear case’s −8.7% price CAGR is a cycle fade, not insolvency; owners who received 2026’s $4-plus of dividends have already taken a large fraction of the bear total-return hit in cash. The bull case needs TCE/day to stay in a high-$30,000s mid-cycle that FY2025 ($2.85 EPS) did not demonstrate.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2027 base revenue and EPS equal visible consensus ($1.54 billion, $6.15). FY2026 company TCE/EBITDA guide and stale street revenue of $1.63 billion are current-year anchors, not year-1 rows.
- All FY2028+ revenue, margins, FCF and EPS figures are extrapolated beyond visible public estimates. Gated StockAnalysis cells are not presented as consensus.
- Year-five (FY2031) revenue matches the workbook: bear $1.16 billion, base $1.51 billion, bull $1.94 billion.
- Operating margins: bear 20% falling to 16%; base 30% falling to 25%; bull 36% falling to 32%. TTM is 39.1% (cycle-peak). FY2025 was 25.2%.
- FCF margins: bear 12% rising to 16%; base 14% rising to 18%; bull 12% rising to 18%. TTM is 11.4% after growth capex. OCF margin TTM is 40%.
- Shares outstanding held at 102.40 million in every year. No buybacks, no material dilution.
- Net income column is GAAP EPS × 102.40 million. Tonnage tax is assumed to remain in the 1–3% range; it is not normalized to 21%.
- Dividend is not in the price path. Starting trailing yield 10.59%. Bear coverage fails as EPS falls toward $2.40; base and bull cover a smaller, still-variable DPS on the FCF implied here.
- Revenue growth is year over year for consecutive displayed years; FY2027 growth is versus street FY2026 of $1.63 billion, which is below the TCE guide; FY2031 and FY2036 growth is annualized from the preceding displayed year.
- Interim stock prices follow a constant annualized path between the October 29, 2026 price and each FY2036 terminal price; year n is n years after the quote date.
- Terminal P/E anchors are 7.0× / 8.5× / 9.0×, the same exit multiples as the workbook, applied to FY2036 EPS rather than FY2031 EPS.

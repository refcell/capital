## Read

- **The bear case is a total dilution and cash-collapse case.** Revenue stays below $20M/yr as consulting and DOE grants dwindle. The $890M cash is consumed within 2-3 years at current burn rates, forcing a dilutive raise at $3-$5/share. The stock converges to $0.10 per share as dilution overwhelms the NAV floor. This is the outcome if NRC certification fails and no customer materializes. The 10-year CAGR of -54.4% compounds the destruction of per-share value.

- **The base case is the steady-state certification outcome.** NRC certification completes on schedule (2027-2028), but it takes additional time for utilities to execute contracts. Revenue grows slowly via grants, consulting, and DOE programs — never reaching the $500M+ required for self-funding. The stock stabilizes at $5.00 per share, which is below the current $8.60 price (40% drawdown) but above the $2.57 cash NAV floor. The 10-year CAGR of -10.1% reflects the time value of money being lost to the burn rate.

- **The bull case requires the "nuclear renaissance" to actually materialize for NuScale specifically.** NRC certification arrives ahead of schedule, the first VOYGR module is ordered by a major utility, and module revenue transforms the company from cash consumer to cash generator. The stock peaks at $30 when the first contract is announced, then settles to $20 as the business matures and the market re-rates from development option to infrastructure builder. The 10-year CAGR of +8.9% is driven by both earnings accretion from module sales and a 4-5x P/B re-rating.

- **Revenue anchors for FY2026 and FY2027 come from Yahoo Finance public estimates** (14 analysts for revenue, 9-10 for EPS). Beyond FY2027, all figures are extrapolated from the research note scenario model and the 6-sheet workbook. The terminal P/E columns are largely meaningless for a pre-commercial developer with years of negative earnings, and are shown only where the stock approaches profitability.

- **FCF yield is not meaningful** for a cash-burning development company. The "FCF Yield" column is blank because NuScale's FCF is structurally negative by design — it's spending cash on NRC certification and R&D. The relevant metric for SMR is cash NAV per share and the optionality premium above that NAV.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2026 and FY2027 revenue anchors taken directly from Yahoo Finance visible consensus estimates ($56.4M avg, $173.2M avg). These are the only years with explicit analyst estimates on the public page. FY2028+ are extrapolated.
- Operating margins are deeply negative throughout the projection: NuScale's operating expenses ($500-700M/yr) vastly exceed any realistic revenue for the foreseeable future. Net losses assumed to narrow modestly as grants and licensing contribute to revenue, but the company never reaches profitability in this horizon.
- Per-share math uses 346.11M current shares for Years 1-2, then applies a 1.60x dilution factor by Year 3 ($256M/$346M = 554M total shares). Bear dilution: 2.00x to ~692M shares by Year 2. Bull dilution: 1.30x to ~450M shares.
- Stock prices work backward from explicit terminal NAV anchors: $0 (bear), $5.00 (base), $20.00 (bull). The base and bull cases reflect the research note scenario model framework (Cash NAV floor + Pipeline Optionality).
- Revenue growth rates for FY2028+ are extrapolated from the research note scenario model and represent the three cases: collapse (bear), steady grants (base), and module sales (bull).

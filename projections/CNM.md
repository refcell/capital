## Read

- **The bear case is mechanical stagnation.** Operating margin fails to recover beyond 8%, keeping operating income essentially flat at $650-730M even as revenue creeps upward. The multiple compresses from 15.4x to 12x as CNM is repriced as a slow-growth consolidator rather than a secular beneficiary. The stock trades in the $35-38 range — a 12-18% drawdown from current levels. The FCF yield of 12.8-15.5% provides a cushion against further decline, and the buyback program ensures the downside is capped. The 10-year CAGR of −8.8% reflects the slow grind of flat margins compressing the multiple over a decade.

- **The base case anchors FY2027 at Yahoo Finance consensus $7.84B revenue and $2.81 EPS**, then compounds revenue at 3% CAGR and EPS at 4% CAGR (buyback tailwind adds ~1% over revenue growth). At 16x terminal P/E — modestly above the current 15.4x but below peer median of 18-22x — the stock reaches $67.52 by Year 10, representing a +55.5% total return and 4.6% annualized CAGR from the $43.37 starting point. The operating margin recovers to ~10% as integration completes, driving absolute earnings growth alongside the buyback-driven per-share expansion. This is the scenario most consistent with current analyst consensus and the company's demonstrated financial profile.

- **The bull case is infrastructure acceleration + margin recovery.** IIJA water spending hits full pace, adding 5%+ annual revenue growth from both volume and mix shift toward higher-margin products. Operating margin expands to 11% as integration synergies, operating leverage, and product mix improvements compound. EPS CAGR of 6% feeds into a 20x P/E re-rating — not extreme for an industrial distributor (Watsco trades at 20-25x, Grainger at 25-30x). The stock peaks near $100 as the re-rating coincides with earnings growth. The 10-year CAGR of +9.4% is the scenario where CNM proves to be a compounder rather than just a consolidator.

- **Revenue anchors for FY2027-FY2028 come from Yahoo Finance public estimates** (11 analysts for FY27: $7.74B-$7.90B range; 13 analysts for FY28: $7.86B-$8.53B range). These are the only years with explicit analyst estimates on the public page. FY2029+ are extrapolated from the scenario model CAGR assumptions (2% bear, 3% base, 5% bull) and the research note's margin recovery thesis.

- **Stock prices work backward from explicit Year-10 terminal P/E anchors:** 12.0x (bear), 16.0x (base), 20.0x (bull). These are grounded in peer comparable analysis: industrial distributors trade 12-25x P/E, with CNM's current 15.4x sitting below peer median. The 20x bull anchor is within range (Watsco ~22x, Grainger ~28x, Applied Industrial ~18x). Revenue growth rates after Year 2 are extrapolated from the workbook scenario model.

- **FCF yield is meaningful** for CNM — it is a cash-generating business with 0.6% capex/revenue. The FCF yield declines modestly over the horizon as the stock price appreciates, but it remains 10-15% throughout all cases, providing a structural floor. In the bear case at 12.8% FCF yield, the stock essentially offers a cash flow return that offsets the lack of capital appreciation.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2027-FY2028 revenue and EPS anchors taken directly from Yahoo Finance visible consensus estimates. These are the only years with explicit analyst data on the pubic page. FY2029+ extrapolated from scenario model CAGR and margin assumptions.

- Operating margin trajectory is the single largest driver of earnings outcomes. TTM margin of 9.27% vs FY23 of 11.7% reflects acquisition integration drag. The base case assumes a return to 10% by Year 5. The bull case assumes recovery to 11%. The bear case assumes further compression to 7-8%.

- FCF margin assumption: 0.6% capex/revenue is structural (distribution model) and held constant across all horizons. FCF/Revenue = Operating Margin × (1 - tax rate) + Depreciation/Revenue - Capex/Revenue - Working Capital changes. Simplified: FCF ≈ Net Income + Depreciation - Capex. With 0.6% capex, FCF ≈ Operating Income × (1 - 23.5% tax) + Depreciation, i.e., FCF margin ≈ Net Margin + 2.5% depreciation add-back, i.e., FCF margin ≈ Net Margin + ~2.5%.

- EPS CAGR = Revenue CAGR + Operating Margin expansion tailwind + Buyback tailwind (~1% from 23% share count reduction over three years annualized).

- Share count assumed stable in base/bull (buybacks roughly offset by option exercises and dilution). In the bear case, buyback decline may lead to slight share count accretion over time.

- Stock prices work backward from explicit Yr-10 terminal P/E anchors (12x/16x/20x). Intermediate prices use P/E trajectories from current 15.4x toward terminal levels.

The bear case assumes the 8.1% Q2 FY26 revenue growth was a cyclical peak, not the start of a trend. Biotech funding remains constrained, capex cycles in life sciences contract, and the company reverts to 4-5% organic growth with operating margin rolling over from 22.3% toward 20%. By terminal year, growth is flat-to-low-single-digit and the forward P/E compresses from 23.6x to 17x — reflecting a demotion from "growth compounder" to "mature steady-state."

Shares assumed flat at 281.97M (no additional buybacks in bear scenario; management redirects capital to debt reduction). Operating margin decline from 22.3% to 20-21% reflects capex pressure and competitive intensity. Revenue growth at 3% is extrapolated beyond visible estimates (which cover Q2 FY26 only).

## Extrapolation Notes

- Revenue growth rates for Years 1-10 in all scenarios are extrapolated beyond visible public analyst estimates. Yahoo Finance analyst consensus covers FY2026 and implied FY2027 only. Q2 FY26 of +8.1% is the most recent confirmed growth data point.
- Operating margin trajectories are the largest model assumption. The current TTM run-rate of 22.3% is approximately 1.3 percentage points below the FY22 peak of 23.6%. Recovery speed determines earnings power and valuation multiple.
- CrossLab segment mix is assumed at 30-35% of total revenue in the base case. Any disclosed segment-level data at the November 25 Q3 earnings call should update this assumption upward (bull) or downward (bear).
- Effective tax rate assumed at 13.5% across all scenarios, consistent with TTM calculation of $218M / $1,658M pre-tax income (13.1%). Slight buffer added for rate normalization.
- Share count assumed flat at 281.97M across all scenarios. In practice, the $380M TTM buyback program would reduce share count by 1-2% annually, boosting per-share metrics by approximately 3-5% over 10 years. The base/bull CAGR figures are conservative relative to actual per-share growth if buybacks continue.
- Terminal P/E anchors (17x bear, 21x base, 25x bull) are calibrated against medtech peer norms: Thermo Fisher ~23x, Danaher ~25x, Waters ~32x, Revvity ~26x. Agilent at 17x would be trading at a discount justified by growth deceleration; at 25x it would be in-line with the upper tier of quality medtech names.
- FCF yield in all scenarios ranges from 2.4-3.1%, which is consistent with quality medtech standards. The TTM FCF yield is approximately 2.85% ($1.267B / $44.46B MC). This is the anchor against which terminal figures are calibrated.
- The 10-year P/E path in the base case compresses from 23.3x to 21.0x — reflecting a steady-state multiple for a mature compounder. This assumes no material multiple expansion or contraction in the medium term, just a gradual normalization.

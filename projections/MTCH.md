## Read

- The base case is a mature-compounder outcome rather than a return to hypergrowth. Revenue grows about 3% annually after FY2027, operating margin holds around 30%, and the share count falls from 229.55M to roughly 170M over ten years.
- The bear case shows the central risk: high current free cash flow does not protect the stock if revenue stagnates, margins normalize downward, and the terminal multiple contracts. A shrinking denominator can mask a weakening franchise only for so long.
- The bull case requires both operating and capital-allocation execution. Hinge must scale without cannibalization, Tinder must reaccelerate, and the company must retire roughly 39% of current shares while keeping leverage controlled.
- At the current price, the one-year analyst target average offers virtually no upside. The long-term return debate therefore depends on business durability and share-count reduction, not a near-term rerating.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2027 base revenue and adjusted EPS are anchored to the visible StockAnalysis aggregate estimates of $3.62B and $4.58. Public FY2027 low/high estimates are gated, so the bear and bull revenue paths are explicit scenarios rather than claimed analyst ranges.
- Operating margins reach 25% bear, 31% base, and 34% bull by FY2036. Net margins reach 14%, 20%, and 23%, respectively.
- Diluted shares decline from 229.55M currently to approximately 210M bear, 170M base, and 140M bull by FY2036. The bull case therefore requires unusually persistent repurchases; it is not an operating-growth-only outcome.
- FCF margins reach 22% bear, 30% base, and 34% bull. The displayed FCF yield equals projected free cash flow divided by projected market capitalization in each row.
- Terminal P/E anchors are applied to projected GAAP EPS, not the higher adjusted EPS estimate. The 11x / 15x / 19x range reflects outcomes from ex-growth cash generator to renewed category leader.
- Interim stock prices are walked back from each case's FY2036 terminal value to $41.86 on a constant annualized price-return path. Dividends are excluded, so total shareholder return would be modestly higher if the dividend persists.
- Revenue growth is year-over-year for consecutive rows and annualized between nonconsecutive FY2029, FY2031, and FY2036 rows.

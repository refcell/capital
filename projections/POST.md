## Read

- The bear case is a multiple-compression and debt-overhang story. Revenue declines from $8.18B (FY2027 low) toward $6.46B by FY2036 — a 7% annual decline that reflects cereal category collapse, private-label margin compression, and interest rate sensitivity. The 10-year CAGR of -14.7% means the stock loses value from current levels. Even in bear, operating income of $499M and net income of $315M in FY2036 shows the business generates cash — the drawdown comes from P/E compression from ~15x to 8x and declining earnings, not business failure.

- The base case anchors FY2027 at Yahoo Finance consensus $8.27B revenue and $8.69 EPS. The 10-year price trajectory is essentially flat to slightly negative (-3.2% CAGR) because revenue is expected to plateau and then slowly decline with the cereal category, and the terminal P/E of 13x compresses the multiple from the current 14.5x trailing. This reflects the reality that at current levels, the stock is roughly fair-valued on a steady-state basis. The $86.70 price implies the market has already priced in the revenue plateau and leverage headwind.

- The bull case requires successful organic margin expansion from 10.5% to 11.5-12%. This is achievable through foodservice tailwinds, private-label mix improvement, and continued cost management. Revenue grows modestly at 1-1.2% annually from foodservice and pet food strength, offsetting cereal weakness. The 10-year CAGR of +3.5% is modest but meaningful — it requires multiple expansion from ~10x forward to 17x terminal, which is reasonable for a defensive CPG with stable cash flows if debt declines. The 17x P/E anchor reflects the upper bound for stable-growth CPG with improving margins.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2026 and FY2027 revenue and EPS anchors are taken directly from Yahoo Finance visible consensus estimates ($8.32B revenue, $7.71 EPS base; $8.18B / $8.06 EPS bear; $8.37B / $10.17 EPS bull). These are the only years with explicit low/avg/high analyst estimates on the public page. FY2028+ are extrapolated.
- Operating margins are set at 10.2% for FY2027 (consistent with TTM 10.5%), then contract to 8.5% bear, hold at 10-10.2% base, and expand to 11.5-12% bull. The key variable is whether the acquired portfolio can sustain or improve margins as revenue growth flattens.
- Net income margin assumes an effective tax rate of approximately 25% (TTM effective rate: $112M / $450M = 24.9%). Slightly above the federal statutory rate of 21%, reflecting state taxes and the blended rate across segments.
- Per-share math uses diluted share counts declining gradually from 58.9M (TTM) to ~52M by FY2036 via buybacks and SBC normalization. Bear assumes fewer buybacks due to cash allocation to debt reduction. Bull assumes 3-4% annual share count reduction.
- FCF Yield column represents unlevered FCF yield (projected OCF minus capex divided by market cap at projected price). Current TTM FCF yield on market cap is 13.2% ($517M / $3.93B). Projected yields decline as stock price appreciates relative to FCF in base/bull cases.
- Revenue growth is YoY from the prior fiscal year for consecutive displayed years. For FY2031 and FY2036, it is the annualized growth rate.
- Terminal P/E anchors are 8.0x bear (cyclical manufacturing norms for a declining CPG), 13.0x base (defensive CPG steady-state), and 17.0x bull (improving CPG with expanding margins). These are consistent with peer multiples: JJSF ~17x, THS ~20x, MZTI ~19x, SMPL ~16x, DLTR ~12x.
- Interim stock prices are walked forward from each case's FY2027 anchor to the current $86.70 price on a constant annualized price-return path, per projections/README.md.
- FY2028, FY2029, FY2031, and FY2036 rows are extrapolated beyond visible public estimates. They are anchored to the research note (`research/POST.md`) scenario model and the 6-sheet workbook (`models/[2026-07-13] Post Holdings Model.xlsx`) terminal-year math.
- The FCF framework was not used as primary valuation because net debt ($7.34B) >> FCF × 10 ($5.17B). Forward P/E was the primary lens. P/E is the appropriate proxy for stock price in the projection tables.

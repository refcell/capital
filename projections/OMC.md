## Bear Projection

Terminal P/E anchor: 7x FY2036 EPS. Revenue CAGR 2.0%, adjusted operating margin 3-5%, exit multiple 7x on terminal EPS. The bear case assumes the Entertainment One acquisition permanently depresses margins. Integration costs don't fade as expected, working capital demands from the entertainment unit consume cash that previously supported advertising operations, and the broader advertising cycle deteriorates. The multiple compresses further from the current 8.37x to 7x — below even the current level — as the market concludes the combined entity is structurally weaker than the pre-acquisition Omnicom. FY27 EPS comes in at the low end of consensus. No meaningful organic growth beyond the acquired base. Shares trade toward the bottom of the 52-week range.

The bull case requires successful integration with genuine synergies between Entertainment One and the advertising portfolio. The content-to-advertising flywheel creates a differentiated offering that no peer matches: Omnicom owns both the creative advertising agencies (BBDO, TBWA, DDB) and the content production/distribution platform (EONE). FY27+ EPS growth accelerates above consensus as the combined entity demonstrates its durability. The multiple re-rates from 8.37x toward 12x — approaching Publicis' current level — as the market recognizes Omnicom as uniquely positioned among the global holdcos.

## Read

- The bear case shows a 10-year CAGR of -2.1%, essentially sideways with slight erosion. This reflects a scenario where the Entertainment One acquisition permanently drags margins below the 5% range and the market progressively discounts the combined entity. At 7x terminal P/E, Omnicom would trade below its current 8.37x — the market has clearly factored in integration risk, and the bear case assumes that risk is underpriced. Revenue growth of only 2% reflects a flat-to-stagnant scenario where the larger platform generates no organic alpha. The dividend of 3.63% provides a natural floor even in this scenario.

- The base case produces a 10-year CAGR of 5.2%, translating to approximately 69% total return over the decade. This is driven by the multiple re-rating from 8.37x toward 10x as integration proves out and the combined entity demonstrates consistent execution. Revenue growth of 4.5% on the larger platform is organic and sustainable. The key assumption is margin recovery toward 10-12% by FY28. If H1 FY26 operating income trends confirm a 6%+ margin trajectory, the base case becomes the more likely path.

- The bull case at 11.8% CAGR requires meaningful synergy from the Entertainment One integration — the content-to-advertising flywheel that no peer currently offers. This is genuinely achievable if BBDO, TBWA, and DDB can leverage EONE's content library for integrated campaigns, creating premium pricing opportunities. Revenue at 7% CAGR on a $25B platform generates $1.75B in incremental annual growth — significant on an absolute basis. The 12x terminal P/E places OMC at Publicis' current level, which is reasonable if the integration proves successful.

- Stock prices in each case work backward from explicit year-10 terminal P/E anchors (7x / 10x / 12x) with FCF yield cross-checks from the workbook scenarios. The P/E trajectory in the bear case declines as the market loses patience; it rises in the base and bull cases as integration uncertainty resolves.

- Revenue growth and earnings trajectories from FY2028 onward are extrapolated beyond the visible FY26-FY27 public estimates and should be treated as projections, not forecasts. The FY27 revenue estimate of $25.11B (down from FY26 $25.84B) suggests the street views FY26 as an anomalous high point. This supports using the FY27 base for long-run growth calculations.

## Key Assumptions

- Revenue growth for FY2026-FY2027 is anchored in Yahoo Finance consensus estimates ($25,840M FY26, $25,110M FY27). Years beyond FY27 are extrapolated at scenario-specific CAGRs.
- Operating margin recovery from the current 5.0% TTM level is the single most important assumption. Without margin improvement, the earnings path does not materialize regardless of the multiple assumption. H1 FY26 operating income of ~$677M (implied from TTM $1,122M minus FY25 $445M) suggests an improvement trajectory.
- Share count normalizes at approximately 274M shares. Future buybacks may reduce this modestly, but the 40% dilution from the acquisition is the baseline.
- The workbook uses forward P/E as the primary framework. WACC of 6.48% (CAPM with beta 0.66, ERP 5%, Rf 4.66%) is the discount rate cross-check. Scenarios use exit P/E multiples (7x/10x/12x) rather than FCF multiples because TTM EBITDA is distorted by integration.
- Net debt adjustment of ~$8.07B (EV-MC proxy) subtracts from equity value in all cases. This is stable assuming no additional deal-related leverage beyond the current balance sheet.
- FY27 EPS consensus of $12.04 represents a 13.7% growth from FY26 $10.59. The EPS revisions over 30 days were modest (FY26 cut 2.8%, FY27 cut 1.6%), suggesting the consensus is relatively stable.
- FCF yield = estimated operating cash flow / market cap for that year. Higher yields in later years reflect improving profitability relative to the stock price.
- Dividend of $3.20 annualized (3.63% yield) is maintained in all scenarios as 30% payout ratio on forward EPS is sustainable.

## Method

Each stock-price row equals projected adjusted EPS multiplied by the P/E shown in that row. The multiple path converges to an explicit FY2036 terminal anchor: **11x in bear, 15x in base, and 17x in bull**. Prices therefore work backward from valuation assumptions rather than being selected independently.

Adjusted-equivalent net income equals projected adjusted EPS multiplied by projected diluted shares. Share counts decline in every case because repurchases remain part of the strategy, but the pace differs: modest in bear because cash is constrained, steady in base, and faster in bull because FCF is stronger. Actual repurchases can vary materially with price, debt, spectrum, and fiber investment.

FCF yield equals projected FCF divided by projected market capitalization at each scenario's share count and stock price. A higher bear-case yield does not mean the bear is economically superior; it means the market assigns a lower equity value because earnings quality, leverage, and growth are weaker.

## Read

The near-term asymmetry is favorable but leverage prevents a simple bullish conclusion. The model's $133.08 bear value implies 20.9% downside, the $216.60 base implies 28.8% upside, and the $272.00 bull implies 61.7% upside. Probability weighting produces $209.57, or 24.6% above the current quote.

The most important forecast variable is not headline subscriber growth. It is sustainable FCF after network capex, handset economics, fiber investment, interest, and working capital. T-Mobile can produce high per-share returns if FCF stays near 20% of revenue and repurchases occur below intrinsic value. It can also trap equity holders in a low multiple if distributions prevent deleveraging.

## Bear Projection

Terminal P/E anchor: **10x FY2036 adjusted EPS of $24**, producing a year-10 stock price of **$240**. The bear assumes the acquisition portfolio remains profitable but fails to compound per-share earnings beyond the FY2026 consensus level. Revenue begins at $4.8 billion in FY2027 and grows 2% annually. The share count stays near 47.91 million because repurchases are offset by compensation, acquisition issuance or reduced cash availability. PPL basis remains wide, PJM policy limits scarcity rents, and additional data-center contracts do not materially improve the mix.

Stock prices are calculated backward from the explicit $240 year-10 terminal value at a constant -2.2% annual price path from the current $299.97. Operating margin moves from 14% to 16%, reflecting a functioning but lower-quality merchant outcome. FCF margin declines from 18% to 15% as maintenance, nuclear fuel, interest and integration costs absorb cash. The negative stock return despite positive earnings demonstrates that balance-sheet and multiple risk—not revenue collapse—is the bear mechanism.

## Base Projection

Terminal P/E anchor: **13x FY2036 adjusted EPS of $50**, producing a year-10 stock price of **$650**. The base starts at the visible FY2027 public headline of $5.24 billion revenue and $30.75 adjusted EPS, then applies 6% annual revenue growth. This is slower than the near-term acquisition-driven step-up and assumes the portfolio matures after integration.

The operating margin rises from 22% to 25% as acquired plants contribute, AWS ramps and the contracted gross-margin mix improves. Adjusted FCF margin stays near 24%, broadly consistent with management's strong conversion outlook but below an extreme merchant-price case. Shares decline from 47.91 million toward 40 million over ten years as repurchases exceed issuance. The terminal multiple remains below a regulated utility or premium infrastructure multiple because merchant and regulatory exposure persist.

Stock prices work backward from $650 at a constant 8.0% annual path from $299.97. The intermediate P/E initially stays around 10-12 times and reaches the explicit 13 times terminal anchor as earnings and contracted quality improve.

## Bull Projection

Terminal P/E anchor: **14x FY2036 adjusted EPS of $80**, producing a year-10 stock price of **$1,120**. The bull starts with $5.6 billion FY2027 revenue and $35 adjusted EPS, both above the visible public headline. Revenue grows 9% annually as PJM remains tight, PPL basis normalizes, acquired assets outperform and the existing AWS load ramps. Talen signs additional contracts without sacrificing all merchant upside.

Operating margin expands from 25% to 30%, and adjusted FCF margin rises from 26% to 29%. Those levels require excellent fleet availability, favorable realized sparks and disciplined maintenance. Shares decline toward 35 million as at least 70% of adjusted FCF is returned through repurchases at attractive prices. Net leverage approaches the 3.5 times target even as absolute debt remains meaningful.

Stock prices work backward from the $1,120 terminal value at a constant 14.1% annual price path. The terminal 14 times multiple assumes a more contracted, infrastructure-like cash-flow mix but still discounts cyclical and regulatory risk.

## Read

The bear case does not assume bankruptcy or power-demand collapse. It assumes the forward adjusted numbers prove less durable than expected and the market applies a merchant-generator multiple. That is the appropriate downside test for a company whose balance sheet is already levered but whose assets remain economically useful.

The base offers an 8.0% annual price CAGR before any dividends, which Talen does not currently pay. This is solid rather than spectacular, despite the model's higher five-year fair value, because the projection forces the stock to earn its way toward a fixed 13 times terminal multiple over a full decade. It also avoids treating the present acquisition growth rate as permanent.

The bull's 14.1% CAGR depends on both earnings growth and successful share reduction. A $1,120 terminal price sounds extreme relative to $300, but it is only 14 times $80 of terminal adjusted EPS. The controversial assumption is the $80 EPS, not the multiple.

Across all cases, the central variable is distributable adjusted FCF per share after recurring capex, nuclear fuel, cash interest, taxes, SBC dilution and debt reduction. Revenue is a weak standalone guide because merchant margins can change sharply without equivalent revenue movement.

## Key Assumptions

The model and projection use forward adjusted P/E as the primary lens. Reported TTM net debt of $9.34 billion is about 21 times reported TTM FCF of $444 million, so an EV/FCF terminal framework would subtract a debt burden from a stale partial-period cash denominator and can create nonsensical equity targets.

FY2027 base revenue and adjusted EPS use the public StockAnalysis headlines of $5.24 billion and $30.75. The detailed public forecast table exposed only FY2026, so no undisclosed analyst ranges are implied. Bear and bull FY2027 values are internal scenarios.

Stock-price paths are geometric interpolations from the September 21, 2026 close to explicit FY2036 terminal P/E values. They are not year-specific price targets. P/E in each row is projected stock price divided by projected adjusted EPS. Net income equals adjusted EPS multiplied by scenario share count, so it is non-GAAP and should not be compared directly with historical GAAP net income.

FCF yield equals projected adjusted FCF divided by projected market capitalization. FCF is estimated from scenario revenue and margins rather than taken from public consensus because detailed long-range FCF estimates were unavailable. This is the largest extrapolation in the tables.

The share-count path is flat in bear, declines to 40 million in base and 35 million in bull. Those assumptions reflect management's stated intention to return at least 70% of adjusted FCF and its outlook for $2.8 billion of repurchases through 2028, but future board approval, prices and cash availability are uncertain.

No dividend is assumed. No explicit value is added for powered land, unsigned PPAs or development projects. Their economics enter only when they improve scenario revenue, margins and EPS. The tables also do not separately discount nuclear decommissioning obligations beyond their effect on cash flow and terminal multiples.

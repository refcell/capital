## Read

- The bear case is a "perpetual growth loser" scenario — revenue grows modestly (5-8%) but gross margin stalls at 20-22%, meaning the company structurally cannot generate operating income because OpEx at 40%+ of revenue always exceeds gross profit. The 1-year target of $3.10 (down 62% from current) reflects the risk of a distressed equity raise if cash runs out. The 10-year CAGR of -25.6% represents per-share value destruction from cumulative dilution.
- The base case is the analyst consensus doing right through FY2026-FY2027, then extrapolating the operating leverage trajectory that should materialize when revenue crosses $1.3B and the fixed cost base represents a smaller percentage of revenue. The key assumption is that gross margin recovers toward 26-28% (reversing the FY2024-to-TTM dip) and OpEx ratio falls toward 35-38%. The FCF yield trajectory (1.0% in year 2 to 9.8% in year 10) reflects capex normalization.
- The bull case is the "Microsoft of sports data" thesis — Genius Sports achieves 12-15% operating margins on $4-5B revenue with a premium multiple. The 10-year CAGR of +1,631% appears extraordinary but is mathematically consistent with $8.08 expanding to $140 over a decade if operating leverage and margin expansion both materialize.
- FY2026-FY2027 data points are anchored in Yahoo Finance analyst consensus (18-19 analysts). Beyond FY2027, all projections are extrapolated from the research model scenario parameters and represent the research analyst's best estimate of operating leverage trajectories, not published consensus data.
- The 10-year CAGR figures in the base and bull cases are arithmetic averages from the starting price of $8.08 to the terminal price — not annualized returns compounded with interim volatility. Actual investor returns would differ based on timing of entry, interim earnings surprises, capital raise events, and macro volatility.

## Read

The base case is the most important output. Despite revenue roughly doubling by FY2036, modeled price CAGR is only 1.8% before dividends because the current enterprise multiple already capitalizes much of the visible growth. Investors are relying on the 6% yield for a substantial portion of expected return.

The bear case illustrates net-debt amplification. Kinetik does not need to fail operationally for equity value to fall; it only needs projects to earn lower returns and the enterprise multiple to normalize. The dividend may soften the path, but a payout funded by persistent borrowing would not create value.

The bull case is attractive but demanding. It requires nearly 12% long-run revenue growth, margin expansion, stable shares, and no destructive financing. A strategic sale could realize some value sooner, but no transaction premium is included because the reported process is early-stage and unconfirmed by the company.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2026 revenue and adjusted EPS use visible StockAnalysis / S&P Global consensus. FY2027 base revenue uses the visible headline value. All FY2028-FY2036 rows are extrapolations because detailed later public estimates were gated.
- The projection holds economic shares constant at 162.38 million, including Class A and Class C interests. This is optimistic if dilution continues and conservative if repurchases reduce the full economic denominator.
- Revenue is a noisy proxy because product sales are reported gross. Operating-income and net-income margins are scenario assumptions, not company guidance.
- FCF yield uses scenario FCF margins of 6% bear, 12% base, and 16% bull divided by projected economic market capitalization. It is not the same as Kinetik's distributable cash flow definition.
- Revenue growth is YoY for consecutive rows. FY2031 and FY2036 growth rates are annualized across skipped years.
- Stock prices work backward from explicit year-10 terminal P/E anchors: 22x bear, 24x base, and 18x bull. Interim prices follow a constant CAGR from the current $53.84 quote to each terminal value.
- P/E uses projected stock price divided by projected EPS, with EPS equal to projected net income divided by 162.38 million economic shares.
- Dividends are excluded from stock-price CAGR. The current $3.24 annualized distribution would materially raise total return if maintained and covered.
- The workbook's five-year probability-weighted EV/adjusted EBITDA value is $66.07. This projection is a separate ten-year GAAP-equivalent P/E cross-check.

## Bear Projection

Terminal P/E anchor: 14x year-10 adjusted EPS of $5.00, producing a $70 stock price in FY2036. The bear case assumes that Wynn Palace's favorable Q2 2026 hold normalizes without enough volume growth to replace it, Las Vegas and Boston property margins stay under pressure, and Wynn Al Marjan either costs more or ramps more slowly than expected. Revenue still grows 1% annually; this is not a demand-collapse case. The damage comes from weak operating leverage, fixed interest, and a compressed equity multiple.

Stock prices work backward from the explicit $70 terminal anchor using a constant price CAGR from the current $91.28. Adjusted EPS rises only gradually as buybacks slow and debt reduction takes priority. FCF yield stays high because the equity value falls, not because cash flow becomes stronger.

## Bull Projection

Terminal P/E anchor: 22x year-10 adjusted EPS of $10.00, producing a $220 stock price in FY2036. The bull case assumes Macau premium-mass volumes remain durable after hold normalization, Las Vegas restores property margin, Encore Boston stops declining, and Al Marjan opens on schedule and becomes a material new earnings pool. Revenue compounds 6.5% annually, partly reflecting the new property rather than same-store organic growth alone.

The stock-price path works backward from $220 at a 9.2% annual CAGR. The workbook's five-year bull target is $168; this table reaches $141.8 in year five because the projection reserves more of the Al Marjan ramp for years six through ten. ## Extrapolation Notes

1. Public numerical estimates are visible for FY2026 and as headline values for FY2027. Every row after FY2027 is an explicit scenario extrapolation, not analyst consensus.
2. Revenue begins from the $7.49 billion FY2026 consensus. Bear, base, and bull long-run growth rates are 1.0%, 4.0%, and 6.5%.
3. Stock prices are mathematical paths working backward from explicit year-10 adjusted P/E anchors: 14x on $5.00, 18x on $8.00, and 22x on $10.00.
4. The EPS figures are normalized adjusted diluted values. Reported GAAP earnings will differ because of pre-opening expense, property charges, derivative fair value, foreign-currency movements, taxes, and noncontrolling interests.
5. Share count is not shown separately. The scenarios embed different repurchase outcomes: minimal reduction in bear, moderate reduction in base, and continued accretive reduction in bull.
6. FCF yield uses normalized scenario FCF relative to scenario market capitalization. It should not be read as distributable yield while Al Marjan is under construction; joint-venture contributions and growth capital consume cash.
7. Enterprise value remains a necessary cross-check. At the quote date, net debt was approximately $10.24 billion, EV/EBITDA was 10.78x, and interest coverage was 1.92x. A P/E-based upside case is not credible if those leverage measures deteriorate materially.
8. The Q2 2026 Wynn Palace mass-market win rate of 29.7% was unusually favorable relative to the prior year's 22.3%. None of the scenarios annualizes that reported hold.
9. The September 2027 Al Marjan opening target comes from Wynn's August 4, 2026 release. The bull and base cases assume opening near that date; the bear assumes cost or ramp pressure.
10. The primary monitoring variables are property EBITDAR after hold normalization, Al Marjan's remaining Wynn contribution, debt refinancing terms, and the direction of FY2027 adjusted EPS revisions from $5.16.

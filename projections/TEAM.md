## Current Run-Rate

Atlassian generated $6.572 billion of fiscal 2026 revenue, up 26.0%, with $1.319 billion of reported FCF and a 20.07% FCF margin. Q4 revenue was $1.766 billion, up 28%, cloud revenue grew 31% to $1.213 billion, subscription ARR grew 23% to $6.606 billion, and RPO grew 44% to $4.817 billion. Those demand signals support the public FY2027 revenue anchor even though management expects total growth to slow to approximately 13% as Data Center revenue declines.

Reported FCF is not owner earnings without qualification. Fiscal 2026 SBC was $1.607 billion, exceeding FCF, while repurchases consumed $1.800 billion and cash acquisitions consumed $1.229 billion. Cash and investments fell to $1.240 billion and almost exactly matched $1.233 billion of debt. The tables therefore use reported FCF margins for comparability, but stock values work backward from normalized net income and explicit terminal P/E anchors. A reader should haircut the paths if SBC does not fall materially relative to revenue.

## Scenario Read

The one-year range brackets the public FY2027 forecast. Bear revenue of $7.40 billion is near the visible low end, base equals the $7.48 billion consensus, and bull equals the visible $7.9 billion high. The FY2027 normalized net income paths similarly bracket the public adjusted-EPS range after allowing for share-count assumptions. None of the later operating rows should be read as sell-side consensus.

The long-term dispersion is driven by owner conversion. Atlassian already has an 85.64% gross margin and low physical capex, so the decisive question is whether product investment and equity compensation become more efficient. If SBC continues to exceed FCF, a 24x or 30x terminal multiple overstates distributable economics even if reported cash flow rises. If SBC declines materially and the share count falls without balance-sheet depletion, the base and bull paths become more credible.

The base 8.3% annual stock-price return is not enough to ignore execution risk at a starting 37.36x P/FCF. The bull offers attractive compounding but requires multiple unproven mechanisms. The bear shows that Atlassian can remain a successful company while shareholders lose purchasing power: slower growth and ordinary multiple normalization are sufficient.

## Key Assumptions

- FY2027 base revenue of $7.48 billion and adjusted EPS of $5.42 are visible StockAnalysis consensus as of September 3, 2026. EPS is non-GAAP adjusted.
- FY2028 base revenue of $8.57 billion and adjusted EPS of $6.74 are visible StockAnalysis headline estimates. Detailed public low/high tables were gated.
- FY2029, FY2031, and FY2036 are extrapolated in every scenario. No gated figures are represented as public consensus.
- Adjusted operating income is scenario-specific and excludes substantial costs in a manner broadly comparable to management's non-GAAP presentation. It is not GAAP operating income.
- Normalized net income applies explicit margins and share counts. It is not a company forecast and should be judged alongside SBC.
- Bear shares fall from about 253 million to 240 million by FY2036; base to 230 million; bull to 215 million. These paths require repurchases to exceed grants on a net basis.
- Reported FCF margins rise toward 16% bear, 22% base, and 25% bull. FCF yield equals projected FCF divided by projected market capitalization at each scenario's share count and price.
- Terminal P/E anchors are 18x, 24x, and 30x normalized diluted EPS. The base and bull multiples require durable recurring growth and much cleaner owner conversion than fiscal 2026.
- Interim prices use a constant annualized path from $194.68 to the explicit FY2036 terminal value. They are valuation paths, not short-term forecasts.
- Revenue growth is year over year for FY2027-FY2029. FY2031 and FY2036 percentages are annualized from the preceding displayed row.

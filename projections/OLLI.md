## Read

- The bear case is a growth-deceleration and multiple-compression case. Revenue still compounds from $2.98B (FY2027 consensus) to $4B by FY2036, and operating margins settle near 8.8%, but growth decelerates to 3-5% and the terminal P/E compresses to 12x — a discount-store floor multiple. The bear 10-year stock price of $66.6 is 4.5% above current, which means even the bear case doesn't destroy capital — just significantly underperforms the market. This is the key insight: Ollie's generates real FCF and earnings even in a deceleration scenario.

- The base case is the most likely outcome: 10% revenue growth for the near term decelerating to 5.5% by year 10, gross margins holding near 40%+ with operating margins stabilizing at 10-12%, and a terminal P/E of 16x. This produces a 10-year stock price of $160 and a CAGR of 9.9% from current. The base case implies that the closeout model has partial durability — enough to sustain above-average growth and margins, but not enough to support the 33x TTM peak multiple.

- The bull case requires the closeout merchandise model to prove structurally superior to traditional discount retail, with growth accelerating toward 13% and operating margins expanding toward 12.5%. The 10-year terminal P/E of 22x reflects a premium for a differentiated business model that captures the value-seeking consumer trend permanently. This is the Truist $80-to-$120 thesis extended, and it represents the upside catalyst if Q2 FY2027 confirms the growth and margin trajectory.

- The model workbook uses a WACC of 6.51% and FCF multiple exit valuation. Revenue and EPS anchors for FY2027 and FY2028 come from Yahoo Finance public estimates (15 analysts each). Beyond FY2028, all figures are extrapolated based on the research note scenario model growth assumptions. The terminal P/E anchors (12x bear, 16x base, 22x bull) are chosen to reflect discount retail multiple ranges: dollar general (DG) currently trades at ~15x, dollar tree (DLTR) at ~12x, and Big Lots at ~14x. Ollie's closeout differentiation above traditional dollar store multiples is the bull case differentiator.

- Shares outstanding assumed constant at 60.45M for all projections. Actuals show declining diluted shares due to buyback program (61.6M TTM vs 62.7M FY2023), which would provide additional per-share value accretion beyond these flat-share-count projections. The buyback program ($110M TTM, ~2.9% of market cap) is a meaningful value driver at current depressed prices.

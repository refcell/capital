## Bear Projection

Terminal P/FFO anchor: 3.3x. FFO CAGR 3.0%. Revenue CAGR 3.0%. The bear case assumes the cost of debt environment remains elevated or worsens, the Virgin Media O2 and data center acquisitions underperform on yield, and the market loses patience with the net-burning cash flow profile. Operating margins stay near current levels because contracted infrastructure revenues are stable, but FFO per-share growth is suppressed by higher interest expense, potential dilution from equity raises, and management fee drag from Brookfield. The stock compresses toward 3.3x P/FFO — consistent with distressed utility platforms facing refinancing risk. At current FFO of $10.06/share and only 3% growth, upside is minimal and the distribution yield becomes the primary value driver.

Revenue growth at 3% for years 3-10 is extrapolated beyond visible public estimates (which are limited to Q2 FY26). This represents a slow-growth utility scenario where acquisitions add top-line but FFO accretion is offset by debt service and fee costs.

## Framework Footnote

P/FFO is used as the valuation framework because: (1) GAAP net income is structurally distorted by $4.3B in D&A flowing through cost of revenue, (2) minority interest of $29.9B means common shareholders capture only $5.6B of $35.5B total equity, (3) free cash flow is negative during the capex investment cycle and unsuitable as a multiple denominator. P/FFO is the standard infrastructure platform metric. EV/EBITDA (7.1x current) serves as a cross-check but produces misleading bear-case targets when net debt of $59.4B overwhelms implied EV at compressed multiples.

FFO is estimated as Net Income + D&A = $319M + $4,293M = $4,612M. FFO per share = $4,612M / 458.65M = $10.06. Current P/FFO = $41.76 / $10.06 = 4.15x.

Revenue growth and operating income growth in the tables are proportional to FFO growth — each scenario applies the FFO CAGR uniformly to derive revenue and earnings trajectories. Net income is estimated at approximately 1.2-1.23% of revenue, consistent with the TTM relationship, but should not be used as the primary valuation metric for the reasons stated above.
